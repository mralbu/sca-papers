## Initial Water Saturation and Oil Recovery from Chalk and Sandstone by Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA1998-14, year: 1998
- Authors: B. G. Viksund, N. R. Morrow, S. Ma, W. Wang, A. Graue
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, initial water saturation, chalk, Berea sandstone, dimensionless time scaling, oil recovery
- Cited by 8 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-14)

Spontaneous imbibition tests on Berea sandstone and several chalks show initial water saturation strongly lowers chalk recovery, barely affects Berea recovery, and shifts scaled imbibition rate differently in each.

Spontaneous imbibition drives recovery in fractured reservoirs, but the effect of initial water saturation (Swi) was little studied for chalk. The authors ran 59 imbibition tests on Berea sandstone and Rørdal, Seaton (low and high porosity), Stevns and other chalks, with Swi from 0 to about 51% set by drying, porous plate, oilflood, evaporation or adsorption. Rates were scaled with a modified Mattax-Kyte dimensionless time and compared with a zero-Swi reference curve. Berea final recovery varied only a few percent (about 52-56% OOIP), while Rørdal recovery fell from 67% to 30% as Swi rose from 7.5 to 51%. Chalk imbibition rate increased with Swi up to about 34% then decreased, whereas Berea rate fell, reached a minimum at 6-15% and rose again above 15%. These trends are attributed to the balance between falling capillary pressure and rising water mobility, with water relative permeability higher in chalk than in Berea.

## Enhanced Oil Recovery by Dilution of Injection Brine: Further Interpretation of Experimental Results

- ID: SCA2001-13, year: 2001
- Authors: Jos G. Maas, Krijn Wit, Norman R. Morrow
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, brine dilution, history matching, water relative permeability, spontaneous imbibition, Buckley-Leverett
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-13)

History matching of Berea core imbibition and waterflood experiments with diluted brine shows dilution mainly lowers water relative permeability endpoint (about 5x); field-scale impact is assessed qualitatively.

Earlier core experiments on Berea with Dagang crude showed that 10- and 100-fold dilution of brine raised oil recovery in spontaneous imbibition and waterflooding, but no pressure data were available for SCAL parameters. The authors history-matched these experiments with Shell's MoReS simulator in 1-D. Spontaneous imbibition required a small positive capillary pressure (about 0.2-0.4 kPa) over a wide saturation range, with the zero-crossing shifting to higher water saturation on dilution. Waterflood breakthrough delay (0.3 to 0.4-0.5 PV) could only be matched by cutting the water relative permeability endpoint from about 0.2 to 0.05, while effects on Sor (<5% PV) and kro (factor <2) were small. Buckley-Leverett analysis suggests delayed breakthrough and accelerated production in virgin reservoirs, with smaller benefits in dipping reservoirs and brown fields; 2-D scouting simulations and SCAL measurements are recommended.

## The Effect of Fluid Viscosities on Counter-Current Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2004-11, year: 2004
- Authors: D. Ruth, G. Mason
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: counter-current spontaneous imbibition, viscosity scaling, mobility ratio, Corey relative permeability, analytical solution, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-11)

Using an approximate analytical solution, the paper tests viscosity scaling of counter-current imbibition and proposes an empirical correlation that fits Berea sandstone over all viscosity combinations.

Existing scaling of counter-current spontaneous imbibition uses the square root of the viscosity product, which degrades when viscosities diverge and lacks a mechanistic basis. The authors use an approximate self-similar analytical solution with Corey relative permeabilities and a logarithmic capillary pressure, parameterized for Berea sandstone (K=1.064 D, porosity 0.219). Non-wetting viscosity was varied from 0.001 to 10000 cp with wetting viscosity of 1 and 10 cp. The analytical time-scale factor agrees well with numerical simulation, except at very high displaced-phase viscosity. The square-root model holds over about three orders of magnitude around unit mobility ratio, a tube-bundle model gives the right trend but poor agreement, and a modified form with exponent m=3 matched to the mobility-ratio limits fits all data without matching at any point.

## Scaling of Viscosity Ratio for Oil Recovery by Imbibition from Mixed-Wet Rocks

- ID: SCA2001-21, year: 2001
- Authors: Zhengxin Tong, Xina Xie, Norman R. Morrow Chemical
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, mixed wettability, viscosity ratio scaling, dimensionless time, initial water saturation, decalin flush
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-21.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-21)

Spontaneous imbibition into mixed-wet Berea sandstone with mineral oils of 3.8–180 cp is correlated by the geometric mean of oil and water viscosities, despite much slower rates than water-wet.

Scaling of imbibition recovery for viscosity ratio had been established for very strongly water-wet rocks but not for mixed-wet conditions. Berea sandstone cores (~80–106 md, 18.5% porosity) were aged in asphaltic Alaska 95 crude at 75 or 95°C, flushed with 5–20 PV decalin, then with mineral oils of 3.8–180 cp, and tested by spontaneous imbibition of brine. Mixed-wet imbibition was orders of magnitude slower than very strongly water-wet, and water-wetness decreased as initial water saturation (11–28%) decreased and increased with decalin flush volume. Duplicate cores gave reproducible results. With Swi held near 21%, recovery curves for different oil viscosities collapsed using dimensionless time with the geometric mean viscosity. Berea 90 also showed lower VSWW recovery (~47%) and longer dimensionless times than higher-permeability Berea.

## A Numerical Study of the Influence of Sample Shape on Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2003-16, year: 2003
- Authors: Douglas Ruth, Geoffrey Mason, Norman Morrow
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, scaling characteristic length, sample shape, annular-radial geometry, finite-difference simulation, counter-current imbibition
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-16)

Finite-difference simulations of counter-current imbibition show sample shape changes curve shape for annular and hollow-spherical samples with one face open; a volume-weighted characteristic length correlates results well.

The study examines how sample geometry affects spontaneous imbibition recovery curves in a water-wet, Berea-like system (k = 600 mD, porosity 0.215). An explicit finite-difference simulator with 298 grid blocks was extended from linear to radial, spherical, annular-radial and hollow-spherical geometries, with inner, outer or both faces open and aspect ratios from 0 to 0.8. Linear, radial and spherical solid shapes give essentially the same curve shape, though rates differ by factors of about 22 to 27. Annular and hollow-spherical samples with only one face open show systematic shape differences, largest at small aspect ratio and vanishing as the ratio approaches one. With both faces open, curves are nearly identical to the linear case. The Ma et al. characteristic length works within experimental error, but a new scale factor weighting distance from the open face by local volume correlates the simulations more closely, except for inner-open cases at the lowest aspect ratios.

## Wettability Alteration by Spontaneous Imbibition of Sulphate-Containing Water into Chalk Cores with Different Boundary Conditions

- ID: SCA2008-42, year: 2008
- Authors: Liping Yu, Steinar Evje, Ingebret Fjelde, Svein M. Skjaeveland
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, chalk, sulphate seawater, boundary conditions, wettability alteration, countercurrent flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-42.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-42)

Spontaneous imbibition of sulphate-containing seawater into Stevns Klint chalk with top, bottom or both ends open shows boundary conditions and gravity affect recovery; 1D simulation supports this.

Seawater spontaneous imbibition studies in chalk usually leave all core surfaces open, so the effect of boundary conditions was unclear. Eleven Stevns Klint chalk plugs, aged in crude oil-based fluids, were sealed with epoxy to leave the top, bottom or both ends open. Imbibition of synthetic seawater (SSW) and Ekofisk formation brine was tested at 50, 70 and 130°C. With both ends open, top-end recovery exceeded bottom-end recovery, and the gap fell from about 15% to 7% as core length dropped from 7 to 4 cm, showing the role of gravity. SSW gave higher recovery than formation brine, and SSW as initial water raised recovery (about 10% to 25% top open; 12% to 27% bottom open). Lower Swi gave lower recovery (11% at 9.1% vs 16% at 25.6%). A 1D model with dynamic wettability change predicted lower recovery for bottom-end-open than top-end-open, but experiments with one end open gave no consistent trend.

## A Mechanism Study of Co-Current and Counter-Current Imbibition Using New Magnetic Resonance Techniques

- ID: SCA2005-38, year: 2005
- Authors: Quan Chena, Karl E. Butlerb, Murray K. Gingrasc, Bruce J. Balcoma
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: DDIF, Conical-SPRITE MRI, spontaneous imbibition, pore size distribution, trapped gas, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-38.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-38)

DDIF pore-size spectra and Conical-SPRITE imaging of Berea sandstone show piston-like co-current imbibition versus film-flow and thickening in counter-current imbibition, with trapped air in pores.

The pore-level mechanisms of co-current and counter-current spontaneous imbibition are not directly proven. The authors combined DDIF, which gives water-filled pore-size distributions in partially saturated rock, with 3D Conical-SPRITE imaging of saturation in a Berea sandstone (19% porosity, 0.2 D) at 2.4 T. DDIF gave a dominant pore size near 70 µm, against a mercury throat peak near 15 µm. In co-current imbibition the DDIF spectra kept the same shape at 35.5% and 57.9% saturation, and images showed a rectangular, piston-like front. In counter-current imbibition, pores were poorly filled while throats filled early (26.3% saturation after 8 s). Images showed uniform saturation behind merged fronts, consistent with film thickening, and equilibrium was reached near 56% saturation with air trapped in pore centers.

## In-Situ Saturation Development During Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA1999-22, year: 1999
- Authors: B. A. Baldwin, E. A. Spinler
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, MRI saturation profiles, initial water saturation, chalk, water films, Amott wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-22.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-22)

MRI monitoring of spontaneous water imbibition in chalk plugs shows sharp fronts at zero initial water saturation but uniform saturation change at high initial water, suggesting water-film thickening.

The paper uses MRI (Varian 2T, ~300-350 µm pixels) to image in-situ decane saturation during spontaneous imbibition of D2O into one-inch reservoir chalk plugs. Initial water saturations of 0, 15, 22 and 33%PV were tested on a highly water-wet plug, plus a less water-wet plug (Amott 0.5) at 15%PV. At Swi=0 a steep front (gradient 2.2 fractional saturation/cm) formed, with no further oil production behind it, and water took about 1.25 hr to reach the plug center. As Swi increased the gradient fell (1.5, 1.0, then 0.1-0.7) and time to reach the center dropped; at 33% in the water-wet plug and 15% in the less water-wet plug no front appeared. The authors propose two mechanisms: bulk displacement and a long-range process such as thickening of water films. Average saturation curves resembled standard imbibition tests and final water saturation was independent of Swi.

## Magnetic Resonance Imaging of Oil Recovery During Spontaneous Imbibition from Cores with Two-Ends Open Boundary Condition

- ID: SCA2011-24, year: 2011
- Authors: S. Wickramathilaka, J.J. Howard, J.C. Stevens, N.R. Morrow, G. Mason, A. Graue, M.A. Fernø
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, two-ends-open boundary condition, MRI saturation profiles, characteristic length, asymmetric oil production, very strongly water-wet
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-24)

Oil production from the two open ends of TEO chalk and limestone cores was asymmetric, and MRI showed piston-like fronts starting from the same end regardless of gravity.

Two-Ends-Open spontaneous imbibition often scales poorly with characteristic length, so oil production was measured separately from each end face of 13 chalk cores. Production was frequently asymmetric, with up to about 85% from one face, independent of initial water and unrelated to total production rate. MRI (2 T, 0.8 mm³ resolution) of Whitestone UZ limestone saturated with n-decane and imbibing D2O brine showed sharp piston-like fronts. In vertical TEO tests the front started from the same end face when the core was inverted, with oil split 84.4/15.6% and 16/84% between ends. Oil recovery and frontal distance were linear in square root of time. The authors conclude that subtle end-face differences, likely in capillary back pressure, outweigh gravity, and that both co- and counter-current flow occur.

## Effect of Salinity on Oil Recovery by Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2010-12, year: 2010
- Authors: Siluni Wickramathilaka, Norman R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, spontaneous imbibition, mixed wettability, imbibition scaling group, reservoir sandstone, cation exchange capacity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-12)

Spontaneous imbibition tests on limestone, Berea and reservoir sandstone show low salinity brine raises oil recovery in sandstones but not in Edwards limestone.

The study examines how invading brine salinity affects oil recovery by spontaneous imbibition, using 27 tests on Edwards Garden City limestone, Berea sandstone and a siderite-rich reservoir sandstone (RS) with two high-asphaltene crude oils. Edwards limestone, tested both very strongly water-wet with mineral oils and mixed-wet with crude, showed no systematic recovery increase with dilution of seawater. Berea sandstone gave about 8% OOIP more oil with RS low salinity brine than with RS seawater, with little change in imbibition rate. RS cores immersed in low salinity brine recovered 16.7-30.5% OOIP versus 6.2-12.7% for seawater, and switching from seawater to low salinity brine produced additional oil, whereas the reverse did not. Effluent pH fell in all RS tests, conductivity rose with low salinity imbibition, and final recovery correlated with total clay content; post-imbibition low salinity waterflood recovered further oil, correlating with CEC.

## Measurement of Core Properties Using a New Technique – Two Ends Open Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2014-006, year: 2014
- Authors: Martin A. Fernø, Åsmund Haugen, Geoffrey Mason, R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, two ends open, capillary back pressure, co-current and counter-current flow, piston-like displacement, chalk
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-006.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-006)

A two-ends-open imbibition setup with brine at one end and oil at the other, plus piston-like theory, yields relative permeabilities and capillary back pressure in chalk.

Conventional AFO and OEO imbibition give square-root-of-time production, so only one lumped parameter can be extracted. The authors use a two-ends-open geometry with brine at one face and bulk oil at the other, measuring oil production separately at each end and pressure at tappings. Rørdal chalk cores were tested with oil/brine viscosity ratios of 1.3, 76 and 126. Production starts counter-current at the brine face and then becomes co-current; the counter-current share rose from about 4-6% (low viscosity) to 25-33% (high viscosity). A piston-like displacement theory fitted production well after counter-current flow ceased, giving NWP relative permeability behind the front of about 0.8 (137 cP) and 0.5 (83 cP). Capillary back pressure at the brine face was about half the front capillary pressure (for example 80 vs 128 kPa), while pressure predictions fit poorly.

## Numerical Simulation of Combined Co-Current/Counter-Current Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2015-002, year: 2015
- Authors: Douglas W. Ruth, Geoffrey Mason, Martin A. Fernø, Åsmund Haugen, Norman R. Morrow, Rasoul Arabjamaloei
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, co-current counter-current, TEO-free boundary condition, history matching, capillary pressure, piston-like flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-002.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-002)

Simulations history-match three published TEO-free spontaneous imbibition experiments with one relative permeability set and fitted capillary pressure curves, largely supporting piston-like flow theory.

The paper simulates combined co- and counter-current spontaneous imbibition with the TEO-free boundary condition, using an explicit upstream-differencing simulator with zero-width boundary cells and 50 grid blocks. Three previously published experiments (samples CHP07, CHP11, CHP25; permeability about 4-5.5 mD, oil/water viscosity ratios from 1.35 to about 125) were history matched using a single relative permeability set and a six-point capillary pressure table as the fitting parameter, with face oil productions as match data. Fitted capillary pressure curves were similar in shape and magnitude across samples, and the unfitted pressure histories were reproduced well, including the pressure spike in CHP07. Saturation profiles showed a frontal displacement with a wedge-shaped region, and front capillary pressures and saturations agreed reasonably with piston-like flow theory, though relative permeabilities at the front differed. Different capillary pressure curves gave comparable errors, and the inlet face saturation was set to the final average saturation, so results need cautious use.

## Quick and Affordable SCAL: Spontaneous Core Analysis

- ID: SCA2015-003, year: 2015
- Authors: M. A. Fernøa, B. Brattekåsa, G. Masond, N. Morrowd
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, TEO-free boundary condition, capillary back pressure, PET imaging, piston-like displacement, chalk
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-003.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-003)

Spontaneous imbibition with a TEO-free boundary, analysed with a piston-like M2HF model and verified by PET imaging, gives brine relative permeability and capillary pressure, including nonzero back pressure.

The paper proposes a quick, low-cost SCAL method based on spontaneous imbibition with the two-ends-open free (TEO-free) boundary, where one core end contacts brine and the other oil. Oil production is measured separately at each end, and pressure is measured along the core. Two epoxy-coated chalk plugs (CHP26, 8.88 cm and CHP27, 11.8 cm) were imbibed with decane, and PET/CT tracked the water front. The front was piston-like and self-similar, and the M2HF model then gave brine relative permeability behind the front of 0.31-0.33 and front capillary pressure of 64-78 kPa. Counter-current production was only 10-11% of the total (recovery 58-61% OOIP). The capillary back pressure at the open face was 22-34 kPa, about 1/3 of the driving pressure, so it cannot be assumed zero.

## Paper - SCA # 9018 Imbibition Flooding with CO2-Enriched Water

- ID: SCA1990-18, year: 1990
- Authors: S. G. Grape, S. W. Poston, J. S. Osoba
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water imbibition, CO2 enhanced oil recovery, fractured reservoirs, capillary retention, limestone dissolution, contact angle
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-18)

Spontaneous imbibition of 1% carbonated water in 1-in. sandstone and limestone cores gives faster, higher oil recovery than brine, by 6-16% OOIP over 72 hours.

Fractured, low-permeability matrix reservoirs leave much oil behind because injected fluids bypass the matrix, so the authors tested carbonated water to improve imbibition rate and recovery. Visual imbibition cells held 1-in. sandstone and limestone cores saturated with 35 API diesel. Cores were soaked 72 hours at 70°F and atmospheric pressure in 20,000 ppm NaCl brine or 1% CO2 water (pH 5). CO2 also reduced capillary retention of oil droplets on the rock face and the contact angle, and depressurization gave solution-gas drive. The Graham and Richardson sqrt(k/φ) correlation held for sandstone but not limestone.

## “Studies of Carbonated Water Imbibition Using MRI”

- ID: SCA1992-21, year: 1992
- Authors: I. ABSTRACT
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water imbibition, MRI saturation profiles, fractured reservoirs, CO2 solution gas drive, oil swelling, spontaneous imbibition
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-21.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-21)

MRI monitoring of carbonated water imbibition in oil-saturated limestone cores shows CO2 acts near the imbibing face, and blow-down gas drive substantially raises oil recovery.

Oil recovery from low-permeability matrix blocks in fractured reservoirs by spontaneous imbibition is slow, so CO2-enriched water imbibition was tested as an accelerant. One-inch by three-inch limestone cores (22-26% porosity, 7-12 md) were tested in conventional displacement cells at 70-150 °F, and in a fiberglass core holder at 2,000 psi using D2O saturation and MRI with reference samples to normalize signals and map oil saturation. MRI showed oil saturation changes concentrated near the imbibing face, and depressurizing below the CO2 mixing pressure drove a localized gas drive that also cleaned oil from the core face. In an Austin Chalk oil test, recovery rose from 7% with water to 24% with carbonated water, and to 31% after two blow-down cycles. Carbonated water also raised permeability by up to 20%, and recovery followed an exponential trend.

## Study of Waterflooding Process in Naturally Fractured Reservoirs from Static and Dynamic Imbibition Experiments

- ID: SCA1999-10, year: 1999
- Authors: Erwinsyah Putra, Yan Fidra, David S. Schechter
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, fractured Spraberry, matrix capillary pressure, critical injection rate, Amott wettability index, fracture capillary number
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-10)

Static and dynamic imbibition experiments on Spraberry and Berea cores, history-matched numerically, give matrix capillary pressures, show weak water-wetness, and yield critical injection rates upscaled to field scale.

Imbibition controls oil recovery in the naturally fractured Spraberry Trend, so matrix-fracture transfer was studied with static and dynamic imbibition tests at 138°F. Spontaneous imbibition on Spraberry plugs followed by forced waterflooding gave an average Amott index of about 0.3, and a 2-D implicit finite-difference model matched recoveries to give a low imbibition capillary pressure, both indicating weakly water-wet rock. ## Oil Recovery by Spontaneous Imbibition from Weakly Water-Wet Rocks

- ID: SCA2000-26, year: 2000
- Authors: Xina Xie, Norman R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, weakly water-wet, initial water saturation, dimensionless time scaling, characteristic length, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-26)

Tests whether a characteristic-length imbibition scaling group works for weakly water-wet Berea cores; initial water saturation dominates rate, and epoxy sealing during aging reduces water-wetness.

The study tests a semi-empirical imbibition scaling group, developed for very strongly water-wet rock, under weakly water-wet conditions. Thirty-two Berea sandstone cores (about 450 md, 20% porosity) of cylindrical, annular and rectangular shape, some partially sealed with epoxy, were set to Swi of 14-31% and aged in Alaska 95 crude at 55°C for 10 days. Spontaneous imbibition of brine at room temperature showed that imbibition times rose by orders of magnitude as Swi decreased. Scaling with the characteristic length Lc correlated cores of different size, shape and boundary conditions well when Swi was held within a narrow range. Cores sealed with epoxy were less water-wet than all-faces-open cores, which an aging test with a separate resin block confirmed. At 20% Swi the 50% recovery time was about 514 times that for very strongly water-wet rock, and gravity could become significant if capillary pressure fell by about three orders of magnitude.

## A Scaling Method for Spontaneous Imbibition in Systems with Different Wettability

- ID: SCA2002-08, year: 2002
- Authors: Kewen Li, Roland N. Horne
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, scaling, wettability, dimensionless time, countercurrent imbibition, oil recovery
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-08)

Derives a dimensionless time including relative permeability, capillary pressure and wettability that scales published countercurrent imbibition data at different wettability, and shows recovery is linear in the square root of time.

Existing scaling groups for spontaneous imbibition require identical wettability and relative permeability and capillary pressure functions, so they fail when wettability varies.
