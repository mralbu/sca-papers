## Interfacial Tension Measurements in an Oil/Water/Bacteria System by Laser-Light Scattering

- ID: SCA2004-10, year: 2004
- Authors: E. Kowalewski, J.Å. Stensen, E. Gilje, G. Bødtker, B. Bjørkvik, K.A. Strand
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: interfacial tension, laser-light scattering, microbial enhanced oil recovery, capillary waves, biosurfactant, Dietzia
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-10)

Interface laser-light scattering monitored dodecane/nutrient-water IFT with Dietzia strain A14101, showing a drop from 35 to 0.17 mN/m over about two weeks.

Microbial improved oil recovery may rely on bacterial IFT reduction, but conventional tensiometers such as pendant drop cannot follow IFT over weeks of bacterial growth. The authors applied interface laser-light scattering, which analyzes thermally excited capillary waves without disturbing the interface, to an n-dodecane/nutrient water system at 20 °C. The reference IFT was 35.4 mN/m before bacteria injection. After adding strain A14101 (genus Dietzia), IFT fell by about a factor of 200 to a minimum of 0.17 mN/m within about 18 days, then stayed roughly constant with a slight rise. The final bacteria density was 8.8e6 cells/ml, and the plateau may reflect oxygen or nutrient depletion, so a larger reduction may be possible. The method gave about 2-3% IFT accuracy with no sign of local IFT variation, whereas an earlier pendant drop study reached only 10 mN/m.

## Analyzing Microbial Improved Oil Recovery Processes from Core Floods

- ID: SCA2005-49, year: 2005
- Authors: E. Kowalewski, Sunde E, G. Bødtker, T. Torsvik
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: microbial enhanced oil recovery, cryo-ESEM, wettability alteration, interfacial tension, image-analysis saturation, Bentheimer sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-49.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-49)

Cryo-ESEM imaging of a Bentheimer core after aerobic MIOR flooding shows it remained mainly water-wet, and image-derived oil saturation (36%) matched flood-derived saturation (35%).

MIOR mechanisms (IFT reduction, wettability change, selective blocking) are reviewed, noting that traditional SCAL methods are poorly suited to growing bacterial systems. A Bentheimer sandstone core (30 cm, 2400 mD, porosity 0.23) was waterflooded to residual oil, then aerobically flooded with bacteria, reducing Sor from 0.44 to 0.34. Slices from inlet, centre and outlet were frozen in liquid nitrogen, fractured under vacuum and imaged by cryo-ESEM with EDS elemental mapping to distinguish oil, brine and minerals without dopants. Most grains remained water-wet, with only a few partially oil-wet quartz grains, suggesting little wettability change. BSE image analysis gave oil saturations of 29-40% by region and an average of 36% versus 35% from the flood, though too few images were analyzed for a reliable saturation profile.

## An Experimental Study of Microbial Improved Oil Recovery by Using Rhodococcus SP. 094

- ID: SCA2005-45, year: 2005
- Authors: C. Crescente, K. Rasmussen, O. Torsæter, E. Kowalewski
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: microbial enhanced oil recovery, Rhodococcus sp. 094, biosurfactant, selective plugging, interfacial tension, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-45.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-45)

Berea core floods with biosurfactant-producing and non-producing Rhodococcus sp. 094 show MIOR recovers more dodecane than waterflooding, via IFT reduction, selective plugging and wettability change.

The study examines how Rhodococcus sp. 094 enhances oil recovery in Berea sandstone cores saturated with dodecane. Bacteria were grown on dodecane (biosurfactant-producing, SP) or acetate (non-producing, NSP) and injected either after waterflooding (MIORWF) or directly from initial oil saturation (MIOR0), at 0.1 ml/min and 30°C. Bacterial flooding recovered more oil than waterflooding in most tests, with MIOR0 generally better than MIORWF; SP gave lower IFT (22.6 vs 41.7 mN/m) and quartz wettability shifted from water-wet to intermediate. NSP bacteria recovered faster early, but SP often gave higher ultimate recovery; residual oil saturations fell to 1-6% in long floods (over 120 PV). Pressure rose continuously, indicating selective plugging, bacterial cakes formed at the inlet, no relation between cell concentration (1e7-1e9 cells/ml) and recovery was found, and some results were anomalous and need repeating.

## To What Degree Thermal Cycles Affect Chalk Strength

- ID: SCA2016-087, year: 2016
- Authors: Tijana Livada, Anders Nermoen, Reidar Inger Korsnes, Ida Lykke Fabricius
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: thermal cycling, Kansas chalk, Brazilian tensile strength, hydrostatic compaction, irreversible strain, bulk modulus
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-087.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-087)

Thermal cycling of Kansas chalk leaves dry tensile strength unchanged but increases irreversible strain and bulk modulus in water-saturated hydrostatic tests, indicating a pore fluid role.

Repeated cold water injection into hot chalk reservoirs may destabilize them, so the study tests how thermal cycling affects Kansas chalk (about 34% porosity, 0.3-0.9 mD). Sixty dry disks were cycled 0-50 times between 135°C and ambient and tested in Brazilian tension; mean tensile strength stayed about 3.1-3.3 MPa, but standard deviation doubled from 0.40 to 0.82 MPa after 50 cycles. Two water-saturated cores were cycled hydrostatically (1.2-5.2 MPa), one at constant 30°C and one with daily 30-130°C cycles over 10 days. The temperature-cycled core accumulated 0.40% volumetric strain versus 0.25% for the constant-temperature core, with irreversible strain fraction of about 18% versus 4% at cycle 10, and a higher bulk modulus. An 11th cycle without temperature change dropped the irreversible fraction to 2.5%, confirming the effect was thermal. The contrasting dry and saturated results suggest the pore fluid matters.

## Mark A. Andersen

- ID: SCA1992-21EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Mark A. Andersen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: chalk compaction, waterflood, rate-type compaction model, creep, uniaxial strain, Valhall
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-21EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-21EURO)

Uniaxial-strain waterflood tests on native-state Valhall chalk show localized compaction of about 1% at the water front, explained by interfacial forces and friction-based rate-type behavior.

High-porosity, weakly cemented North Sea chalk compacts under stress, and brine injection has been reported to trigger additional compaction. Andersen tested Valhall Tor chalk with preserved native saturation (about 5% water) in uniaxial strain, loading at 0.1% strain/hour with creep periods, then waterflooding with simulated seawater after creep at 20.6 or 27.5 MPa. Water arrival caused rapid, localized compaction following the flood front, about 1% bulk strain over six days, seen above yield but negligible in pre-yield material. An estimate of the capillary shear force on particle structures (about 0.2 dyne against 3.2 dyne normal force per particle) supports a meniscus-triggered cascade of frictional failure. After further loading the waterflooded sample returned to the virgin compaction curve, so the long-term compaction matches the unflooded case. Calcium dissolution and thermal cooling (about 0.2%) cannot explain the volume loss.

## The Effects of Temperature on the Water Weakening of Chalk by Seawater

- ID: SCA2006-37, year: 2006
- Authors: Reidar I. Korsnes, Merete V. Madland, Tor Austad, Stig Haver, Geir Røsland
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: chalk water weakening, seawater flooding, hydrostatic yield point, creep compaction, Mg2+/Ca2+ substitution, sulfate
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-37)

Static and flooding creep tests on Stevns Klint chalk at 70-130 °C show seawater sulfate and magnesium cause temperature-dependent enhanced weakening and compaction versus sulfate-free brine or distilled water.

Seawater injection in North Sea chalk causes extra compaction, and this study examines how temperature affects the weakening mechanism. Stevns Klint outcrop chalk was tested hydrostatically, either after static aging in distilled water or seawater at 80-130 °C, or during flooding and creep at 70, 90 and 130 °C with seawater (SSW) and sulfate-free seawater (SSW-U). Aged in distilled water, chalk was stronger at 130 °C (10.3 MPa yield) than at 80 °C (6.1 MPa), whereas seawater-aged cores gave about 6.9 MPa at both temperatures. In flooding tests at 90 °C, yield was 4.8 MPa with SSW versus 7.3 MPa with SSW-U, and creep strain differed by a factor of about 2.7 after 8-9 PV. At 70 °C the yield points were similar (7.7 vs 7.3 MPa), but SSW still compacted about twice as much in creep. Effluent Ca2+ rose about 10% at 90 °C, supporting Mg2+–Ca2+ substitution at grain contacts that requires sulfate and weakens with falling temperature.

## Investigation into the Mechanisms of Heavy Oil Recovery by Waterflooding and Alkali-Surfactant Flooding

- ID: SCA2007_24, year: 2007
- Authors: J. Bryan, A. Kantzas
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: heavy oil waterflooding, alkali-surfactant flooding, NMR T2 spectra, water imbibition, emulsification, unconsolidated sand
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_24)

Low-field NMR of heavy oil sand-pack waterfloods and alkali-surfactant floods shows water imbibition aids recovery at low rates and AS flooding generates emulsions that add 11% oil.

Heavy oil (11,500 mPa·s) recovery by waterflooding and alkali-surfactant (AS) flooding was studied in unconsolidated Lane Mountain sand packs using low-field NMR. Static vial tests showed brine imbibing into small pores of the oil-coated sand, with water T2gm decreasing over 1702 hrs, and a Bond number of about 4.7e-6 ruled out gravity. In waterfloods at 0.45 and 8.89 cm3/hr, NMR spectra and recovery data indicated water films from imbibition at the low rate, which gave better recovery. In a 53 cm, 0.79 D pack, DI-water AS flooding raised average Sw from 0.291 to 0.345 (5.5% additional oil) and a following brine AS flood reached 0.403 (11% total additional oil), uniformly along the core. Produced black low-viscosity fluid indicated in-situ O/W emulsion flow, while a slow-relaxing bulk water peak in NMR indicated W/O emulsions that plug water channels and improve sweep, especially with NaCl.

## Microbial Enhanced Oil Recovery - Mechanism

- ID: SCA2007_25, year: 2007
- Authors: Aksel Hiorth, Krista Kaster, Arild Lohne, Ola K. Siqveland, Harald Berland, Nils H. Giske, Arne Stavland
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: microbial enhanced oil recovery, biofilm, Lattice Boltzmann, wettability alteration, capillary tube meniscus, contact angle
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_25)

Argues microbial concentrations are too low for surfactant or gas mechanisms, so biofilm-driven wettability change dominates; supported by Lattice Boltzmann modelling, capillary tube and contact angle experiments.

Core-scale MEOR results are hard to interpret, so the authors assess which microbial mechanisms are physically plausible. Simple mass estimates with about 10^6 cells/ml give polymer, surfactant or acid concentrations around 10^-5 kg/kg water, at least two orders of magnitude too low, and gas saturations of only about 10^-4. They model microbes as immiscible drops in a 2D Lattice Boltzmann simulation; drops adhering to a capillary wall reduce meniscus height by about 50%, against 10% for non-adhering drops, which indicates a wettability change rather than an interfacial tension change. Capillary tube experiments with Bacillus licheniformis showed a roughly 50% height decrease over 14 days, while North Sea anaerobic microbes changed the polystyrene tube height only with stirring. Static sessile drop tests on glass, carbonate and steel showed no contact angle change, so the wettability evidence remains tentative and needs more tests.

## Displacement of Viscous Oil by Polimeric Solution - Experimental Evaluation

- ID: SCA2007_46, year: 2007
- Authors: R.B.Z.L. Moreno, E.J. Bonet, A.T.A. Waldmann, A.L. Martins
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: polymer invasion, xanthan, PHPA, viscous oil displacement, X-ray saturation profile, polymer adsorption
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_46.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_46)

Twenty-five linear displacement tests in Botucatu sandstone show how polymer type, oil viscosity, pressure and rock surface treatment control xanthan and PHPA invasion into viscous-oil-saturated cores.

The study examines mechanisms of viscous oil displacement and polymer invasion, relevant to polymer flooding and drill-in fluid damage. Twenty-five linear displacement tests were run on Botucatu sandstone saturated with nujol (~100 cp) or Lubrax (~300 cp), injecting xanthan or PHPA at ~100 or ~200 psi, in natural, polymer-adsorbed or hydrophobic-treated cores. About 30% PV was injected, with mass, pressure and volumes recorded and final saturation profiles obtained by X-ray and tomography. Lubrax displacement was much slower (e.g. 245 min at 100 psi for xanthan) and often showed water breakthrough or solution bypass. Invasion depended on fluid mobility, pressure and polymer type; adsorption was an important retention mechanism, wettability change had no significant influence, and PHPA elongational effects can increase oil sweep depending on velocity.

## Micromodel Study of the Displacement Mechanisms of Enhanced Heavy Oil Recovery by Alkaline Flooding

- ID: SCA2007_47, year: 2007
- Authors: Mingzhe Dong, Qiang Liu, Aifen Li
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: alkaline flooding, heavy oil, micromodel, W/O emulsion, wettability alteration, alkaline/surfactant
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_47.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_47)

Glass micromodel floods show alkaline flooding of heavy oil works by in situ W/O emulsion with partial wettability alteration, or by O/W emulsification when surfactant is added.

Alkaline flooding can improve heavy oil recovery, but its displacement mechanisms were unclear. Glass micromodel tests were run with Western Canadian heavy oil: water saturation, oil injection at 60°C, 2 PV waterflood, then alkaline or alkaline/surfactant flooding at ambient temperature. Injecting NaOH + Na2CO3 formed viscous in situ W/O emulsion and partly oil-wet pore walls, which blocked water channels, diverted flow to unswept regions and raised pressure drop up to 12.4 kPa. After about 1 PV of A/S injection new water channels formed and sweep efficiency declined.

## Enhanced Chemical Weakening of Chalk Due to Injection of CO2 Enriched Water

- ID: SCA2008-24, year: 2008
- Authors: R.I. Korsnes, M.V. Madland, K.A.N. Vorland, T. Hildebrand-Habel, T.G. Kristiansen, A. Hiorth
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: chalk creep, CO2 flooding, water weakening, triaxial test, carbonated brine, CaSO4 precipitation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-24)

Triaxial creep tests on Roerdal chalk flooded with CO2-enriched brines show higher creep strain rates, about 19% more with seawater than distilled water, indicating chemical weakening.

CO2 injection into chalk reservoirs acidifies pore water and may enhance chemical compaction (water weakening). Roerdal outcrop chalk cores (43-48% porosity) were hydrostatically loaded beyond yield and crept at 12-14 MPa at ambient temperature, with alternating flooding of non-carbonated and CO2-enriched (8 bar) distilled water, synthetic seawater and sulphate-enriched seawater (SSW4) at 2 PV/day with 10 bar backpressure. Tests with backpressure gave strain rates 1.6 times higher than earlier tests without backpressure. Conclusions are limited by few samples and ambient temperature.

## Impact of Petropyhysical Properties on Water Flood Efficiency in Cretaceous and Tertiary Chalk

- ID: SCA2008-27, year: 2008
- Authors: Shahram Pourmohammadi, Sverre Hetland, Kristine Spildo, Arne Skauge
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: waterflood recovery, chalk, spontaneous imbibition, single-phase dispersion, Amott-Harvey and USBM wettability, NMR pore size distribution
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-27)

Waterflood tests on Tertiary and Cretaceous chalk show high recovery regardless of wettability or spontaneous imbibition, while higher single-phase dispersivity correlates with lower recovery.

The study examines which parameters control waterflood oil recovery in Tertiary (Ekofisk) and Cretaceous (Tor, Oseberg) chalk cores. Cores were characterized by mercury injection J-function, NMR pore size distribution and single-phase dispersion, then waterflooded in cleaned (water-wet) and crude-oil-aged states (90°C, one month) with Amott-Harvey and USBM wettability measurements. Aging shifted cores to mixed-wet (mostly small-pore oil-wet) states and strongly reduced spontaneous imbibition, yet waterflood recovery remained high. Tertiary chalk showed higher flowing fraction and less dead-end porosity, correlating with higher recovery, and cores with higher dispersivity gave lower recovery. The authors conclude that petrophysical properties matter more than spontaneous imbibition for chalk waterflood efficiency, provided some imbibition occurs.

## The Effect of Temperature and Brine Composition on the Mechanical Strength of Kansas Chalk

- ID: SCA2008-55, year: 2008
- Authors: M.V. Madland, K. Midtgarden, R. Manafov, R.I. Korsnes
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: water weakening, Kansas chalk, seawater composition, magnesium, anhydrite precipitation, creep
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-55.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-55)

Hydrostatic and creep tests on Kansas chalk flooded with modified seawaters show that removing Mg2+ markedly weakens chalk, likely through anhydrite precipitation and calcite dissolution.

Seawater injection in North Sea chalk causes extra compaction (water weakening), and the role of specific ions at reservoir temperature is debated. Eight Kansas outcrop chalk cores (about 38% porosity) were tested in a triaxial cell with hydrostatic loading followed by 5-day creep at 19 MPa. Continuous flooding used synthetic seawater (SSW), seawater without sulphate (SSW-U) or without magnesium (SSW-U3) at 70, 90 and 130 °C. SSW-U3 cores were weakest, with average yield about 10.7 MPa, roughly 30% below SSW-U cores (15.3-15.6 MPa), and they showed the most creep. SSW cores had intermediate yield of 13-14 MPa. Switching a core from SSW to SSW-U3 during creep gave about 30% additional strain. Temperature effects were not systematic. The authors attribute the weakening to Mg2+ removal facilitating anhydrite precipitation and enhanced calcite dissolution.

## Surfactant Enhanced Gravity Drainage: Laboratory Experiments and Numerical Simulation Model

- ID: SCA2009-06, year: 2009
- Authors: S.K. Masalmeh, S. Oedai
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: surfactant enhanced gravity drainage, centrifuge capillary pressure, interfacial tension reduction, connate water banking, effective diffusion coefficient, fractured carbonate
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-06)

Multi-speed centrifuge experiments and simulation on aged limestone show surfactant gravity drainage scales with IFT reduction, is unaffected by connate water, and is faster than molecular diffusion predicts.

Surfactant enhanced gravity drainage in fractured oil-wet carbonates may be limited by connate water banking and diffusion. Multi-speed centrifuge experiments were run on 10 aged limestone plugs (2.5-13.6 mD), eight with connate water and two without, using C12TAB surfactant at 0.1-1 wt%. Surfactant-oil IFT was about 15 times lower than water-oil, and the imbibition Pc curves scaled with this reduction, with oil produced at 500 rpm (0.43 psi) where water alone gave none. History matching with 1D/2D MoReS models needed a diffusion coefficient of 1e-10 m2/s, versus an expected ~1e-11 m2/s for molecular diffusion, and the absence of connate water did not change the Pc curves. The results indicate that connate water does not form a buffer and that diffusion is not limiting at core scale, while upscaling requires care regarding adsorption and dilution.

## Effects of Wettability and Salinity on Microbial Enhanced Oil Recovery with Rhodococcus SP. 094

- ID: SCA2009-29, year: 2009
- Authors: L. Hultmann, A. Strøm
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: microbial enhanced oil recovery, Rhodococcus sp. 094, mixed-wet Berea, low salinity, selective pore plugging, coreflooding
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-29)

Coreflooding of Berea cores shows Rhodococcus sp. 094 MEOR is more effective in mixed-wet than water-wet cores, and lowering salinity can further boost recovery.

The study tests how wettability and salinity affect microbial enhanced oil recovery with Rhodococcus sp. 094 using n-dodecane in Berea cores. Wettability runs used paired water-wet and Surfasil-treated mixed-wet cores (14 and 15 successful runs) flooded with the non-surfactant-producing variant after waterflooding. Mixed-wet cores had higher waterflood recovery (72.9% vs 57.1% OOIP) and lower residual oil (18.7% vs 29.3%). Bacterial flooding gave equal recovery on an OOIP basis (about 3%), but 11.1% vs 7.3% of residual oil for mixed-wet vs water-wet cores, indicating continuous oil films favor MEOR. Seven salinity runs (3%, 0.3%, 0.03%) with the surfactant-producing variant showed that lower salinity increases recovery in secondary mode, whereas primary bacterial flooding was insensitive to salinity, likely due to faster near-inlet pore plugging. The proposed MEOR mechanism was updated accordingly.

## Monitoring the Surfactant-Enhanced Oil Dissolution and Mobilization in Porous Media

- ID: SCA2009-36, year: 2009

- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: surfactant flushing, SDS micelles, n-decane dissolution, resistivity index, non-Archie equation, effective dissolution rate
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-36)

Multipoint electrical resistivity monitors n-decane mobilization and dissolution during SDS flushing of a soil column, yielding segment-wise effective dissolution rates: mobilization dominates early, dissolution late.

The study separates oil mobilization from dissolution during surfactant flushing of residual n-decane in a 29 cm soil column (porosity 0.40, permeability 25.2 D). After drainage and imbibition, a brine solution with 4.9 g/L SDS was injected at 0.5 ml/min for about 63 pore volumes. Mobilization dominated early (t<100 min), especially in upper segments, while dissolution dominated late (t>200 min).

## Improved Measurement Technique for Studying the Effect of Bacterial Solution on Ift and Contact Angles

- ID: SCA2009-37, year: 2009
- Authors: Shabani Afrapoli M, Alipour S, Torsæter O
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: microbial enhanced oil recovery, pendant drop goniometer, interfacial tension, contact angle, Rhodococcus biosurfactant, dynamic flow cell
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-37)

A pendant-drop goniometer with continuous bacterial flow shows greater IFT reduction and lower contact angles than static measurements for Rhodococcus bacteria with dodecane.

Static pendant-drop goniometer measurements can underestimate bacterial effects in microbial improved oil recovery because nutrients and oxygen deplete in the small cuvette. The authors added a circulation system (3 ml/hr) so fresh Rhodococcus sp. 094 suspension (10^7 CFU/ml, SPB and NSPB variants) continuously contacts n-dodecane drops at 30°C. The dynamic method is argued to be more relevant for studying IFT and wettability mechanisms in MIOR.

## Effects of Salinity on the Morphology of Dissolution Pits in Calcite -Implication for

- ID: SCA2009-39, year: 2009
- Authors: Christine Putnis, Christopher. J. Spiers
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: calcite dissolution, atomic force microscopy, pressure solution, salinity, etch pits, compaction creep
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-39.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-39)

AFM dissolution of cleaved calcite and uniaxial compaction of calcite aggregates both show a critical NaCl salinity near 0.8 M, linking etch-pit morphology to pressure-solution creep.

The study asks how pore fluid salinity affects calcite dissolution morphology and pressure-solution compaction. Fluid-cell AFM was run on cleaved {1014} Iceland Spar with NaCl solutions from 0.1 M to saturation, and dead-weight uniaxial compaction tests were run at 4 MPa on pure 15 µm granular calcite, after 8 MPa dry precompaction, with pre-saturated CaCO3 solutions. The same critical salinity in both datasets suggests that flattening of grain boundaries thins the water film and limits diffusion, with implications for nano porosity and CO2 sequestration.
