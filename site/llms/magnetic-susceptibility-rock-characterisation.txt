## A Novel Flow Cell and Integrated Sensor Technique for Simultaneous Magnetic Monitoring of Core Samples During Fluid Flow Experiments

- ID: SCA2012-14, year: 2012
- Authors: Shahjahan Khan, David K. Potter, Ergun Kuru
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, acrylic flow cell, magnetic nanoparticles, nanoparticle transport, fines migration, in-line monitoring
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-14)

An acrylic flow cell with an integrated magnetic susceptibility sensor enables in-line monitoring of nanoparticle transport through bead and sand packs; anionic DDBS dispersant gave the best transport.

Stainless steel flow cells prevent magnetic measurements during flooding, so the authors built an acrylic flow cell (57 mm ID, 200 mm long) with a Bartington MS2C susceptibility sensor that can be positioned along the cell at 1 cm intervals during flow. They tested it by injecting 20 nm maghemite and magnetite nanoparticle suspensions into glass bead and sand packs (permeability 2.2–22.8 D), followed by water flooding. Sonicating before adding the cationic CTAB dispersant left the particles agglomerated at the inlet, while dispersing with anionic DDBS before sonication gave good transport, with about 90% of the nanoparticles removed by 4 PV water flooding. Higher permeability and injection rate (5–60 cc/min) flushed out more nanoparticles, and magnetite behaved similarly to maghemite. The cell may also be used to monitor fines migration in consolidated cores.

## Sensitive Carbonate Reservoir Rock Characterisation from Magnetic Susceptibility: Mineral Quantification, Correlation with Petrophysical Properties, and Anisotropy

- ID: SCA2008-10, year: 2008
- Authors: David K. Potter, Tariq M. AlGhamdi, Oleksandr P. Ivakhnenko
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, hysteresis curves, carbonate permeability, paramagnetic clay, ferrimagnetic impurities, anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-10)

Magnetic hysteresis measurements on Middle East carbonates show high-field susceptibility correlates strongly with permeability and porosity, while low-field values are disturbed by trace ferrimagnetic impurities.

The paper extends magnetic susceptibility characterisation, previously used for clastics, to carbonate reservoir rocks. Hysteresis curves from Arab-D Middle East carbonates were measured with a variable field translation balance, and compared with US, UK and North Sea carbonate samples. Low-field susceptibility (0-50 mT) correlated poorly with plug permeability and porosity because small ferrimagnetic (likely magnetite) impurities, visible as low-field kinks, dominated it. High-field susceptibility (above 750 mT), reflecting diamagnetic plus paramagnetic minerals, correlated extremely well with both permeability and porosity, possibly through calcite/dolomite ratio or ~1% paramagnetic clay. A North Sea dogger sample had a positive high-field slope, low permeability (0.01 mD), higher anisotropy (~5% vs ~1%) and a high-field susceptibility that decreased with temperature, while pure calcite/dolomite showed no temperature dependence.

## Integration of Laboratory Temperature Dependent Magnetic Properties of Sediment and Crystalline Basement Core Samples with Borehole Data: Applications to Geothermal Studies in the Oil Sands Region of Northern Alberta, Canada

- ID: SCA2012-49, year: 2012
- Authors: Arfan Ali, Khalid Miah, David K. Potter
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, magnetic hysteresis, crystalline basement, ferrimagnetic minerals, geothermal oil sands, superparamagnetic grains
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-49.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-49)

Temperature-dependent magnetic hysteresis and susceptibility of sediment and crystalline basement cores from Northern Alberta distinguish basement from sediment and characterize ferrimagnetic minerals, supporting borehole susceptibility use for geothermal heat-source location.

Radiogenic heat sources in crystalline basement beneath the Fort McMurray oil sands could support geothermal energy for oil sands extraction, so locating basement depth and its ferrimagnetic minerals is needed. Powdered samples from the Hunt Well were measured for low-field susceptibility (Bartington MS2B) and hysteresis (VFTB) at room and in situ temperatures. Low-field mass susceptibility was 12.6e-8 m3/kg for the sediment versus 311.8e-8 and 207.6e-8 for two basement samples, a contrast larger than the gamma ray difference (34 vs 169 API). Hysteresis showed stable single-domain behavior in the sediment (remanence about half of saturation) and superparamagnetic behavior in the basement sample. High-field susceptibility decreased slightly with temperature, consistent with Curie-law paramagnetism. A borehole susceptibility value of 39.92e-5 SI at the sediment depth was close to the lab value of 30.22e-5 SI at 34°C.

## Effects of Mechanical Anisotropy on Core Strain Measurements for In-situ Stress Determination

- ID: SCA1989-08, year: 1989
- Authors: G. G. Ramos, J. J. Rathmell
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: anelastic strain recovery, differential strain analysis, velocity anisotropy, in-situ stress orientation, microfractures, tight gas sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1989/SCA1989-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1989-08)

Differential strain, velocity anisotropy, coring-induced fractures and thin-section microfractures resolved conflicting on-site strain azimuths in two tight sandstone wells, giving Shmax near 75±45 degrees.

On-site anelastic strain relief tests on tight-gas sandstone cores from two Texas wells, about five miles apart, gave orthogonal maximum-strain azimuths (0-30° and 90-120°). To resolve this, laboratory differential strain analysis (21 tests), ultrasonic Vp/Vs velocity anisotropy, mapping of coring-induced macrofractures, and fluorescent thin-section microfracture mapping were applied. Velocity anisotropy indices were 1.019-1.070, and Vp gave less scatter than Vs. Thin sections showed a major aligned microfracture set and a minor orthogonal set, supporting the inferred stress orientation.

## Anisotropy of Resistivity in Oil Bearing Thin-Bedded Formation: Experiment and Modeling

- ID: SCA2003-28, year: 2003
- Authors: J-B. Clavaud, J. LaVigne
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: resistivity anisotropy, thin-bedded formation, laminated sandstone, dual water model, water saturation equation, centrifuge desaturation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-28)

Twin-plug measurements show resistivity anisotropy in laminated sandstones rises as water saturation falls; forward modeling shows Sw equations using both rv and rh beat horizontal-only ones.

Thinly laminated reservoirs often show large resistivity anisotropy, and the paper tests whether it is induced by saturation differences between oil-bearing coarse layers and water-bearing fine layers. Twin plugs of homogeneous Berea and laminated Navajo, Cutbank and Lyons sandstones were desaturated by centrifuge with dodecane, and rv/rh was measured at 100 kHz. In the laminated samples rv/rh rose as Sw fell (Navajo from 1.2 to 3.4 between 100% and 10% Sw), while Berea stayed near 1. Forward modeling of four geological units with a dual water model then tested Archie, dual water and three rv/rh-based Sw algorithms (SPC, HDA and a new SDR equation that does not need water resistivity). Horizontal-only methods underestimated hydrocarbon volume by about 20-47%, whereas the rv/rh methods were mostly within a few percent. On the experimental data the SPC model gave Sw close to the measured bulk values, though it tended to overestimate oil.

## Magnetic Susceptibility as a Rapid, Non-Destructive Technique for Improved RCAL and SCAL Parameter Prediction

- ID: SCA2005-02, year: 2005
- Authors: David K. Potter
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, illite content, permeability prediction, cation exchange capacity Qv, wireline gamma ray, core plugs
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-02)

Low-field magnetic susceptibility on core plugs gives rapid illite content and permeability predictions, correlating with gamma ray and Qv where porosity-permeability is poor.

Magnetic susceptibility is proposed as a rapid, non-destructive complement to routine and special core analysis. Susceptibility of 297 routine plugs from a North Sea shoreface well was measured with a Molspin bridge; the sign of the raw signal matched permeability zones (negative for clean sands above 100 mD, positive for muddy sands and shales). A quartz-illite mixing model converted susceptibility to illite content, with a critical value of about 3.54% at zero susceptibility. Magnetically derived illite correlated with permeability (power fit r2 = 0.73, 0.80 without two outliers), far better than porosity-permeability (r2 = 0.25). Susceptibility also correlated with wireline gamma ray (cut-off near 20 API) and with Qv for a few plugs, and probe measurements on slabbed core suit unconsolidated core.

## The Use of Magnetic Hysteresis and Remanence Measurements in Rapidly and Non-Destructively Characterising Reservoir Rocks and Fluids

- ID: SCA2006-08, year: 2006
- Authors: Oleksandr P. Ivakhnenko, David K. Potter
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: magnetic hysteresis, isothermal remanent magnetisation, magnetic susceptibility, illite content, ferrimagnetic minerals, reservoir fluids
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-08)

Magnetic hysteresis and IRM curves up to 1000 mT identify diamagnetic, paramagnetic and ferrimagnetic components in reservoir rocks and fluids, extending single low-field susceptibility methods.

Single low-field susceptibility readings can be biased by small amounts of strongly magnetic minerals, which affects estimates of permeability-controlling clays such as illite. The authors measured hysteresis curves (fields to 1000 mT) and isothermal remanent magnetisation on about 0.5 g samples with a Variable Field Translation Balance, taking 10-15 minutes per curve. Samples came from shoreface, turbidite and carbonate reservoirs, plus crude oil and formation water. Slopes at high field separate diamagnetic (negative) from paramagnetic (positive) components, low-field kinks or loops reveal ferrimagnetic minerals such as magnetite, and models show 4-5% illite in quartz makes the slope positive. Clean and muddy shoreface sands, turbidite sands and shale, limestone and calcite dogger, and oil versus water were distinguished. Turbidite sand and shale from the same unit gave near-identical normalised curves, implying a similar ferrimagnetic-to-paramagnetic ratio. The method complements but does not replace XRD, since it cannot distinguish different paramagnetic clays.

- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_05)

Magnetic hysteresis and susceptibility measurements quantify clay content in synthetic and natural samples, agree with XRD, improve permeability correlation at high field, reveal clay loss in cleaning, and AMS tracks illite.

The paper evaluates magnetic susceptibility and hysteresis as a rapid, sensitive complement to XRD for clay typing and quantification. Model hysteresis curves were compared with VFTB measurements on synthetic clay–quartz samples (illite, montmorillonite, kaolinite, chlorite) and on turbidite samples; measured high-field susceptibility matched XRD-derived values well, except for chlorite where XRD appeared to overestimate. ## Quantifying the Relative Roles of Illite and Hematite on Permeability in Red and White Sandstones Using Low and High Field Magnetic Susceptibility

- ID: SCA2009-11, year: 2009
- Authors: David K. Potter, Arfan Ali, Oleksandr P. Ivakhnenko
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, hematite, illite, magnetic hysteresis, probe permeability, tight gas sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-11)

Magnetic hysteresis and low-field susceptibility separate illite and hematite in North Sea red and white tight sandstones, showing fine-grained hematite dominantly reduces permeability in red sandstones.

Permeability in North Sea tight gas reservoirs is generally lower in red sandstones than adjacent white sandstones. Magnetic hysteresis measurements (VFTB) were used to identify hematite from low-field loops and quantify illite from the high-field slope. At similar illite content, red sandstones have systematically lower permeability, implicating fine-grained hematite. A rapid method was derived to estimate hematite and illite simultaneously from low-field susceptibility on slabbed core, assuming quartz fraction 0.9 and hematite susceptibility about 400×10-5 SI. Along several core sections of 0.8 to 6 m, hematite content mirrored probe permeability, with hematite peaks coinciding with permeability minima, while illite did not track permeability. The authors conclude small hematite amounts dominate permeability in red sandstones, though validation with combined low and high field measurements is still needed.

## Quantifying the Effects of Core Cleaning, Core Flooding and Fines Migration Using Sensitive Magnetic Techniques: Implications for Permeability Determination and Formation Damage

- ID: SCA2010-01, year: 2010
- Authors: David K. Potter, Arfan Ali, Salim Imhmed, Norbert Schleifer
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, hot soxhlet core cleaning, fines migration, illite clay content, magnetic hysteresis, formation damage
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-01)

Magnetic susceptibility and hysteresis measurements quantify illite loss during soxhlet cleaning and fines migration in core floods and water injection filters, showing cleaned-plug permeability is overestimated.

The paper uses low-field magnetic susceptibility and hysteresis (VFTB) measurements to quantify mineral removal from reservoir sandstones during core cleaning and flooding. Six North Sea quartz-illite plugs were measured before and after hot soxhlet cleaning with toluene and methanol; susceptibility fell in all, with illite removal from 0.01% in the 1.8 mD sample to 0.17% in the 1558 mD sample. This implies cleaned-plug permeability overestimates the uncleaned value, e.g. an estimated 1021 mD versus 1558 mD for Sample 1. Seawater-brine flooding of two ~7.5 cm plugs at 50 ml/hour reduced susceptibility at every measured point, consistent with illite fines removal. Hysteresis curves indicated mainly paramagnetic clay with minimal ferrimagnetic material. Hysteresis of filter papers from a North African water injection line detected paramagnetic and ferrimagnetic fines, including at the cartridge outlet, below XRD detection limits.

## Novel Techniques to Rapidly Determine the Magnetic Anisotropy of Reservoir Rocks and Shales, and Comparisons with Permeability and Acoustic Anisotropies

- ID: SCA2011-31, year: 2011
- Authors: David K. Potter
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility, anisotropy of magnetic remanence, permeability anisotropy, acoustic anisotropy, tight gas sandstone, shale
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-31.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-31)

Rapid single-plug magnetic anisotropy methods (low/high field AMS, AMR) are compared with permeability and acoustic anisotropy in tight gas red sandstones and shales.

Permeability and acoustic anisotropy need many plugs and are slow, so the paper presents single-plug magnetic methods: low field AMS, ferrifluid AMS, high field AMS (torquemeter or VFTB hysteresis) and anisotropy of isothermal remanence (AIRM). On two tight gas red sandstones, permeability and acoustic anisotropy came from 18 plugs, while high field AMS and AMR came from one plug each. The maximum acoustic, high field AMS and AMR axes coincided with the minimum permeability axis, possibly because fine hematite blocks pore connections along the maximum framework axis. In two shales, all methods showed planar fabrics, and high field AMS axes matched acoustic axes better than low field AMS, which resembled AMR. This indicates high field AMS reflects paramagnetic clay alignment while low field AMS is affected by small ferrimagnetic fractions.

Curie Law template curves of susceptibility versus temperature for clay-quartz mixtures match turbidite and shoreface sample data and give illite contents agreeing with hysteresis and XRD results.

Magnetic susceptibility correlates with clay content and permeability, but prior measurements were made at room temperature, not at downhole temperature. ## A Comparison of Quantitative Acoustic, Magnetic and Permeability Anisotropy Measurements on a North Sea Caprock

- ID: SCA2013-009, year: 2013
- Authors: Muhammad Arif, David K. Potter, Ross Bishop, Norbert Schleifer
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: caprock anisotropy, anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility, anisotropy of isothermal remanent magnetization, S-wave velocity anisotropy, petrofabric, permeability anisotropy
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-009.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-009)

Acoustic, magnetic (AMS, AIRM) and permeability anisotropy measured on horizontal and vertical North Sea caprock plugs consistently show higher values parallel to the petrofabric.

Caprock anisotropy is rarely quantified, yet may affect seal efficiency. Two siltstone caprock plugs (horizontal 4H and vertical 2V) were measured for P- and S-wave velocity up to 40 MPa hydrostatic pressure, AMS, AIRM and Klinkenberg-corrected gas permeability. The parallel-to-perpendicular velocity ratio at 40 MPa was 1.084 for P-waves and 1.061 for S-waves (1.061 using visually determined petrofabric axes). AMS and AIRM maximum axes lay in the petrofabric plane for the vertical plug, whereas low magnetic signals made the horizontal plug's orientations less reliable. The combined results suggest real permeability anisotropy with lower vertical permeability, which contributes to caprock efficiency.

Presents template crossplots relating mass magnetic susceptibility to spectral gamma ray and bulk density logs to identify clay type, mineral content and porosity, including temperature effects.

Magnetic susceptibility measurements on core or cuttings correlate with clay content and permeability, but have not been tied to standard wireline logs. ## A Comparison of Quantitative Techniqueques for Determining the 3D Anisotropy of Shale Samples: Application to Horn River Basin Shales, British Columbia, Canada

- ID: SCA2014-011, year: 2014
- Authors: Vivian Ebufegha, David K. Potter
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility, anisotropy of magnetic remanence, inverse magnetic fabric, anisotropy of electrical resistance, Horn River shale, sodium silicate impregnation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-011.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-011)

Compares magnetic susceptibility, remanence and electrical resistance anisotropy in Horn River shales, finding inverse AMS fabrics from single-domain ferrimagnetic grains, so AMR is needed to infer true fabric.

Fissile Horn River shales (Muskwa, Otter Park, Evie) are hard to plug, so samples were impregnated with sodium silicate and cut under propylene glycol into 2.2 cm cubes; some consolidated cylindrical plugs were also cut. AMS was measured in 18 directions on 57 cubes, along with isothermal remanence parallel and perpendicular to bedding, thin and polished section petrography, and AER on six plugs. All samples were magnetically anisotropic, with %AMS highest in the Evie (likely organic matter) and variable in the Otter Park (pyrite content). Normal and inverse AMS fabrics alternate with depth; IRM was always higher parallel to bedding, indicating that inverse fabrics arise from uniaxial stable single-domain ferrimagnetic grains, so AMS alone can mislead. In the six plugs, maximum electrical resistance was near-perpendicular to bedding and minimum near-parallel, broadly matching Kmax and Kmin axes respectively. Otter Park resistance (0.1-0.5 x 10^9 ohm) was lower than Muskwa (2-5 x 10^9 ohm).

## Correlation Between Magnetic Properties and Permeability: Results from a New Case Study in the North Sea

- ID: SCA2014-077, year: 2014
- Authors: Arfan Ali, David K. Potter, Andrew Tugwell
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, illite content, permeability prediction, shoreface reservoir, slabbed core probe, North Sea
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-077.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-077)

Magnetic susceptibility on plugs and slabbed core from a North Sea shoreface reservoir gives illite content that correlates with permeability, matching an earlier field's relationship.

The study tests whether magnetic susceptibility can predict clay content and permeability in a Jurassic North Sea shoreface reservoir, following earlier work on a different field. Susceptibility was measured over a 50.4 m interval on 1.5 x 3 inch plugs (Bartington MS2C coil) and on slabbed core at 10 cm spacing (MS2E probe). Illite content derived from susceptibility closely follows Klinkenberg-corrected air permeability, with a power-law crossplot R2 of 0.62. Slabbed-core probe profiles resemble the plug profiles and predicted permeability matches measured values in many intervals. Mismatches are attributed to other minerals such as calcite, kaolinite and chlorite, and to core heterogeneity and scale differences.

## Magnetic Susceptibility of Drill Cuttings in a North Sea Oil Well: A Rapid, Non-Destructive Means of Characterizing Lithology

- ID: SCA2015-036, year: 2015
- Authors: Arfan Ali, David K. Potter, Andrew Tugwell
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: magnetic susceptibility, drill cuttings, mass susceptibility, hematite, LWD gamma ray, mineralogy
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-036.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-036)

Mass magnetic susceptibility of North Sea drill cuttings maps lithology and reveals unexpected paramagnetic/ferrimagnetic minerals in low-gamma sandstones, possibly explaining low well productivity.

Drill cuttings are an underused resource for mineralogy and petrophysics. The authors made 421 volume magnetic susceptibility measurements on 10 cc vials from 157 cuttings bags over a 9,000 ft section of a North Sea well, using a portable Bartington MS2B sensor, and converted them to mass susceptibility by dividing by bulk density. Results tracked lithological zonation and were inversely related to LWD gamma ray: low-gamma sandstones showed anomalously high positive susceptibility (cut-off about 17 x 10^-8 m3/kg) instead of the negative values expected for diamagnetic quartz. Limited XRD from another field well (about 85% quartz, no siderite) suggests small amounts of magnetite or hematite rather than siderite or clays. These minerals may reduce permeability and explain the lower productivity; high-field susceptibility or remanence measurements are proposed to confirm.

## Quantifying Shale Mineralogy and Anisotropy from Low Temperature Magnetic Susceptibility Measurements

- ID: SCA2016-033, year: 2016
- Authors: Vivian T. Ebufegha, David K. Potter
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: low temperature magnetic susceptibility, anisotropy of magnetic susceptibility, illite content, Curie law, Horn River shale, paramagnetic minerals
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-033.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-033)

Low temperature magnetic susceptibility of Horn River shales, compared with Curie-law template curves, quantifies illite content and reveals hidden anisotropy and mineral sources of AMS fabrics.

Shale mineralogy and anisotropy are difficult to quantify quickly and nondestructively. The authors cooled 54 sodium-silicate-impregnated Horn River Group cubes to about 173 K in liquid nitrogen and measured susceptibility with a Bartington MS2B meter as they warmed to 294 K. Measured curves were compared with theoretical Curie-law curves for 11 illite-quartz mixtures to estimate illite content. Changes in principal axis orientation with temperature distinguished paramagnetic illite-dominated normal fabrics, iron carbonate inverse fabrics, and stable single domain ferrimagnetic inverse fabrics.
