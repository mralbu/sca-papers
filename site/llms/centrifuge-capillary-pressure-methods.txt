## Simple and Accurate Methods for Converting Centrifuge Data into Drainage and Imbibition Capillary Pressure Curves

- ID: SCA1991-07, year: 1991
- Authors: P. Forbes
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, Hassler-Brunner inversion, forced imbibition, USBM wettability index, ill-conditioned inversion
- Cited by 22 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-07)

Two analytical methods invert the centrifuge equation for drainage and imbibition capillary pressure; the second needs no smoothing or fitting and matches artificial and experimental data well.

Converting centrifuge average-saturation data into local capillary pressure curves requires inverting an ill-conditioned equation, and common methods smooth or fit the data, which can bias the results. The first method approximates the centrifuge equation with an Abel-type equation, giving a closed-form solution that also yields the corrected USBM wettability index directly, but it still needs data fitting. The second method uses the proven bounds S_HB ≤ S ≤ S_D (Hassler-Brunner and van Domselaar) to build S_alpha and S_beta solutions, combined as S_alphabeta, in a simple recursive differencing scheme. It processes a dataset in under one second on a PC, with no iteration, smoothing or averaging, and works with about five or more points. It reproduces artificial curves, including step functions, and handles noisy experimental drainage data (Bentsen and Anli, North Sea sandstones, Berea with mercury injection comparison). For forced imbibition the simple S_alpha solution is accurate for all B, and it reconstructs the raw data better than the averaged solution of Hermansen et al.

## Water-Oil Capillary Pressure and Wettability Measurements Using Micropore Membrane Technique

- ID: SCA1994-26, year: 1994
- Authors: Daniel LONGERON, Wibeke L. HAMMERVOLD
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: micropore membrane, capillary pressure hysteresis, wettability index, Amott index, USBM index, porous plate
- Cited by 10 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-26)

A micropore membrane coreholder measures full four-curve water-oil capillary pressure cycles in days, and a new area-based HL wettability index is proposed and tested on sandstone and carbonate.

Full capillary pressure cycles (spontaneous and forced imbibition and drainage) by the porous plate method take months. The authors built a coreholder with thin water-wet and oil-wet micropore membranes and short, large-diameter cores (5 cm diameter, 2 cm thick) to speed this up. Tests used Vosges sandstone and Estaillade carbonate, both native water-wet and aged in Lagrave crude oil. Drainage curves agreed well with mercury injection and porous plate data, and full cycles took 20-30 days, about 30 times faster than standard porous plate. They propose the Hammervold-Longeron index, an Amott-like index weighting the four saturation intervals by the areas under the capillary pressure curves. HL values were 0.85 (water-wet sandstone), 0.97 (water-wet carbonate) and -0.01 (aged carbonate), compared with Amott and USBM indices.

## A Novel Method for the Determination of Water/Oil Capillary Pressures of Mixed Wettability Samples

- ID: SCA1993-22, year: 1993
- Authors: R. Lenormand, A. Eisenzimmer
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary pressure, mixed wettability, semi-dynamic method, ultrasonic saturation measurement, negative capillary pressure, USBM wettability index
- Cited by 15 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-22.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-22)

A semi-dynamic steady-flow method with outlet washing and ultrasonic saturation measurement gives full positive and negative capillary pressure cycles for sands and Berea of varying wettability without membranes.

Standard porous plate and centrifuge methods struggle to give imbibition and negative capillary pressure curves for mixed-wettability rocks. The authors propose a semi-dynamic method: one fluid is injected at stepped rates (5–500 cc/h) while the in-situ fluid washes the outlet face at 20 cc/h, so Pc is obtained from a pressure tap and local ultrasonic saturation measurements. Tests used sand packs (about 4.6 D) with silane-treated oil-wet fractions f=0, 0.5, 1, and a 130 mD Berea sample. Water-wet and oil-wet sands gave mirror-image cycles with plateaus near 40 mbar, while f=0.5 gave more symmetrical curves with smaller plateaus, interpreted with two interpenetrating wettability networks. The Berea positive Pc agreed with porous plate data, though the maximum rate did not reach irreducible water saturation. Results are considered qualitative owing to low pressures and dead volumes.

## Centrifuge Data Analysis Techniques : An Sca Survey on the Calculation of Drainage Capillary Pressure Curves from Centrifuge Measurements

- ID: SCA1997-14, year: 1997
- Authors: Pierre Forbes
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, inversion of centrifuge data, radial correction, Hassler-Brunner solution, interlaboratory survey, synthetic data
- Cited by 21 papers in the corpus, cites 8
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-14)

An SCA survey tests about 19 centrifuge capillary pressure interpretation solutions on ten synthetic datasets, finding geometry-dependent errors up to 10 saturation units and only a few reliable methods.

The 1993 SCA centrifuge survey showed large scatter between laboratories, and it was unclear whether measurement or interpretation caused it. This survey gave 14 contributors ten synthetic centrifuge datasets, built from known Pc curves and varied core/centrifuge geometries (B, N parameters) with added experimental error. Contributors used 19 solutions (HB, DOM, Sα, Sαβ, RW, HER, spline, parameter estimation etc.), with and without radial correction, giving about 50 interpretations per case. Interpretation error depended mainly on geometry: for 1"x1" cores at 21.5 cm many solutions stayed within ±3 saturation units, but elsewhere errors of ±10 units were common. Most methods bias saturation negatively, so averaging methods does not help, and more rotation steps or lower measurement error did not help. Only SPL and Sαβ with radial correction and consistency constraints and proper numerical implementation kept errors near ±3 saturation units across geometries.

## Advances in Centrifuge Methodology for Core Analysis

- ID: SCA1990-11, year: 1990
- Authors: M. J. King, K. R. Narayanan, A. J. Falzone
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: centrifuge gravity drainage, Hassler-Brunner analysis, stretched exponential extrapolation, history matching, oil relative permeability, capillary pressure
- Cited by 9 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-11)

Reviews centrifuge capillary pressure analyses, presents an automated ultracentrifuge and stretched-exponential extrapolation, and shows history matching with fixed Pc gives less biased oil relative permeability than simultaneous Pc estimation.

The paper reviews centrifuge methods for capillary pressure and relative permeability, recasting Hassler-Brunner and later finite-core-length corrections (Hoffman, van Domselaar, Rajan) in a common form alongside an exact solution. An automated Beckman ultracentrifuge with a line camera records oil and water production at 4 µl resolution for six cores. Multi-stage gravity drainage on ~200 mD Berea and reservoir samples showed equilibrium was not reached after 24 hours, and a stretched exponential with β = 0.77 extrapolated one-day data to ten days. History matching with an automatic simulator (rotational discrimination) compared fixed Pc (Procedure I) with simultaneous Pc and kr estimation (Procedure II). Procedure II misrepresented the sharp Pc elbow, and the kr curves differed by up to an order of magnitude below 30% saturation, so fixing Pc from Hassler-Brunner points is recommended.

## Calculation of Capillary Pressure Curves from Data Obtained by the Centrifuge Method

- ID: SCA1988-02, year: 1988
- Authors: Douglas Ruth
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, Hassler-Brunner equation, van Domselaar method, Bentsen parameter estimation, linear interpolation method
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1988/SCA1988-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1988-02)

Compares centrifuge capillary pressure data reduction methods, introduces a linear interpolation method that matches Bentsen's parameter estimation on simulated data, and recommends using multiple methods.

Centrifuge capillary pressure curves must be inferred from production versus speed data, and existing reduction methods are approximate. The authors compare the Hassler-Brunner approximation, van Domselaar's equation, Bentsen's parameter estimation and a new linear interpolation method (LIM) on simulated data with four curve shapes (a = 12 kPa, b from -96 to -1.5 kPa). Hassler-Brunner systematically underestimates and van Domselaar overestimates capillary pressure, while LIM agrees well, with errors within the ±0.15 cc reading error. LIM deviates near irreducible saturation for low b, traced to derivative estimation, and denser data at low pressure fixes this. Applied to Skuse's data and three new samples (dolostone, brownstone, Berea), LIM and Bentsen agree well except near irreducible saturation, where data quality limits LIM. The authors recommend taking data before the inner face reaches irreducible saturation and analyzing with more than one method.

## Comparative Study of Drainage Capillary Pressure Measurements Using Different Techniques and for Different Fluid Systems

- ID: SCA1994-24, year: 1994
- Authors: Lionel Sabatier
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: drainage capillary pressure, centrifuge, porous plate, mercury injection, interfacial tension scaling, clay content
- Cited by 9 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-24)

Drainage capillary pressure on six water-wet samples compared across porous plate, centrifuge and mercury injection and three fluid pairs; gas-liquid data do not transfer to oil-brine.

The paper compares drainage capillary pressure curves from porous plate, centrifuge and mercury injection on six samples (sandstones, dolomite, oolitic limestone) cleaned to be water-wet. Centrifuge runs used air-brine, air-oil and oil-brine on big plugs, while porous plate and mercury injection used small companion plugs. Air-brine curves from all three techniques agree well, with mercury data scaled by the interfacial tension ratio (6.85) and zero contact angle; no size effect was seen, and centrifuge differences at high saturation were attributed to insufficient equilibration time in low-permeability samples. Air-oil curves lie 3 to 8 saturation points from air-brine, while oil-brine curves end about 5 to 10 saturation points higher in saturation, so gas-liquid data are not transposable to oil-brine. Mercury injection diverges from air-brine centrifuge data when clay exceeds about 7%, by over 20 saturation points at 20% kaolinite/dickite.

## Capillary Pressure From Centrifuge —A New, Direct Method

- ID: SCA1991-24EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Hroar Hermansen
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, Volterra integral equation, Hassler-Brunner method, forced imbibition, modified midpoint method, mercury injection
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-24EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-24EURO)

A direct modified-midpoint solution of the centrifuge Volterra equation gives capillary pressure curves without iteration, validated on synthetic data, nine cores against diaphragm and mercury methods, and forced imbibition.

The Hassler-Brunner interpretation of centrifuge data for long cores requires iteration and numerical differentiation. The authors solve the resulting Volterra integral equation of the first kind directly using a modified midpoint method, with a simple three-point smoothing (1/4, 1/2, 1/4) to suppress oscillations. They validated it on synthetic curves (Bentsen-Anli and Ayappa et al.), which were reproduced accurately, and on drainage of six Berea and three North Sea cores compared with porous diaphragm and mercury injection. Agreement with converted mercury data was very satisfactory, and the method was also applied to forced imbibition data from three North Sea sandstone cores. At least 5-6 datapoints are recommended; with only six points, Core 8's curve bend was not captured. A modified Hassler-Brunner procedure gave nearly the same results.

## Comparison Study of Capillary Pressure Curves Obtained Using Traditional Centrifuge and Magnetic Resonance Imaging Techniques

- ID: SCA2007_30, year: 2007
- Authors: Derrick P. Green, Josh R. Dick, John Gardner, Bruce J. Balcom, Bing Zhou
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary pressure, centrifuge, SPRITE MRI, saturation profile, Leverett J function, Hassler-Brunner
- Cited by 10 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_30)

Centrifuged core plugs are scanned with 1D centric-scan SPRITE MRI to get saturation profiles and capillary pressure curves, matching traditional centrifuge results with 3-5 times fewer speeds.

Traditional centrifuge capillary pressure measurements need 7-10 equilibrium speeds, each taking days, and infer inlet-face saturation from expelled fluid. The authors present GIT-CAP, which centrifuges plugs and then measures the saturation profile directly with 1D double half k-space centric scan SPRITE MRI on a 0.18 T instrument. Four low-permeability gas-water samples (0.9-2.3 mD, 12.6-14.5% porosity) were spun at five speeds chosen from Leverett J values, with MRI profiles normalized to the fully saturated profile. MRI-derived primary drainage curves agreed very well with separately measured traditional curves (Forbes method). As few as three speeds defined the curve, and two may suffice with longer cores, cutting measurement time by 3-5 times. Fluid redistribution during MRI acquisition was insignificant, with profiles stable even after days.

## Interpretation of centrifuge data using local saturation

- ID: SCA1994-28, year: 1994
- Authors: M. Fleury, R. Lenormand, F. Deflandre
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, ultrasonic local saturation, multi-speed centrifuge, relative permeability, forced imbibition, wettability
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-28)

Ultrasonic local saturation measured during multi-speed centrifuge runs matches computed Pc curves on water-wet to oil-wet samples but does not improve relative permeability determination.

Centrifuge Pc and Kr are normally derived from production data, so the authors measured local saturation during spinning using ultrasonic transit times at up to 5 locations on 7 cm plugs. Three samples were tested (water-wet, intermediate-wet, oil-wet) in drainage and forced imbibition with a 15 cP oil. Rotation effects on transit time required empirical corrections, up to 100% at the largest radii, and the saturation error is about 4% PV. Direct local Pc curves agreed with those computed from production and confirmed zero Pc at the outflow face, and local data revealed quasi-spontaneous drainage and fluid redistribution after the centrifuge stopped. Kr curves from production, single channels and all channels differed, because the inversion is very sensitive to Pc and to small heterogeneities, so local saturation gave no practical improvement.

## Oil/Water Imbibition and Drainage Capillary Pressure Determined by MRI on a Wide Sampling of Rocks

- ID: SCA2008-01, year: 2008
- Authors: Derrick P. Green, Josh R. Dick, Mike McAloon, P.F. de J. Cano-Barrita, Jon Burger, Bruce Balcom
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: SPRITE MRI, centrifuge capillary pressure, imbibition and secondary drainage, D2O brine, T2 cutoff, Brooks-Corey fit
- Cited by 9 papers in the corpus, cites 5
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-01)

A centrifuge combined with quantitative SPRITE MRI saturation profiles gives drainage and imbibition capillary pressure curves from few centrifuge speeds, including negative Pc portions, tested on sandstone and chalk.

Traditional centrifuge capillary pressure measurements need 7-10 speeds and rely on expelled-fluid volumes and approximate inlet-face saturation. The authors instead use centric-scan 1D SPRITE MRI on a 0.18 T system to measure saturation profiles in the core after centrifugation, pairing each position with the Hassler-Brunner capillary pressure. ## Experimental Measurements of Capillary Pressure and Relative Permeability Hysteresis

- ID: SCA2001-23, year: 2001
- Authors: Shehadeh K. Masalmeh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary pressure hysteresis, contact angle hysteresis, wettability change, water trapping, scanning curves, Pc-probe
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-23)

Measures capillary pressure and relative permeability hysteresis on water-wet and non-water-wet cores, proposing a model based on trapping, contact angle hysteresis and wettability change.

Existing hysteresis models rely mostly on water-wet data, so the paper measures capillary pressure and relative permeability hysteresis on carbonate and sandstone cores of varying wettability using centrifuge, steady-state, CAPRICI and Pc-probe techniques. A conceptual model attributes hysteresis to trapping, contact angle hysteresis and wettability change. In aged low-permeability limestones, primary imbibition and secondary drainage Pc curves do not form closed loops, and connate water rose by up to 15 saturation units after secondary drainage because of water trapping. Effective contact angle hysteresis calculated for two samples was 45-50° and 25-30°. Oil relative permeability showed strong hysteresis between primary drainage and imbibition, while water relative permeability showed little hysteresis unless the plugs were oil-wet. Oil kro end points ranged from above 1 for water-wet plugs to 0.2 for oil-wet ones.

## Analysis of Centrifuge Relative Permeability Data

- ID: SCA1997-11, year: 1997
- Authors: Douglas Ruth
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: centrifuge relative permeability, history matching, Corey exponents, Bentsen capillary pressure, sensitivity analysis, speed ramp-up
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-11)

Simulation of multi-speed centrifuge drainage shows wetting-phase exponents are well determined, non-wetting exponents poorly so, and capillary pressure should be fitted separately; ignoring speed ramp-up biases results.

The paper simulates multi-speed centrifuge relative permeability experiments using Corey relative permeability and Bentsen capillary pressure curves with a finite-difference model including finite capillary pressure, non-uniform acceleration and finite non-wetting mobility. Grid-block number (N=40) and saturation-change time step (0.001) were chosen from convergence studies. Sensitivity tests with an assumed production error of 0.06 cm3 show the wetting exponent is determined accurately in all cases, while the non-wetting exponent is poorly determined unless non-wetting viscosity is high (error bar ±0.29 base case, ±2.59 at 0.01 cp). Capillary pressure parameters influence the whole experiment, and the wetting and non-wetting exponents influence different time regions after speed changes. History matching of random-error-corrupted data recovered exponents near 2.0, whereas ignoring a 1000 rpm/min ramp-up shifted the wetting exponent to 2.195. Recommendations are to compute capillary pressure separately, choose speeds near threshold pressure, weight early data after speed changes, and account for ramp-up.

## Local Saturation Measurement While Centrifuging (MWC) for Improving Centrifuge Capillary-Pressure Curve Determination

- ID: SCA1992-15, year: 1992
- Authors: P. Forbes, C. Chardaire-Rivière, F. Deflandre, M. Fleury
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, ultrasonic transit time, local saturation measurement, Hassler-Brunner boundary condition, Vosges sandstone, CT scanner calibration
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-15.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-15)

Ultrasonic transit-time measurements at three core locations during centrifuging give local saturations and direct capillary pressure curves, and show centrifuge assumptions can fail at fairly low speeds.

Standard centrifuge capillary pressure interpretation uses only average saturation from production and relies on ill-conditioned inversion and unverified assumptions. The authors built a centrifuge holder with ultrasonic transducers at three positions along a 7 cm core and recorded transit time during rotation, correcting for temperature and rotation effects. Calibration against CT scanner saturations on Vosges sandstone (about 23% porosity, 500-600 mD) gave a quasi-linear relation, So = a(Δt/t0) with a of 19-23, enabling a rapid one-point calibration; Berea gave a≈37. Capillary pressure curves from local saturations agreed with standard centrifuge interpretation and scaled mercury injection data. Signal changes at low production indicated failure of the boundary condition or fluid continuity at about 1800-2200 RPM (inlet Pc of 650-1000 mbar), much lower than commonly reported, while cavitation was not observed.

## A Method for Measuring in-Situ Capillary Pressures at Different Wettabilities Using Live Crude Oil at Reservoir Conditions, Part 1: Feasibility Study

- ID: SCA2002-18, year: 2002
- Authors: Arne Graue, Thomas Bognø, Eugene A. Spinler, Bernard A. Baldwin
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: nuclear tracer imaging, centrifuge capillary pressure, in-situ saturation, Na-22 tracer, collimator geometry, Portland chalk
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 11
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-18)

A nuclear tracer imaging system on a centrifuge measures in-situ saturations during spinning; feasibility tests on chalk show reproducible saturations and a plausible primary drainage Pc curve.

The paper presents a feasibility study of Nuclear Tracer Imaging (NTI) to measure in-situ saturation profiles in core plugs while spinning in a Beckman J-6B centrifuge, aiming at capillary pressure with live crude oil at reservoir conditions. ## Radial effect and sample heterogeneity effect on centrifuge capillary pressure curves

- ID: SCA1995-34, year: 1995
- Authors: M. FLEURY, P. FORBES
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, radial effect correction, sample heterogeneity, porous plate, drainage, double porosity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-34)

Centrifuge experiments at different radii confirm and validate a radial-effect correction, and show that centimeter-scale heterogeneity makes capillary pressure curves depend on sample orientation.

Two possible error sources in centrifuge capillary pressure measurement are examined: neglecting the radial geometry of the centrifugal field and sample heterogeneity. Short, wide Vosges sandstone and Estaillade limestone plugs were drained at small and large radii of rotation. Without correction the curves differed by up to about 10 saturation units, for example 0.255 versus 0.270 against 0.33 from the porous plate at about 460 mB. Applying the radial correction (parameter N of 0.064–0.114) as a data pre-process gave a single curve that matched the porous plate data. For heterogeneous Fontainebleau sandstone and St Maximin limestone, orientation barely mattered for lamination parallel to acceleration. For perpendicular lamination, entry pressure changed from 100 to 250 mB and "bumps" appeared, as reproduced by two-medium simulations. The authors recommend running both orientations, or placing the most porous face farthest from the axis if only one run is possible.

- ID: SCA1997-05, year: 1997
- Authors: E. A. Spinler, B. A. Baldwin
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary pressure scanning curves, MRI saturation profile, centrifuge, frozen octadecane, imbibition hysteresis, free water level
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0

Centrifuge-established saturation profiles, frozen in place with octadecane and mapped by MRI, give direct capillary pressure drainage and imbibition scanning curves for Berea sandstone and chalk.

Conventional capillary pressure methods infer saturation indirectly or take weeks to months, so complete drainage-imbibition scanning curves are rarely reported. ## Calillary Pressure Curve Measurement Using a Single-Moderate-Speed Centrifuge and Quantitative Magnetic Resonance Imaging

- ID: SCA2005-44, year: 2005
- Authors: Quan Chen, Bruce J. Balcom
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, SPRITE MRI, saturation profile, Berea sandstone, mercury intrusion porosimetry, single-speed centrifuge
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-44.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-44)

A single-speed centrifuge run on a long core, with 1D SPRITE MRI saturation profiling, gives a drainage capillary pressure curve matching mercury intrusion for Berea sandstone.

Traditional centrifuge capillary pressure measurements need 7-10 speeds and approximate solutions to convert average saturation to Pc. ## Centrifuge Capillary Pressure Data Interpretation: Gravity Degradation Aspect Consideration

- ID: SCA1994-25, year: 1994
- Authors: Zhigang Andy CHEN, Douglas W. RUTH
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: centrifuge capillary pressure, gravity degradation, three-dimensional centrifuge theory, equipotential surfaces, parameter estimation, Beckman centrifuge
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-25)

A 3D centrifuge theory including gravity at low rampup speeds is used to interpret capillary pressure, showing 2D models misestimate the high-saturation zone for high-permeability sandstones.

At low initial speeds (<500 rpm) in horizontal-rotor Beckman centrifuges, gravity distorts the centrifugal field in high permeability and porosity sandstones, which biases capillary pressure interpretation near threshold pressure. The authors extend a three-dimensional centrifuge theory with equipotential surfaces and use implicit parameter estimation with simple capillary pressure functions (Bentsen log, Chen reversal-curvature) on synthetic data (k>700 md, φ>20%, 50–1000 rpm) and two SCA survey samples (EF3, FA4). Curves recovered with the 3D theory lie above those from Christiansen's 2D model at high saturation, with differences of 1 to 5 saturation percent. Simple functions with few parameters can shift or force-bend the recovered curve beyond the low-speed range. Pivoted rotor heads are suggested to minimize the effect, and experimental verification remains to be done.

## Simultaneous Measurement of Multiple Capillary Pressure Curves from Wettability and Rock Property Variations Within Single Rock Plugs

- ID: SCA1999-57, year: 1999
