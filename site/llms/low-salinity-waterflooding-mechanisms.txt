## Low Salinity Oil Recovery - an Experimental Investigation

- ID: SCA2006-36, year: 2006
- Authors: A. Lager, K. J. Webb, C. J. J. Black, M. Singleton, K. S. Sorbie
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, multicomponent ionic exchange, effluent chemistry, pH variation, fines migration, wettability alteration
- Cited by 18 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-36)

Low salinity coreflood data and effluent chemistry argue against fines migration and pH rise as causes, and support multicomponent ionic exchange as the main mechanism.

The paper investigates what drives increased oil recovery in low salinity (LoSal) waterfloods of sandstone reservoir plugs (3 in. x 1.5 in.), run in secondary and tertiary mode at ambient and reservoir conditions. Effluent pH and ICP ion analyses were measured. No fines migration or permeability loss was seen in BP floods, and recovery gains occurred with low acid number crude (AN < 0.05) and no correlation with acid number. PHREEQC modelling and floods where pH rose only from 5 to 6 indicate pH rise is not the cause. Effluent Ca2+ and Mg2+ were strongly adsorbed, indicating multicomponent ionic exchange. In a North Slope core stripped of divalent cations with NaCl at 102°C, high salinity NaCl flood recovered 48% OOIP versus 35% for conventional flood, and low salinity floods gave no additional oil, supporting cation exchange removing organo-metallic complexes as the mechanism.

## Oil Recovery by Waterflooding and Imbibition - Invading Brine Cation Valency and Salinity

- ID: SCA1999-11, year: 1999
- Authors: Guoqing Tang, Norman R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, cation valence, spontaneous imbibition, Berea sandstone, wettability, fines migration
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-11)

Berea sandstone imbibition and waterflood tests show higher cation valence lowers water-wetness but raises waterflood recovery, and dilute brine injection improves recovery regardless of cation type, except AlCl3.

The paper examines how invading brine cation valence and salinity affect wettability and oil recovery in crude oil/brine/Berea sandstone systems. Cores (about 23% porosity, 500-650 md brine permeability) were aged with CS or A-95 crude oil and tested by spontaneous imbibition and waterflooding with NaCl, KCl, CaCl2, MgCl2, AlCl3 and synthetic reservoir brines. At 1% salinity, imbibition rate fell and waterflood recovery rose with increasing cation valence (CS oil: 56.7% NaCl, 60.9% CaCl2, 71.6% AlCl3). Lower salinity generally increased recovery, except AlCl3, where pH effects dominated. Injecting 0.0085 M single-cation brines into cores with PB connate brine raised final recovery by 9-15% OOIP, and switching to dilute brine at high water cut gave 6-12% additional recovery, with minor fines production but no obvious permeability damage. Earlier dilute brine injection gave better breakthrough and final recovery.

## Impact of Dopants on SCAL Experiments, Phase I

- ID: SCA2023-007, year: 2023
- Authors: Fabrice Pairoys, Cyril Caubit, Laurent Rochereau, Ata Nepesov, Quentin Danielczick, Nicolas Agenet, Franck Nono
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: sodium iodide dopant, wettability alteration, Amott spontaneous imbibition, centrifuge forced imbibition, Bentheimer sandstone, residual oil saturation
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2023/SCA2023-007.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2023-007)

Six Bentheimer samples show that NaI dopant in connate brine makes rock more water-wet, raising Sor and lowering recovery, while NaI only in imbibing brine has little effect.

NaI is routinely added to brines for X-ray saturation monitoring, but it may alter wettability and bias SCAL results. Six Bentheimer sandstone plugs were saturated with undoped or NaI-doped (1, 6, 12 g/l) synthetic seawater, drained to Swi by centrifuge, aged four weeks at 80°C with a Middle East crude, then tested by Amott spontaneous imbibition (42 days) and centrifuge forced imbibition. Samples with no NaI in connate brine showed low spontaneous imbibition and similar Amott water index (Iw 0.13-0.23) regardless of NaI in imbibing brine. Samples with NaI in connate brine were more water-wet (Iw 0.44-0.74, rising with concentration), with Sor about 8% higher at 1 and 6 g/l and up to 15% higher at 12 g/l, giving lower recovery. Proposed mechanisms (iodide interaction with oil polar species and clays) remain unverified, and carbonate and organic-dopant phases are pending.

## Experimental Evidence of Low Salinity Water Flooding Yielding a More Oil-Wet Behaviour

- ID: SCA2011-16, year: 2011
- Authors: K. Sandengen, M.T. Tweheyo, M. Raphaug, A. Kjølhamar, C. Crescente
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, wettability alteration, ion exchange, history matching capillary pressure, CO2 pH buffering, siderite kaolinite sandstone
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-16)

Low-salinity flooding of North Sea sandstone composites halted oil production, interpreted via history matching as a shift to more oil-wet conditions; CO2 buffering is proposed to control pH.

Most low-salinity studies report a shift toward water-wetness, but this work presents sandstone core floods (Rannoch-type, kaolinite- and siderite-bearing, basic oil) at 124 °C where low-salinity water appeared to make the rock more oil-wet. Three composite-core floods were run: high-salinity secondary, high-to-low switch at ~2.5 PVI, and secondary low-salinity. Low-salinity injection gave lower water saturation at breakthrough and less production, and stopped production when switched early; no response occurred when switched after more than 25 PVI. History matching in Eclipse yielded relative permeability and Pc curves needing a significant downward Pc shift for low salinity, and these curves reproduced the switched-flood behaviour qualitatively. Pore network modelling showed that a moderate wettability change can shift Pc substantially. An ion exchange framework (cation exchange vs. bridging) explains alteration in either direction, with pH acting opposite to salinity; a CO2/bicarbonate buffer was shown to control outlet pH in low-pressure floods.

- ID: SCA2010-16, year: 2010
- Authors: Nina Loahardjo, Norman R. Morrow, James Howard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: sequential waterflooding, MRI saturation profiles, residual oil saturation, Berea sandstone, D2O brine, wettability change
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-16)

MRI with D2O brine on Berea sandstone confirms that sequential waterfloods without re-aging progressively lower residual oil saturation, from 36.2% to 21.9%, matching tracer-test and volumetric results.

Earlier volumetric tests showed increased oil recovery in successive waterfloods without salinity change or re-aging; this study checks that independently with MRI. A Berea sandstone core (518 mD, 21% porosity) was flooded with D2O synthetic seawater and WP crude oil at 60 °C, so only oil gave MRI signal. The authors attribute the gains to a shift toward greater water-wetness, supported by imbibition results.

## Direct Experimental Evidence of Wettability Modification by Low Salinity

- ID: SCA2009-12, year: 2009
- Authors: S. Berg, A. W. Cense, E. Jansen, K. Bakker
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, wettability modification, montmorillonite clay, oil droplet detachment, double layer expansion, clay swelling and fines migration
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-12)

Flow-cell visualization shows crude oil droplets detaching from montmorillonite-coated slides when brine salinity is lowered, supporting wettability modification; diluted brine releases oil without clay deflocculation.

Evidence for low salinity waterflooding has been indirect, so the authors built a flow cell where crude oil droplets are attached to Na-montmorillonite particles glued on glass slides under high salinity brine. Brine was switched to 2 g/l NaCl or diluted high salinity brine at about 1 cm/s, and droplet release was quantified by image analysis (error 5-60%). With 2 g/l NaCl, oil release averaged 66.6 ± 20 vol-% (up to about 98%), but the clay swelled several hundred percent and deflocculated, releasing fines. A 4x diluted high salinity brine, containing divalent ions, released about 59% of the oil with only minor swelling and no fines release ("controlled formation damage"). A force-balance analysis shows gravity dominates in the cell while viscous forces dominate in reservoir pores. The results support weakening of oil-clay adhesion, but they cannot separate double layer expansion from ion exchange.

## Low Salinity Flooding: Experimental Evaluation and Numerical Interpretation

- ID: SCA2013-022, year: 2013
- Authors: Shehadeh K. Masalmeh, Tibi G. Sorop, Bart M.J.M. Suijkerbuijk, Esther C.M
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, capillary end effect, history matching, relative permeability, tertiary mode coreflood, wettability alteration
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-022.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-022)

Sandstone tertiary and secondary low salinity corefloods, history-matched by simulation, show low-rate tests overstate LSF benefit through capillary end effects; multi-rate tertiary plus secondary tests are needed for relative permeability.

Many published low salinity flooding (LSF) corefloods use ~1 ft/day rates, where capillary end effects can inflate the apparent extra recovery. The authors ran tertiary and secondary mode sandstone floods, including multi-rate unsteady-state tests and long composite cores up to 20 cm, and history-matched them with a passive-tracer salinity-dependent relative permeability and Pc model in MoReS. A low-rate tertiary test showed about 7% extra oil, but simulations showed much of it came from the Pc shift reducing end effect, and the match was non-unique. A high-rate HS flood followed by LS gave only 2-3% extra recovery (Sorw about 15%), and pressure drop generally fell on LS injection, indicating no formation damage. Reliable HS and LS relative permeability curves required combining tertiary and secondary multi-rate floods, while Pc remained only loosely constrained.

## Low Salinity Waterflooding of a Reservoir Rock

- ID: SCA2007_29, year: 2007
- Authors: Nina Loahardjo, Xina Xie, Peigui Yin, Norman R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, reservoir sandstone, kaolinite, tertiary recovery, crude oil/brine/rock interactions, effluent pH
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_29)

Low salinity waterflooding of four kaolinite-bearing reservoir sandstone cores gave large secondary and tertiary oil recovery gains, but repeated cycles and restorations altered core properties.

Low salinity brine flooding is crude oil-brine-rock specific, and no outcrop sandstone serves as a model, so four friable, poorly sorted kaolinite-bearing reservoir cores were tested with two crude oils at 60°C. Secondary and tertiary floods used seawater, 0.1 and 0.01 diluted seawater, 1,500 ppm dilute seawater and NaCl brines from 350 to 35,000 ppm. In Core 2060/4, secondary recovery rose from 50.9% OOIP (seawater) to 66.7% (0.1 seawater) and about 80% (0.01 seawater). Tertiary gains reached up to about 17% OOIP, often with sustained pressure drop increases and brine-in-oil emulsions, while effluent pH stayed near neutral. Repeated flooding and restoration changed the cores: seawater recovery rose to 75% in later cycles, tertiary response disappeared, brine and gas permeability increased with some fines production, and one core ended up fractured and friable. The authors conclude cycle-to-cycle comparisons on the same core must be treated with caution.

## Low Salinity Oil Recovery on Clayey Sandstone: Experimental Study

- ID: SCA2009-05, year: 2009
- Authors: M. Cissokho, S. Boussour, Ph. Cordier, H. Bertin
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, clayey sandstone, tertiary oil recovery, salinity threshold, effluent pH and ion exchange, temperature effect
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-05)

Low salinity waterflooding of a kaolinite-free clayey sandstone gave about 10% OOIP extra recovery at 35°C, with a salinity threshold and no benefit at 60–90°C.

The study tests low salinity waterflooding on DU3 outcrop sandstone (9.2% clays, no kaolinite, 398–825 mD) with a crude oil, monitoring pressure drop, pH, effluent ions and recovery. One-phase dispersion tests and secondary and tertiary floods were run with brines from 50 g/l down to 0.1 g/l. At 35°C, tertiary injection of 1 g/l brine gave +10–11% OOIP, even for NaCl-only brine, with the gain occurring in the first three pore volumes. Stepwise dilution gave no extra oil until 1 g/l, indicating a threshold between 2.5 and 1 g/l, although pH and pressure drop rose at each step. Final secondary recovery with high salinity brine rose with temperature (67.8, 73.6 and 80.8% OOIP at 35, 60 and 90°C), but low salinity gave no significant extra oil at 60–90°C. The results argue against kaolinite or divalent-ion exchange being essential mechanisms.

- ID: SCA2013-021, year: 2013
- Authors: Igor Bondino, Shashvat Doorwar, Raed Ellouz, Gerald Hamon
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, glass micromodel, kaolinite coating, oil adhesion, pH effect on wettability, emulsion
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-021.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-021)

Test-tube and glass-micromodel experiments, including kaolinite-coated micromodels, show oil adhesion depends on pH, salinity, sand type and clay; no low salinity recovery gain was observed.

The study probes mechanisms of low salinity waterflooding with simple visual tests. Oil adhesion tests used Fontainebleau and Landes sands and glass vials with high- and low-salinity brines at pH 4–8.5 and 50°C. Oil adhesion rose sharply at acidic pH (below about 5), Fontainebleau and glass behaved alike, Landes sand and kaolinite-coated sand showed no adhesion, and low salinity at high pH produced stable oil-in-water emulsions. - ID: SCA2011-07, year: 2011
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2-saturated brine flooding, CO2 exsolution, residual oil mobilization, dual-energy CT saturation, Berea sandstone, three-phase flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-07)

CO2-saturated brine flooding with gradual pressure depletion in a water-wet Berea core recovered over 50% of waterflood residual oil via in-situ CO2 exsolution and disconnected gas flow.

Waterflood residual oil is high in water-wet rock, and this study tests recovery by exsolving CO2 from injected CO2-saturated brine as pressure is reduced. A 10-inch Berea core (89 mD, 20.8% porosity) was drained to 31.6% water saturation and waterflooded to 41% residual oil. ## Assessing the impact of dopants on electrokinetic rock properties as potential indicators for dopant induced wettability changes

- ID: SCA2024_1031, year: 2024
- Authors: Matthias Halisch, Fabrice Pairoys, Cyril Caubit, Thomas Grelle
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: zeta potential, electrokinetic sonic amplitude, X-ray dopants, NaI, electrical double layer, selFrag
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1031.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1031)

Electroacoustic zeta potential measurements on three sandstones show iodide and chloride dopants give negative zeta potentials without irreversible surface change, but do not explain the NaI-driven wettability increase.

X-ray dopants such as NaI used in SCAL floods were previously found to make Bentheimer more water-wet. To probe the mechanism, Bentheimer, Berea and Obernkirchen sandstones were disaggregated by selFrag to preserve grain surfaces, and zeta potentials were measured by electrokinetic sonic amplitude on 2 wt% suspensions. Dopants NaI, KI, CsCl and BaCl2 were tested at 1, 6 and 12 g/l, with NaCl reference runs between cycles. Reference runs showed no irreversible surface change; compensated zeta potentials averaged about -5 mV (NaI), -3 mV (KI), -10 mV (CsCl) and -7.5 mV (BaCl2). Iodides gave a linear zeta potential versus ionic strength trend, while chlorides showed non-linear behavior indicating surface conductivity, and CsCl interacted with mica and clays. All dopants gave negative zeta potentials consistent with more water-wet surfaces, but the data do not explain the large Iw increase with NaI; biofilms, iodine redox species and pH effects are suggested.

## Mechanisms of Improved Oil Recovery from Sandstone by Low Salinity Flooding

- ID: SCA2010-25, year: 2010
- Authors: Munish Kumar, Andrew Fogden, Norman R. Morrow, Jill S. Buckley
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, fines migration, Berea sandstone, registered SEM imaging, asphaltene deposits, x-ray micro-CT
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-25)

Registered before/after SEM and CT imaging of Berea mini-plugs shows fines migrate with oil during waterflooding, more so with low salinity brine, suggesting a recovery mechanism.

The study examines why low salinity flooding increases oil recovery in sandstone, focusing on movement of clays and fines. Eight Berea mini-plugs (four Berea types) underwent single-phase high-to-low salinity brine flow or two-phase crude oil drainage, aging and high or low salinity waterflooding. Registered FESEM images of cut faces before and after, plus x-ray micro-CT on one uncut plug, were compared; asphaltene deposits were quantified by UV-visible spectroscopy. CT could not resolve changes well, while SEM showed small-particle changes of about 1.5-2.5% of image area in single-phase flow. In two-phase floods, small changes were ~4% for high salinity and more than twice that for low salinity, concentrated in oil-wet pores where fines accompany asphaltene films, and asphaltene mass fraction rose from high to low salinity. The authors interpret this as the moving oil-brine meniscus stripping fines that stabilize the interface and aid recovery.

## Comparison of High/Low Salinity Water/Oil Relative Permeability

- ID: SCA2008-39, year: 2008
- Authors: Kevin Webb, Arnaud Lager, Cliff Black
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflood, reservoir condition coreflood, water/oil relative permeability, residual oil saturation, in-situ saturation monitoring, live crude oil
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-39.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-39)

Reservoir-condition live-fluid corefloods on plugs from many basins show low salinity waterflooding gives about 5-40% incremental oil, lowers Sor, and leaves water endpoint permeability unchanged.

Most published low salinity corefloods used dead oil at reduced conditions, and no interpreted relative permeabilities were available for simulation. BP ran unsteady-state waterfloods at full reservoir conditions (60-130 °C, 2500-7500 psi) with live crude and brine on restored plugs, using gamma-ray in-situ saturation monitoring and an in-line ultrasonic separator. ## Contradictory Trends for Smart Water Injection Method: Role of pH and Salinity from Sand/Oil/Brine Adhesion Maps

- ID: SCA2015-005, year: 2015
- Authors: Mathilde Didier, Annabelle Chaumont, Thibaut Joubert, Igor Bondino, Gérald Hamon
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: smart water injection, adhesion map, pH control, salinity, Fontainebleau sand, wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-005.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-005)

Test-tube oil adhesion maps on Fontainebleau and Ottawa sands with controlled pH show oil adhesion increases at low salinity, contradicting the usual smart-water water-wetting claim.

Smart water injection mechanisms are unclear, and earlier adhesion maps ignored divalent ions and long equilibration times. The authors built adhesion maps for one crude oil on Fontainebleau and Ottawa sands (both >99.9% SiO2) in NaCl/CaCl2 (9/1) brines of 0.5–50 g/L TDS and pH 2–12, using a protocol with 60°C equilibration, repeated pH adjustment to remove sand buffering, and 48 h oil contact. Three areas emerged: adhesion at pH ~2.5–6, non-adhesion at pH ~6–8.5, and adhesion at pH >8.5–10. For Fontainebleau, oil adhesion extends into the reservoir-relevant pH 6–8 range at TDS <3 g/L, and small pH or salinity changes there switch the outcome, while Ottawa shows a larger water-wet zone and less salinity influence. The trends are explained by electrostatic interaction between charged oil acid/base groups and quartz, with sand roughness and specific surface area proposed as a cause of the sand differences.

## Potential wettability alteration/IOR study for pre-salt carbonate by low salinity brines: from experiments to field scale simulation

- ID: SCA2024_1046, year: 2024
- Authors: Omid Karoussi, Rex M.S. Wat, Claudio De Lima, Lidiana Ribeiro
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: low salinity waterflooding, wettability alteration, pre-salt carbonate, steady-state relative permeability, spontaneous imbibition, desulphated seawater
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1046.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1046)

Spontaneous imbibition and steady-state relative permeability on pre-salt carbonates show salinity-driven wettability alteration; sector and full-field simulations show about 2% oil gain and reduced water production.

Pre-salt carbonates with 330k ppm formation brine face large salinity shifts during seawater injection, and their wettability response is poorly studied. Spontaneous imbibition on three plugs (about 300 days) at reservoir temperature showed incremental oil recovery with each switch from formation water to desulphated, synthetic and low-salinity seawater. ## Oil Recovery from Bentheim Sandstone by Sequential Waterflooding and Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2012-22, year: 2012
- Authors: Nina Loahardjo, Winoto Winoto, Norman R Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: sequential waterflooding, Bentheim sandstone, spontaneous imbibition, Amott index, residual oil saturation, wettability aging
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-22.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-22)

Sequential waterfloods of aged Bentheim sandstone cores progressively cut residual crude oil, and imbibition rates show wettability shifts despite Amott indices of unity.

Sequential waterflooding (SWF), in which initial water saturation is re-established with crude oil between floods, was studied on Bentheim sandstone (about 2 D, 21-28% porosity) with an asphaltic crude at 60°C. Residual oil fell systematically over the first four floods, for example from 50% to 12% for core Bh 1 and 46% to 29% for Bh 3. Aging at residual oil usually increased the next flood's recovery, while aging at initial water saturation reduced it. Fired and acidized core showed no reduction in residual oil.
