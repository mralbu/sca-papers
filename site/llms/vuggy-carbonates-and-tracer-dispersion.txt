## A Dual-Network Model of Pore Structure for Vuggy Carbonates

- ID: SCA2000-09, year: 2000
- Authors: M.A. Ioannidis, I. Chatzis, Porous Media
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: vuggy carbonates, dual-network model, invasion percolation, breakthrough capillary pressure, secondary porosity connectivity, formation factor
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-09)

A dual-network model superimposes partially connected vugs on a matrix continuum to simulate drainage in vuggy carbonates; Fletcher field core data qualitatively support it.

Vuggy carbonates are hard to interpret because secondary porosity varies in amount, size and connectivity. The authors build a dual-network model of cubic matrix blocks with centered cubic vugs and throats, where vug connection probability depends on local vug porosity and a parameter ω. Invasion percolation simulations on 30³ blocks (matrix φ=0.03, k=2.72 mD) show breakthrough at 0.46–1.16 psi, far below the matrix breakthrough pressure of 59.2 psi, with plateau saturations depending on connectivity and throat aspect ratio R. Core tests on 17 Fletcher field dolomite plugs gave F=1.15φ^-1.72 (m≈1.72) and breakthrough pressure Pc=3.66k^-0.34, with a prefactor much lower than the ~16.8 for sandstones. Gas saturations at breakthrough were 20–43%, and mercury porosimetry on ~1 cm³ cubes showed heterogeneity in core #4. These results indicate a sample-spanning network of large secondary pores.

## Stochastic Description of Experimental 3D Permeability Fields in Vuggy Reservoir Cores

- ID: SCA1998-28, year: 1998
- Authors: D. Lasseux
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: probe permeameter, vuggy carbonates, semivariogram, sequential Gaussian simulation, permeability upscaling, correlation length
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-28)

Dense probe permeameter maps on seven vuggy cores feed geostatistical 3D permeability fields; upscaled permeability often matches measured values, and correlation lengths of 2-7 cm limit waterflood suitability.

Anomalous waterflood relative permeabilities in vuggy cores are attributed to small-scale heterogeneity. Flow simulation of a unit cell with one vug (20% vug volume) gave K* = 1.48 Km, so vug size or porosity distributions poorly indicate permeability. Seven full-size cores (0.8-29 mD) were mapped with a probe permeameter at 5 mm spacing (>1000 points each), semivariograms were computed, and 3D fields were generated by sequential Gaussian and indicator simulation. Upscaled Kzz matched measured permeability for four samples (A5, A7, A2, A6), overestimated it for A1 (probe leaks at large vugs), and underestimated it for A3 and A4, where a tracer test on A3 showed parallel preferential paths missed by surface data. Vertical correlation lengths of 2-7 cm (10-44% of core length) and s_logk of 1.14-2.65 meant only sample A6 appeared suitable for relative permeability measurement.

## Permeability Mapping on Vuggy Core Sample Using Tracer Experiments and Stream-Line Simulations

- ID: SCA1999-19, year: 1999
- Authors: Andrés Moctezuma-B, Marc Fleury
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vuggy carbonate, tracer experiments, streamline simulation, CT scan porosity map, NMR T2 relaxation, Koval heterogeneity factor
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-19)

Combining CT porosity maps, NMR, miscible tracer tests and streamline simulation on a vuggy andesite yields a two-value permeability map with Kv/Km of 360.

Carbonate vug heterogeneity complicates two-phase flow interpretation, so the authors build a 3D permeability map for a vuggy andesite core (20 cm long, 5 cm diameter, 22% porosity, ~0.5 mD). CT scanning (197 slices) gave the porosity map, and the variogram showed no spatial correlation of vugs. NMR T2 indicated vugs make up about 60% of pore volume. Miscible displacements at two rates and viscosity ratios of 0.3 to 3 gave nearly identical curves, with early breakthrough near 0.25 Vp and a long tail, as in layered systems. A 3DSL streamline simulation with a porosity cut-off of 0.20 and two permeability values matched the data at Kv/Km=360. Koval (H=3.4) and MHD (H=3.15) models fit better than convection-dispersion (dispersivity 7 cm), but the predicted viscosity effect was not observed.

## Tracer Tests for Various Carbonate Cores Using X-Ray CT

- ID: SCA2005-56, year: 2005
- Authors: M. Fourar, G. Konan, C. Fichen, E. Rosenberg, P. Egermann, R. Lenormand
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: tracer dispersion, X-ray CT, carbonate heterogeneity, non-Fickian transport, arrival time variance, Forchheimer inertial coefficient
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 7
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-56.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-56)

KI tracer displacements in six heterogeneous carbonate cores imaged by X-ray CT show a space-dependent dispersion coefficient and power-law arrival-time variance, so Fickian dispersion fails.

Dispersion in heterogeneous carbonate cores is poorly described by the standard convection-dispersion model. Six carbonate cores (38 mm by 80 mm, porosity 27-40%, permeability 56-2500 mD) were characterized by CT porosity and gas flow (Forchheimer). KI brine displacements (10 and 150 g/l, at 100 and 200 cc/h) were imaged in-situ with X-ray CT, and local tracer flux was computed from the mass balance. Concentration profiles and outlet flux were independent of rate and brine concentration, so molecular diffusion is negligible and a dead-end pore model is unsuitable. The dispersion coefficient varied along the core, and the mean arrival time was proportional to distance. The arrival-time variance followed a power law with exponent b of about 0.8-1.4, and the standard approach is unsuitable for heterogeneous samples.

## A Dual Network Model for Relative Permeability of Bimodal Rocks: Application in a Vuggy Carbonate

- ID: SCA2003-12, year: 2003
- Authors: Andrés Moctezuma, Samir Békri, Catherine Laroche, Olga Vizika
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: dual-porosity network model, bimodal pore size distribution, NMR T2 relaxation, mercury injection capillary pressure, vuggy carbonate, relative permeability
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-12)

A dual pore-network model built from combined NMR-centrifuge and mercury porosimetry data reproduces a vuggy limestone's properties and shows matrix and vugs control water relative permeability, vugs control oil.

Carbonates with vugs and matrix porosity have transport properties that single-porosity models cannot capture. The authors combined NMR T2 spectra at stepwise centrifuge water saturations with mercury injection on a bimodal limestone (porosity 33.8%, permeability 115 mD) to separate matrix and connected-macropore capillary pressure and pore size distributions, and to correlate T2 with pore radius (T2 ∝ r²). Single-porosity networks were tuned for the matrix (22.1% porosity, ~0.1 mD) and the connected vugs (14.9% porosity, ~115 mD), then coupled in a dual-porosity network with invasion percolation. The model matched the experimental capillary pressure curve, porosity and permeability. Calculated drainage relative permeabilities show water relative permeability depends on both vugs and matrix, especially at low water saturation, while oil relative permeability is controlled by the vugs.

## Multiphase Flow Behaviour in Vugular Carbonates Using X-Ray CT

- ID: SCA2004-13, year: 2004
- Authors: P. Olivier, L. Cantegrel, J. Laveissière, N. Guillonneau
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: vuggy carbonate, X-ray CT saturation monitoring, unsteady-state relative permeability, 3D fine-grid history matching, double porosity, miscible tracer test
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-13)

Unsteady- and steady-state waterfloods on one vuggy carbonate, with CT-derived 3D two-facies models, yield smooth relative permeabilities where 1D homogeneous interpretation gives distorted curves.

Vuggy carbonates cause early water breakthrough and anomalous relative permeability curves when unsteady-state floods are interpreted with 1D homogeneous models. One vugular grainstone/wackestone core with bimodal pore throats (cut-off 1-3 µm) was subjected to multi-rate unsteady-state and steady-state waterfloods with in-situ saturation monitoring by CT. Water breakthrough occurred at 0.31 PV injected with only 29.8% oil recovered, and oil production continued after breakthrough, especially at higher rates, consistent with slightly oil-wet behaviour. CT porosity maps and a miscible tracer test were used to build a 2x2x2 mm 3D model, calibrated for permeability. A two-facies (vugs/matrix) 3D model matched production, pressure and saturation profiles with smooth relative permeabilities (Swi 2% vugs, ~6.7% matrix; Sor 5% vugs, 45% matrix), whereas a one-facies model reproduced the distorted curves. Steady-state flow was less affected by heterogeneity but still needs in-situ saturation monitoring.

## Water-Oil Relative Permeability in Vugular Porous Media: Experiments and Simulations

- ID: SCA2002-06, year: 2002
- Authors: A. Moctezuma-Berthier, O. Vizika, P. Adler
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vugular carbonates, relative permeability, double porosity simulation, CT-scan saturation profiles, imbibition and secondary drainage, history matching
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-06)

Water-oil imbibition and drainage with CT saturation monitoring in two bimodal limestones, history-matched with 1D single and double porosity models, show macropore connectivity controls displacement and relative permeability.

Vuggy carbonates violate the homogeneity assumption of conventional relative permeability interpretation. Forced imbibition and secondary drainage water-oil displacements (50 cc/hr, capillary number ~1e-6) were run on 20 cm Estaillades (ESTA) and Brauvilliers (BRAU) limestone cores of similar porosity and formation factor, with CT-scan saturation profiles. BRAU, with well-connected vugs, showed very early breakthrough (0.16 PV, only 46% of oil recovered at breakthrough) and flat profiles, whereas ESTA showed piston-like displacement with 95% of oil recovered at breakthrough. 1D single-porosity history matching gave strongly hysteretic pseudo-homogeneous Kr curves with discontinuities, especially for BRAU. A 1D double-porosity model for BRAU matched the data with smooth, contrasting Kr curves for vug and matrix systems, though uniqueness remains to be established.

## Special Core Analysis Studies in Vuggy Porous Media of Controlled Microstructure

- ID: SCA2005-76, year: 2005
- Authors: G.S. Padhy, M.A. Ioannidis, C. Lemaire
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vuggy carbonates, model porous media, resistivity index, mercury intrusion porosimetry, NMR DDIF, vug porosity
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-76.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-76)

Synthetic vuggy media made from sintered glass beads with acid-dissolved CaCO3 particles are characterized for porosity, permeability, electrical, capillary and NMR/MRI properties as a basis for validating vuggy carbonate models.

Modeling of vuggy carbonates lacks experimental data on systems with controlled vug and matrix porosity. The authors sintered glass beads mixed with CaCO3 particles of known size (about 10x larger), then dissolved the carbonate with HCl to create vugs in 3.8 cm plugs. Total porosity ranged from about 0.25 to 0.48 and vug porosity from 0.12 to 0.37. Permeability enhancement from vugs agreed with the Neale et al. prediction within a factor of 2 for vug porosity below 0.2 and a factor of 3 above it. Electrical tests gave m about 1.3-1.5 and n about 1.8-2.3, and a Berea check gave m=1.90 and n=1.99. MIP and air-brine capillary pressure differed by about a factor of two, and NMR DDIF gave bimodal pore size distributions of 10-250 µm with the matrix pores at 10-185 µm. MRI confirmed the vug and matrix porosity distribution.

## Determination of Accessible Pore Volumes of a Porous Media During Miscible Displacement Using Tracer Analysis Techniques

- ID: SCA2003-64, year: 2003
- Authors: Seyyed Majid Hashemi
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: accessible pore volume, tracer analysis, miscible displacement, dispersion coefficient, Coats-Smith model, convection-dispersion equation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-64.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-64)

Tracer tests on four sandstone and dolomite cores give accessible pore volume and dispersion via the convection-dispersion solution; Coats-Smith model with these parameters matches n-C7/n-C10 miscible displacement.

Miscible displacement models need dispersion, diffusion and accessible (flowing) pore volume, which are hard to determine. The authors developed a tracer analysis method using brine tracer injection in four Iranian reservoir cores (two sandstones, two dolomites; porosity 8-19%, air permeability 14-160 mD). The slope of the effluent concentration profile at 1 PV and the solution of the convection-dispersion equation give dispersion coefficient and flowing fraction f. Flowing fraction ranged from 0.72 (homogeneous sandstone) to 0.585 (moldic dolomite), and dispersion coefficient rose from 6.41e-4 to 9.32e-4 for the heterogeneous dolomites. Miscible displacement tests (n-C7 displacing n-C10) on samples 1 and 3 were matched well by the Coats-Smith capacitance model using the tracer-derived parameters.

## Determination of Dispersion Coefficient in Carbonate Rock Using Computed Tomography by Matching in Situ Concentration Curves

- ID: SCA2013-053, year: 2013
- Authors: J. A. Vidal Vargas, P. C. Pagotto, R. G. dos Santos, O. V. Trevisan
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: dispersion coefficient, X-ray computed tomography, miscible displacement, convection-dispersion equation, carbonate limestone, retention factor
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-053.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-053)

CT-measured in-situ brine concentration profiles in a 77 cm limestone core are fitted with convection-dispersion models, giving dispersion coefficients of about 0.02 and 0.09 cm²/s at two rates.

Dispersion coefficients are needed for modeling miscible displacement in heterogeneous carbonates. NaI and NaCl brines were displaced in a 77.2 cm Morro do Chaves limestone core (porosity 14%, kw 30 mD) at two rates and two overburden pressures, with in-situ concentrations obtained from X-ray CT. All concentration histories were globally fitted by nonlinear least squares (Gauss-Marquardt) to convection-dispersion models with and without a retention factor. Dispersion rose with velocity (about 0.016-0.019 cm²/s at 0.0065 cm/s versus 0.088-0.093 cm²/s at 0.034 cm/s), while overburden pressure had only a slight effect. The retention model improved fits, with retention factors of 0.89-0.97 suggesting partly stagnant fluid due to heterogeneity.

## Experimental Investigation and Modelling of Waterflooding Performance of a Bioturbated Carbonate Formation

- ID: SCA2007_19, year: 2007
- Authors: I. Abu-Shiekah
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: bioturbated carbonate, upscaling relative permeability, imbibition capillary pressure, capillary number, representative element volume, Natih formation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_19)

Small-plug measurements of nodules and matrix feed fine-grid waterflood simulations of bioturbated Natih carbonate, showing nodule fraction, oil-wet imbibition Pc and capillary number control recovery, with upscaling methods.

Bioturbated Natih carbonate has centimetre-scale nodules (porosity 0.15-0.20) in a higher-porosity matrix (0.30-0.40), with permeability differing by up to two orders of magnitude. Twenty-four 1 cm plugs were measured for porosity, permeability and mercury-air capillary pressure, and the data were used to build fine-grid 3D core models (REV of 20 cm diameter by 40 cm length) with separate Pc and Corey relative permeabilities for each rock type. Simulated waterfloods showed recovery falling as nodule volume increases because water bypasses the oil-wet nodules, and recovery was sensitive to imbibition contact angle (cos θ from -0.8 to 0) and injection rate (0.04-4 ft/day). An analytical pore-volume-averaged Pc upscaling was validated against a numerical multi-speed centrifuge experiment (average porosity 28%, effective permeability 4.7 mD). Upscaled relative permeability from numerical steady-state experiments shows strong rate (Nc) dependence, with higher rates increasing oil recovery from nodules.

## An Experimental and Numerical Investigation of Water-Oil Flow in Vugular Porous Media

- ID: SCA2003-20, year: 2003
- Authors: F. PAIROYS, D. LASSEUX, H. BERTIN
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vugular porous media, drainage and imbibition, gamma-ray attenuation saturation, relative permeability in vugs, history matching, generalized Darcy law
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-20)

Drainage and imbibition experiments on artificial vugular sandstone cores, with and without density contrast, were simulated in 2D; vug relative permeabilities must keep high mobility, and matrix capillary pressure matters.

Two-phase flow in vugular carbonates is poorly described, so the authors drilled three 2.5 cm holes through square Vosges sandstone cores fitted with glass windows. Drainage and forced imbibition were run with brine and either Marcol 52 or density-matched dibutyl-phthalate, with gamma-ray saturation mapping, pressure drop and visual observation. In drainage, the vugs fill with oil sequentially at near-zero capillary pressure (three pressure plateaus) while the matrix front stays nearly immobile. In imbibition, breakthrough is early (0.32 PV for core E2), water enters the vugs and matrix simultaneously, and matrix Sor was 0.37. Gravity produced phase segregation in the vugs, which was absent with matched densities. 2D simulations with ATHOS used matrix Kr/Pc history-matched on homogeneous cores and zero Pc in the vugs. Near-constant vug relative permeabilities (Corey exponent 0.01) reproduced drainage, whereas exponent 20 failed, and imbibition depended mainly on matrix capillary pressure.

DDIF-NMR pore size distributions of synthetic and real vuggy carbonates are compared with image analysis, mercury porosimetry and 3D MRI; agreement is qualitative, vug fractions mostly match.

Vuggy carbonates have pores spanning nanometres to millimetres, and DDIF-NMR had not been tested on such multiscale media. ## Residual Gas Distribution in Vuggy Carbonate by Use of X-Ray CT, NMR and Cryo-ESEM

- ID: SCA2008-46, year: 2008
- Authors: Ann Lisbeth Bye, Lars Rennan, Hege C. Widerøe, Tony Boassen, Jon K. Ringen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vuggy carbonate, residual gas saturation, X-ray CT saturation, NMR T1-T2, cryo-ESEM, waterflooding
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-46.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-46)

NMR, X-ray CT and cryo-ESEM are combined to map matrix and vug saturations in vuggy carbonate, showing vugs can stay unflooded or hold high residual gas in waterflood.

Vuggy outcrop carbonate (24% porosity, 12 D brine permeability, vugs 0.2-10 mm) was studied to see how residual gas distributes between vugs and matrix. NMR T1-T2 and diffusion measurements gave a vug T2 cutoff of 1 s, with vugs about 55% of porosity. Medical CT during centrifuge drainage and waterflooding (5 ml/h, then 30-600 ml/h) mapped saturation by pixel porosity, with a CT porosity cutoff of 25% for vugs. Swi was fairly uniform, slightly higher in matrix, while Sgr after low-rate flooding was higher in vuggy regions. Higher rates mobilized gas from vugs. Cryo-ESEM showed some vugs fully flooded and others with high residual saturation, and the material was judged water-wet to mixed-wet.

## Characterization of Heterogeneities at the Core-Scale Using the Equivalent Stratified Porous Medium Approach

- ID: SCA2006-49, year: 2006
- Authors: Mostafa FOURAR
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: tracer test, stratification factor, advection-dispersion equation, X-ray CT, carbonate heterogeneity, dispersion coefficient
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-49.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-49)

Heterogeneous carbonate cores are modeled as equivalent stratified media with a distance-dependent stratification factor, a decreasing power law that predicts CT-measured tracer flux well.

The advection-dispersion equation with constant dispersion coefficient fails for cores with significant heterogeneity. The paper represents a heterogeneous core as an equivalent stratified medium with piston-like flow in each layer and no diffusion or cross-layer transfer. The heterogeneity factor of a lognormal permeability stratified model is replaced by a stratification factor Fs that depends on distance from the inlet. Tracer tests on carbonate cores (38 mm diameter, 80 mm long) used 10 g/l NaCl brine displaced by 150 g/l brine at 200 cc/h, with X-ray CT measuring concentration at multiple cross sections. Fitting the tracer flux at each cross section gave Fs as a decreasing power function of distance (Fs = a x^-b), and using this relation predicted tracer flux versus distance and time well.

## Characterization of Vuggy Carbonate by Miscible Processes

- ID: SCA2008-28, year: 2008
- Authors: B. Vik, K. Djurhuus, D. Doublet, K. Spildo
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: miscible displacement, dispersivity, Coats and Smith capacitance model, NMR profile imaging, D2O tracer, vuggy carbonate
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-28)

Effluent tracer tests and dynamic NMR of H2O/D2O flooding in vuggy carbonate quantify dispersion across scales, finding a ~30 cm representative length and distinct vug and matrix transport.

The study examines connectivity and representative elementary volume for miscible displacement in vuggy carbonate with 30-40 Darcy permeability. Effluent tracer tests on 20 samples from cm to m length were fitted with the Coats and Smith capacitance model. Dispersivity scattered for short samples but followed a linear trend with length above about 30 cm, implying a representative length near 30 cm. 1D NMR profiling of D2O displacing H2O in a 4.46 cm core at 0.002 and 0.001 cm/s gave in situ concentration profiles, showing in situ concentration stays below effluent concentration and full exchange takes about 8 PV versus 3-3.5 PV in effluent tests. Dynamic T2 measurements, calibrated on a Berea core with a drilled cavity (37% of pore volume), separated vug and matrix response. At higher velocity the vugs were swept faster than matrix, indicating touching vug channels.

## Improved Methodology for the Characterization of Complex Vuggy Carbonate

- ID: SCA2009-10, year: 2009
- Authors: A. Bersani, B.B. Bam, F. Radaelli, E. Rossi ENI E
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vuggy carbonate, permeability anisotropy, X-ray CT, MRI imaging, touching vugs, mercury intrusion
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-10)

An integrated multi-scale workflow combining directional permeability, X-ray CT, MRI, MICP and capillary pressure links lithofacies to permeability anisotropy in vuggy Carboniferous carbonates from Kazakhstan.

Vuggy carbonates are highly heterogeneous, and the work seeks a link between sedimentological lithofacies and petrophysical behaviour. Fifteen preserved whole cores from the Carboniferous of a Pre-Caspian Basin well were tested for porosity, gas permeability in six transversal directions plus vertical, centrifuge oil-brine capillary pressure, MICP, X-ray CT, MRI and fracture density (P21). Permeability ranged from <0.01 to 1040 md; the in-situ bioherm facies (MBDST) is matrix-controlled with the narrowest range, fine-grained reworked facies (GRST/PKST) is controlled by microfractures, and coarse reworked facies (RDST) by touching vugs, with up to four orders of magnitude anisotropy. Neither porosity nor fracture density correlated simply with permeability, and MICP showed no facies trend. MRI-based vug quantification failed because of water loss from vugs during long acquisitions, so tomography was used qualitatively.

## Effect of Wettability on the Petrophysical Parameters of Vuggy Carbonates: Network Modeling Investigation

- ID: SCA2004-25, year: 2004
- Authors: S. Békri, C. Nardi, O. Vizika
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: dual-porosity network model, vuggy carbonate, mixed wettability, relative permeability, capillary pressure, matrix-vug permeability contrast
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-25)

A dual pore-network model of a bimodal vuggy carbonate shows how matrix/vug wettability and permeability contrasts control water/oil capillary pressure and relative permeability during waterflooding.

Vuggy carbonates have bimodal pore systems in which matrix and vugs may differ in wettability, but the effect on capillary pressure and relative permeability is poorly understood. The authors built a dual-network model for a carbonate with 33.8% porosity and 115 mD permeability, using mercury porosimetry and NMR to derive pore-size distributions. Matrix properties came from a single-porosity network (about 0.1 mD, 22% porosity) and were coupled to an explicit network of well-connected macropores (43% of pore volume). With both systems strongly oil-wet, the Krw=Kro crossover occurs at low Sw (about 17%) from early water breakthrough through the vugs. Water relative permeability is controlled by the vug system and oil relative permeability by the matrix at higher water saturation. Matrix contact angles from 180° to 90° and matrix/vug permeability contrasts (Km/Km0 of 1, 10, 100) show that high contrast favors water mobility, while a moderately oil-wet matrix favors oil mobility and shrinks the Pc curves. Unrealistic extreme oil-wet matrix restoration would underestimate oil relative permeability.
