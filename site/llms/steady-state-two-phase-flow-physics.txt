## Transition from Laminar to Non-Darcy Flow of Gases in Porous Media

- ID: SCA1990-23EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: Rashed Noman, M. Zubair Kalam
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: non-Darcy flow, critical velocity, Klinkenberg gas slippage, Forchheimer inertial coefficient, critical Reynolds number, gas permeability
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-23EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-23EURO)

Gas flow tests on 23 North Sea plugs show slippage-corrected plots give the onset of non-Darcy flow, which correlates with permeability and √(k/φ) rather than a Reynolds number.

The paper investigates the gas velocity at which flow in porous media departs from Darcy's law, which matters for core analysis and high-rate gas wells. Porosity (helium expansion), Klinkenberg permeability and Forchheimer inertial coefficients were measured on 23 North Sea core plugs using air at varied flow rates. Plotting pressure drop squared against flow rate, with and without a Klinkenberg slippage correction, gave the onset of deviation. Including slippage raised the critical velocity, by up to about 20 times in low-permeability plugs and only about 5% near 100 md. Critical Reynolds numbers based on the β·k length were about 1 for 10–100 md, about 0.1 for 1–10 md and about 0.05 for 0.1–1 md, and the length scale decreased with increasing permeability, which contradicts expectation. Critical velocity correlated with permeability and better with √(k/φ), so the authors recommend measuring it directly from slippage-corrected plots.

## A Perfect-Cross-Flow Model for Two Phase Flow in Porous Media

- ID: SCA2002-05, year: 2002
- Authors: Douglas Ruth, Jonathan Bartley
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: perfect-cross-flow model, parallel tube model, modified Darcy law, Corey medium, boundary conditions, two-phase displacement
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-05)

A perfect-cross-flow parallel tube model reproduces modified Darcy law simulator results, but implies conventional inlet/outlet capillary boundary conditions are inconsistent and Darcy law may not hold for real media.

The paper develops a perfect-cross-flow model (PCFM), a parallel tube bundle in which same-phase fluid equalizes pressure between tubes with no resistance. Governing Hagen-Poiseuille equations are solved with a fully implicit scheme for a 60-tube Corey medium (λ=3), with water injection and water at the exit face. Results at varied viscosity ratios and flow rates show strong sensitivity of saturation profiles. Compared without history matching against a modified Darcy law simulator using PCFM-derived relative permeability and capillary pressure, production, pressure and saturation profiles agree closely, with small differences attributed to numerical dispersion. Using the saturation-based exit capillary pressure rather than the PCFM condition changes the differential pressure significantly. Since real media lie between PCFM and the parallel tube model (which is inconsistent with Darcy law), the modified Darcy law may not be universally valid.

## Universal, Flow Dependent Relative Permeability Scaling for Steady-State Two-Phase Flows in Porous Media

- ID: SCA2018-060, year: 2018
- Authors: Marios S. Valavanides, Ag. Spyridonos
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: flow-dependent relative permeability, DeProF model, capillary number, flow rate ratio, steady-state two-phase flow, reduced pressure gradient
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2018/SCA2018-060.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2018-060)

DeProF pore-network simulations over five decades of Ca and flow rate ratio yield a universal analytical scaling of pressure gradient and relative permeabilities with flow conditions.

Relative permeability is conventionally treated as a function of saturation, but mobile disconnected non-wetting phase makes it depend on flow rates. The study uses the DeProF true-to-mechanism stochastic model to simulate steady-state two-phase flow in a typical pore network, spanning -9 ≤ log Ca ≤ -4 and -2 ≤ log r ≤ 2 for nine viscosity ratios. The reduced pressure gradient (inverse of wetting-phase relative permeability) follows x = A(log Ca) + κ r, with A fitted by an implicit function of log Ca (x0 = -4.3, C0 = 0.003, C3 = -1.16 for κ = 1.5). This reproduces the simulated values and the physical asymptotes: linear Darcy-type behavior at high Ca and r, and a Bingham-like regime with x ∝ 1/Ca below log Ca ≈ -7.5. Relative permeability maps for both phases follow from the fit, supporting flow-dependent kr in SCAL and simulators.

## Flow in Porous Media with Slip Boundary Condition

- ID: SCA2007_13, year: 2007
- Authors: S. Berg, A. W. Cense, J. P. Hofman, R. M. M. Smits
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: endpoint relative permeability, slip boundary condition, slip length, wetting films, capillary tube model, sandstone SCAL database
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_13)

Endpoint relative permeabilities above one are explained by a capillary-tube slip boundary model; fitting Shell SCAL data from 43 sandstone reservoirs gives slip lengths of roughly 0-700 nm.

Endpoint relative permeabilities greater than one are often renormalized to 1 as suspected measurement errors. The authors treat immobile wetting films as an effective Navier slip boundary for the non-wetting phase in capillary tubes. This gives kr = 1 + 4b/R, so kr increases as pore radius decreases. They compare this with 130 endpoint measurements from 43 sandstone reservoirs in a Shell SCAL database, using an equivalent radius re ≈ 5√(K/φ). Oil endpoint kr rises to as much as 14 for small radii, and the best fit gives an average slip length of 318 ± 44 nm. Individual fields group at about 0, 200 and 700 nm. The authors conclude that renormalizing kr endpoints to 1 is unjustified and can bias production and pressure-drop predictions.

## Flow Regimes During Immiscible Displacement

- ID: SCA2016-009, year: 2016
- Authors: R. T. Armstrong, J. E. McClure, M. A. Berill, M. Rücker, S. Schlüter, S. Berg
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: relative permeability, capillary number, ganglion dynamics, Euler characteristic, lattice Boltzmann, fast micro-CT
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-009.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-009)

Lattice Boltzmann simulations initialized from fast micro-CT images show relative permeability depends on capillary number through ganglion dynamics and NWP connectivity (Euler characteristic) in a homogeneous sample.

Relative permeability is normally treated as a function of saturation only, yet it depends on experimental conditions. The authors imaged decane-water fractional flow in a water-wet borosilicate glass filter (porosity 0.35, 22 D) by fast synchrotron micro-CT. The segmented images then initialized GPU lattice Boltzmann color-model simulations on the Titan supercomputer at fixed saturation over a range of capillary numbers. Relative permeability increased with capillary number, mostly above Ca of 10^-5, and did not match connected-pathway-only permeability. Corey exponents decreased and end-point permeabilities increased with Ca. The NWP Euler characteristic became more negative with Ca, except at the highest Sw. Ganglion flux was a significant fraction of NWP flux for Sw > 0.5 and generally decreased with Ca through coalescence.

## Simulation of the Immiscible Displacement in Porous Media Using Capillary Pressure and Relative Permeability Curves from Transient and Steady-State Experiments

- ID: SCA2004-12, year: 2004
- Authors: C. D. Tsakiroglou, D.G. Avraam, A. C. Payatakes
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: history matching, steady-state relative permeability, capillary number, glass micromodel, wettability, unsteady-state imbibition
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-12)

Relative permeability and capillary pressure from transient history matching and steady-state tests on a glass micromodel differ significantly and both depend on capillary number and wettability.

The paper asks how reliable steady-state relative permeabilities are as input to transient displacement simulators, and how much capillary number and wettability dependence matters. Transient imbibition experiments were run at varying Ca on a glass-etched chamber-and-throat network for two fluid systems (intermediate wettability: hexadecane/water, contact angle 42°; strong wettability: nonanol/formamide, 9°). Pressure drop and total saturation after breakthrough were history matched with a 1-D model to estimate Pc, krw and kro, which were compared with steady-state curves measured on the same network. For intermediate wettability, transient krw was always greater than steady-state krw and converged with it at high Ca. Steady-state kro was higher or lower than transient kro at high or low Ca, respectively, and transient Pc decreased with Ca. The differences are explained by the transient growth pattern (frontal, viscous or capillary fingering) versus steady-state flow regimes, and 2-D finite element simulations are said to give very different results depending on which curves and Pc treatment are used.

## Explicit Correlation of the Steady-State Two-Phase Relative Permeability Functions of Porous Media with the Local Flow Rates

- ID: SCA2014-041, year: 2014
- Authors: C.D. Tsakiroglou, C.A. Aggelopoulos, K. Terzi, D.G. Avraam, M. Valavanides
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, capillary number, Archie saturation exponent, two-phase flow regimes, sand column, glass-etched pore network
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-041.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-041)

Steady-state oil/water flow data from a sand column and 2-D networks are fitted with power laws in water and oil capillary numbers, showing relative permeability depends on flow rates.

Relative permeability in field-scale simulation is usually treated as a function of local saturation only, ignoring dependence on local flow rates. Steady-state oil/water coinjection tests were run on a 30 cm long, 5 cm diameter sand column (k=25 D, porosity 0.42, viscosity ratio 1.0) with pressure transducers and ring electrodes; segment water saturation was derived from resistivity via Archie's law (n=1.06). Saturation and oil and water relative permeability were fitted as power functions of water and oil capillary numbers, and earlier 2-D glass-etched network data (viscosity ratios 1.45 and 3.35) were treated the same way. Water relative permeability was mainly sensitive to Ca_w and oil relative permeability to Ca_o, with weak cross effects attributed to viscous coupling and wetting films. The authors conclude that local relative permeability should be a function of local flow rates of both fluids rather than saturation.

## Operational Efficiency Map & Flow Characterization for Steady-State Two- Phase Flows in Porous Media

- ID: SCA2014-047, year: 2014
- Authors: Marios S. Valavanides, TEI Athens, Ag. Spyridonos
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: steady-state two-phase flow, relative permeability, energy utilization index, optimum operating conditions, capillary number, DeProF
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-047.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-047)

Reconstructing 83 published relative permeability diagrams into energy utilization curves shows optimum operating conditions, yielding a universal efficiency map and a capillary- versus viscous-dominated flow criterion.

Steady-state relative permeability data are usually plotted against saturation, which hides how efficiently the flow uses pumping energy. The paper transforms 83 published relative permeability data sets (19 studies; real cores, glass and virtual networks) into energy utilization index (oil produced per power dissipated) versus oil/water flow ratio. Every case shows a local maximum, the optimum operating condition, consistent with DeProF theory. An asymptotic optimum flow ratio of 1/sqrt(kappa) and maximum efficiency 1/(1+sqrt(kappa))^2 are derived for high capillary number. The results are combined into a universal operational efficiency map over Ca and r, and a scaling form for the optimum locus is used to define a critical capillary number separating capillary- from viscous-dominated flow.

## Physical origin of pressure- and saturation fluctuations in steady-state core floods

- ID: SCA2021-020, year: 2021
- Authors: Steffen Berg, Hilbert van der Linde, Niels Brussee, Maja Rücker, Harm Dijk, Evren Unsal, Tibi G. Sorop, Matthias Appel
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, pressure fluctuations, travelling saturation waves, fractional flow theory, hysteresis, Fontainebleau sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2021/SCA2021-020.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2021-020)

Artefact-minimized steady-state floods in Fontainebleau sandstone show large pressure and saturation fluctuations caused by travelling saturation waves, consistent with hysteretic fractional flow theory rather than instrument noise.

Large pressure and saturation fluctuations in steady-state relative permeability floods are often dismissed as artefacts. The authors ran drainage/imbibition cycles on a water-wet Fontainebleau sandstone (K=539 mD, porosity 0.13) with pulse-free pumps, recirculated fluids, no back-pressure regulator and in-situ linear X-ray saturation monitoring. Fluctuations beyond noise (up to 50 mbar, ±5% Sw) occurred reproducibly for 0.02≤fw≤0.67, with bimodal histograms and non-linear dominant frequencies. Space-time saturation plots show travelling water banks. Their velocities match drainage df/dS at fw=0.10 and imbibition df/dS at fw=0.05, and a rough mobility estimate shows waves transport more flux than flat profiles at Sw<0.4–0.5. Bentheimer floods aged with three crudes show fluctuations decreasing from water-wet to intermediate-wet, so fluctuations are physical and the measured kr lies on scanning curves.

## Effect of the Flow Rate on an Imbibition Capillary Pressure Curve: Theory Versus Experiment

- ID: SCA1992-09EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: François Kalaydjian
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: dynamic capillary pressure, imbibition, relative permeability, flow rate dependence, ultrasonic saturation measurement, waterflood
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-09EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-09EURO)

Waterfloods at 1, 5 and 15 cc/hr show imbibition capillary pressure and relative permeabilities increase with flow rate; an averaging-based dynamic capillary pressure term is proposed.

The standard two-phase flow framework assumes capillary pressure and relative permeability depend only on saturation. Waterfloods were run at 1, 5 and 15 cc/hr on a limestone (L13) and a sandstone (M5500), with local pressures in both phases and ultrasonic saturation measured in situ to obtain dynamic capillary pressure curves. Higher flow rates gave higher capillary pressure and a lower dPc/dS. Relative permeabilities history-matched with the measured dynamic Pc increased with flow rate: in L13, end-point water kr rose 30% and oil kr 40% from 1 to 15 cc/hr, and curvature vanished. Using quasi-static Pc instead overestimated the water end point (0.12 vs 0.09 at 5 cc/hr). A theoretical dynamic term, Pc,dyn = Pc,stat + λ ∂(φS)/∂t, was derived by averaging and irreversible thermodynamics, and λ was found to decrease roughly inversely with flow rate.

## A New Concept for the Correlation of Relative Permeability Data and Capillary Pressure for Microfissured Rocks

- ID: SCA1996-08, year: 1996
- Authors: K. Nyikes, G. Pusch, V. Reitenbach
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: relative permeability, microfissured rocks, crack network model, viscosity ratio, drainage capillary pressure, Grimsel granite
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-08)

A crack-network model with friction-controlled displacement links drainage capillary pressure to non-wetting phase relative permeability, including viscosity ratio; it matches Odeh's data and Grimsel granite only with fitted parameters.

Relative permeability of low-permeability crystalline and cap rocks is hard to measure, and classical capillary-bundle correlations ignore the viscosity ratio. The authors derive a model for a 2D network of parallel cracks, using Hagen-Poiseuille flow under constant pressure drop and friction-controlled piston-like displacement. The drainage capillary pressure curve (Brooks-Corey type, with curvature exponent m and irreducible saturation Swc) defines the crack-width distribution, and the viscosity ratio V enters the non-wetting phase relative permeability. The model was matched to Odeh's steady-state oil/brine data on a 15 mD core, where m=0.38 fit both V=2 and V=0.012. For Grimsel granite (about 5 µD, porosity 0.005) with m=0.55 and Swc=0.5, the measured gas/water data matched only when V was reduced from the actual 56.8 to 10, possibly because gas compressibility was neglected. The authors state that the matches do not prove the concept and that more imbibition measurements are needed, but the correlation adds V as an extra fitting parameter beyond Brooks-Corey.

## A Viscous Coupling Model for Relative Permeabilities in a Fracture

- ID: SCA1998-25, year: 1998
- Authors: Parc de, R. LENORMAND
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: fracture relative permeability, viscous coupling, Lockhart-Martinelli parameter, two-phase flow, Corey exponent, air-water
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-25)

A viscous coupling model for two-phase flow in a fracture gives analytical Kr relations that match air-water data well when plotted against the Lockhart-Martinelli parameter.

Two-phase relative permeabilities in fractures lack a general predictive model, and measured Kr values often sum to less than 1. The authors model the fracture as two parallel planes with a planar interface, with the wetting fluid at the walls. Solving Stokes' equation with no slip at the interface gives analytical Kr expressions that depend on saturation and viscosity ratio. Corey-type power-law fits give a wetting exponent of 1.66 and a non-wetting exponent a_G = 3exp(-1.6m), which is 2.91 for air/water. Against air-water data from Fourar and Bories (glass plates with 1 mm aperture, and clay bricks), Kr versus saturation fits poorly. Kr versus the Lockhart-Martinelli parameter fits well, and the model can convert flow rates to saturation for simulators.

## Numerical Investigations of the Steady State Relative Permeability Functionality

- ID: SCA1999-41, year: 1999
- Authors: Kare Langaas
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: lattice Boltzmann, cocurrent countercurrent relative permeability, viscous coupling, driving force, viscosity ratio, pore-scale simulation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-41.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-41)

Lattice Boltzmann pore-scale simulations show how co- and countercurrent steady-state relative permeabilities vary with saturation, wettability, driving force and viscosity ratio, indicating process-dependent mobility tensors.

The paper examines how steady-state co- and countercurrent relative permeabilities depend on saturation, wettability, driving force and viscosity ratio. A 2D thermodynamically consistent lattice Boltzmann model was run in a 288x288 lattice with 64 octagonal grains, with periodic boundaries and body-force driving, for neutrally wet and phase-1-wet systems. Relative permeabilities are generally convex in saturation. Cocurrent values rise with driving force (a factor of 2 to 2.5 for a 15-fold force increase) as phases form stationary channels, while countercurrent values fall as phases mix. Countercurrent values are always below cocurrent ones, and the phase distributions differ so much that a single mobility tensor cannot cover both flow types. Non-wetting relative permeability can exceed unity at M=0.1 because of lubrication by the low-viscosity phase.

## Investigation of Pulse Injection Using Laboratory Special Core Analysis

- ID: SCA2001-26, year: 2001
- Authors: A.S. Cable, M.P. Dorey, S.G. Goodyear, A.J. Jayasekera
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: pressure pulsing, Darcy's law, waterflood, in-situ saturation monitoring, relative permeability, viscous fingering
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-26)

Pulsed injection tests in sandpack and consolidated core show apparent single-phase flow enhancement is an artefact of higher pressure drop; pulsed waterflood raised brine mobility but not oil recovery.

Published work claimed pressure pulsing violates Darcy's law and suppresses viscous fingering. The authors ran over 60 single-phase tests in a 10,000 mD sandpack and a 100 mD consolidated core with 2.5 and 25 cp oils, at pulse frequencies of 0.2-2 Hz and with mechanical and manual pulsing. Regression of average flow rate against average pressure drop gave the same effective permeability for pulsed and unpulsed flow; apparent enhancements (e.g. 81.4 vs 3.96 mL/h with manual pulsing) coincided with inlet pressure rising to 9-12 psi. Oil production was lower for the pulsed flood (0.65 PV vs 0.74-0.78 PV at similar throughput), with no evidence of improved front stability, and high-rate floods were similar.

## Prediction of Optimum Operating Conditions for Steady-State Two-Phase Flow in Pore Network Systems Using the

- ID: SCA2003-18, year: 2003
- Authors: DeProF TRUE-TO-MECHANISM THEORETICAL MODEL, M.S. Valavanides, A.C. Payatakes
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: DeProF, steady-state two-phase flow, energy utilization factor, pore network model, ganglion dynamics, capillary number
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-18)

Using the DeProF model on 2D and 3D pore networks, the paper defines an energy utilization factor and finds a locus in (Ca, r) where oil transport per energy is maximal.

Steady-state two-phase flow in porous media is modeled with DeProF, which decomposes flow into connected-oil pathway flow, ganglion dynamics and drop traffic flow. The authors introduce an energy utilization factor, f_EU = r/W, the oil/water flowrate ratio over reduced mechanical energy dissipation. Simulations covered 2D and 3D chamber-and-throat networks with oil/water viscosity ratios of 0.66, 1.45 and 3.35, over a Ca–r domain (Ca up to 10^-5, log r from -1 to 2). For every system and each Ca, a unique flowrate ratio r*(Ca) maximizes f_EU, forming a continuous ridge line in the (Ca, r) domain. f_EU rises with Ca as connected-oil flow expands and viscous dissipation dominates, and it rises more strongly at lower viscosity ratio. At fixed Ca it rises at small r and falls at larger r as oil organizes into larger, harder-to-mobilize ganglia that increase capillary dissipation.

## An Alternative Approach to the Determination of the Inertia Flow Coefficient

- ID: SCA2005-24, year: 2005
- Authors: L. Kollbotn, F. Bratteli.
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: inertial flow coefficient, beta factor, non-Darcy flow, Forchheimer equation, formation resistivity factor, friction factor
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-24)

Proposes beta = λF/k, correlating the non-Darcy inertial coefficient with permeability and formation factor, fitting 83 cores (R2 0.96) and linking porous-media flow to pipe friction factors.

The inertial (non-Darcy) flow coefficient β normally requires special core measurements or multi-rate well tests. The authors derive a correlation using actual fluid velocity, which equals flux times the Archie formation resistivity factor F, giving β = λF/k. Regressing about 82-83 cores (0.01 mD to 3.6 D, porosity 2.2–27%, sandstones and some dolomite) with β = λφ^-1.7/k gave R2 = 0.96 and a log(β) standard error of 0.28, versus R2 0.85 and 0.56 for Geertsma's correlation. The formulation yields a porous-media friction factor analogous to the Fanning friction factor, suggesting laminar and turbulent-like flow may coexist in pores. An average λ of 2.5 μm is suggested for practical use, with F taken from logs when core data are unavailable.

## A Piv Study of Fluid Flow Through Parallel Communicating Layers of Porous Media

- ID: SCA2008-32, year: 2008

PIV measurements of water flow through model porous layers of rod arrays show flow concentrates in free zones and higher-porosity layers, with complex distributions not predicted by permeability ratios.

## The Viscous-Capillary Paradox in 2-Phase Flow in Porous Media

- ID: SCA2009-13, year: 2009
- Authors: A.W. Cense, S. Berg
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: capillary number, Darcy's law, relative permeability, capillary desaturation, residual oil, viscous coupling
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-13)

Resolves the apparent paradox between Darcy's viscous structure and low capillary numbers by showing that relative permeability lumps capillary effects and that the microscopic capillary number is ~1 for connected phases.

The paper addresses the apparent paradox that two-phase Darcy's law looks like a purely viscous law, yet capillary numbers of 1e-7 to 1e-5 imply capillary dominance. It analyzes the macroscopic picture (Darcy, relative permeability, capillary pressure, various capillary number definitions) and the microscopic picture (Stokes drag versus Young-Laplace force on a residual oil blob, and film flow in an oil-wet triangular duct). For a residual blob with typical values (1 ft/day, 1 mPa·s, porosity 0.25, 30 mN/m), the microscopic capillary number is of order 1e-7, consistent with desaturation curves. For connected phases, viscous and capillary forces balance at equilibrium (Nc≈1), and during displacement viscous forces exceed capillary ones (Nc>1). The paradox arises because Darcy's law lumps viscous coupling and capillary effects into relative permeability, and because the capillary number definition applies to disconnected blobs.

## Experimental Investigation of the Inertial Effects of Flow Through Porous Media

- ID: SCA2010-47, year: 2010
- Authors: James K. Arthur, Douglas W. Ruth, Mark F. Tachie
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Forchheimer equation, Darcy flow transition, particle image velocimetry, inertial effects, model porous media, Reynolds number
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-47.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-47)

PIV and differential pressure measurements on square rod arrays (porosity 0.51-0.88) suggest the Forchheimer equation applies even at weak inertia, with transition beginning at Re below 1.

The paper addresses whether the Darcy, Forchheimer quadratic, or cubic law best describes the transition from Darcy to inertial flow in two-dimensional porous media. Square arrays of acrylic rods with porosities of 0.51, 0.78 and 0.88 were placed in a channel with a refractive-index-matched fluid. Velocity fields were measured by high-resolution PIV, and differential pressure by a low-range gauge, over Re of about 0.2 to 3.7. Streamlines stayed close to the rod surfaces at Re near 1, became skewed at Re of 1.53, and showed separation at Re above 2, without eddies.
