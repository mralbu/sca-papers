## Quantification of Permeability Variations Across Thin Laminae in Cross Bedded Sandstone

- ID: SCA1992-02EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Alle Brendsdal, Christian Halvorsen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: probe permeameter, laminae, permeability anisotropy, crossbedded sandstone, arithmetic and harmonic averaging, well test permeability
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-02EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-02EURO)

Probe permeameter measurements on plugs, cubes and whole core quantify lamina-scale permeability in crossbedded Statfjord sandstone and give a unit permeability estimate consistent with well test.

The study uses an automated probe permeameter to characterize permeability of thin laminae in tabular crossbedded fluvial sandstone from the Statfjord Formation (Gullfaks Sør). Fine-grid probe scans, 1-inch plugs (23 Kh, 27 Kv) and 23-24 mm cubes with confined three-direction permeability were compared. Arithmetic probe averages best matched Kh (R² 0.91) and harmonic averages best matched Kv (R² 0.89, 0.93 after removing five plugs with thin laminae). The unconfined probe overestimates permeability of laminae thinner than the tip-seal, with laminae of about 1-5 mD read as 13-15 mD on cube surfaces. Cubes of visually homogeneous or micaceous material showed Kv/Kh of 0.54-0.78 not explained by probe data, indicating sub-probe-scale anisotropy. For a 7 m unit, crossbed-level averaging with a residual-saturation correction of 1.7 gave about 806 mD versus a thickness-corrected well test permeability of 816 mD.

## Measurement of "Rock Properties" in Coal for Coalbed Methane Production

- ID: SCA1991-19, year: 1991
- Authors: Bruce W. Gash
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: coalbed methane, cleat porosity, miscible displacement tracer, gas-water relative permeability, fines migration, JBN unsteady-state method
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-19)

Modified core-analysis methods on undried coal cores give cleat porosity near 2% or less, declining water permeability, and consistent gas-water relative permeability from unsteady- and steady-state tests.

Standard porosity, permeability and relative permeability methods need modification for coal, where flow occurs in cleats and drying alters structure. Four undried cores (three San Juan Basin, one Warrior Basin) were tested using a lithium chloride miscible displacement tracer, water permeability measurements and helium-water unsteady-state relative permeability with JBN analysis. Cleat porosities were 1.6% ± 0.3 or less and matched mobile water porosity within error, implying little irreducible water. Water permeability fell by up to an order of magnitude (to 0.04 md) with continued flow, attributed to fines migration, possibly face plugging, but did not significantly affect relative permeability. Dead volume uncertainty strongly affects unsteady-state results, while sodium iodide adsorption biases steady-state saturations; the two methods nonetheless agreed within error on Core B.

## Permeability Measurement on Small Rock Samples

- ID: SCA2010-32, year: 2010
- Authors: R. Lenormand, F. Bauget, Gabriel Ringot Cydarex
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: Klinkenberg effect, pulse decay permeability, resin disc, nanoDarcy permeability, Darcylog, inertial effects
- Cited by 8 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-32)

Resin-disc, miniplug, Darcylog and open-surface pulse-decay methods measure permeability on cm-scale samples from sub-nanoD to Darcy, interpreted with Klinkenberg and inertial numerical models.

Small rock samples (0.5-2 cm) are needed when plugs are unavailable, such as with cuttings, sidewall cores or laminated rock. The authors test miniplugs in silicone tubes, resin-embedded discs (1-5 mm) under a press, Darcylog liquid pulse decay, and a gas pulse-decay open-surface device (PDOS). Steady- and unsteady-state data are interpreted with a numerical simulator including local Klinkenberg and Forchheimer effects and automatic history matching. Resin discs measure from about 0.1 nanoD to several Darcy, with stabilization near 1 hour in the nanoD range. Unsteady-state data above about 1.5-3 bar require local Klinkenberg correction, and both b and Kl can be obtained from one experiment above 4 bar. PDOS is limited to below about 50 microD by thermal effects, and for laminated argilite AT1 it differs from the disc result by a factor of 6.

## Klinkenberg Permeability Measurements: Problems and Practical Solutions

- ID: SCA1991-20EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Colin A. McPhee, Kevin G. Arthur
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Klinkenberg permeability, gas slip factor, backpressure flow mode, non-Darcy flow, Forchheimer equation, net effective stress
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-20EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-20EURO)

Shows Klinkenberg permeability and slip factor depend strongly on flow mode, transducer accuracy, sleeve pressure and non-Darcy flow, and recommends backpressure measurement and corrections.

Klinkenberg gas permeability parameters are shown to be sensitive to how data are acquired and analyzed. Steady-state nitrogen measurements were made on reservoir and outcrop plugs and a 160 mD sintered glass core, comparing atmospheric versus backpressure flow, constant differential pressure versus constant mass flow, transducer error, sleeve pressure, and non-Darcy flow. Atmospheric flow gave negative slip factors and no slip factor–permeability correlation (r=0.158), while backpressure gave strong correlations (r=0.94–0.98). A ±0.1 psid transducer error changed slip factor by about ±72–74% under constant mass flow, with much less effect at constant differential pressure. Non-constant net effective stress, non-Darcy flow (corrected with an iterative Forchheimer-slippage fit) and apparent pressure-dependent slip factor at higher permeabilities further affect results, so gas permeability at 1 atm is suggested as a more robust base value.

## Permeability Characterization on Tight Gas Samples Using Pore Pressure Oscillation Method

- ID: SCA2010-30, year: 2010
- Authors: Y. Wang, R. J. Knabe
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: pore pressure oscillation, tight gas permeability, Klinkenberg permeability, FFT amplitude attenuation, phase shift, storage capacity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-30)

Applies a sinusoidal pore pressure oscillation permeameter with FFT analysis to tight gas and shale samples, finding good agreement with steady-state Klinkenberg permeability and better accuracy at microdarcy levels.

Measuring microdarcy and lower permeability in tight gas and shale samples is difficult with steady-state and pulse-decay methods. The authors use a sinusoidal pore pressure oscillation permeameter (SPPOP) in which a small upstream pressure wave (e.g. 5 psi at 100 psig mean pressure) is applied and permeability is derived from the attenuation and phase shift of the downstream signal via FFT. Tests on 1-inch plugs with 3-12% porosity at 1000 psi effective stress gave Klinkenberg permeabilities from about 2.5e-5 to 3.6e-2 mD. Results agreed with steady-state measurements between 1e-4 and 1e-2 mD, and SPPOP uncertainty grew far less than steady-state uncertainty as permeability fell. A 15% error in attenuation or phase shift changes permeability by about 20%, while storage capacity is far more sensitive, up to 2.4 times higher for a 15% phase error. Oscillation period must be increased for lower permeability so that attenuation exceeds roughly 0.01-0.02.

## Steady State Stress Dependent Permeability Measurements of Tight Oil Bearing Rocks

- ID: SCA2014-012, year: 2014
- Authors: Shreerang S. Chhatre, Edward M. Braun, Somnath Sinha, Matthew D. Determan, Quinn R. Passey, Timothy E. Zirkle, Alex C. Wood, Jeff A. Boros, Daniel W. Berry, Sergio A. Leonardi, Ryan A. Kudva
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: steady-state liquid permeability, stress creep, net confining stress, permeability hysteresis, capillary standards, Vaca Muerta tight oil
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-012.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-012)

A steady-state liquid permeability method for intact tight oil plugs under net confining stress, validated with capillary standards, shows strong stress creep, permeability decline and hysteresis.

Crushed-rock permeability does not represent in-situ tight oil rock, so the authors developed a constant-pressure-difference steady-state liquid permeability apparatus for intact, CT-screened, as-received Vaca Muerta plugs, using toluene or decalin and back pressure to dissolve gas. The apparatus measures from about 10 µD down to 30 nD and was validated with stainless steel capillary standards whose permeability follows k = d^4/(32D^2). At constant net confining stress (NCS) of 2,500 psi, permeability crept down from 5,500 to 1,500 nD over 30 days, with an asymptote of 1,290 nD, so plugs are stress-aged about 4 weeks. Initial permeability exceeded steady-state values by a factor of 2 to 10 at the first stress state, with less creep at later stages. Steady-state permeability fell exponentially with NCS (plug B: 760 nD at 2,500 psi to 260 nD at 6,500 psi) and recovered only to 440 nD on unloading; permeability modulus was 2–6×10^-4 /psi for drawdown and 1–2×10^-4 /psi for buildup.

## The Effects of Cleat Orientation and Confining Pressure on Cleat Porosity, Permeability and Relative Permeability in Coal

- ID: SCA1992-24, year: 1992
- Authors: Bruce W. Gash, Richard F. Volz, Gary Potter, John M. Corgan
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: coal cleat, gas-water relative permeability, confining pressure, permeability anisotropy, unsteady-state method, coalbed methane
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-24)

Measurements on La Plata coal show cleat orientation does not affect gas-water relative permeability, while raising confining pressure from 450 to 1000 psig cuts permeability about fivefold.

Coal permeability is expected to be anisotropic and stress-dependent, but gas-water relative permeability had not been tested for either effect. Three aligned 3.5-in. La Plata (Fruitland) coal cores were tested: flow perpendicular to bedding, parallel to face cleat, and parallel to butt cleat, at 450 and 1000 psig confining pressure. Permeability was highest along the face cleat (0.6-1.7 md at 1000 psig), lower along the butt cleat (0.3-1.0 md), and lowest perpendicular to bedding (0.007 md). Raising confining pressure reduced permeability about 5-fold and cleat porosity about 1.7-fold, consistent with Jones' fracture relation. Apparatus modifications (low-range mass flow meters, ballast vessel) improved relative permeability reproducibility from ±5 to ±2 saturation percent; simulations showed production forecasts are sensitive to this. Relative permeability showed no dependence on cleat orientation, and higher confining pressure reduced krw more than krg.

## A Detailed Analysis of Permeability and Klinkenberg Coefficient Estimation from Unsteady-State Pulse-Decay or Draw-Down Experiments

- ID: SCA2007_08, year: 2007
- Authors: Yves Jannot, Didier Lasseux, Guillaume Vizé, Gérald Hamon
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: pulse-decay permeability, Klinkenberg coefficient, draw-down experiment, sensitivity analysis, tight rock gas permeability, inverse estimation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_08)

A 1D gas-flow model with Klinkenberg effect and sensitivity analysis identify optimal pulse-decay/draw-down conditions for estimating permeability and Klinkenberg coefficient from a single pressure decay; draw-down is best.

Steady-state permeability measurements on tight rocks are slow, and separating intrinsic permeability and Klinkenberg coefficient normally needs several mean pressures. The authors built a 1D isothermal ideal-gas finite-difference model including Klinkenberg slip and computed parameter standard deviations from sensitivity matrices, assuming a pressure error of 0.5% of the initial pressure step. Three synthetic materials were studied (k = 1e-17 and 1e-19 m², porosity 0.02-0.1, b from Jones' correlation). Errors are largest when upstream and downstream volumes are equal because mean pressure barely changes, so the draw-down configuration is optimal. Upstream volume, sample diameter and length do not affect precision but control test duration. An initial upstream pressure near 10-15 bar (up to about 50 bar for high b) minimizes error, and longer recording down to P0 = 0.1 P0i improves precision.

## Non-Darcy Flow in Core Plugs: A Practical Approach

- ID: SCA1991-19EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Stuart J. Morrison, Terry P. Duggan
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: non-Darcy flow, Forchheimer equation, Klinkenberg slip factor, gas permeability, limiting flow rate, Morecambe Bay
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-19EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-19EURO)

Derives a permeability-dependent limiting gas flow rate keeping non-Darcy pressure loss under 1% in routine plug measurements, using Forchheimer β–k correlations from 46 Morecambe Bay plugs.

Routine single-point gas permeability must be free of significant non-Darcy effects before a Klinkenberg slip correction is valid. The authors argue the Forchheimer equation is continuous, so a 'critical' Reynolds number is a subjective tolerance, and they set it at 1% non-Darcy contribution. They measured Klinkenberg permeability, slip factor and non-Darcy coefficient β on 46 one-inch Sherwood plugs from Morecambe Bay (channel, sheetflood, aeolian facies, illite-free and illite-affected) at 400 psi net confining pressure with nitrogen. Field-wide correlations were β = 1.56e11 k^-1.84 and b = 8.4 k^-0.347, with facies and illite having surprisingly little influence and porosity adding little. This gives a limiting flow rate Q_L = 8.28 k^0.844 cm3/min for 1-inch plugs, presented as charts and an iterative procedure to convert kg to Klinkenberg permeability.

## Low Permeability Measurements Using Steady-State and Transient Methods

- ID: SCA2007_07, year: 2007
- Authors: P. Carles, P. Egermann, R. Lenormand, JM. Lombard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Klinkenberg correction, pulse decay permeability, Darcygas, tight caprock, mercury injection correlations, steady-state gas permeability
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_07)

Compares steady-state, pulse-decay, Darcygas and Darcylog gas permeability methods and mercury-injection correlations on tight caprock samples after Klinkenberg correction; unsteady-state reads about twice steady-state.

Permeability below 10 microDarcy is hard to measure because of very low flow rates, so the authors compared several methods on carbonate and clayey sandstone caprock samples (porosity 2-22%). Steady-state and pulse-decay gas measurements on plugs, the Darcygas pulse method on miniplugs or crushed rock, Darcylog liquid invasion, and mercury-injection correlations (Swanson, Thomeer, Kamath) were applied. A Klinkenberg factor database of 46 samples was built, and the Jones correlation fitted best for 0.0001-1 mD. On 8 plugs, unsteady-state permeability was roughly twice the steady-state value, with the difference proportional to permeability. Kamath's correlation matched Darcygas and Darcylog best (maximum differences 0.4 and 2 microDarcy), while Swanson underestimated. Permeability was 1.1-1.7 times higher at 60 bar than at 110 bar confining pressure.

## Permeability, Porosity and Klinkenberg Coefficient Determination on Crushed Porous Media

- ID: SCA2011-32, year: 2011
- Authors: Sandra Profice, Didier Lasseux, Yves Jannot, Naime Jebara, Gérald Hamon
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: pulse decay, crushed samples, Klinkenberg coefficient, gas shale permeability, sensitivity analysis, parameter inversion
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-32)

Numerical simulation and inversion of pulse decay on crushed packed spheres show that permeability and Klinkenberg coefficient are strongly correlated and hard to identify together, while porosity is robust.

Pulse decay on crushed tight or shale samples is cheap and fast but cannot apply overburden. The authors model a monodisperse sphere pack with a Darcy-Klinkenberg model, solve it numerically, and validate against an approximate analytical solution (residual about 1 mbar). Particle radius controls test duration, which drops about tenfold from 1 cm to 1 mm. Sensitivity analysis shows pressure decay is most sensitive to porosity, and kl and b are almost perfectly correlated, so inversions give errors up to 62% on b and 56% on kl at kl=1e-21 m2. With a reduced gas volume and a single large particle, inversion becomes reliable. A 6% bias on sample volume then causes about 48% error on kl and large porosity error, whereas b is unaffected and a 2% chamber volume bias gives only about 2% error.

## Principles, Practice and Applications of Laboratory Minipermeametry

- ID: SCA1990-26EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: Christian Halvorsen, Andrew Hurst
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: minipermeameter, Hassler-sleeve permeability, automated gas permeability, permeability heterogeneity, sampling density, net-to-gross
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-26EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-26EURO)

Describes Statoil's automated laboratory minipermeameter, its calibration against Hassler-sleeve plugs and an analytical solution, core handling changes, and applications showing dense grid data reveal heterogeneity missed by plugs and logs.

The paper argues that laboratory minipermeametry should become a standard core analysis method and documents Statoil's automated x,y-table minipermeameter (nitrogen, constant pressure 10-900 mbar, 1 mD to 15 D, <3% uncertainty, <1% repeatability). Calibration was done on homogeneous plugs and by the Goggin et al. analytical solution; for the large probe, calculated/measured permeability ratios were 0.89-1.01, while the small probe gave values about 20% too low. Fresh versus dried core showed drying matters for 2-20 mD material but not above 100 mD. Edge effects were under 5% even with half the tip over the edge, and revised core handling (minipermeametry before slabbing and 1" plugging) is proposed. Dense grids revealed more heterogeneity than CT images, up to 41% differences between orthogonal faces, and in a laminated reservoir gave a net-to-gross of 0.66 at a 20 mD cutoff versus 1.00 from plugs and logs; N0 tests suggested optimal spacing of 7-10 cm for plugs.

## "Correlations of Liquid and Air Permeabilities for Use in Reservoir Engineering Studies"

- ID: SCA1990-12, year: 1990
- Authors: Virgil J. Pugh, David C. Thomas
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: air permeability, brine permeability, effective oil permeability, Klinkenberg effect, clayey reservoir, permeability correlation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-12)

Correlates air permeability with oil and brine permeability for 1868 samples, showing air permeability approximates liquid permeability and can predict effective oil permeability in a clayey reservoir, cutting costs.

Amoco analyzed 40 years of specific permeability data from 875 wells to test whether routine air permeabilities can represent liquid permeabilities. Air-versus-oil data for 1868 samples (1117 sandstones, 422 dolomites, 329 limestones; 0.1 to 10,000 md) correlate closely, with oil slightly lower, attributed to the Klinkenberg effect. Brine permeability is lower, with a brine-to-air ratio of about 0.5 across the range, and rock types show similar deviations. For a clayey reservoir (16% smectite, 4% chlorite, 3% kaolinite), effective oil permeability measured on 71 of 282 samples was correlated with air permeability. The oil-to-air ratio is about 1.0 at 10,000 md, 0.53 at 1000 md and 0.12 at 1 md. The correlation estimated oil permeability for the other 211 samples, so oil permeability was measured on only about 25% of samples, reducing costs.

## Measurement of Gas Permeabilities Under Different Compaction Modes on Tight Reservoir Samples

- ID: SCA1995-19, year: 1995
- Authors: Ludovic D. JAUMOUILLE, Philippe R. CORDELIER, Adel KHARROUBI
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: uniaxial compaction, hydrostatic stress, pulse decay permeability, Klinkenberg correction, tight sandstone, stress-dependent permeability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-19)

Gas permeability and porosity of tight sandstones and carbonates were measured under hydrostatic and uniaxial compaction; uniaxial loading gave smaller permeability and porosity reductions.

Permeability is usually measured under hydrostatic load, which does not reproduce reservoir stress paths during depletion. The authors built an unsteady-state gas permeameter (pulse decay for low permeability, draw-down for higher) with Klinkenberg correction. Three tight sandstones and two dolomite samples (about 0.01 to 0.9 mD) were loaded hydrostatically and uniaxially up to 1200 bar (sandstone) or 700 bar (carbonate). Hydrostatic permeability reductions were 57-65% in sandstones versus 32-45% uniaxial, while carbonates showed only 5-8% uniaxial reduction. Lower-permeability rocks lost proportionally more permeability, and higher-permeability sandstones (6.5 and 12.6 mD) lost under 21% (uniaxial) and 29% (hydrostatic). Hydrostatic tests alone overestimate property loss, and no general correction factor can be derived.

## Low Water Permeability Measurements of Clay Sample. Contribution of Steady State Method Compared to Transient Methods

- ID: SCA2010-31, year: 2010
- Authors: P.F. Boulin, P. Bretonnier, N. Gland, J.M. Lombard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: steady state permeability, pulse decay, pore pressure transmission test, clay rock, nanoDarcy permeability, specific storage
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-31.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-31)

Steady-state push-pull measurement with a dual-piston pump gave 0.8 nD water permeability of Tournemire argillite in three days, outperforming pulse decay and PPTT in reliability.

Very low permeability clays are usually measured by transient pulse decay because steady state is thought too slow. The authors tested one Tournemire argillite core (40 mm diameter, 20 mm long, 15 MPa confining, 10 MPa mean pore pressure) with steady state push-pull using a dual-piston Quizix pump, a pore pressure transmission test (PPTT), and a pulse decay test. PPTT did not reach equilibrium in six days and gave no unique (kw, Ss) pair; with kw fixed it gave Ss of 3.4e-6 1/m. Pulse decay was affected by a micro leak, reservoir storage factor and initial pulse uncertainty, so kw could only be bracketed between 0.2 and 2 nD.

## A Simultaneous Determination of Permeability and Klinkenberg Coefficient from an Unsteady-State Pulse-Decay Experiment

- ID: SCA2008-09, year: 2008
- Authors: Yves Jannot, Didier Lasseux, Laure Delottier, Gérald Hamon
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: pulse-decay permeability, Klinkenberg coefficient, draw-down experiment, inverse modelling, Levenberg-Marquardt, tight rock gas permeability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-09)

An inverse Levenberg-Marquardt method with a complete flow model estimates permeability and Klinkenberg coefficient from draw-down pressure decay; porosity and dead volume errors matter most.

Interpreting pulse-decay and draw-down gas experiments on tight rocks with simplified constant-mass-flow models requires long records and can give unphysical results, such as a negative Klinkenberg b. The authors couple a Levenberg-Marquardt inversion to a complete 1D finite-difference flow model including compressibility and Klinkenberg slip. Tests used noisy synthetic signals for three materials (k = 1e-17 and 1e-19 m², porosity 0.02 and 0.1). For material 2, the simplified model gave b = -5.2e5 Pa, while the full inversion recovered k = 1.0e-17 m² and b = 2.487e5 Pa. Time sampling, errors in sample diameter and length (1e-4 m) and errors in upstream volume (1e-8 m³) had little effect (below about 1.5% extra error on k and 3.3% on b). Porosity errors (±20% for ε ≤ 0.05) and a 1% upstream dead volume caused large errors, up to about 59% in k and over 150% in b, so both must be known precisely.

## The “Step Decay”: A New Transient Method for the Simultaneous Determination of Intrinsic Permeability, Klinkenberg Coefficient and Porosity on Very Tight Rocks

- ID: SCA2012-25, year: 2012
- Authors: Didier Lasseux, Yves Jannot, Sandra Profice, Mathieu Mallet, Gérald Hamon
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: Step Decay, pulse decay, Klinkenberg coefficient, tight rock permeability, history matching, sensitivity analysis
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-25)

A transient Step Decay method uses measured upstream pressure steps as input and matches downstream response to get permeability, Klinkenberg coefficient and porosity in one test, reproducible across two labs.

Classical pulse-decay tests for tight rocks are sensitive to porosity bias, upstream dead volume, initial pulse pressure error and leaks. Synthetic tests show a 20% porosity error or a dead volume under 1% of V0 can produce errors of roughly 65–940% on k1 and b. The Step Decay method imposes the measured upstream pressure P0(t) as a boundary condition and history-matches only the downstream build-up P1(t), removing dependence on V0, dead volume and leaks. Applying multiple pressure steps raises sensitivity to porosity and decorrelates k1 and b. Synthetic cases (10 µD to 10 nD) recovered parameters within 0.5%. Three plugs tested independently in two laboratories agreed within about 27% on k1, 30% on b and 6% on porosity.

## Low Permeability Measurements: Insights

- ID: SCA2015-021, year: 2015
- Authors: Sandra Profice, Gérald Hamon, Benjamin Nicot
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Step Decay permeability, Pulse Decay, Klinkenberg coefficient, pyrophyllite, shale permeability, round-robin test
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-021.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-021)

Step Decay, Pulse Decay and steady-state tests on pyrophyllite and shale agree, supporting Darcy-Klinkenberg flow; a round-robin shows modest inter-laboratory discrepancies when plugs are untreated.

Reported inter-laboratory permeability discrepancies of several decades in tight rocks motivated a comparison of methods and interpretation models. Five pyrophyllite plugs (10-60 nD, ~20 nm pore throats) and two shales were tested with Step Decay, Pulse Decay and gas steady-state, plus liquid steady-state on one plug, with porosity checked by helium pycnometry. Step Decay and Pulse Decay kl differed by 5-17% and steady-state gas versus Pulse Decay by 19-40%. Klinkenberg plots were linear despite Knudsen numbers of 0.04-3.8, supporting Darcy-Klinkenberg flow, with deviations only at short times. In a round robin on untreated whole plugs, laboratory results differed from Total's by 9-27% (steady-state) and 31-118% (unsteady-state), a maximum factor of 3.8. The authors conclude that sample preparation, not interpretative models, likely causes large discrepancies and recommend whole-plug measurements.

## Low permeability measurement on crushed rock: insights
