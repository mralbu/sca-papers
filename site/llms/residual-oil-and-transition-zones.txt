## Pore-Scale to Core-Scale Study of Capillary Desaturation Curves Using Multi-Scale 3D Imaging

- ID: SCA2013-027, year: 2013
- Authors: Rezki Oughanem, Souhail Youssef, Yannick Peysson, Brigitte Bazin, Eric Maire, Olga Vizika
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, trapping number, micro-CT, surfactant flooding, residual oil saturation, pore network
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-027.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-027)

Coreflood CT and micro-CT on four water-wet sandstones show capillary desaturation curves depend on pore structure; a permeability-modified trapping number reduces the dispersion between curves.

Capillary desaturation curves (CDC) show large scatter between rocks, and this study links that scatter to pore structure. Berea, Bentheimer, Fontainebleau and Clashach cores were flooded vertically with KI brine/n-decane, then with SDBS surfactant (IFT 0.3 dyn/cm) at rates up to 499 cc/hr, with CT scanning of saturation profiles after each step. Residual oil mobilized at trapping numbers of 1e-6 to 1e-3, with clear shifts between sandstones. Micro-CT of mini-plugs (3 µm voxels) gave pore body radii (mean 28.6-41.4 µm), throat radii (10.5-14.8 µm), aspect ratios (mean ~5) and trapped oil blob sizes (mean 31-40 µm, about one pore body). A force-balance model gives Nt = 2KaKrw/(rL), and plotting normalized Sor against Nt/(KaKrw) reduces the dispersion; the remainder is attributed to blob length variation.

## Visualizing and Quantifying the Residual Phase Distribution in Core Material

- ID: SCA2009-16, year: 2009
- Authors: Munish Kumar, Tim J. Senden, Adrian P. Sheppard, Jill P. Middleton, Mark A. Knackstedt
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: micro-CT imaging, residual saturation, blob size distribution, spontaneous imbibition, pore partitioning, wettability
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-16)

Micro-CT imaging of registered cores after spontaneous and forced imbibition quantifies residual phase blob sizes versus initial water saturation, rate and wettability in sandstone and carbonates.

The paper images the pore-scale distribution of residual gas and oil after waterflooding using micro-CT of the same small cores registered across saturation states. Samples were Fontainebleau sandstones, sucrosic dolomites and a bimodal reservoir carbonate, subjected to spontaneous and forced imbibition at varying initial water saturation, capillary number and wettability (steric-acid mixed-wet treatment). Residual gas saturations in Fontainebleau (e.g. 28.9% at Swi=0) agree with literature data and Aissoui's relationship. Residual phase is held in large ramified blobs spanning from tens to thousands of pores. Forced imbibition at Ca~2x10-4 in water-wet dolomite cut Sor from 37% to 28% by mobilizing the larger blobs. Mixed-wet samples showed larger blobs after spontaneous imbibition, but blob distributions were similar after forced imbibition.

## Oil ganglia dynamics in natural porous media during surfactant flooding captured by ultra-fast x-ray microtomography

- ID: SCA2014-023, year: 2014
- Authors: Souhail Youssef, Elisabeth Rosenberg, Herve Deschamps, Rezki Oughanem, Eric Maire, Rajmund Mokso, INSA-Lyon MATEIS, Swiss Light Source, Paul Scherrer
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: synchrotron microtomography, surfactant flooding, oil ganglia mobilization, capillary number, Bentheimer sandstone, phase contrast imaging
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 5
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-023.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-023)

Ultra-fast synchrotron microtomography (5 μm, 3 s) imaged drainage, imbibition and surfactant flooding in Bentheimer sandstone, showing ganglia coalescence and breakup, with mean cluster size following a power law in capillary number.

The study images oil ganglion trapping and mobilization in natural sandstone during surfactant flooding, which is hard to observe with conventional methods. A 5.8 mm Bentheimer miniplug (22% porosity, 3.8 D) underwent drainage, spontaneous imbibition and surfactant injection (IFT 0.3 mN/m, five rates 0.05–4 cc/min) at the TOMCAT beamline, with 3D tomograms every 3 s. Drainage reached 52% oil saturation and imbibition left a residual of 35%, with oil trapped in the largest pores (mean radius 62.2 μm versus 52.2 μm sample mean) through snap-off. During surfactant flooding, residual oil fell to 4% at 1.4 cc/min and 4 cc/min recovered no more oil. The largest cluster grew right after each rate increase through coalescence, then decreased as it was evacuated, and mean cluster size followed a power law in capillary number (about 10^-4 to 10^-2).

## Waterfloodings on Intermediate Wet Porous Media : New Considerations on Two Phase Flow Properties Determination

- ID: SCA1992-13EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Renaud Gauchet
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, imbibition capillary pressure, centrifuge, intermediate wettability, gamma-ray saturation profiles, waterflooding
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-13EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-13EURO)

Combining waterflood and centrifuge imbibition capillary pressure on Aerolith 10 and Berea shows coreflood ROS exceeds true residual oil by 5-10% in intermediate-wet cores.

Waterflood remaining oil saturation often overestimates residual oil in intermediate-wet, permeable media. Waterfloods at various rates, some with gamma-ray saturation monitoring, and centrifuge imbibition capillary pressure measurements were run on Aerolith 10, Berea and a Lagrave reservoir core at water-wet and aged wettability states. In intermediate-wet Aerolith 10, ROS fell from 36.7% to 21.8% as rate rose from 16.7 to 600 cm3/h, while the water-wet case showed no extra recovery. Points (Sw, ΔP) from rate increases fell on the centrifuge Pc curve, showing ROS is capillary-limited; centrifuge reaches larger gradients (800 vs 100 mbar). Coreflood ROS was 5-10% higher than centrifuge ROS, so relative permeabilities should be determined down to the centrifuge ROS, and the two tests should be combined.

## The Structure of Residual Oil as a Function of Wettability Alteration Using Pore-Scale Network Modelling

- ID: SCA2011-37, year: 2011
- Authors: K.S. Sorbie, A.V. Ryazanov, M.I.J. van Dijke
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: pore network modelling, residual oil structure, oil layers, mixed-wet, Amott-Harvey index, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-37)

Pore-network modelling of waterflooding on a Berea network predicts residual oil shifts from layer-dominated in oil-wet cases to bulk-oil-dominated in water-wet cases, with testable structural predictions.

Low residual oil in mixed-wet waterfloods requires oil layers in pore corners, so the authors use a Berea micro-CT-derived network with thermodynamic oil-layer existence criteria. Relative permeabilities from the network feed 1D Buckley-Leverett calculations of remaining oil versus pore volumes injected, matched to experimental data at breakthrough and then predicting 3 and 20 PV well. Four wettability cases (Iow = -0.47, 0.13, 0.31, 0.59) are visualised in 3D and analysed statistically by pore size. For the oil-wet case Sor falls from 51.9% at breakthrough to 2.0% at infinite PV, while the moderately water-wet case stays at 24.7% as trapped bulk oil. Residual oil in oil-wet cases is mostly in many low-volume layers across all pore sizes, whereas in water-wet cases it is bulk oil in larger pores. The predictions are semi-quantitative and testable by micro-CT or NMR.

## Transition Zone Characterization with Appropriate Rock-Fluid Property Measurements

- ID: SCA1999-39, year: 1999
- Authors: Richard L. Christiansen, Michael J. Heymans, Anand Kumar
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: transition zone, trapped oil relationship, residual oil saturation, saturation profile, video image gray level, unconsolidated pack
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-39.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-39)

Using video-imaged saturation profiles in unconsolidated packs, the paper shows residual oil in transition zones depends on initial oil saturation, so oil there is more recoverable than conventionally assumed.

Conventional transition-zone analysis pairs drainage capillary pressure with waterflood relative permeability and assumes a constant residual oil saturation. The authors argue that residual oil saturation instead depends on initial oil saturation and saturation history, so trapped-oil relationships and a suite of relative permeabilities are needed. They built a vertical 48.3-cm acrylic tube apparatus packed with glass beads (70-100 mesh), sand (20-40 mesh) and plastic beads (20-40 mesh), saturated with water and nonane. Saturations were estimated from video gray level using a two-point calibration. Cross-plots of initial versus final oil saturation gave trapped-oil relationships, with little scatter for glass beads and more for sand. Material balance errors on the video saturations ranged from about 4% to 57% across steps. The paper outlines how reservoir rock could be tested with flooding or centrifuge methods and why reservoir charging history must be known.

## The Fate of Oil Clusters During Fractional Flow: Trajectories in the Saturation-Capillary Number Space

- ID: SCA2015-007, year: 2015
- Authors: M. R¾cker, R. Armstrong, A. Georgiadis, H. Ott, L. Simon, F. Enzmann, M. Kersten, S. de With
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: ganglion dynamics, fractional flow, synchrotron micro-CT, cluster-based capillary number, connected pathway flow, steady-state relative permeability
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 8
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-007.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-007)

Fast synchrotron micro-CT of decane-brine co-injection in sintered glass tracks oil clusters in saturation-capillary number space, distinguishing connected pathway flow from ganglion dynamics that cause pressure and saturation fluctuations.

Steady-state relative permeability experiments show pressure and saturation fluctuations whose origin is unclear. The authors co-injected n-decane and brine into a water-wet sintered glass sample (35% porosity, 22 D) at different fractional flows and total rates (4e-6 to 4e-4 m/s), imaging at 2.2 µm voxel size once per minute at the TOMCAT beamline. Individual clusters were tracked: coalescence moves them up in volume and capillary number, while snap-off moves them down. Cluster volumes follow a power law with exponent about 2.5, consistent with percolation scaling. At fw = 0.5 ganglion dynamics gave up-and-down trajectories in the saturation-Ca diagram, while at fw = 0.2 connected pathway flow stayed at a fixed point, even though the average Ca was below 1. Ganglion disconnection and reconnection changed the relative permeability of the connected phases, mirroring fluctuations seen in 5 cm core floods.

## Oil Mobility in Transition Zones

- ID: SCA2000-02, year: 2000
- Authors: Shehadeh Masalmeh, Sjaam Oedai
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: transition zone, residual oil saturation, initial oil saturation, Land correlation, centrifuge relative permeability, mixed-wet carbonate
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-02)

Centrifuge measurements on low-permeability carbonates show residual oil is independent of initial oil saturation after aging, but oil relative permeability rises as initial saturation falls.

Oil recovery from transition zones depends on how residual oil saturation (Sor) and relative permeability vary with initial oil saturation (Soi), which is rarely measured; Land's gas/water correlation is commonly used despite ignoring wettability. The authors ran multi-speed and single-speed ultracentrifuge drainage/imbibition cycles on low-permeability carbonate plugs (1-6 md) at 60 °C, aging in crude at 70 °C and 100 bar, and interpreted the data numerically with the MoReS simulator. Sor stayed about constant (0.04-0.05, within 2 saturation units) for Soi of 20-80%, and fell only for Soi below 20%. Cyclic testing without cleaning did not change trapped oil, indicating that trapped oil reconnects on re-injection. Oil relative permeability at a given saturation increased with decreasing Soi, and remaining oil saturation at a kro cut-off exceeded Sor by more than 10% at high Soi, so Sor is approached faster nearer the free water level.

## Trapped Non-Wetting Phase Clusters: An Experimental Investigation of Dynamic Effects at the Pore Scale Using a Micro-CT Scanner

- ID: SCA2013-024, year: 2013
- Authors: M. Khishvand, M. Akbarabadi, M. Piri
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: micro-CT imaging, trapped oil clusters, capillary number, residual oil saturation, imbibition, Bentheimer sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-024.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-024)

Micro-CT imaging of three drainage-imbibition cycles in Bentheimer sandstone shows that higher capillary number imbibition reduces residual oil saturation and trapped oil cluster size and oil-brine contact area.

The study examines how dynamic (viscous) effects change trapping of non-wetting oil during imbibition at the pore scale. A custom micro core holder was integrated with a two-phase flooding apparatus in a Versa XRM 500 micro-CT scanner. Three unsteady-state drainage-imbibition cycles were run on a 4.9 mm by 13 mm Bentheimer core with n-decane and 1.5 wt% NaI brine, at 24 °C and 2.76 MPa, with the same Swi of 0.34 and increasing imbibition rates (0.015, 0.075, 0.75 cc/min). Middle-section residual oil saturation was 0.41 and 0.37 for the first two imbibitions but fell to 0.16 for the third at the highest rate. Trapped blobs became smaller and shorter, the cumulative volume shifted toward smaller blob volumes, and oil-brine contact area decreased while brine-grain contact increased. Blob aspect ratios were less scattered at high rate and differed from glass bead pack results.

## Onset of Oil Mobilization and Non-Wetting Phase Cluster Size Distribution

- ID: SCA2014-022, year: 2014
- Authors: R. T. Armstrong, A. Georgiadis, H. Ott, A. Schwing, R. Neiteler, N. Brussee, A. Makurat, M. Rücker, L. Leu, M. Wolf, F. Khan, F. Enzmann, M. Kersten
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: synchrotron micro-CT, imbibition, cluster size distribution, snap-off, invasion percolation, macroscopic capillary number
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-022.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-022)

Fast synchrotron micro-CT of imbibition in sandstone shows the largest oil cluster breaks into smaller ones by snap-off, with occasional coalescence, contradicting static-cluster percolation assumptions.

Pore-scale oil mobilization during imbibition was imaged with fast synchrotron micro-CT (2.2 µm) in sintered glass, Gildehauser sandstone and Estaillades limestone, using n-decane and brine. Sandstone showed the most mobilization (about 60% of oil), with saturation dropping from 75% to 30% after ~1200 s at Ca_macro of 1.6–2.3×10^-3, i.e. capillary-dominated flow. Snap-off at narrow throats or across several pores dominated breakup, with rare coalescence, showing disconnected clusters continue to evolve.

- Authors: Souhail Youssef, Daniela Bauer, Yannick Peysson, Olga Vizika
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, trapping number, surfactant flooding, micro-CT ganglia size distribution, water relative permeability, sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-064.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-064)

Surfactant and brine floods on four water-wet sandstones show capillary desaturation curves collapse with a permeability- and pore-size-based trapping number, enabling CDC prediction from micro-CT ganglia statistics.

The paper examines how pore structure controls mobilization of trapped oil and the capillary desaturation curve (CDC). ## Impact of Capillary Forces on Residual Oil Saturation and Flooding Experiments for Mixed to Oil-Wet Carbonate Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2012-11, year: 2012
- Authors: Shehadeh K. Masalmeh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, capillary end effect, Bond number, centrifuge imbibition, non-water-wet carbonate, surfactant IFT reduction
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-11)

Centrifuge and flooding tests on non-water-wet carbonates show capillary end effect inflates remaining oil at field rates, while Sorw (~10-15%) stays stable up to Bond number ~1e-3.

Residual oil saturation measurements in non-water-wet carbonates are often corrupted by capillary end effect, especially at field rates of ~1 ft/d. The study used over 100 plugs of varying permeability, aged in crude, with multispeed imbibition centrifuge tests (with and without surfactant) and steady/unsteady-state floods on 16 samples, interpreted by numerical simulation. Remaining oil at equilibrium was independent of pore volumes injected.

## Capillary Desaturation Curve Prediction Using 3D Microtomography Images

- ID: SCA2015-008, year: 2015
- Authors: Souhail Youssef, Yannick Peysson, Daniela Bauer, Olga Vizika
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, trapping number, micro-CT oil cluster size distribution, surfactant flooding, residual oil saturation, sandstone pore structure
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-008.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-008)

Capillary desaturation curves of four sandstones are rescaled with a modified trapping number and predicted from oil cluster size distributions measured by micro-CT at residual oil saturation.

The paper links capillary desaturation curves (CDC) to pore structure and trapped oil cluster size in water-wet Berea, Bentheimer, Fontainebleau and Clashach sandstones. Macro-plug coreflood with CT scanning measured residual oil versus trapping number (brine/surfactant, IFT 40 to 0.3 mN/m). ## Impact of Wettability on Residual Oil Saturation and Capillary Desaturation Curves

- ID: SCA2013-025, year: 2013
- Authors: K. J. Humphry, B. M. J. M. Suijkerbuijk, H. A. van der Linde, S. G. J. Pieterse, S. K. Masalmeh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, residual oil saturation, Bond number, spontaneous imbibition, Berea sandstone, wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-025.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-025)

Berea sandstone cores with varied wettability show that less water-wet rock has lower residual oil saturation and critical capillary and Bond numbers at least ten times higher than 1e-5.

Capillary desaturation data for non-water-wet porous media are scarce, and it is unclear whether differences between water-wet sandstone and mixed-wet carbonate come from wettability or pore structure. Eighteen outcrop Berea plugs were prepared with five brine/oil/aging combinations (including n-decane for strongly water-wet), and wettability was characterized by spontaneous imbibition at 70°C. Desaturation curves were measured with multi-speed centrifuge (Bond number) and unsteady-state flooding, including polymer to extend the range (capillary number). Residual oil saturation ranged from about 15% for mixed-wet samples to nearly 50% for strongly water-wet ones. The critical Bond number was about 1e-5 for n-decane cores and about 1e-4 for aged crude-oil cores, even though both had an Amott index of 1. Mixed-wet samples showed critical numbers at least an order of magnitude higher and incomplete desaturation. The results indicate desaturation depends primarily on wettability rather than pore structure, which matters for surfactant and ASP flood design.

## Continuous vs Discontinuous Capillary Desaturation and Implications for IOR/EOR

- ID: SCA2018-066, year: 2018
- Authors: Rui Xu, Bernd Crouse, David M. Freed, Andrew Fager, Nathan Lane, Qiang Sheng
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, digital rock simulation, lattice-Boltzmann, critical capillary number, wettability, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2018/SCA2018-066.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2018-066)

Lattice-Boltzmann pore-scale simulations on a digital Berea sandstone generate continuous and discontinuous capillary desaturation curves for water-wet and oil-wet cases, reproducing lab trends in critical capillary number.

The study uses digital rock pore-scale simulation to generate capillary desaturation curves (CDC) for a Berea sandstone micro-CT image (2.02 µm voxels, 500³ voxels, porosity 15.7%, 235 mD). Lattice-Boltzmann waterfloods start at Swi of 10%, with water-wet and oil-wet cases set by contact angles (30° and 150° on oil-contacted surfaces; Amott indices Iw=0.9 and 0 respectively). Continuous CDCs use separate floods at different capillary numbers, while discontinuous CDCs step up the pressure gradient on one flood. In water-wet rock the discontinuous CDC has a higher critical capillary number than the continuous one, the curves merge above Nc of about 6E-4, and the results resemble Chatzis & Morrow lab data. In oil-wet rock, remaining oil depends on injected pore volumes because of film flow; the critical Nc is about 7.4e-5, oil-wet recovers more oil at low Nc, and water-wet recovers more at high Nc.

## Capillary Transition Zones from a Core Analysis Perspective

- ID: SCA2000-20, year: 2000
- Authors: Johne Alex Larsen, Trond Thorsen, Geir Haaskjold
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary transition zone, Land correlation, residual oil saturation, Killough hysteresis, X-ray saturation monitoring, gravity drainage
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-20)

Gravity-drained one-metre Berea composite core with X-ray saturation monitoring shows residual oil depends on initial oil saturation and permeability; Killough hysteresis simulations reproduce waterflood profiles.

The paper addresses flow in oil-water capillary transition zones, where residual oil saturation (ROS) depends on the historical maximum oil saturation (HMOS). A 95 cm vertical Berea composite core (five elements, ~100 and ~700 mD) was primary-drained by gravity using decane and NaI-doped brine, taking 2.5 months, then waterflooded from the bottom with X-ray in-situ saturation monitoring. ROS versus HMOS followed a Land-type relation, with C≈5 at low HMOS and ≈2 at high HMOS, so a single constant overpredicts ROS at low HMOS. Recovery was about 58% at HMOS 0.6 falling to 45% at 0.2 for the high-permeability Berea, and lower for the low-permeability Berea, so the Land constant depends on permeability (2 versus 3 in simulation). Simulations with Killough hysteresis in oil relative permeability and capillary pressure predicted the dynamic saturation profiles well, whereas bounding-curve or end-point-scaled models matched poorly.

## Shuaiba Transition Zone Fields: From Laboratory SCAL Experiments to Field Development Challenges

- ID: SCA2008-02, year: 2008
- Authors: I. Abu-Shiekah, S.K. Masalmeh, X.D. Jing
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: capillary transition zone, saturation height function, mercury injection capillary pressure, relative permeability scanning curves, Archie saturation exponent, Shuaiba carbonate
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-02)

SCAL data from Shuaiba transition zone fields feed upscaled saturation height functions and scanning relative permeability models that history-match well and field production.

Shuaiba fields in Oman hold much of their oil in thin, low-relief capillary transition zones, making initial saturation and oil mobility hard to predict. A ten-year SCAL programme used MICP, centrifuge and porous plate drainage Pc, formation factor and resistivity index, and relative permeability/imbibition Pc tests. MICP on trim ends of twin plugs varied widely, so Pc curves were volume-weighted upscaled and matched log saturations in horizontal and vertical wells. Simulations with Sor/Soi-dependent scanning relative permeability and imbibition Pc matched crestal and flank well and field production, whereas bounding curves alone underestimated oil and overestimated water production.

## An Improved Method for Estimating Oil Reserves in Oil-Water Transition Zones

- ID: SCA1999-36, year: 1999
- Authors: John R. Fanchi, Richard L. Christiansen, Michael J. Heymans
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: trapped oil relationship, transition zone, residual oil saturation, reserves estimation, saturation profile, black oil simulation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-36)

Shows experimentally that residual oil depends on initial oil saturation in transition zones, and that including this trapped oil relationship raises estimated reserves by roughly 11-38%.

Conventional reserve estimates assume constant residual oil saturation in oil-water transition zones, although residual oil depends on initial oil saturation (a trapped oil relationship). Video-based saturation profile experiments in unconsolidated glass bead and sand packs, using nonane and water with flow reversal, corroborated the trapped oil relationship for water-wet media. Saturation-profile material balances agreed within 5% for many experiments and within 20% for others. An analytical model gave reserve increases of 10.7% for a hyperbolic and 36.8% for a quadratic saturation profile. A layered extended black oil simulation of a quadratic profile produced 1.24 MMSTB with variable Sor versus 0.90 MMSTB with constant Sor, about 38% more, in agreement with the analytical result.

## The Accessibility of Waterflood Residual Oil by the Oil Bank When Slugs of Solvent Alternate with Slugs of Water (Saw) Under Oil-Wet and Water-Wet Conditions

- ID: SCA2003-06, year: 2003
- Authors: Ioannis Chatzis, Dianbin Liu, Porous Media

- Authors: Sushobhan Pradhan, Gbeu Kone, Ryan Antle, Clint Aichele, Haifeng Jiang, Prem Bikkina
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: microfluidics, aspect ratio, residual oil saturation, wettability, immiscible gas flooding, ImageJ saturation analysis
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2018/SCA2018-063.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2018-063)

Microfluidic chip floods with water then methane show that higher pore-throat aspect ratio raises residual oil, and wettability and channel depth also matter.

The study examines how pore-throat aspect ratio (1.97–60.16), wettability and channel depth affect residual oil after waterflooding and immiscible gas flooding. Hydrophilic and hydrophobic linear and uniform microfluidic chips were saturated with dyed water and n-decane, then flooded with water (0.1 µL/min) and methane (0.3 sccm) at 100 psig, with saturations measured by ImageJ. In 20 µm linear networks, Sorw rose semilogarithmically with AR in both wettabilities, attributed to lower displacing velocity in larger pores; 40 µm hydrophobic chips had much lower residual oil than 20 µm chips. In uniform networks, Sorw showed no clear AR trend (41–51 %PV hydrophilic, 57–72 %PV hydrophobic), but Sorg correlated semilogarithmically with AR. At high AR the hydrophilic and hydrophobic Sorg converge and the Sorw–Sorg gap narrows, implying less incremental gas EOR potential.

## An experimental and digital investigation into the impact of diagenesis above and below the oil-water contact

- ID: SCA2019-052, year: 2019
- Authors: Francis Mujica, Lesley A. James, Carl F. Berg, Derek H. C Wilton
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: oil-retarded diagenesis, quartz cementation, MICP, micro-CT digital rock, Ben Nevis Formation, oil-water contact
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2019/SCA2019-052.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2019-052)

Combining MICP, micro-CT and digital rock simulations on eleven Ben Nevis samples, the study finds more quartz cement below the OWC but similar pore structure and transport.

The study examines whether diagenetic differences above and below the oil-water contact in the Ben Nevis Formation (Hebron field, offshore Newfoundland) change pore structure and transport properties. SEM and cathodoluminescence imaging of thin sections near the OWC showed more quartz cementation in the water zone, consistent with the oil-retarded diagenesis theory. Eleven core samples from the oil and water zones were characterized by MICP and micro-CT. Digital rock simulations modeled mercury injection (quasi-static morphological method), electrical conductivity (Laplace equation) and single-phase flow (Stokes equation). Despite the differing quartz overgrowth, pore structure was surprisingly consistent, so flow properties were not substantially affected.

## A Pore-Level Analysis on Residual Oil Structure post Secondary and Tertiary Displacements

- ID: SCA2019-067, year: 2019
- Authors: Felix Servin
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: micromodel, residual oil structure, surfactant flooding, snap-off, coordination number, image analysis
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2019/SCA2019-067.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2019-067)

Micromodel waterflood and surfactant flood images show remaining oil shifts from large, irregular, multi-pore globules to small, round, single-pore snapped-off globules, with saturation falling from 35% to 15%.

The paper examines how the structure of remaining oil evolves during waterflooding and tertiary surfactant flooding. A 2D micromodel mimicking sandstone topology was saturated with connate brine and oil (initial oil saturation 87%), then waterflooded and surfactant flooded. 25 microscopic images were taken after each stage and analyzed with in-house Matlab code for size, roundness and coordination number. Remaining oil saturation fell from 35% after waterflooding to 15% after surfactant flooding. After waterflooding, globules were irregular, spanned multiple pore bodies and were mainly trapped by bypassing, whereas after surfactant flooding nearly all were circular, occupied part of a single pore body and were trapped by snap-off. IFT reduction allowed viscous forces to mobilize globules, which the authors relate to the narrowest connected pore throat.

## Geomechanical Deformation of Saturated Porous Media under Various Wettability Conditions: A Pore-scale Investigation

- ID: SCA2022-25, year: 2022
- Authors: Ahmed Zankoor, Rui Wang, Maziar Arshadi, Mohammad Piri
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: pore-scale imaging, geomechanical deformation, in-situ capillary pressure, interfacial curvature, wettability, sandpack micro-CT
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2022/SCA2022-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2022-25)

Micro-CT imaging of water-wet and oil-wet sandpacks under hydrostatic compression shows oil desaturation and capillary pressure drops, less uniform in oil-wet, with local pressure increases from pinned interfaces.

Little is known about how pore space deformation affects fluid occupancy and capillary pressure at the pore scale, especially under different wettabilities. Two 10 mm by 75 mm Northern White sandpacks (porosity about 0.30, permeability 6.6 and 7.6 D) were drained with Soltrol (water-wet, contact angle 41.7°) or aged with crude oil (oil-wet, 107°). They were then compressed hydrostatically and imaged by micro-CT, with in-situ capillary pressure derived from interfacial curvature. Oil volume fell by 18% in the water-wet sample versus 6% in the oil-wet one, which was also less uniform. Capillary pressure dropped uniformly in the water-wet sample as interfaces moved to wider pores. In the oil-wet sample, capillary pressure rose locally in the middle, which is attributed to interfaces hinging in place and becoming more curved.

## Integrated Field and Core Data Analysis of Residual Oil Saturation Measurements from ASP Flooding Candidate Sandstone Reservoir in North Kuwait

- ID: SCA2023-035, year: 2023
- Authors: Bodoor Baroon, Chen Chao, Issa Abu Shiekah
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, ASP flooding, restored wettability, steady-state relative permeability, centrifuge imbibition, bond number
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2023/SCA2023-035.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2023-035)

Restored-wettability steady-state and centrifuge floods on Zubair sandstone plugs, integrated with logs, Dean Stark and SWCTT data, show residual oil saturation below 0.20 for ASP baseline.

The paper determines the waterflood baseline residual oil saturation for a planned ASP pilot in the Upper Zubair sandstone, Raudhatain field, North Kuwait. Restored-wettability experiments were run on 6 plugs from the pilot well (1000-2700 mD) and 8 plugs from an analogue field (180-650 mD): steady-state floods, Amott tests and multi-speed centrifuge imbibition/drainage. Steady-state Sor was 0.10-0.14 and centrifuge end-face Sor 0.12-0.16; analogue plugs gave 0.13-0.19. Amott tests showed no spontaneous imbibition, indicating non-water-wet rock. Centrifuge desaturation showed no onset up to a bond number of 1.9E-3, implying IFT below about 2E-3 mN/m is needed. Combined with logs, Dean Stark and SWCTT (0.24±0.02), remaining oil is 0.20±0.05 while true residual oil is below 0.20.

## Evaluation of Residual Oil in the Wara, Mauddud, and Burgan Formations, Burgan Field, Kuwait

- ID: SCA2024_1021, year: 2024
- Authors: Antia Jonathan, Hayat Laila, Salter Greg, Chen Feiyan, Drylie Stephen, Ramoin Joe
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, capillary number, imbibition capillary pressure, waterflood, Land trapping model, bypassed pay
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1021.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1021)

Legacy and new capillary pressure and waterflood data from Burgan Field formations are used to build a porosity-based Sor model, revealing low core Sor from filtrate invasion and bypassed pay.

Residual oil saturation (Sor) is critical for reserves, bypassed pay and EOR design but correlates poorly with rock quality. The study integrates legacy porous plate and centrifuge capillary pressure data and waterflood tests (extracted and wettability-restored samples) from the Wara, Mauddud and Burgan formations in the Burgan Field, Kuwait. Sor averages about 25% and ranges up to about 55-60%, consistent with published sandstone trends for the Wara. Mauddud and Burgan show many points below the published porosity trend, tied to argillaceous, mud-dominated and dolostone rock types with microporosity, and likely to filtrate invasion exceeding the capillary number during coring. Measured Sor approaches maximum trapped values from Land and Jerauld models (median Land C of 3.01). Petrophysical models show bypassed pay of 10-25% in much of the Middle Burgan, and residual oil needing EOR of about 25-30%.

## Investigation of residual oil saturation at different capillary number regions through Surfactant-Polymer flood experiments

- ID: SCA2024_1043, year: 2024
- Authors: Shouq Alabdumuhsen, Meshal Algharaib, Osamah Al-Omair, Mahmoud Ali
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary desaturation curve, surfactant-polymer flooding, capillary number, residual oil saturation, ultra-low interfacial tension, coreflooding
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1043.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1043)

Eight secondary-mode surfactant-polymer coreflood experiments on Berea show recovery rises with capillary number, but ultra-low IFT conditions give unstable displacement and inconsistent residual oil saturation.

The study examines how capillary number affects residual oil saturation when surfactant-polymer (SP) flooding is applied in secondary mode, before waterflood. Eight corefloods were run on synthetic Berea cores (3.78 cm by 30.2 cm, 21-43 md) in horizontal and vertical orientation at capillary numbers from about 1E-6 to 6E-4, varying polymer (2,000-3,000 ppm), surfactant (Triton X-100 or ENORDET J071), and salinity (5,000 or 200,000 ppm). Waterflood baselines at Nc ~1E-6 gave about 54% recovery and Sor of 0.33-0.35 with no orientation effect. At Nc ~7E-5, ultimate recovery rose to about 72% with Sor of 0.21-0.24. At Nc ~5-6E-4 with ultra-low IFT (0.08 mN/m), emulsions and unstable displacement appeared, with recoveries of 61-83%, and the vertical case sometimes broke through earlier. The results indicate that ultra-low IFT does not necessarily improve recovery and that Sor does not fall consistently at high capillary number.
