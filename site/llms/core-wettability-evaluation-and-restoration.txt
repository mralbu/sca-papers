## Wettability Evaluation During Restored State Core Analysis

- ID: SCA1990-16, year: 1990
- Authors: G. J. Hirasaki, J. A. Rohan, S. T. Dubey
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: restored state core analysis, USBM and Amott indices, core cleaning solvents, mixed wettability, Brownell-Katz number, capillary end effect
- Cited by 13 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-16)

Sequential solvent cleaning, unlike Dean-Stark, makes North Sea sandstone cores water-wet; aging restores mixed wettability, and much remaining oil in mixed-wet cores is a capillary end effect.

The study evaluates wettability changes through restored state core analysis of North Sea sandstone cores (200–500 md, porosity about 0.25) to estimate remaining oil saturation after waterflooding. An automated centrifuge gave USBM and Amott indices; as-received cores were oil-wet (USBM about -1.12), and Dean-Stark toluene plus chloroform-methanol cleaning left them intermediate-wet. A multi-solvent sequence (alphamethylnaphthalene, toluene, chloroform, chloroform-methanol, methanol) raised USBM to about 1.0 and remaining oil saturation from 9% (crude, as received) to 28% (refined oil), still below the 37.5% water-wet value. Aging 28 days in crude made cores mixed-wet and lowered residual oil by about 0.13; Dean-Stark cores stayed more oil-wet, differing by about 0.04 in residual oil. Brownell-Katz analysis and CT scans showed that remaining oil in mixed-wet cores is mainly held by capillary end effects with film-drainage production, so reservoir remaining oil must be computed from relative permeability and buoyancy.

## Wettability of Asab Reservoir Rock : Comparison of Various Evaluation Methods Role of Lithology

- ID: SCA1991-09, year: 1991
- Authors: Louis Cuiec, Fathi A. Yahya, Abu Dhabi, United Arab
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Amott/IFP wettability index, n-heptanol adsorption, contact angle, mixed wettability, wettability restoration, carbonate facies
- Cited by 8 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-09)

Wettability of Asab Thamama Zone B carbonate was assessed by contact angle, n-heptanol and Amott/IFP tests; methods agree, rock is mixed, mostly neutral to oil-wet, and restoration works.

The study evaluates wettability of the Asab Thamama Zone B carbonate reservoir (Abu Dhabi) to compare evaluation methods and test the role of facies. On one M2 facies core, contact angle, n-heptanol adsorption and Amott/IFP tests (25, 85 and 121°C, up to 204 bar, different oils) all indicated neutral to slightly oil-preferential wettability. Amott/IFP spontaneous recoveries were 1.3-4.2% PV in both directions, with WI of -0.07, -0.05 and -0.01, indicating mixed wettability independent of oil type or temperature. n-heptanol gave 78% oil-wet surface for preserved and 62% for restored rock. Ten samples from five facies (M1, M2, R2, M3A, LM) were tested preserved, cleaned and restored; M1, M2 and M3A were slightly to strongly oil-preferential, R2 neutral to slightly oil-wet, and LM neutral to slightly water-wet. Even after extensive solvent cleaning, upper facies M1 and M2 could not be made strongly water-wet, suggesting non-extractable hydrophobic surface material, and restored wettability was similar to preserved.

## Alteration of Wettability by Drilling Mud Filtrates

- ID: SCA1994-08, year: 1994
- Authors: J.S. Buckley, N.R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wettability alteration, drilling mud filtrate, Amott-Harvey index, Berea sandstone, water-based mud additives, fresh core
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-08)

Amott indexes on Berea cores show water-based mud filtrates alter wettability far less than individual additives, with a KCl/FCLS/Drispac mud causing minimal change across initial wettabilities.

Drilling fluids can alter core wettability, compromising measurements on fresh cores. Seven common water-based mud additives were flushed individually through strongly water-wet Berea sandstone (about 600 md), and filtrates of seven practical water-based muds were flushed through cores prepared with initial wettability from strongly water-wet to strongly oil-wet. Wettability was assessed by Amott and Amott-Harvey indexes before and after exposure. All additives reduced water-wetness and increased oil-wetness, with polymers showing strong concentration dependence, and Drispac and XC caused plugging. Filtrates caused much less alteration than individual additives, attributed to adsorption of surface-active components on clay removed in the mud cake. The KCl/FCLS/CMC/Drispac filtrate (DF-4) changed the index by less than 0.20 overall and less than 0.1 for moderately oil-wet to moderately water-wet cores, while the XC-polymer muds (DF-3, DF-7) caused the largest changes.

## Imaging of Pore Scale Distribution of Fluids and Wettability

- ID: SCA2008-16, year: 2008
- Authors: Munish Kumar, Tim Senden, Mark A. Knackstedt, Shane J. Latham, Val Pinczewski, Robert M. Sok, Adrian P. Sheppard, Michael L. Turner
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: wettability alteration, plasma cleaning, micro-CT image registration, pore-scale fluid distribution, percolation network model, residual gas saturation
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-16)

Water-plasma cleaning sets defined wettability states in small cores; registered micro-CT images of air-water drainage and imbibition are compared pore-by-pore with percolation models.

The paper addresses how wettability controls pore-scale fluid distribution and presents a cold water-plasma method to clean cores to a strongly water-wet state and a freezing/hydrophobing route to mixed-wet states (modified Carter WI of 0.08 and 0.16). Seven small cores (5-6 mm; Fontainebleau sandstone, sucrosic dolomite, oomoldic grainstone) were imaged by micro-CT at ~3 micron voxels, with voxel-registered dry and partially saturated images using CsI brine. Centrifuge drainage and spontaneous imbibition showed gas residing in the larger pores, and imaged saturations agreed with measured values. Drainage matched invasion percolation, including in the oomoldic sample where poorly connected large molds stayed water-filled. In imbibition the ordinary percolation threshold gave trapped gas of 48-58%, well above measured values of 21-40%, but cluster distributions matched once the covering radius was increased to match saturation. Short contact time gave Sgr of 37% versus 21-24% after 24 h in water-wet Fontainebleau sandstone.

## Core Wettability : Should Iah Equal Iusbm ?

- ID: SCA1998-09, year: 1998
- Authors: A.B. Dixit, J.S. Buckley, S.R. McDougall, K.S. Sorbie
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: USBM wettability index, Amott-Harvey index, pore network modelling, mixed-wet, snap-off, fractional wettability
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-09)

Analytical and pore-network models show USBM and Amott-Harvey indices agree only for fractionally-wet, snap-off-free systems; mixed-wet large pores and snap-off raise IUSBM above IAH.

USBM and Amott-Harvey wettability indices are used interchangeably, yet literature data show considerable scatter between them. The authors derived analytical IUSBM–IAH relations for fractionally-wet (FW), mixed-wet with large oil-wet pores (MWL) and mixed-wet with small oil-wet pores (MWS) media with uniform pore size distribution. They then ran 20³ and 25³ pore network simulations varying PSD, pore size range, coordination number (4, 6), volume exponent (1–3), displacement mechanism and oil-wet fraction (0.187–0.567). IAH equals IUSBM only for FW systems with piston-like displacement; MWL and snap-off in water-wet pores push IUSBM above IAH, while IUSBM < IAH arises only when small pores are oil-wet and snap-off is suppressed. Simulated trends reproduce the main features of literature data, including most points above the 1:1 line and an almost empty quadrant of IUSBM<0, IAH>0.

## Impacts of Wettability on Capillary Pressure and Relative Permeability

- ID: SCA1999-07, year: 1999
- Authors: A. Graue, T. Bognø, R.W. Moe, B.A. Baldwin, E.A. Spinler, D. Maloney, D.P. Tobola
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wettability alteration, Amott water index, centrifuge capillary pressure, steady-state relative permeability, chalk, forced imbibition
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-07)

Capillary pressure and relative permeability were measured on Rørdal chalk aged to three wettability states, showing reproducible, stable wettability alteration and consistent trends toward less water-wet conditions.

Capillary pressure and relative permeability in fractured-chalk reservoir studies depend on wettability, so outcrop Rørdal chalk plugs (1-5 mD, 43-48% porosity) were aged in crude oil at 90°C at Swi=25% to give strongly water-wet, moderately water-wet (Iw≈0.5-0.6) and near-neutral-wet (Iw≈0.3-0.4) states. Wettability was characterized by Amott indices and imbibition rate and endpoint, and duplicate plugs reproduced the imbibition behavior. Repeat Amott tests after 50 PV water and 70 PV oil flushing and shipping showed the alteration was stable. Centrifuge secondary drainage curves at less water-wet conditions had lower threshold pressure, more curvature and higher immobile water. Forced imbibition curves became flatter and residual oil decreased, but higher pressure drops were needed. Steady-state water relative permeability increased at a given saturation toward less water-wet states, although the Iw=0.5 curve was somewhat offset. History matching of waterfloods in chalk blocks indicated the capillary pressure data were more reliable than the steady-state relative permeability data.

## Application of Differential Scanning Calorimetry to Core Analysis

- ID: SCA2013-055, year: 2013
- Authors: Evgeny Dyshlyuk
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: differential scanning calorimetry, specific heat capacity, thermoporometry, pore size distribution, immersion calorimetry, controlled pore glass
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-055.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-055)

Outlines how heat-flow Calvet DSC can measure core specific heat capacity, pore size distribution by thermoporometry, wettability and surface area via immersion calorimetry.

The paper presents differential scanning calorimetry, using a Setaram BT2.15 Calvet-type heat-flow instrument, as a versatile tool for core analysis. Specific heat capacity is determined from blank, reference (Al2O3) and sample runs, reaching about 1% precision in step mode and 1.7% in continuous mode over -196 to 200 °C. Pore size distribution is obtained by thermoporometry from the freezing-point depression of confined water, demonstrated on a mixture of controlled pore glasses (50, 100, 300 nm) that gave distinct endothermic peaks plus a bulk water peak. Wettability and surface area are addressed through immersion calorimetry, relating immersion enthalpy to contact angle and using a modified Harkins-Jura procedure with pre-coated samples. The thermoporometry range is limited to pores up to about 1 μm, narrower than mercury injection.

## Wettability Laboratory Evaluation Under Reservoir Conditions: A New Apparatus

- ID: SCA1995-29, year: 1995
- Authors: L. Cuiec
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: Amott-Harvey wettability index, reservoir conditions apparatus, temperature effect on wettability, recombined oil, mixed wettability, sandstone and carbonate
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-29)

A new apparatus performs the Amott-Harvey wettability test at reservoir conditions; tests on three sandstones and a carbonate show condition effects are reservoir-specific and unpredictable.

Wettability measured at room conditions may not represent reservoirs, and no apparatus could run all four Amott-Harvey stages without releasing pressure and temperature. The author built an apparatus operating up to 450 bar and 170°C, plus an intermediate cell (25 bar, 80°C). Amott-Harvey tests were run at room, intermediate and reservoir conditions on cleaned samples from three clayey sandstones and one Middle East carbonate, using synthetic brine and stock-tank, topped or recombined oil. Sandstone No. 2 shifted from I_AH of about -0.75 at room conditions to +0.25 intermediate and +0.42 reservoir. Sandstone No. 1 went from -0.11 to +0.10 to -0.28, and the carbonate stayed near zero (-0.05 to -0.01) under all conditions. Temperature increases often raised water affinity in sandstones, but the response could not be predicted, so testing at reservoir conditions is recommended.

## Determination of Wettability of Iranian Carbonate Reservoir Rocks in Restored-State

- ID: SCA2003-43, year: 2003
- Authors: Mohammad-Reza Esfahani
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Amott-USBM combined index, restored-state wettability, carbonate reservoir, centrifuge capillary pressure, relative permeability crossover saturation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-43.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-43)

Wettability of eleven restored-state Iranian carbonate plugs at 90°C was measured; Amott tests gave intermediate wettability, while combined Amott/USBM indicated mostly oil-wet.

Wettability of Iranian dolomite and limestone reservoir rocks was characterized on eleven plugs from Fields M and R, cleaned, saturated with formation water, flooded with crude oil and aged 40 days at 90°C. Qualitative relative permeability results were contradictory: crossover saturations (33-46%) indicated oil-wet, while Krw at Sor was below Kro at Swi. ## Fractional Wettability and Petrophysical Parameters of Porous Media

- ID: SCA1992-20EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: J. M. Lombard, R. Lenormand
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: fractional wettability, silanated sand, Amott and USBM indices, percolation threshold, relative permeability hysteresis, saturation exponent
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-20EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-20EURO)

Sand packs mixing water-wet and silanated oil-wet sand show how fractional wettability controls capillary pressure, wettability indices, relative permeability hysteresis and saturation exponent, with percolation thresholds at f=0.25 and 0.75.

The study addresses how fractional wettability affects flow and electrical properties. Synthetic unconsolidated packs (70-100 µm sand, porosity 40%, K≈2.3 D) were made by mixing water-wet sand with silanated oil-wet sand at fractions f from 0 to 1; the silane treatment was stable over three months. Wettability indices fall monotonically with f (e.g. Amott-IFP from 1 to -1), with plateaus and spontaneous displacements linked to the 3D percolation threshold of 0.25 (and 0.75). Primary drainage Pc decreases with increasing f, relative permeability hysteresis depends on f, and the saturation exponent n increases with f while the cementation factor stays at 1.5.

## Experimental Evidence of Different Intermediate Wetting States

- ID: SCA2004-04, year: 2004
- Authors: A. Skauge, K. Spildo, L. Høiland, B. Vik, B. Ottesen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: mixed-wet large, mixed-wet small, fractional wettability, ESEM, USBM Amott-Harvey indices, disjoining pressure
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-04.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-04)

ESEM and thin-section imaging of MWL and MWS reservoir cores show local mineral wetting consistent with the wetting classes inferred from USBM and Amott-Harvey indices.

Intermediate-wet reservoirs have been classified as fractional-wet, mixed-wet large or mixed-wet small by comparing USBM and Amott-Harvey indices, and this study seeks direct pore-scale evidence. A disjoining-pressure argument based on pore shape (circular, star-shaped, triangular) explains why oil-wetting may occur in large pores, small pores, or independent of size. Cores from an MWL and an MWS reservoir were examined by thin section, SEM and ESEM water condensation. In the MWL core, quartz and K-feldspar in large pores showed water droplets (less water-wet) while kaolinite and mica in small pores were water-wet. In the MWS core, quartz was more water-wet and only high-surface-area minerals such as mica deviated from water-wet behaviour. Literature cases (Prudhoe Bay, Rueslåtten et al.) support the classification, though wettability indices are still needed to separate the classes.

## Petrophysics of Shu'Aiba Reservoir, Shaybah Field

- ID: SCA2000-35, year: 2000
- Authors: Taha M. Okasha, James J. Funk, Yaslam S. Balobaid
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: reservoir quality index, flow zone indicator, Krw/Kro correlation, USBM and Amott wettability, mercury injection pore size distribution, Shu'aiba carbonate
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-35.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-35)

Conventional and special core analysis of Shaybah's Shu'aiba carbonate shows strong heterogeneity, intermediate to water-wet behavior, bimodal pore systems, and yields a Krw/Kro correlation and RQI-based zonation.

The study characterizes the heterogeneous Shu'aiba limestone reservoir of Shaybah field using routine and special core analysis on preserved plugs and composite cores. ## Effect of Pressure on Wettability Restoration and Crude Characterization Using Infra-Red Spectrometry

- ID: SCA1991-10, year: 1991
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wettability restoration, aging pressure, Amott index, core cleaning, FTIR crude oxidation, core preservation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-10)

Aging North Sea sandstone cores at low pressure gave more oil-wet Amott results than high-pressure aging; core drying and crude oxidation, tracked by infrared, also alter wettability.

The paper examines how aging pressure affects wettability restoration of cores from two North Sea sandstone reservoirs (H and V), with wettability measured by the Amott index. ## The Measurement of Core Sample Surface Wettability Heterogeneity by Adsorption Calorimetry

- ID: SCA2014-088, year: 2014
- Authors: Evgeny Dyshlyuk, Natalya Bogdanovich, Dmitry Korobkov, Vera Pletneva
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: adsorption calorimetry, wettability heterogeneity, water vapor adsorption isotherm, differential scanning calorimetry, dolomite, enthalpy of adsorption
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-088.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-088)

Water vapor adsorption isotherms and DSC calorimetry on dolomite miniplugs before and after extraction show native cores are homogeneously oil-wet, while extracted cores are heterogeneous and more water-affine.

Standard wettability tests such as Amott and USBM yield a single number and say nothing about spatial heterogeneity of wettability. The authors combined water vapor adsorption isotherms (desiccator with salt solutions, microbalance) with isothermal Calvet-type DSC heat measurements at 25 °C. They tested five pairs of low-permeability (~3 mD) dolomite miniplugs (~85% dolomite) before and after Soxhlet extraction with benzol. Adsorption isotherms were lower for extracted cores, although BET surface area grew about 1.7 times after extraction, so mass-based hydrophobicity coefficients would give a wrong wettability interpretation here. Integral specific heat of non-extracted cores lay below the water liquefaction enthalpy (2444 kJ/kg), whereas extracted cores lay above it. Differential heat curves showed extracted cores to be heterogeneous and non-extracted cores homogeneous, similar to carbon surfaces, indicating oil-wet surfaces without water-wet small pores.

## Wetting and Waterflood Oil Recovery of a Moderately Viscous Crude Oil

- ID: SCA1993-23, year: 1993
- Authors: Jean-Michel Villard, Jill S. Buckley, Norman R. Morrow, Renaud Gauchet
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wettability alteration, Amott index, aging temperature, viscous crude oil waterflood, spontaneous imbibition, micromodel visualization
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-23)

Aging Berea cores in 46 cp Schuricht crude at higher temperature lowers water-wetness (Amott index) and improves waterflood recovery; micromodels and history matching support this.

The study tests whether wettability alteration trends seen for lighter crudes also hold for a 46 cp Schuricht crude oil. Berea sandstone cores (about 550-735 md, 21% porosity) were aged at initial water saturation for 3-30 days at 26-80°C. Amott indices fell to about 0.4 after 12 days of aging at 53°C. The Amott index to water was 0.61 at 26°C aging and about 0.5 at 53°C, while imbibition rate and recovery decreased with aging temperature (32% to 27% from 26°C to 80°C). Waterflood recovery improved as cores became less water-wet, but it decreased slightly with injection rate (7.5-30 ml/hr). Micromodel floods showed that short aging gave water-wet displacement with ganglia trapping, whereas 80°C aging gave intermediate wetting and more efficient displacement. History matching with a 1D simulator gave Pc and kr curves, and Amott indices from the simulated Pc were reasonably consistent with measured values. The expected leftward shift of the kr crossover with decreasing water-wetness was not observed.

## Wettability Analysis Using Micro-CT, Fesem and Qemscan, and Its Applications to Digital Rock Physics

- ID: SCA2015-010, year: 2015
- Authors: Nasiru Idowu, Haili Long, Pål-Eric Øren, Anna M. Carnerup, Andrew Fogden, Igor Bondino, Lars Sundal
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: wettability, micro-CT registration, FESEM asphaltene, QEMSCAN, pore network modeling, Amott index
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-010.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-010)

Registered micro-CT, FESEM and QEMSCAN imaging of two sandstone miniplugs reveals water-wet versus oil-wet behavior, and the wettability informs pore network simulations that match SCAL data.

Conventional wettability tests give only averaged values, so the authors combine micro-CT, FESEM and QEMSCAN on 6 mm miniplugs of an outcrop and a reservoir sandstone with similar porosity (~22.5%) and permeability (524 and 853 mD). Tomograms were taken after drainage and ageing, spontaneous imbibition and forced imbibition. The outcrop sample imbibed spontaneously (Sw from 3.0 to 59.3%, Amott Iw 0.60-0.94), while the reservoir sample did not (Sw 3.2 to 4.5%, Iw 0.01-0.04) but responded strongly to forced imbibition. FESEM showed little asphaltene on the outcrop and near-complete asphaltene coverage, including on clay linings, in the reservoir sample, which the authors attribute mainly to the different crude oils. Pore network models with oil-wet parameters (contact angles 100-150°) for the reservoir sample reproduced MICP, spontaneous imbibition volume and Iw (0.018 vs 0.014 measured), and gave relative permeability qualitatively consistent with core data.

Radial waterfloods on 22 Berea cores, with Amott wettability and mercury porosimetry on plugs, link surface area and pore-throat size to wettability, oil recovery and residual oil.
