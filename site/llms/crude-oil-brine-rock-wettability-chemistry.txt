## New Method to Prepare Outcrop Chalk Cores for Wettability and Oil Recovery Studies at Low Initial Water Saturation

- ID: SCA2007_37, year: 2007
- Authors: Tina Puntervold, Skule Strand, Tor Austad
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: chalk wettability, sulfate adsorption, spontaneous imbibition, chromatographic wettability test, porous plate drainage, Stevns Klint chalk
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_37)

Trace sulfate in outcrop Stevns Klint chalk concentrates during porous-plate drainage and makes cores more water-wet; pre-flushing with 4 PV distilled water gives representative wettability.

Outcrop chalk used for wettability and oil recovery studies at low Swi (~10%) can contain trace sulfate, which is up-concentrated in the residual water during porous-plate drainage. Stevns Klint chalk cores were flooded with 4 PV distilled water; about 5.3 mg SO4 per core (PV ~34 ml) was eluted, and drainage alone removed only 2.2 mg, leaving an apparent 0.74 g/l in the residual water, about one-third of seawater. Cleaned cores were much less water-wet: spontaneous imbibition recovered about 70% of oil in 4 days for non-cleaned cores versus 30% in 13 days for cleaned cores. Chromatographic wettability indices were 0.84 (non-cleaned) and 0.65 (cleaned). Cleaned cores responded strongly to seawater at higher temperature (up to ~40-50% extra recovery at 110-130 °C), while Swi of 10-22% had little effect. The authors recommend flushing chalk with at least 4 PV of sulfate-free brine or distilled water before saturation.

## The Effect of Different Crude Oil/Brine/Rock Combinations on Wettability Through Spontaneous Imbibition

- ID: SCA2003-02, year: 2003
- Authors: Hongguang Tie, Zhengxin Tong, Norman R. Morrow Chemical
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: mixed wettability, spontaneous imbibition, asphaltene precipitation, Amott index, crude oil solvency, limestone
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-02)

Spontaneous imbibition and Amott indices compare MXW and MXW-F wetting in Berea sandstone and Mt. Gambier limestone with two crude oils. Limestone is more sensitive to crude solvency; MXW-F is less water-wet.

The study compares mixed-wet (MXW) and MXW-F (crude removed, organic film left) wetting states in Berea sandstone and Mt. Gambier limestone using Minnelusa (9.0% asphaltene) and Cottonwood (2.3% asphaltene, higher acid/base number ratio) crude oils. Crude solvency was reduced by adding 14-30 vol% n-decane. Imbibition rates were scaled with a dimensionless time group, and Amott indices were measured on selected cores. Cottonwood oil reduced imbibition rate more than Minnelusa oil in both rocks, and limestone showed a systematic rate decrease with added alkane while sandstone changed little except for Minnelusa at 22% decane. MXW-F cores imbibed more slowly than MXW cores (Cottonwood recovery about 10% limestone and 11% sandstone vs 28% and 33%), and direct displacement of crude by mineral oil almost suppressed imbibition, attributed to asphaltene surface precipitation. Amott Iw-o was water-wet for MXW cores and intermediate for MXW-F cores, with the lowest value -0.88 for a limestone core flooded directly with mineral oil.

## Oil Recovery by Spontaneous Imbibition Before and After Wettability Alteration of Three Carbonate Rocks by a Moderately Asphaltic Crude Oil

- ID: SCA2005-11, year: 2005
- Authors: Hongguang Tie, Norman R. Morrow Chemical
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, limestone wettability, Amott indices, mixed wettability, asphaltene precipitation, scaled imbibition
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-11)

Spontaneous imbibition and Amott indices on three outcrop limestones show wettability shifts from very strongly water-wet to MXW, MXW-F and MXW-DF states after Cottonwood crude exposure, with the largest change for MXW-DF.

Carbonate wettability alteration by crude oil was studied using three outcrop limestones: Whitestone UZ (7 md grainstone), Edwards (GC) (about 11-13 md grainstone) and Gambier (about 4000 md reef boundstone). Very strongly water-wet imbibition scaled well with correlations for other rock types, though final oil recoveries differed (62.8%, 53.8% and 41.5% for Gambier, Whitestone UZ and Edwards). Cores were aged in Cottonwood Creek crude at initial water saturation (16-29%) for 10 days at 75°C to give MXW, MXW-F (solvent then mineral oil) and MXW-DF (direct displacement) states. For all rocks, imbibition rate and extent decreased in the order MXW > MXW-F > MXW-DF. MXW Amott-Willhite indices ranged from 0.44 to 0.63, while MXW-DF cores were near neutral (-0.05 to -0.1). Total recovery from the grainstones peaked at weakly water-wet MXW-F conditions, and Edwards (GC) is proposed as a model limestone.

## Experimental Investigation of Oil Compositional and Surfactant Effects on Wettability at Reservoir Conditions

- ID: SCA2005-53, year: 2005
- Authors: Wei Xu, Subhash C. Ayirala, Dandina N. Rao The Craft
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: dual-drop dual-crystal contact angle, live crude oil, anionic surfactant, wettability alteration, dolomite, four-region adsorption model
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-53.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-53)

DDDC contact angle measurements at Yates reservoir conditions show live oil is weakly water-wet while stocktank oil is oil-wet, and an anionic surfactant shifts both toward oil-wet states.

Wettability data at reservoir conditions with live oil are scarce, so the authors measured dual-drop dual-crystal contact angles on Yates and outcrop dolomite at 82°F and 700 psi using synthetic brine, stocktank oil, and live synthetic oil. Stocktank oil gave a strongly oil-wet advancing angle of 152°, whereas live oil gave about 55°, weakly water-wet. An anionic ethoxy sulphate surfactant (500–3500 ppm) reduced the stocktank angle to 135–141°, but raised the live-oil angle to a maximum of 165° at 1000 ppm and 120–135° at 1500–3500 ppm. At higher concentrations the stocktank oil drops would not mingle, likely due to charge repulsion between adsorbed surfactant heads. The concentration dependence was explained with a four-region surfactant adsorption model, and the authors suggest possible mixed-wettability development for oil recovery.

## Sulfate in Rock Samples from Carbonate Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2008-19, year: 2008
- Authors: Ingebret Fjelde
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: sulfate concentration, chalk wettability, reservoir core plug preparation, spontaneous imbibition, easily accessible sulfate, Stevns Klint chalk
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-19)

Trim-offs and core plugs from two North Sea chalk fields contain sulfate above levels known to alter chalk wettability, so core preparation procedures should be modified.

Sulfate is known to shift outcrop chalk toward water-wet conditions, so its presence in reservoir chalk matters for core preparation. Trim-off samples and core plugs from two chalk fields were crushed and extracted with distilled water, 3 M HCl or formation water, and sulfate was measured by BaCl2 turbidity and ICP. Easily accessible sulfate was determined by injecting formation water through toluene/methanol-cleaned plugs and analysing the effluent. Outcrop Liege and Stevns Klint chalks contained about 0.03 mg/g, while reservoir trim-offs reached 0.67 mg/g (field A) and 0.58 mg/g (field B). Easily accessible sulfate was 0.38-0.78 mg/g in field A plugs and 0.10-0.20 mg/g in field B plugs, all above the roughly 0.05 mg/g reported to affect wettability. Low Al/sulfate ratios argued against mud contamination, suggesting the sulfate is native, possibly carbonate-associated, and core preparation should depend on its origin.

## Fluid distribution in kaolinite- or illite-bearing cores. Cryo-SEM observations versus bulk measurements

- ID: SCA1996-27, year: 1996
- Authors: Claudine Durand, Elisabeth Rosenberg
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: cryo-SEM, wettability, kaolinite, illite, Amott-IFP wettability index, aging
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-27)

Cryo-SEM of four Brent sandstones shows kaolinite and platy illite retain oil after aging while fibrous illite stays water-wet, qualitatively consistent with Amott-IFP bulk wettability.

Cryo-SEM was used to image oil and brine distribution in four Brent sandstone samples (CAR1-CAR4) rich in kaolinite, dickite, platy illite or fibrous illite. Companion samples were centrifuged to immediate Swi, immediate Sor, aged Swi and Sor after one month of aging in crude oil, and imaged by backscattered electrons with EDS. Kaolinite/dickite clusters retained oil, and aging increased the association of oil with clay; fibrous illite remained hydrophilic. Platy illite (CAR1, 13.8 wt% illite, no kaolinite) behaved as the most oil-wet sample (Amott-IFP WI -1 after aging), suggesting kaolinite-like Al-OH surfaces. Bulk wettability depends on clay type, morphology, amount and distribution, with a minimal amount of oil-prone clay needed; agreement with Swi, Sor and WI is only qualitative because of 2D versus 3D scale differences.

## A Chemical Model for the Seawater-CO2- Carbonate System – Aqueous and Surface Chemistry

- ID: SCA2008-18, year: 2008
- Authors: A. Hiorth, L. M. Cathles, J. Kolnes, O. Vikane, A. Lohne, R. I. Korsnes, M. V. Madland
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: surface complexation model, zeta potential, chalk water weakening, calcite dissolution, seawater injection, anhydrite precipitation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-18)

A surface complexation and aqueous chemistry model for calcite-brine-CO2 matches zeta potentials and suggests calcite dissolution, not surface potential change, explains chalk wettability change and water weakening in seawater at high temperature.

Seawater injection into chalk changes oil recovery and mechanical strength at elevated temperature, and the mechanism is debated. The authors built a chemical model combining aqueous speciation (HKF equation of state, SUPCRT data) with a surface complexation model for calcite using the Grahame equation, with temperature dependence of surface constants assumed equal to aqueous ones. The model reproduces room-temperature zeta potential data from Thompson and Pownall (0.005 M NaCl) and UiS measurements (0.573 M NaCl with added Mg or sulphate). Predicted surface potential for seawater and Ekofisk formation water varies only weakly and almost linearly with temperature (25-130°C), so it cannot explain the effects seen only above about 70°C. Anhydrite precipitation at high temperature forces calcite dissolution, and the calculated dissolved calcium correlates with oil recovery in six imbibition experiments, though this is not proof.

## Kaolinite Wettability – the Effect of Salinity, pH and Calcium

- ID: SCA2010-11, year: 2010
- Authors: Evgenia V. Lebedeva, Andrew Fogden, Tim J. Senden, Mark A. Knackstedt
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: kaolinite, contact angle hysteresis, asphaltene deposition, low salinity, calcium chloride, micro-CT
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-11)

Thin immobilized kaolinite coats on glass are used to measure crude oil adhesion and asphaltene deposition versus NaCl, CaCl2 and pH; adhesion rises at low salinity and low pH.

Kaolinite strongly influences reservoir wettability, but local wettability in rock is hard to measure. The authors developed ~1 µm thick, smooth kaolinite (KGa-1b) coats on glass and measured contact angle hysteresis of Minnelusa crude drops in 24 brines varying NaCl, CaCl2 and pH (4, natural, 9). Hysteresis, a proxy for adhesion, rose with decreasing NaCl concentration and pH; at natural pH, brines above 0.1 M ionic strength gave hysteresis below 17°, with adhesion only for 0.001–0.01 M CaCl2 alone. After 8 days of aging at 60 °C, asphaltene deposition (scanner grayscale) generally decreased with salinity and correlated with hysteresis. SEM/AFM showed high NaCl limits deposit to kaolinite edges, while high CaCl2 restricts oil penetration into aggregates, and micro-CT of a kaolinite-rich sandstone showed no movement of large particles during low-salinity flooding.

## Changes in Wettability State Due to Waterflooding

- ID: SCA2011-15, year: 2011
- Authors: Andrew Fogden, Evgenia V. Lebedeva
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: waterflooding wettability alteration, FESEM oil deposits, kaolinite-coated glass, low salinity flooding, asphaltene adsorption, contact angle
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-15.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-15)

FESEM, contact angle and fluorescence show waterflooding leaves oil nano-droplet residues on aged glass and kaolinite-coated glass, with residue amount depending on flood salinity and pH.

The study probes how waterflooding changes the wettability state set by crude oil drainage and 30-day aging at 60°C on bare glass and kaolinite-coated glass. Substrates were aged in Na-Ca brines (50 or 1 g/l, pH 4, 6, 9) and flooded with a matrix of brines, then analyzed by FESEM image analysis, pendant-drop contact angles (glass) and fluorescence spectroscopy of extracted asphaltenics (kaolinite). On glass, the oil film retracts and detaches during flooding, leaving nano-droplets in foam-like trails. Residues were least at flood pH 6 for 50 g/l floods, but increased with flood pH for 1 g/l floods, attributed to precursor brine film thickening that disconnects surface oil. Contact angles varied up to 17° (receding) and 27° (advancing) and correlated best with deposit acircularity. On kaolinite-coated glass, residual asphaltenics were greatest at flood pH 6 and systematically higher for 1 g/l floods, with rough porous coats stranding more oil.

## Mixing Small Amounts of Crude Oil with Large Amounts of Asphaltene Precipitant

- ID: SCA2006-02, year: 2006
- Authors: Jill S.Buckley
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: asphaltene precipitation, wettability alteration, MXW-DF, crude oil compatibility, spontaneous imbibition, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-02)

Asphaltene skins forming when small crude volumes meet large volumes of precipitant block mixing, trapping crude oil in Berea cores and making them less water-wet after S220 displacement.

When an asphaltic crude is displaced by an asphaltene precipitant such as Soltrol 220, wettability becomes much less water-wet than with a solvent displacement. This paper tests whether small pockets of crude are retained because asphaltenes precipitate at the crude/precipitant boundary and retard mixing. Compatibility tests (test tubes, pendant drop) mixed small crude volumes into n-C10, decalin, toluene and S220. Minnelusa-02 (8.6% n-C7 asphaltenes) formed rigid, non-mixing interfaces in n-C10, and about 0.02 mL of crude in 3 mL S220 gave roughly 10% denser phase until ultrasonic agitation. In Berea cores (74–78 md) flooded with S220 after Minnelusa crude, 7.9–8.6% of the original oil in place was retained, versus 0.1% for the low-asphaltene Gullfaks crude. The compatibility test correlates mainly with asphaltene content and would miss damage from small amounts of unstable asphaltenes.

## Wettability Control by Adsorption from Crude Oil – Aspects of Temperature and Increased Water Saturation

- ID: SCA2005-07, year: 2005
- Authors: Norman R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: spontaneous imbibition, mixed wettability, wettability alteration, temperature effect, residual oil saturation aging, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-07)

Spontaneous imbibition in crude-oil-aged and mineral-oil-probed Berea sandstones shows wettability shifts toward water wetness with elevated temperature and aging at residual oil saturation.

The study examines how temperature and increased water saturation alter wettability in Berea sandstone cores prepared as MXW (aged in Minnelusa crude at Swi ~24%, 75°C, 10 days) and MXW-F (crude replaced by decalin then mineral oil). Spontaneous imbibition was measured at 22°C and 75°C, including sequential cycles with re-aging at Swi or at Sor (imbibition or forced) in brine. Imbibition at 75°C was about five times faster in scaled time than at 22°C for MXW cores, and eight pressurized cores at 200 psi confirmed that gas evolution did not cause the effect. Re-aging in crude oil at Swi gave no change or reduced water wetness, whereas aging at Sor in brine (e.g., 25 days at 75°C, Sw rising from 43.2% to 68.3%) shifted cores toward water wetness. MXW-F cores also became more water wet at 75°C but remained less water wet than MXW cores, and later imbibition curves repeatedly crossed the initial ones.

## A Systematic Investigation of Wetting Stability in Aged Chalk

- ID: SCA2014-037, year: 2014
- Authors: M. Steinsbø, A. Graue, M.A. Fernø
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Amott-Harvey index, wettability stability, dynamic aging, spontaneous imbibition, Rørdal chalk, initial water saturation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-037.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-037)

Rørdal chalk plugs aged dynamically in crude oil showed stable Amott-Harvey water indices (average change 0.04) over two spontaneous and forced imbibition cycles, across aging times and initial water saturations.

Reliable EOR wettability studies need stable, uniform wetting states in aged cores. Twelve outcrop Rørdal chalk plugs were aged dynamically with North Sea crude at 80°C for 2 or 6 days at low (~0.23), medium (~0.29) or high (~0.35) initial water saturation. End-point Swsp rose with higher initial Sw and shorter aging (e.g. 0.46 to 0.56 for 6-day aging) but was repeatable within ±0.02 between cycles. Stevns and Niobrara chalk gave less consistent wettability results.

## Predicting the Interfacial Tension of Brine/Gas (or Condensates) Systems

- ID: SCA1992-08EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Michel J. Argaud
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: interfacial tension, brine surface tension, salinity correction, parachor, Firoozabadi-Ramey correlation, gas/brine IFT
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-08EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-08EURO)

Reviews literature data to predict brine surface tension and gas/brine interfacial tension, improves the Firoozabadi-Ramey correlation with a parachor/molar mass correction, and tests it on a gas/brine case.

Capillary pressure conversion from ambient to reservoir conditions requires estimates of brine surface tension and gas or condensate/brine interfacial tension (IFT). The paper compiles literature data and derives salt-specific coefficients for the increase in brine surface tension (e.g. 1.63 mN/m per molal for NaCl, about 3 for MgCl2 and CaCl2). Alkane/brine IFT increments are 0.84 to 0.95 of the surface-tension values, and may scale with Kelvin temperature. The Firoozabadi & Ramey correlation is re-examined against pure hydrocarbon, gas and C1-C10 mixture data. It is improved with a parachor/molar mass correction, which gives separate gas (δρ>0.7 g/cm3) and liquid hydrocarbon (δρ<0.5) domains, but it still fails for gas-liquid mixtures. In a real methane-rich gas/brine case at 120-130°C and 353-453 bar, predicted IFTs agreed with measured values of 42-45 mN/m within about ±0.5 to 2 mN/m, though the temperature trend was wrong.

- Authors: J. J. Funk, P. L. Dahlstrom, W. C. Haltmar, J. M. Perdue
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: FT-IR ATR spectroscopy, carbonate wettability, sputter-coated calcite, crude oil adsorption, asphaltenes, XPS
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-12)

ATR FT-IR on ZnSe crystals sputter-coated with calcite tracks crude oil component adsorption over time, showing polar O, S and N species adsorb and equilibrate near 130 hours.

Bulk wettability measurements give little information on molecular-level oil-carbonate interactions. The authors sputter-coated zinc selenide ATR crystals with an 8.5 nm calcium carbonate layer (verified by XPS and IR) and contacted them with a 27° API Middle East stock tank oil, its cyclohexane maltenes, and weak acid and aromatic fractions. Asphaltenes (0.42 wt%) significantly altered the adsorption and hydrogen bonding behavior, and aromatics adsorbed similarly to whole crude. Adsorption leveled off at roughly 130 hours, and residual absorbance after cleaning suggested strongly bonded material.

- ID: SCA1995-32, year: 1995
- Authors: C. Bousseau, Y. Liu, J.S. Buckley
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wetting alteration, brine pH, Amott index, spontaneous imbibition, Aerolith, adhesion test
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-32)

Brine pH (4 vs 8) controls crude-oil wetting alteration on glass, Aerolith and Berea; wetting trends match across scales, but recovery depends on pore structure.

The study links wetting alteration of flat glass surfaces to that in cores, using pH 4 and pH 8 0.1 M NaCl brines with Alaska A-93 asphaltic crude. Adhesion and adsorption tests on glass at 80°C showed an adhesion-to-nonadhesion transition between pH 4 and 6. ## Asphaltene Molecules Association Effect on Crude Oil Flow Through Porous Medium

- ID: SCA1996-32, year: 1996
- Authors: Galtsev V.E, Ametov I.M, Kuznetsov A.M, Kovalev A.G, Salnikov D.I
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: asphaltene adsorption, filamentary structures, electron paramagnetic resonance, crude oil permeability, resin-asphaltene fraction, flow direction dependence
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-32)

Adsorption of resin-asphaltene fractions forms filamentary asphaltene structures in pores, explaining reduced oil permeability and its dependence on flow direction, rate and rate-change history.

Crude oil permeability in porous media differs strongly from gas permeability and depends on flow direction and rate. Kara-Chukhur crude oil was flowed through ground quartz sand columns and a Bentheimer sandstone plug (0.29 µm² gas permeability), and resin-asphaltene fraction (RAF) in effluent was measured by EPR. RAF adsorption (1.6-2.6e-7 g/cm² on sand) coincided with permeability dropping to a lower constant value; the layer thickness implied (160-270 Å) rules out monolayer or polyassociate layers, supporting filamentary structures with diameter of 23-39 Å and length near 1.3 µm. Pressure stabilized in ~120 s after a rate increase but 3000-6000 s after a decrease, attributed to diffusion-controlled filament growth; fitted P~Q^n gave n of 0.47-0.63 versus the predicted 0.5.

## Crude Oil/Brine Contact Angles on Quartz Glass

- ID: SCA1997-12, year: 1997
- Authors: X. Xie, N.R. Morrow
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Wilhelmy plate contact angle, wettability alteration, asphaltene adsorption, brine salinity and pH, aging temperature, spontaneous imbibition
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-12)

Wilhelmy plate contact angles on crude-oil-aged quartz glass vary with pH, aging time and temperature, oil composition and brine; porous media prove more water-wet than angles suggest.

The study quantifies wetting alteration of quartz glass by adsorption from crude oils using dynamic Wilhelmy plate contact angles against decane under brine. Variables were brine pH (4-10) and NaCl molarity, aging time (1-500 h), aging temperature (room vs 88°C), oil composition (A'93, A'95, CS crudes and A'93 maltenes), measurement temperature, and synthetic reservoir brines with 10- and 100-fold dilutions. Aging at room temperature left plates water-wet (angles up to about 60°), whereas aging at 88°C in A'93 crude gave advancing angles of 150-170° beyond 96 h. The asphaltic crude altered wettability much more than its maltenes or the low-asphaltene CS oil. Advancing angles were lower at reservoir temperature and rose with brine dilution. Compared with imbibition and waterflood results in Berea and Aerolith cores, trends agreed qualitatively, but porous media appeared more water-wet than flat-quartz angles indicated.

## Making Successful Permeability Measurements with Asphaltic Crude Oils

- ID: SCA2002-10, year: 2002
- Authors: Kory Holmes, Gary Potter, Dan Scott, Wade Williams, Tom Fate
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: asphaltene flocculation, live crude oil, oil permeability, wettability restoration, sacrificial core, relative permeability
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-10)

Three lab approaches (sacrificial core filtration, live crude oil, mineral oil replacement) avoid asphaltene plugging and give stable oil permeability in sandstone and carbonate cores.

Flocculated asphaltenes in dead crude oil plug core samples and alter wettability, making oil permeability and relative permeability measurements unstable. The authors describe three approaches: an inline sacrificial core upstream of the test plug, use of live recombined crude oil, and wettability restoration with dead crude above flocculation pressure followed by displacement with refined mineral oil. Pore-lining mineralogy from QEMSCAN feeds a surface complexation (BPS) model; it predicts more oil-wet behavior and different salinity sensitivity than bulk-mineralogy inputs.

Brine design for low-salinity and engineered waterflooding is typically based on bulk mineralogy (XRD), which misrepresents the mineral surfaces exposed to oil. The authors used SEM-EDS automated mineralogy (QEMSCAN WellSite, 1 or 4 micron steps) on two sandstones to separate pore-lining from bulk mineral fractions. These were scaled by assumed surface areas (quartz 0.1, calcite 0.1, kaolinite 10, illite 50 m²/g) and fed into a Bond Product Sum surface complexation model at 90°C, TAN/TBN of 1/3 and PCO2 of 0.032 atm. For Rock A, bulk inputs (70–80% quartz) predicted strongly water-wet behavior with little salinity sensitivity, whereas pore-lining illite gave substantially more oil-wet behavior, with higher salinity reducing adhesion. For Rock B, about 55% of the pore lining was microcrystalline quartz, and BPS rose with assumed surface area (1, 10, 100 m²/g) relative to the bulk case. The work is modeling only; core-flood validation, roughness characterization and BET comparison are left to future work.
