## Image Registration: Enhancing and Calibrating X-Ray Micro-CT Imaging

- ID: SCA2008-35, year: 2008
- Authors: Shane Latham, Trond Varslot, Adrian Sheppard
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: image registration, micro-CT, BSEM thin section, dry/flooded alignment, correlation coefficient, parallel computing
- Cited by 10 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-35.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-35)

A parallel multi-resolution, multi-start 3D registration method aligns BSEM thin-section images and flooded micro-CT scans with dry micro-CT volumes, enabling combined analysis of fine features and fluid distribution.

Micro-CT analysis is limited by poor mineral discrimination, sub-resolution features and limited fluid contrast, so the authors developed a distributed-memory (MPI) registration algorithm. It uses a correlation-coefficient metric, a 7-DOF similarity transform, an exhaustive low-resolution search, and Powell local optimization at progressively finer resolutions. BSEM montages of carbonate and clay-rich sandstone thin sections were registered to micro-CT volumes (2.59-2.69 micron voxels) with correlation coefficients of 0.641 and 0.716, revealing porosity and structure below micro-CT resolution. A dry and a CsI-flooded sucrosic dolomite core (6.72 micron voxels) were also aligned using a modified metric restricted to dry-pore voxels with an overlap penalty, allowing the dry image to mask the flooded one and isolate contrast liquid. BSEM alignment takes under 1 hour on 64 CPUs and flooded/dry under 2 hours, using up to 32 and 64 GB RAM respectively.

## Multiphase flow in porous rock imaged under dynamic flow conditions with fast x-ray computed microtomography

- ID: SCA2013-011, year: 2013
- Authors: S. Berg, R. Armstrong, H. Ott, A. Georgiadis, S. A. Klapp, A. Schwing, R. Neiteler, N. Brussee, A. Makurat, L. Leu, F. Enzmann, J.-O. Schwarz, M. Wolf, F. Khan, M. Kersten, S. Irvine, M. Stampanoni
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: fast synchrotron microCT, Haines jump, snap-off, capillary desaturation, invasion percolation, cooperative pore filling
- Cited by 9 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-011.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-011)

Fast synchrotron microCT imaged Haines jumps and snap-off in real time under flow; drainage events are cooperative, spanning 10-20 pores, with 64% of work dissipated in Berea.

Pore-scale displacements were imaged in real time with fast synchrotron microCT (16.8-60 s per tomogram) using an in-situ micro-pump and pressure monitoring. Samples were 4 mm sintered glass, Gildehauser and Berea sandstones and Estaillades limestone, with decane displacing water at Ca of 4e-9 to 4e-8. Haines jump events drained multiple pores cooperatively, with event volumes up to 100-200 nL versus 1-10 nL for the largest pores. In Berea, 61% of displaced volume occurred in irreversible jumps dissipating 64% of the work, and about 36% went to interfacial energy. Event size distributions followed a power law with exponent 1.0 for small events, but large events were more frequent than invasion percolation predicts. Snap-off and capillary desaturation were also observed, with a critical Ca near 3e-8 in sintered glass.

## 4D Imaging of Fluid Flow Dynamics in Natural

- ID: SCA2013-012, year: 2013
- Authors: Swiss Light Source
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: synchrotron microtomography, spontaneous imbibition, trapped gas, snap-off, Fontainebleau sandstone, saturation profile
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-012.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-012)

Ultra-fast synchrotron microtomography at 1 s per 3D image captures spontaneous imbibition in Fontainebleau sandstone, revealing saturation evolution and gas trapping by water blocking and snap-off.

The paper addresses the lack of transient 3D pore-scale data on multiphase flow in natural rocks. Spontaneous imbibition of viscosified KI brine (15 cP) into a dry 5.8 mm Fontainebleau mini-core (17% porosity) was imaged at the TOMCAT beamline with 5.5 µm voxels and 1 s per 3D image. Imbibition finished within about 8 s, with saturation reaching 69% and varying linearly with the square root of time after the first 2 s, consistent with Washburn behavior. Saturation profiles became self-similar with a non-sharp front, and trapped gas was found mainly in larger pores (mean radius 60 µm versus 52.2 µm for all pores). Local mechanisms of gas trapping were water blocking and snap-off, the latter occurring far from the meniscus, which suggests precursor films.

## Computed Microtomography of Reservoir Core Samples

- ID: SCA1994-01, year: 1994
- Authors: P. Spanne, E. L. Muegge, K. W. Jones
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: computed microtomography, synchrotron X-ray, pore connectivity, sandstone porosity, mineral identification, attenuation coefficient
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-01)

Synchrotron X-ray computed microtomography at about 5 µm resolution images sandstone pore structure, minerals and connectivity, with porosity compared against helium porosimetry and SEM.

Medical CT scanners are too coarse (about 0.25 x 0.25 x 1.5 mm) for pore-level imaging, so the authors applied synchrotron X-ray computed microtomography (CMT) at the NSLS X26C beamline. Three sandstones were epoxy-impregnated and 2-3 mm microcores were scanned at 5 µm voxel resolution, with imaging times of 8-90 minutes per slice. Comparison with medical CT showed core-scale and pore-scale heterogeneity can differ. Minerals such as quartz, feldspar and siderite or pyrite were identified from attenuation coefficients (810, 1320 and 2690 m-1 in sandstone #2). CMT porosity agreed with helium porosity for sandstones #1 and #2 (24.5% vs 27.3%, 22.1% vs 21.6%), but sandstone #3 gave 12.7% vs 21.3%, attributed to sub-resolution pores. Stacking 95 slices gave a 3D volume and pore-connectivity maps usable as input for pore-level flow simulation.

## Use of Attenuation Standards for CAT Scanning Applications Within Oil and Gas Production Research

- ID: SCA1992-23, year: 1992
- Authors: M. E. Coles, E. L. Muegge, B. F. Marek
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: CT scanning, attenuation standards, scanner drift calibration, saturation determination, three-phase saturation, fused quartz
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-23)

Fused quartz and aluminum attenuation standards correct day-to-day CT scanner drift, improving two- and three-phase saturation accuracy in a Berea core to within about 1.1 saturation percent of volumetrics.

CT-derived saturations from multi-day core floods are degraded by unpredictable drift of the X-ray source and detectors, which varies with CT number. The authors scan fused quartz and aluminum secondary standards before each slice series and fit the deviation from baseline values as a function of CT number to correct the core CT values. The method was tested on a 2 in by 12 in Berea core in a Lucite cell with brine, hexadecane/iododecane and air, scanned at 140 and 100 kV. Calibration reduced the maximum two-phase water saturation error versus volumetrics from 4.54 to 1.09 saturation percent and the 140/100 kV spread from 2.54 to 0.88. Three-phase average deviations fell from about 3.0 to 0.74-0.85 saturation percent, though gas saturation at very low values (3.9% vs 2.63%) remained poorly determined.

## The Use of Attenuation Standards for CT Scanning

- ID: SCA1995-13, year: 1995
- Authors: M. E. Coles, E. L. Muegge, Producing Technical, F. M. Auzerais, P. Frulla, A. Kantzas
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: CT scanning, attenuation standards, CT number drift, saturation determination, bulk density calibration, x-ray tube stability
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-13)

Describes CT number drift in core scanning and three laboratories' attenuation-standard calibration methods, showing standards improve saturation accuracy to about 1%, with recommendations to minimize drift.

CT numbers measured on core drift over time because of tube and detector aging, beam filtration, temperature, beam hardening and noise. A drift of only 10-20 CT units can cause significant saturation errors. Three calibration methods are presented. Schlumberger-Doll scans plastic and magnesium rods with each Berea core to normalize every slice to true density. Mobil scans fused quartz and aluminum standards before each core, and water saturations then agree with volumetric values within about 1% rather than about 4% without standards (average error 0.98% vs 3.54% in two-phase tests). Calgary checks a reference material of known density and adjusts tube gain or reruns the scanner standardization. The paper ends with eight recommendations, including using reference materials of similar effective atomic number, documenting the calibration, and warming up the tube.

## Analysis of Rock Microstructure Using High-Resolution X-Ray Tomography

- ID: SCA2006-26, year: 2006
- Authors: A.P. Sheppard, R. M. Sok, H. Averdunk, V. B. Robins, A. Ghous, Applied Mathematics
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: micro-CT, grain partitioning, pore network extraction, watershed transform, Delaunay tetrahedra, Castlegate sandstone
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-26)

Partitions both pore and grain space of micro-CT images of clastic and carbonate rocks into networks, finding grain partitioning more robust than pore analysis and Delaunay-pore duality weak.

The paper extends micro-CT network partitioning from pore space to grain space to relate microstructure to macroscopic properties. Eight samples were analysed (unconsolidated sands, Castlegate and Berea sandstones, two carbonates, and an acrylic sphere pack) using covering radius maps, medial axis and watershed transforms with region merging. Two Castlegate pieces segmented independently gave porosities of 26% and 21%, yet had identical grain density (179 grains/mm3) and similar grain distributions, indicating they are statistically equivalent. Pore-space results were much more sensitive, since throats are often only 2-3 voxels wide. In the sphere pack, only 28% of Delaunay tetrahedra contained a pore center, so grain-contact duality with pores is poor. Volume-weighting distributions is needed to reveal carbonate heterogeneity (coordination and aspect ratios above 20).

## Direct Measurement of the Constituent Porosities in a Dual Porosity Matrix

- ID: SCA1990-03, year: 1990
- Authors: Robert M. Moss, Gregory P. Pepin, Lorne A. Davis
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray CT porosity, dual porosity, fractured shale, xenon contrast gas, porosity histogram, beam hardening
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-03.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-03)

A two-scan X-ray CT method with xenon contrast gas measures matrix and fracture porosities directly in dual porosity cores without mineralogy or fracture-width assumptions, agreeing with helium porosity.

Fractured, dual porosity cores are hard to characterize because single-scan CT porosity needs mineralogy assumptions and thresholding misses narrow fractures. The authors subtract CT images of the evacuated core from images of the xenon-saturated core in an aluminum pressure vessel, using a fourth-generation scanner at 120 kVp with 2 mm slices. Mean CT porosities agree with helium porosimetry for Berea, carbonates and a porous ceramic. Beam hardening gives a minimum porosity distribution width of about 4.5% of the mean. Time-sequenced scans of Monterey shale showed a high-permeability bed filling to 26.5% porosity and a bimodal porosity histogram. Bakken shale showed thin high-porosity beds along the core axis, and a coal sample had 10% fracture porosity at 30 s and stored 2.9 times the surrounding gas density after 4.3 days. Longer scans (8 s, 75 mA) and a minimized annulus gave the best images.

## Core Imaging – Twenty Five Years of Equipment, Techniques, and Applications of X-Ray Computed Tomography (CT) for Core Analysis

- ID: SCA2011-25, year: 2011
- Authors: J. J. Funk, E. M. Withjack, S. Siddiqui, Sultan M. Al-Enezi, Sinan Caliskan
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: X-ray CT, micro-CT, dual-energy CT, coreflood saturation monitoring, core-log integration, acid wormholing
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-25)

Reviews 25 years of X-ray CT for core analysis, covering scanner equipment, calibration and display techniques, static and dynamic applications, and micro-CT, concluding CT is cost-effective and valuable.

This review traces X-ray CT in core analysis from the 1984 isotope-source and modified medical scanners to modern multi-slice helical and micro-CT systems. It describes calibration and CT-number porosity determination, contrast agents (NaI, xenon), dual-energy density and Zeff measurements, scout and helical acquisition, and image analysis software. Static applications include core-log depth matching, lithology and electrofacies identification, and fracture and orientation analysis using unrolled CT images compared with FMI logs. Dynamic applications include coreflood saturation monitoring, acid wormholing, perforation analysis, hydrate dissociation and unconventional rocks. Scan times fell from hours to seconds, and micro-CT (focal spots 0.6-10 microns, resolution 1-50 microns) now supports pore-scale modeling. The authors conclude CT cost is small relative to project value.

## Developments in Synchrotron X-Ray Microtomography for Application to Flow in Porous Media

- ID: SCA1996-30, year: 1996
- Authors: R. D. Hazlett, M. E. Coles, K. W. Jones, B. Andrews, B. Dowd, P. Siddons, A. Peskin
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: synchrotron microtomography, pore network skeletonization, tortuosity, network model drainage, lattice Boltzmann waterflood, successive erosion connectivity
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-30)

Describes upgraded synchrotron microtomography giving large, ~3 µm rock images in hours, and uses them for connectivity, tortuosity, network-model drainage/imbibition and lattice Boltzmann waterflood simulation.

The paper reports upgrades to the Brookhaven NSLS computed microtomography system: a cooled CCD array detector with YAG scintillator and expansion optics now yields volumes of ~16-30 million voxels at 2.7-3 µm resolution in a few hours. A North Sea Brent sandstone (470 md) is imaged as an example, and a 3-D stereo visualization theater is described. Lattice Boltzmann simulation of a water-wet waterflood (Nca ~1e-4, 7400 md sample, Siw 12%) ended at Sor of 35%.

## Combining High-Fidelity Helical Micro-Tomography with Region-of-Interest Scanning for Improved Core Characterisation

- ID: SCA2011-26, year: 2011
- Authors: Trond K. Varslot, Andrew M. Kingston, Shane J. Latham, Jill Middleton, Mark A. Knackstedt, Adrian P. Sheppard
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: helical micro-CT, Katsevich reconstruction, region-of-interest tomography, tight gas sand, wet/dry image registration, unconsolidated sand
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-26)

Helical-trajectory micro-CT with Katsevich reconstruction and region-of-interest scanning gives faster, higher-quality images of tight gas sand, coal and unconsolidated sand without physical subsampling.

Imaging heterogeneous, tight or unconsolidated core is limited by the trade-off between field of view and resolution. The authors implemented exact helical (Katsevich) cone-beam reconstruction for micro-CT with software autofocus alignment, allowing 40° cone angles, about 20% X-ray capture, and long samples. Dry and CsI-saturated helical scans of a tight gas sand (7% porosity, 0.03 mD) were registered to give porosity maps, and formation factor on 1.75 mm subvolumes gave cementation exponents of 2.8 (X, Y) and 2.4 (Z), versus 2.1 measured at core scale. A coal sample was also imaged to map fractures and microporosity. Region-of-interest scanning using projection extrapolation improved a 38 mm unconsolidated sand from 28 μm to 10.2 μm voxels with a bimodal histogram suitable for segmentation, and a 2.4 μm ROI scan of the tight gas sand resolved pore-filling kaolinite.

## High Resolution Laboratory X-Ray Tomography on Rocks

- ID: SCA1997-24, year: 1997
- Authors: E. Rosenberg, P. Guéroult, J. Lynch, M. Bisiaux, R. Ferreira
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray microtomography, electron microprobe source, pore network connectivity, grey level histogram, kaolinite, wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-24)

An electron microprobe is converted into an X-ray microtomograph with about 10 µm 3D resolution, used to image pore networks and minerals in sandstones, explaining permeability differences.

The authors convert an electron microprobe into a laboratory X-ray microtomograph, using a focused electron beam on a niobium thin target as a point source, with a rotating specimen and CCD detector. Radiographic resolution reaches 2 µm, and 256³ reconstructions from 120 projections at 3° steps give better than 10 µm resolution. Tests on glass beads in a capillary, Fontainebleau sandstone and three Brent sandstone samples (CAR1, CAR3, CAR4) were compared with SEM images, showing reliable reconstruction of surface morphology. Grey-level histogram deconvolution gave total pore-plus-clay fractions in agreement with global measurements (e.g., 29 vs 28% for CAR1). CAR1 has large, poorly connected macropores, explaining its lower permeability, while CAR3 and CAR4 have well-connected macropores. Feldspars can be segmented, but kaolinite cannot be reliably separated because its absorption is close to quartz and its size is near the resolution limit.

## Dynamic X-Ray Micro-Tomography for Real Time Imaging of Drainage and Imbibition Processes at the Pore Scale

- ID: SCA2011-27, year: 2011
- Authors: Glenn R. Myers, Andrew M. Kingston, Trond K. Varslot, Michael L. Turner, Adrian P. Sheppard
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: dynamic tomography, micro-CT, drainage and imbibition, Bentheimer sandstone, iterative reconstruction, hysteresis
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-27)

A dynamic tomography algorithm using fluid-phase constraints images air-water drainage and imbibition in Bentheimer sandstone at about 80 s per 512^3 frame, ten times faster than conventional CT.

Conventional micro-CT needs a static sample during acquisition, which forces flow to be halted during pore-scale displacement imaging. The authors apply a SIRT-based dynamic reconstruction that uses a static pore-structure support and a two-grey-level fluid constraint to reconstruct from only 72 projections per revolution. They ran air-water drainage and imbibition on a 5 mm Bentheimer sample with KI-doped brine. Each displacement took about 2 h, and 512^3 images at 12 µm voxels were obtained at roughly 80 s intervals. Haines-jump-type pore draining was captured, and drainage and imbibition at Sw=0.6 showed hysteresis with more partially filled pores on imbibition. The drainage Pc curve was reasonable, but the imbibition curve was unphysical because of an experimental pressure problem. Resolution and noise were too poor to compute RI or relative permeability, and reconstruction accuracy remains unverified.

## Impact of Wetting Film Flow in Pore Scale Displacement

- ID: SCA2013-016, year: 2013
- Authors: E. Unsal, Emmanuel Moulet-Vargas
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: wetting film flow, snap-off, dynamic pore network model, counter-current imbibition, direct Navier-Stokes simulation, low salinity EOR
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-016.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-016)

Reviews wetting film flow and snap-off, presents a dynamic pore network model with film flow coupled to PHREEQC, and compares it with direct Navier-Stokes simulation.

Wetting films and snap-off control trapping and chemical transport during imbibition but are too thin to observe or resolve directly. The authors present a dynamic pore network model in which films are coupled to element flow without sub-gridding. They simulate counter-current imbibition in a three-capillary network at mobility ratios of 100 and 1000: forced injection lets the bubble grow, while spontaneous imbibition lets water short-cut through the outlet and trap oil. Compared with a direct VoF Navier-Stokes approach, the PNM needed about 0.5-1 day against 3-7 days for the direct method on a similar-sized domain, and resolving 1 µm films directly would need about 50³ times higher resolution. Coupling the PNM with PHREEQC shows low-salinity brine moving through films ahead of the front, which suits EOR studies, though PNM reliability depends on network extraction quality.

## Computerized Neutron Tomography for Core Analysis

- ID: SCA1991-28, year: 1991
- Authors: R.C. Lanza, E.W. McFarland, G.W. Poulos
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: neutron tomography, thermal neutron attenuation, porosity imaging, San Andres dolomite, CCD scintillator detector, x-ray CT comparison
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-28)

Describes a thermal-neutron CT system at the MIT reactor that images water in a San Andres dolomite core, showing more internal detail than x-ray CT.

X-ray CT is relatively insensitive to hydrocarbons and water in dense rock, and steel barrels degrade it further. The authors built a thermal-neutron tomography system using a 5 cm beam (2.4e8 n/cm2/s) from the MIT reactor, a 6LiF-ZnS scintillator, a mirror and lens, and a cooled CCD camera. A 2.54 cm water-saturated San Andres dolomite core was imaged at 45 angles in about three minutes, with an optical resolution of about 0.2 mm. Neutron slices showed more internal detail than 120 kVp x-ray CT. A counting-statistics analysis gives porosity errors of roughly 0.6-44% depending on core diameter, porosity, and barrel and mud layers, for 1 mm voxels and 1e9 neutron fluence. Practical use needs compact accelerator or plasma-focus neutron sources, as D-T generators would need about three hours per scan.

## In-situ, real time micro-CT imaging of pore scale processes, the next frontier for laboratory based micro-CT scanning

- ID: SCA2016-018, year: 2016
- Authors: Van Loo, Van Hoorebeke
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: dynamic micro-CT, 4D imaging, solute transport, tracer breakthrough curve, Savonnières limestone, in-situ flow cell
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-018.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-018)

A gantry-based lab micro-CT with fixed sample achieves 12 s temporal resolution, imaging CsCl tracer transport in Savonnières limestone and revealing advective pathways versus diffusion-controlled vuggy pores.

Lab micro-CT normally has minute-to-hour temporal resolution, limiting imaging of fast pore-scale processes. The authors present a gantry-based system (rotating source and detector, fixed sample, 130 kV, 39 W, 5 µm focal spot, fast CMOS detector) that gives a full 3D image every 12 s. They imaged 10 wt% CsCl tracer injection at 0.25 and 1 µl/s into a 6 mm Savonnières limestone in an X-ray-transparent PMMA cell, using 14.6 µm voxels and 45000 projections over 15 min. Breakthrough curves gave dispersion coefficients of 3.89E-3 mm2/s (1 µl/s) and 1.44E-3 mm2/s (0.25 µl/s), indicating advection-dominated transport. Images showed well-connected macropores saturating within ~3 min, while intragranular ooid pores connected via micropores filled over more than 10 min, consistent with diffusion control. Noise was mitigated by median filtering and masking with a high-quality 7.3 µm pre-scan.

## Use of CT Scanning in the Investigation of Damage to Unconsolidated Cores

- ID: SCA1989-02, year: 1989
- Authors: R. E. Gilliland
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: CT scanning, unconsolidated cores, core damage, mud invasion, slabbing orientation, plug fractures
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1989/SCA1989-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1989-02)

Shows how CT scans of sleeved unconsolidated cores reveal bedding, slabbing orientation, inclusions and damage types such as fractures, core spin, rubble and mud invasion, with likely causes.

Unconsolidated Gulf of Mexico cores are often damaged during coring, handling and transport, even with rigid inner barrel liners. The authors used CT scanning (surview and slice images) to inspect sleeved cores nondestructively before slabbing and plugging. Examples show how CT determines bedding dip for slabbing, distinguishes cross-bedding from structural dip, and identifies missing sections and shell inclusions. Damage examples include separation at shale laminations, shock-induced diagonal fractures, core spin, rubble, plug fractures, deep barite mud invasion and concentric fractures that coincide with the mud invasion depth, possibly linked to freezing of water-filtrate-invaded zones. The paper concludes that identifying damage and its probable cause lets coring and handling procedures be revised to reduce it. The results are qualitative image examples.

## Non-Destructive Local X-Ray Tomography for Multi-Length Scale Analysis of Reservoir Rocks: Validations and Observations

- ID: SCA2012-59, year: 2012
- Authors: J. Gelb, S. Roth, H. Dong, D. Li, A. Gu, S. Yun, W. Yun
