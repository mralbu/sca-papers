## In-Situ Saturation Distributions: The Key to Understanding Core Analysis

- ID: SCA1994-05, year: 1994
- Authors: P Naylor, D A Puckett
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: in-situ saturation, gamma attenuation, X-ray attenuation, gamma emission, gravity drainage, three-phase saturation
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-05)

Reviews gamma attenuation, X-ray attenuation and gamma emission methods for in-situ saturation measurement in core floods, with accuracy analysis and wettability and gravity drainage examples.

The paper reviews ionising-radiation techniques for measuring in-situ saturation distributions of up to three phases during core floods: gamma attenuation, X-ray attenuation and gamma emission. It gives the saturation equations and a statistical error analysis relating photon intensity accuracy to saturation accuracy. For 1% PV saturation accuracy, intensity accuracy of 0.004% is needed for decane/brine/air and 0.04% for decane/brine, relaxed to 0.089% and 0.47% with 10% NaI dopant. X-ray scanning improves temporal resolution by about an order of magnitude over gamma attenuation, while gamma emission is the practical option for steel pressure vessels. Examples show waterflood ROS of 0.42 in a water-wet core versus 0.21 after ageing, and gravity drainage profiles revealing end effects that would cause mass balance to overestimate residual oil to gas.

## Towards a Better Understanding of Multiphase Flow in Porous Media: 3D in-Situ Fluid Distribution Imaging at the Pore Scale

- ID: SCA2009-17, year: 2009
- Authors: S. Youssef, D. Bauer, S. Bekri, E. Rosenberg, O. Vizika
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: X-ray micro-tomography, in-situ fluid imaging, drainage and imbibition, residual oil trapping, image segmentation, Fontainebleau sandstone
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-17.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-17)

A PEEK Hassler microcell combined with lab micro-CT images oil/water distribution through a full drainage-imbibition cycle in Fontainebleau sandstone, revealing trapping configurations, IFT effects and hysteresis in interfacial area.

The paper presents a micro-CT setup with a small PEEK Hassler-type flow cell (5 µm pixels) that images fluid distributions in situ during a capillary pressure cycle without interrupting flow. A 6 mm Fontainebleau sandstone miniplug (22% porosity, 2.6 D) was drained with iododecane-doped dodecane at 0.01–0.2 cc/min, then imbibed by positive spontaneous, free (Pc=0) and forced imbibition. Saturation profiles from mean grey level agree with those from 3-phase segmentation, except that segmentation misses water films at Swi. The drainage Pc curve derived from the images matches mercury intrusion on a sister plug; drainage showed microfingering along walls at low capillary number. Free imbibition left oil trapped in many small ganglia; raising IFT from 16 to 40 mN/m produced more connected blobs. At equal So of 23%, the oil–water interfacial area in imbibition was 1.5 times that in drainage, which may explain hysteresis.

## State-of-the-art X-ray Steady State Apparatus for advanced core flow studies. SCA-9853

- ID: SCA1998-53, year: 1998
- Authors: J.G.C. Coenen, P. Koornneef.
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray saturation measurement, steady state relative permeability, carbon fibre core holder, saturation profiles, resistivity electrodes
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-53.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-53)

Shell describes a new steady-state relative permeability apparatus using an X-ray source for fast, undoped in-situ saturation measurement to ±1 sat%, with multi-port pressure and resistivity.

Conventional steady-state relative permeability tests rely on gamma-ray saturation measurement, which needs long counting times (about 20 min/point) and doped fluids that risk altering wettability. Shell Research built an X-ray steady state (SSX) apparatus with a high-flux industrial X-ray source, allowing 0.2 s per position, a 15 cm sweep scan in 12 s, and 40 discrete scan positions. Custom CdWO4/photodiode current-mode detectors resolve the only 4% signal change between 100% water and 100% oil, and a monitor detector in the primary beam compensates for source drift. The carbon-fibre epoxy core holder is X-ray transparent, with multiple pressure ports and resistivity electrodes (full core plus 4 positions), and tandem HPLC pumps give pulse-free flow of 0.01-20 ml/min. Saturation is accurate to ±1 sat% with natural undoped fluids for porosities down to 25%, and the profiles allow correction for entrance and capillary end effects and numerical-simulation interpretation.

## How Reliable Is in Situ Saturation Monitoring (Issm) Using X-Ray?

- ID: SCA2014-009, year: 2014
- Authors: A.W. Cense, H.A. van der Linde, N. Brussee, J. Beljaars, A. Schwing
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray saturation monitoring, low salinity waterflooding, Welge weighing under back pressure, Dean-Stark, unsteady-state coreflood, calibration scans
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-009.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-009)

X-ray saturations in unsteady-state floods are compared with oil production measured by a weighing-under-pressure balance; X-ray can deviate substantially, especially in low salinity floods, while the balance matches Dean-Stark.

X-ray monitoring of in situ saturation is common in core floods, but its uncertainty is rarely quantified, and low salinity EOR increments are small (about 1.2 ml of oil for a typical plug). The authors built a Welge-type set-up in which produced oil floats into a cap hanging from a balance at 5-6 bar back pressure, so no gas evolves; dead volumes (about 1.2 ml inflow, 1.0 ml outflow) give under 0.2 ml uncertainty. In one sandstone polymer flood, X-ray and balance saturations agreed within 0.01-0.03 PV. In a low salinity sandstone flood they diverged, owing to brine mixing, salinity contrast, possible dissolution/precipitation and imperfect calibration scans (residual water of 0.3% and 2.7% after oil scans). Against Dean-Stark on 14 samples, the balance gave a mean saturation difference of 0.016 ± 0.015 (0.25 ml). The authors recommend combining X-ray profiles with the balance for quality control.

- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2017/SCA2017-012.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2017-012)

CAL-X, a miniaturized X-ray radiography coreflood setup for 10 mm plugs, cuts experiment time by an order of magnitude and reproduces macro-plug relative permeability and CDC results.

Chemical EOR studies need fast coreflood measurements, so the authors built CAL-X, combining an X-ray radiography facility, an automated multi-fluid injection platform and a beryllium core holder for 10 mm by 20 mm mini-plugs (up to 150 bar, 150 °C). ## Cxbox: An Innovative Tool for Fluid Dynamic Quantification During Corefloods

- ID: SCA2017-037, year: 2017
- Authors: Gilles PUYOU, Michel N'GUYEN, Sabine SAVIN
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: 2D X-ray imaging, in situ saturation monitoring, Beer-Lambert law, flat-panel detector, carbon coreholder, reservoir-condition coreflood
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2017/SCA2017-037.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2017-037)

A self-shielded 2D X-ray bench with flat-panel detector and carbon coreholder images fluid saturation in corefloods at reservoir conditions without dopants, matching material balance within a few saturation units.

Conventional 1D NaI X-ray saturation monitoring is slow and cannot capture heterogeneous flow, while CT is costly. TOTAL developed the CXBOX, a cabinet 2D X-ray system with a 40x40 cm flat-panel detector (200 µm pixels), a hyper-stable generator (<0.5% dose drift over 24 h), and a low-attenuation carbon coreholder rated to 650 bar, 155°C and 100 mm samples. On a 50 mm homogeneous sandstone, Swi was 26.6% by material balance versus 26.0% and 25.8% for methods 1 and 2; final So was 41.6% versus 40.0% and 39.2%. Images take 1-10 s, and a miscible tracer test with two similar brines and no dopant showed preferential flow at the sample edge. Method 1 can drift by about five saturation units over months-long tests.

## Laboratory Evaluation of Waterflood Residual Oil Saturation in Four Carbonate Cores

- ID: SCA2001-12, year: 2001
- Authors: Jairam Kamath, Frank Nakagawa, Robert Meyer, Shah Kabir, Randall Hobbet
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, CT flow imaging, carbonate waterflood, capillary number, Amott wettability, capillary end effect
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-12)

CT-monitored waterfloods on four whole carbonate cores show residual oil saturation of 30-60% PV at field rates, falling substantially with higher pressure gradient, most strongly in rocks with large pore aspect ratios.

Residual oil saturation in carbonates is uncertain because of pore-size distribution, heterogeneity and wettability, so four whole limestone cores (6-85 md, 17-26% porosity) were characterized with thin sections, mercury injection, CT porosity maps and miscible floods. Unsteady-state and steady-state oilfloods and waterfloods were run on cleaned and restored-state samples at stepped rates, with CT scanning used to separate capillary end effects, bypassing and true ROS reduction. ROS at field-rate conditions (~1 psi/ft, Nc ~1e-8) was 30-60% PV and fell as pressure gradient rose to ~100+ psi/ft; for example K2 fell from 32 to 20% PV and K4 from 60 to 32% PV. Samples K4 and K5, with large pore body-to-throat ratios, had the highest ROS and strongest rate dependence. Amott water indices were near zero and oil indices 0-0.3, and steady and unsteady state saturation profiles agreed. The authors conclude that a critical capillary number concept does not hold for these carbonates and that coreflood rates should be varied with visualization.

## Fluid Velocities in Oil Cores During Water Injection

- ID: SCA1991-13, year: 1991
- Authors: M. A. Robinson, W. A. Edelstein
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: MRI velocity imaging, 3DFT flow encoding, water injection, flow channeling, Casper sandstone, San Andres dolomite
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-13)

A 3DFT MRI flow pulse sequence maps axial water velocities in Casper sandstone and San Andres dolomite cores, showing flow channeling into high-porosity regions and detecting channels.

Conventional coreflood experiments give only core-averaged properties, and CT-based flow imaging needs a displacement front. This work uses a modified 3DFT MRI flow-encoding sequence on a 2-tesla imager with a bird-cage coil to measure steady-state axial velocities during water injection. Two cores were studied: Casper sandstone (T2 5.4 ms, 77-88 md, 16.1% porosity) and heterogeneous San Andres dolomite (T2 35 ms, 10-60 md, ~16% porosity). Echo time was 15 ms, with 16 slices and 32 averages. In Casper sandstone, flow near the inlet was piston-like, then became channeled into high-porosity layers further along, with some apparent backflow regions. In the dolomite, flow channeled into a few high-porosity regions and along a channel where the epoxy sleeve separated, leaving the second half of the core largely unswept. Low signal-to-noise remained a limitation near the core ends.

## Quality Control of Waterfloods on Heterogeneous Core Samples Using Computer Tomography

- ID: SCA1990-17EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: Helga Baardsen, Victor Nilsen, Arvid O. Hove
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: X-ray CT, miscible displacement, heterogeneous sandstone, initial water saturation, residual oil saturation, core firing wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-17EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-17EURO)

X-ray CT visualization of water- and oilfloods on a layered North Sea sandstone shows channeling that makes Swi, Sor and JBN relative permeability unreliable; firing changes wettability and clay structure.

Displacement tests on heterogeneous North Sea reservoir sandstone gave high Swi, high Sor and unusual relative permeability curves, so a layered 1.5-inch sample was studied with X-ray CT during miscible, oil- and waterfloods. The miscible displacement revealed a high-permeability middle zone (~2700 mD) and outer zones (~900 mD). Swi depended on the method: about 0.13 by centrifuge, 0.40 with viscous oil flood and about 0.50 with low-viscosity oil flood at 400 ml/h. Sor was about 0.30, nearly independent of Swi and only slightly dependent on rate, because oil was produced mainly from the permeable layer. Firing at 600 °C made the sample strongly water-wet (Amott water index 0.9, oil index 0.0), lowered and evened out Sor, and altered clay morphology (kaolinite, illite) and porosity. The authors conclude that JBN-type relative permeability calculation should be avoided on strongly layered samples and that CT should be used to select homogeneous samples.

## Improved Flooding Experiments in Heterogeneous Rocks Using in-Situ Saturations from X-Ray CT Measurements

- ID: SCA2000-40, year: 2000
- Authors: Svein Håvard Midtlyng, Jan Åge Stensen
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray CT saturation, core flooding, heterogeneous rock, three-phase flow, WAG, capillary end effect
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-40.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-40)

X-ray CT in-situ saturation measurements in four core flood cases show they improve interpretation, especially for heterogeneous rocks and complex three-phase floods.

Core floods are usually interpreted from effluent production and pressures alone, which can mislead in heterogeneous samples or complex floods. The authors used a dual-energy medical CT scanner with NaI dopant for two-phase floods, and with tungstate and iododecane dopants for three-phase saturation calculation. Four cases are shown: a homogeneous core in steady-state relative permeability tests, a heterogeneous cross-bedded core, a 10x10x2 cm heterogeneous slab with miscible, oil, gas and water floods, and a three-phase WAG flood at 100 °C and 300 bar. CT showed capillary end effects that invalidated the drainage endpoint, within-section saturation variations of 0.1-0.4 after drainage and 0.5-0.8 after waterflood, and permeability-controlled fingering and gas flooding patterns. In-situ saturations are recommended for heterogeneous rocks and complex floods, since mass balance becomes inaccurate, for example with evaporation during gas flooding.

## An Experimental Study of Waterflooding from Layered Sandstone by CT Scanning

- ID: SCA2013-088, year: 2013
- Authors: Zhang Zubo, Zhang Guanliang, Lv Weifeng, Luo Manli, Chen Xu, Danyong Li
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CT scanning, oil crossflow, layered sandstone, waterflooding, permeability variation coefficient, saturation profile
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-088.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-088)

CT scanning and per-layer effluent measurement in a three-layer sandstone model quantify oil crossflow during waterflooding, showing flow from low to high permeability layers and better recovery at lower permeability variation.

The study investigates crossflow between communicating layers during waterflooding and its effect on oil recovery. Three water-wet outcrop sandstone layers of equal thickness (high, medium, low permeability, e.g. 1188/441/176 mD for Vk 0.71 and 1266/808/375 mD for Vk 0.45) were loaded in a PEEK coreholder that collects outflow from each layer separately, with 12 cP oil and NaBr brine. Comparing volumetric recovery with CT-derived local displacement showed that oil crossflows mainly from low to high permeability layers and increases markedly after water breakthrough. Lower permeability variation coefficient gave higher recovery, especially below 1.5 PV injected, though both groups reached the same final recovery.

## Significance of Absorption Coefficients When Determining In Situ Core Saturations by Linear X-ray Scans

- ID: SCA2000-13, year: 2000
- Authors: D. R. Maloney, D. C. Wegener, D. R. Zornes
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: linear x-ray scanning, Lambert's equation, linear absorption coefficient, beam hardening, three-phase saturation, polychromatic x-ray
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-13)

Applies Lambert's equation directly with fluid absorption coefficients to compute two- and three-phase saturations from polychromatic linear x-ray scans, showing narrow energy integration minimizes beam-hardening errors.

Many labs use heuristic correlations between ln(intensity) and saturation in linear x-ray scans, which fail when fluid density or composition changes. The authors present a corollary of Lambert's equation using emergent reference intensities, with closed-form solutions (Cramer's rule) for two- and three-phase saturations using one, two or three energy ranges. Using a dry North Sea chalk plug scanned at 52 kV and 95 kV in a carbon-fiber coreholder at 70 bar, they calculated pulse-height spectra for CsCl or KBr brine, decane and nitrogen saturations with XCOM absorption coefficients. Integrating intensity over wide energy ranges introduces saturation errors between endpoints due to beam hardening, while narrow or symmetric ranges give calculated saturations within about 0.001-0.01 of true values. Guidance is given for reservoir-condition tests, including measuring fluid absorption coefficients in a separator and correcting for confining fluid density changes.

## Imaging-based Dispersion in CT Coreflooding Experiments

- ID: SCA_2025_1012, year: 2025
- Authors: Ali Fadili, Steffen Berg
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: imaging-based dispersion, 4D CT imaging, tracer dispersivity, advection-dispersion equation, coreflooding, motion blurring
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2025/SCA_2025_1012.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA_2025_1012)

Finite CT scan time blurs moving fronts in coreflooding, inflating apparent dispersivity; scanning 10x faster than flow is insufficient, and 3D heterogeneity gets misread as dispersion.

Finite acquisition time in 4D imaging (CT, MRI, PET) of coreflooding causes motion blurring of fluid fronts, termed imaging-based dispersion, which can be mistaken for hydrodynamic dispersion. Using a synthetic 1D advection-dispersion model of a 5 cm core and 30 s scan time, the authors fit effective dispersivity and velocity versus the ratio of front velocity to scanner velocity (v/vs). At v/vs≈0.1 the fitted dispersivity is about 25% too high and velocity about 10% too high; v/vs≈0.01 reduces the error to below 5%, and the fit diverges as v approaches vs. A 3D simulation on a heterogeneous carbonate digital twin (porosity from dry/wet CT, power-law permeability, flow rates 0.1–2 cm3/min) shows local concentration errors of about 20% in high-velocity regions, so resolved heterogeneity appears as extra dispersion and spatial resolution is lost. Two-phase flow is expected to be similarly affected, and the authors suggest imaging at two fixed positions to obtain effluent curves instead of full 3D scans.

## HPLC Chromatography Packing as a Tool to Evaluate Mechanisms of Formation Damage by Fines Migration

- ID: SCA1992-28, year: 1992
- Authors: José Guzmán, Ivanka Kancev, Mario Lattanzio
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: fines migration, critical salinity concentration, HPLC packed column, formation damage, kaolinite, sandpack permeability
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-28)

Steel HPLC columns packed with Ottawa sand and kaolinite reproduce fines-migration permeability damage seen in core plugs, in 40 minutes instead of 8 hours.

Fines migration causes permeability loss in reservoirs, but conventional core-plug displacement tests take hours and give poor control over pore geometry and mineralogy. The authors packed 316 steel HPLC columns (0.7 cm i.d., 9.2 cm long) with 600–900 µm Ottawa sand and a small fraction of ~30 µm kaolinite, and ran tests at room temperature. Two programs were used: decreasing NaCl or BaCl2 salinity at 1.5 ml/min, and variable flow rate (0.5–8 ml/min) at 3% NaCl. NaCl gave a critical salinity concentration near 0.3% (0.27% in Berea core tests), with permeability falling to about 0.33 of initial in fresh water, while BaCl2 caused no decline. In the rate test, permeability first rose from 2.61 to about 5.1 mD and then declined as fines accumulated at the column frit. The test took 40 minutes versus about 8 hours for conventional displacement.

## A Novel Approach to Testing Core Samples at Reservoir Conditions

- ID: SCA1993-25, year: 1993
- Authors: B.T. Ngwenya, G.B. Shimmield, S.C. Elphick
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: seawater injection, core flood apparatus, on-line HPLC, permeability reduction, Clashach sandstone, formation damage
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-25)

A re-circulating, inert core flood rig with on-line HPLC shows Clashach sandstone permeability falls with brine injection but rises with distilled water, depending on fluid chemistry and sample preparation.

Seawater injection in North Sea reservoirs may trigger poorly understood diagenetic reactions, so the authors built a re-circulating plug-flow core flood rig with on-line HPLC sampling of 10-20 µl volumes at reaction temperature and pressure. The rig uses PEEK and titanium wetted parts, confines cores to 68.9 MPa and 34.5 MPa pore pressure, and heats to 100°C. A chloride pulse tracer test checked flow behaviour. The authors conclude that waterflooding is chemically active and that sample preparation strongly affects permeability results.

## Flow Imaging in Core Samples by Electrical Impedance Tomography

- ID: SCA2001-06, year: 2001
- Authors: J.J.A. van Weereld, M.A. Player, D.A.L. Collie, A.P. Watkins, D. Olsen
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: electrical impedance tomography, 3D resistivity imaging, bipolar DC pulse, truncated-Newton reconstruction, flow front monitoring, Bentheim sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-06)

A 192-electrode 3D electrical impedance tomography system images fluid distribution in 38 mm core plugs using bipolar DC pulses and a fast truncated-Newton reconstruction.

The paper presents 3D electrical impedance tomography for imaging resistivity and fluid distribution in core plugs under confining pressure in a Hassler or Hoek-type holder. A flexible printed circuit with 192 electrodes (8 rings of 24) is wrapped around the plug, and a bipolar DC pulse technique performs four-wire measurements and removes electrode offsets. Reconstruction uses a finite element model with 1080 prismatic elements and a regularised Levenberg-Marquardt scheme accelerated about 9 times by truncated-Newton with preconditioned conjugate gradients. A full data set is acquired in under 1 minute and reconstruction takes under 10 minutes at roughly 4x4x4 mm resolution, with about 5% accuracy in uniform regions. Phantom tests and brine flooding of an oil-saturated Bentheim sandstone show a conical flow front, though some anomalies from contact and geometry errors remain and conversion to saturation is left for future work.

## Two-Dimensional Fluid Saturation Imaging Improves Estimates of Oil Recovery in Heterogeneous Carbonates

- ID: SCA2004-15, year: 2004
- Authors: P. Brockley, Integrated Core
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: gamma attenuation, 2D saturation imaging, waterflood, heterogeneous carbonates, residual oil saturation, slab coreholder
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-15.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-15)

A low-cost gamma-attenuation scanner images 2D saturation during waterfloods of slabbed cores, showing heterogeneities such as cemented fossils and bedding divert flood fronts and create localized residual oil.

Conventional coreflood interpretation assumes one-dimensional flow, which heterogeneous rocks violate, while CT/MRI is costly. The authors built a 2D gamma-attenuation scanner with a flat alloy coreholder (slabs up to 100 x 200 mm, 3000 psi confinement) and four 200 mCi sources with detectors on robotic arms, giving 4 mm pixels and about 300 pixels per hour. Waterfloods with KBr-doped brine at 1.5 ml/hr were imaged on three Middle East limestone slabs and one sandstone slab with a cemented fracture. Cemented fossils, bedding and porosity contrasts split and diverted flood fronts, producing local zones of high residual oil and strong end effects. Preserved or aged samples showed fingering and oil-wet behavior such as stripping after breakthrough. The authors conclude that 2D monitoring gives more insight into heterogeneity than 1D saturation profiles.

CFD (VOF) simulations of brine-oil waterflooding in a core show that coreholder head geometry changes front homogeneity and oil production by up to 32%, favoring a triangular groove design.

Coreholder end-piece groove design may disturb the displacement front in waterflooding experiments. The authors built a 3D ANSYS Fluent VOF porous-media model with Corey relative permeability and Brooks-Corey capillary pressure, validated against a published Berea imbibition experiment (residual oil 26.08% simulated vs 27.67% measured). Three heads (Spiderweb, Waffle, Triangular) were compared on a 7.00 cm by 3.82 cm core at 1 cm³/min in horizontal orientation. Triangular geometry gave the lowest residual oil saturation (54.25% vs 58.17% Spiderweb and 60.35% Waffle) and about 32% more oil produced than Waffle, with reduced gravity segregation. Variogram sills indicated the front lost homogeneity about 8.7 to 9.8 mm from the inlet, and results apply only to horizontal flooding.

CFD (VOF) simulations with a factorial design show that wider coreholder head gutters sharply reduce near-inlet oil saturation during waterflooding, while inlet diameter has no effect.

Coreholder head geometry may affect front homogeneity in coreflooding, but building multiple heads from expensive alloys is costly. The authors simulated waterflooding of a 7 cm by 3.82 cm core (597 mD, porosity 0.225) with ANSYS Fluent using FVM and VOF, with Corey relative permeability and Brooks-Corey capillary pressure. The model was validated against published data, with residual oil saturation 0.2608 versus 0.2767 (error under 6%). A three-level factorial design varied gutter width (1.0–2.0 mm) and inlet diameter (1.8–2.6 mm) across nine cases. Gutter width significantly reduced oil saturation near the inlet: 65.19%, 61.17% and 28.33% at 3, 5 and 10 mm for 1 to 2 mm widening. Inlet diameter and head geometry did not significantly change residual oil saturation (mean about 59.4%).
