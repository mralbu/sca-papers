## Archie'S Dream: Petrophysics from Sidewall Samples and Cuttings

- ID: SCA1998-05, year: 1998
- Authors: T.W. Fens, M.A. Kraaijveld, R. Visser
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: SEM backscattered electron imaging, sidewall samples, cuttings, image analysis porosity, Carman-Kozeny permeability prediction, multiple linear regression
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-05)

Porosity and permeability are predicted from SEM/BSE images of small samples via regression on about 250 plugs, with R 0.94 and 0.89 and validation on 32 samples.

Coring in HP/HT wells is costly, so the authors predict petrophysical properties from sidewall samples and cuttings using SEM backscattered-electron (BSE) images of impregnated, polished samples. A reference set of about 250 siliciclastic samples from many provinces was used to regress image parameters against plug measurements. Porosity (open plus micro-porosity) correlates at 0.94 with a standard error of about 2 porosity units. Permeability, from a Carman-Kozeny-type model using porosity, specific surface and pore body/neck aspect ratio, correlates at 0.89 with error within a factor of 3. A 32-sample Middle East validation set gave a porosity correlation of 0.87, with outliers explained by plug-scale heterogeneity (images cover about 3% of a plug section) and clay-swelling cracks from cleaning. The minimum sample size is about 3x3x3 mm, with 2x2 mm scanned areas at about 1 micron resolution.

## A Fast and Direct Method of Permeability Measurement on Drill Cuttings

- ID: SCA2002-23, year: 2002
- Authors: P. Egermann, R. Lenormand, D. Longeron, C. Zarcone
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: drill cuttings permeability, trapped gas compression, pressure diffusion, viscous oil injection, history matching, rig-site measurement
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-23)

A fast method measures permeability of drill cuttings by viscous-oil invasion compressing trapped gas, history-matched with a diffusion model, agreeing with core permeability from 0.1 to 2000 md.

Permeability is hard to obtain during drilling, and existing cuttings methods need conditioning or work only at very low permeability. The authors place about 100 cc of cuttings in a cell, saturate with viscous oil (200-1200 cP) leaving trapped helium, then apply a 10 bar nitrogen-driven pressure pulse. Oil invasion, driven by compression of trapped gas, is recorded at 500 Hz and history-matched with a spherical pressure-diffusion model (finite differences) to extract permeability. Tests on crushed cores of known permeability (0.14-1700 md) agreed well with core values: up to 2000 md with 3-5 mm cuttings, 200 md with 2-3 mm, and 50 md with 1-2 mm. Measurements take minutes, and porosity can be roughly estimated assuming 25% initial gas saturation.

## Reservoir Rock Properties of Devonian Shale from Core and Log Analysis

- ID: SCA1989-10, year: 1989
- Authors: D.L. Luffel, F. Kent Guidry
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Devonian shale, gas saturation, Dean Stark extraction, cation exchange capacity, mercury injection capillary pressure, fibrous illite
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1989/SCA1989-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1989-10)

Core and log analyses of a Devonian shale well show porosity and gas saturation far higher than earlier reports, with Dean Stark ineffective and permeability results puzzling.

Devonian shale has been regarded as a seal with little free gas storage, so core data are scarce. The authors measured porosity and saturations on 121 preserved core samples from a 150-ft air-drilled interval in West Virginia and compared them with a shale-specific log model. Core porosity averaged 4.5% and gas saturation 32.4% (bulk volume gas 1.3%); Dean Stark toluene extraction failed to remove water fully, and oven drying was needed. SEM showed fibrous illites unaffected by oven drying, and CEC and humidity-drying gave effective/total porosity of about 0.8. Mercury injection on ten plugs gave pore throats mostly 0.01-0.1 micron, while six plugs showed brine permeability far below gas permeability at native saturation, with no coherent relationship.

## Extracting Information from Digital Images of Core

- ID: SCA1991-25, year: 1991
- Authors: Craig Phillips, Rocco DiFoggio, Kathryn Burleigh
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: digital core images, color discrimination, principal component analysis, paylog, thin-bed analysis, borehole image calibration
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-25)

Describes ImageLog, a color-discrimination system using ellipsoid models in RGB space to quantify sand, shale and fluorescing oil sands in core images for log calibration and thin-bed analysis.

The paper presents ImageLog, a system for quantitative analysis of digital core images captured in white, UV and infrared light. Users pick a few training pixels, and an ellipsoid fitted to the color cloud by principal component analysis (covariance eigenvectors) classifies similar colors, avoiding the inappropriate colors included by rectangular RGB boxes. Outputs include dip-oriented net/gross ("paylog"), sand flag, bed thickness and global net/gross every 1/120 ft. Applications shown include calibrating FMS and CBIL borehole images, orienting core fractures, depth shifting, and image enhancement. In a thin-bed example, FMS pad 1 versus SFL cross plots with beds under 1 ft removed gave a 4 ohm-m sand/shale cutoff, whose predictions compared favorably with core-image sand/shale ratios in a second cored well.

## Formation Evaluation by Multidimensional Petrophysical Correlations in Azerbaijan

- ID: SCA1992-03, year: 1992
- Authors: Alexander Rutman, Haim Fligelman
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: multidimensional regression, well log interpretation, porosity-permeability correlation, clay content, SP and gamma-ray logs, Azerbaijan Productivnaja Tolshcha
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-03.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-03)

Multidimensional regression polynomials relate old Azerbaijan well logs to core porosity, permeability and clay content, giving good porosity agreement and showing log errors dominate uncertainty.

Many older Azerbaijan wells have only early-type logs with no porosity device, so quantitative interpretation is difficult. The authors regressed core-measured porosity, permeability and clay content from about 500 cores of the Neogene Productivnaja Tolshcha formation (1500-2000 m; porosity 18-30%, permeability 20-800 md) against resistivity, SP, gamma-ray, neutron, depth, temperature and caliper data. Polynomials were fitted by least squares, validated on a second random subsample using the Fisher criterion, and ranked by correlation coefficient and a variable-contribution measure. Single-log permeability correlations were poor (θ 1.04-1.11), while multivariable correlations including depth were better (e.g., r = 0.80 for porosity, 0.84 for clay parameter q). Dimensionless SP and resistivity groups performed worse than raw log values. Calculated porosity agreed well with core porosity, and error analysis showed logging errors dominated the correlation error (e.g., S for ln k of 0.81 from logs versus 0.50 from cores).

## Constructing Core Petrophysical Groups: A Key Step to Reconcile Petrophysical and Geological Core Data in Reservoir Modelling

- ID: SCA1995-17, year: 1995
- Authors: H. N. Greder, B. Caline, F. M. Petit, F. M. Pellerin, P. M. Calatayud
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: petrophysical groups, cluster analysis, reservoir modelling, sequential indicator simulation, mixed carbonate-siliciclastic reservoir, diagenesis
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-17.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-17)

Clustering plug porosity, permeability and grain density into six Petrophysical Groups, then distributing them within geostatistically modelled sedimentary bodies, reduces property dispersion versus 17 lithofacies in a mixed carbonate-siliciclastic reservoir.

In a mixed carbonate-siliciclastic Albian reservoir, properties vary widely within sedimentary facies because of diagenesis, so facies are poor predictors of rock properties. Over 1000 plug measurements of porosity, gas permeability and grain density from two wells were clustered into six Petrophysical Groups (Group 0 for k<0.1 mD). Capillary pressure and relative permeability were assigned per group, and thin sections and XRD linked groups to dolomitization and dissolution. Five sedimentary bodies were generated by 3-D object modelling, Groups were simulated within them by sequential indicator simulation, and porosity and permeability were simulated per group. Within groups porosity dispersion stayed below 3.9 p.u. and permeability below 0.48 decades, versus 4.5-7 p.u. and 0.7-1 decade within the original 17 lithofacies.

## Porosity from Cuttings : Optitions and Answers

- ID: SCA1996-06, year: 1996
- Authors: M.Della Martera, M.R. Saunders
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: cuttings porosity, NMR relaxometry, helium pycnometry, Boyle's law porosity, wellsite formation evaluation, vuggy porosity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-06)

Three wellsite-compatible methods (two volumetric devices and NMR) for measuring porosity on simulated cuttings were tested against core plugs; cuttings of 2 mm or larger give meaningful porosity.

The paper evaluates ways to obtain porosity from drill cuttings at the wellsite as an alternative to coring or logging. Simulated cuttings (2-4, 1-2 and 0.25-0.5 mm) were crushed from sandstone, limestone, dolomite, siltstone and shale plugs and measured with a glass-bead/Boyle's law device (A), a helium pycnometer plus bulk volume device (B), and low-field NMR (MARAN-2, 2.2 MHz). Cuttings porosity correlates with core porosity for 2-4 mm sandstones and dolomites (A: r=0.93 and 0.67; B: 0.79 and 0.78), but poorly for vuggy limestones (r about 0.25) and for smaller chips; 0.25-0.5 mm results were unreliable. NMR gave r² of 0.88 (limestone), 0.84 (sandstone) and 0.52 (dolomite), with dolomite overestimated because NMR sees total rather than effective porosity. Device A was very sensitive to ambient pressure (54% porosity change for 40 mb) and device B was too costly, while NMR could determine porosity in about 30 minutes once cuttings of about 0.5 cc are saturated.

## Predicting Rock Properties from Digital Core Images

- ID: SCA2002-34, year: 2002
- Authors: Christopher M. Prince, Petro Image, Reservoir Management
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: spatial analysis, Fourier transform power spectrum, grain size distribution, laser particle size analysis, digital core imaging, permeability estimation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-34)

Fourier-based spatial analysis of high-resolution slabbed core images estimates grain size matching LPSA and direct measurement, and permeability estimates from modal grain size agree with measurements.

Archived core photos and low-resolution images support only qualitative work, so the authors test whether high-resolution digital images of slabbed core can give grain size and permeability logs. A 2-D Fourier transform radial power spectrum of the image, originally developed for thin sections, is used to derive grain size distributions. Images from four unlithified core intervals and three plug ends were taken with a Nikon D1X, and results were compared with laser particle size analysis and direct measurement of 100-150 grains per image. Mean and modal grain sizes agree well among the three methods, though sorting and higher moments cannot be compared because LPSA is a whole-sample method and the image methods see only framework grains. Permeability estimated by regression of log K on modal grain size agreed well with measured values. An appendix discusses resolution needs (about 1000 dpi for fine sand, about 5000 dpi for coarse silt), file sizes, and two-stage digitization.

## A Density-Based Imaging Technique to Supplement Fmi Images for Sedimentary Facies Modeling

- ID: SCA2003-58, year: 2003
- Authors: Shameem Siddiqui, Aon A. Khamees, R. Kumbe Sadler, G. Wyn
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: CT scanning, FMI image logs, rudist, bulk density, carbonate facies, core-log integration
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-58.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-58)

An algorithm extracts near-surface density data from CT-scanned cores to build FMI-type images, helping identify rudist facies in a Lower Aptian carbonate reservoir.

FMI image interpretation needs validation against core, but visual core description can miss heterogeneity. The authors developed an algorithm that converts CT numbers to bulk density, draws the largest circular region of interest near the periphery of each slice, and stacks these circumferential density strips with depth-shifted positions to form an unwrapped, FMI-like image. It was applied to eight roughly 60 ft cores from a vertical well in a Lower Aptian carbonate reservoir in Saudi Arabia, with Core #2 studied in detail. Twelve tubes with rudist-like features were rescanned at 5 mm spacing and 5 mm beam width instead of the usual 2 inch spacing. Rudists appeared as highly resistive features on FMI and matched the CT density images, allowing in-situ and displaced rudist assemblages to be distinguished and supporting facies interpretation in uncored horizontal wells.

## Stochastic Approach to Computation of Uncertainties in Petrophysical Properties

- ID: SCA1989-07, year: 1989
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: uncertainty analysis, error propagation, core porosity, formation factor, well test permeability, density log porosity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1989/SCA1989-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1989-07)

Root-mean-square error propagation equations are derived for core, log and well-test properties, with worked examples showing uncertainties in pore volume, porosity, formation factor and permeability.

Petrophysical properties carry random errors from their input variables, and the paper develops root-mean-square uncertainty equations for core, log and well-test quantities. Partial-derivative-based equations are tabulated for pore volume, bulk and grain volume, porosity, saturations, electrical properties, permeability, relative permeability, build-up permeability and log porosity. Worked examples show that a 0.1% uncertainty in all inputs gives a saturation pore volume uncertainty of ±1.67%, while 0.5% uncertainty in bulk and grain volume gives ±6.4% relative porosity uncertainty at 10% porosity, exceeding the API ±0.5 pu limit. Formation factor uncertainty is ±2.8% for 2% errors in Ro and Rw, and predicted ±4% agreed reasonably with observed ±5% repeat scatter. For density-log porosity, relative error is about twice as large at 10% porosity as at 20%, so the absolute error is about ±2 porosity units in both cases, and bulk density is the dominant contributor. The equations can also set tolerable limits on input variables, but they do not capture systematic errors.

## Deciphering Core and Log Porosity Differences and Evaluating the Scale Dependence of Core Analyses in a Complex Lithology

- ID: SCA1994-11, year: 1994
- Authors: June Gidman, Frank. J. Conner, Dennis J. Fischer
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: full-diameter vs plug permeability, minipermeameter, core-log porosity discrepancy, bioturbated mottled siltstone, density log rugosity, permeability scale-up
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-11)

Core porosity (~20%) versus log porosity (~30%) in a bioturbated West African reservoir is explained by borehole rugosity from washed-out burrows; plugs underestimate permeability below 80 mD.

Core porosities averaged about 20% while density and neutron-density log porosities averaged about 30% in a mixed siliciclastic/carbonate reservoir offshore West Africa, creating reserves uncertainty. Three new wells were cored and measured at full-diameter, 1-inch plug and minipermeameter scales, with redundant Archimedes, caliper and Boyle's Law measurements for quality control. Vugs made up under 4% of the section, so they did not explain the gap. Mottled siltstones with porous burrow fills in tight silty dolomite were heterogeneous: plug ends differed by up to three orders of magnitude, and plugs underestimated permeability below about 80 mD, so a power-law scale-up was applied. Full-diameter porosity exceeded plug porosity in carbonates by 1.6 p.u. on average. The log porosity excess coincided with burrow mottling, where washed-out burrows created small-scale borehole rugosity that the density pad read as extra porosity.

## Upscaling from Core Data to Production: Closing the Cycle. a Case Study in the Santa Barbara and Pirital Fields, Eastern Venezuela Basin

- ID: SCA2001-02, year: 2001
- Authors: Juan Carlos, Petróleos de Venezuela S.A. BARBATO, Petróleos de Venezuela S.A. SALAZAR, Universidad de Oriente
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: Pittman R45 pore throat radius, mercury injection capillary pressure, rock typing, production log analysis, flow units Lorenz plot, Winland equation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-02)

Core porosity, permeability and MICP data define pore-throat (R45) rock types that are correlated with normalized production-log flow contributions, with stronger relations in oil wells than gas/condensate wells.

The study links core-derived rock types to well production in the Santa Barbara and Pirital fields, Eastern Venezuela Basin. Routine core data from 17 wells and MICP from 11 wells were used to test Winland/Pittman pore-throat equations. The R45 equation best matched MICP radii and was used to define five rock types (mega- to nannoporous). Residual water saturation was found to be controlled by pore geometry. Production logs were normalized for differential pressure and interval thickness (flow contribution per foot, APP) and compared with normalized permeability and K/phi-based variables. A clear relation between rock quality and flow contribution was found, stronger for oil wells (28-35 °API) than for gas/condensate wells (35-41 °API), enabling estimation of well production profiles from petrophysics.

## Factors Affecting Relative Permeability Measurements for the Miocene and the Rotliegend Poorly Consolidated Sandstones

- ID: SCA2003-61, year: 2003
- Authors: Piotr Such, Poland Grzegorz Lesniak
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: relative permeability, poorly consolidated sandstone, residual water saturation, fractal dimension, hairy illite, mercury injection capillary pressure
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-61.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-61)

Steady-state gas and water relative permeability on 113 Rotliegend and Miocene sandstones shows pore space damage from clay effects and cement destruction, linked to residual water saturation and fractal dimension.

Gas reservoirs in Polish Rotliegend and Miocene sandstones require multiphase flow data, but many of these rocks are poorly consolidated and damaged by contact with reservoir water. Steady-state relative permeability tests under reservoir conditions were run on 68 Rotliegend and 45 Miocene samples, supported by mercury injection capillary pressure, fractal dimension and thin section analysis. Base relative permeability to gas reached 0.86-0.9 at most, but many samples were low, and 13 samples closed completely during water flow. Over 50% of samples showed pore space damage, and undamaged samples had gas relative permeability above 0.6. Damage in the Rotliegend came from hairy illite webs, extraction of illite/chlorite plates and swelling of mixed-layer clays (20-35 min reaction). In the Miocene, damage came from mechanical extraction of cement and grain collapse, which sharply raised residual water saturation.

## Petrophysical Sampling Strategy for a Model-Dominated User Group Using an Experimental Design Checklist Approach

- ID: SCA2001-01, year: 2001
- Authors: Patrick W.M. Corbett, David K. Potter, Dave Bowen
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: experimental design, sampling strategy, genetic petrophysics, shoreface sandstone, poroperm relationships, reservoir modelling
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-01)

Applies a 13-step experimental design checklist to plan core sampling for reservoir modellers, illustrated retrospectively with a North Sea shoreface reservoir where one parasequence represented the whole cored well.

Core sampling has traditionally been based on regular plug spacing and sample availability, whereas reservoir modellers now need petrophysical data by rock type and at model block scale. The authors adapt Robinson's experimental design checklist (consult, clarify objectives, use existing data, list variables, broad strategy, update beliefs) to core analysis and apply it retroactively to a North Sea shallow marine sandstone. A single 7.3 m (24 ft) shoreface parasequence, containing upper and lower shoreface units, was tested as representative of the 122 m (400 ft) cored section. Results showed distinct poroperm relationships for each unit, linked to quartz overgrowth in the upper shoreface and illite in the lower shoreface, with barite-filled fractures as outliers. Existing special core analysis data came only from the upper shoreface, revealing sampling bias. A staged budget (10% preliminary, 50% main trial, 40% reserve) and Genetic Petrophysics screening are recommended.

## The Use of Core Derived Quantitative Mineralogical Data to Improve Formation Evaluation

- ID: SCA1990-10EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: Kenneth Guest
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: FTIR mineralogy, grain density, Brent Group, statistical log analysis, clay volume from gamma ray, siderite
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-10EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-10EURO)

FTIR-based quantitative mineralogy (Mineralog) on Brent Group core improves deterministic and statistical (Optima) log evaluation of porosity and clay, and aids sand correlation.

Log evaluation usually ignores geological information, so mineral effects on density, neutron and gamma ray responses cause errors. The paper applies FTIR spectroscopy (Mineralog, accurate to within ±5 wt%) to a North Sea Jurassic Brent Group well and compares log-derived results with core porosity, grain density and clay. Using a constant 2.65 g/cc grain density gives clear porosity errors in siderite-rich intervals (siderite up to ~50% in the Broom sand, grain density up to ~3.3 g/cc); each 1% siderite lowers porosity by ~0.8 porosity percent. Mineralog grain density improves the match, and a mica correction improves it further (maximum grain density discrepancy 0.04 g/cc at 16% muscovite). Mineralog total clay calibrates gamma-ray Vcl, which is distorted by K-feldspar, mica and non-radioactive kaolinite. Feeding mineral volumes into the Optima statistical program gives excellent agreement with core porosity, and distinct mineral assemblages can fingerprint the four Lower Ness sands for correlation.

## A Fresh Look at Predictive Equations for Compressional Wave Velocity - Porosity

- ID: SCA1990-14EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: N. Robin Brereton, David M. McCann
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: compressional wave velocity, porosity-velocity transform, Wyllie time average, Wood equation, acoustic impedance, ODP Leg 123
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-14EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-14EURO)

Using ODP Leg 123 sediment and basalt data, the authors compare porosity-velocity models and propose a semi-empirical acoustic impedance transform that fits better than Wyllie, Wood or Wyllie-Wood.

Established porosity-velocity equations (Wyllie time average, Wood, Wyllie-Wood, Nafe-Drake, Raymer, Raiga-Clemenceau) are tested against shipboard index property and 500 kHz velocity measurements on sediments and basalts from ODP Leg 123 (Sites 765 and 766), covering porosity from near 0 to 100%. The time average equation fails over the full range, and fitting it requires unrealistically low matrix and pore water velocities (5800-6200 and 1050 m/s). The Wood equation predicts well above about 62% porosity, and a Wyllie-Wood form with porosity-weighted rigidity terms improves the fit. A new semi-empirical acoustic impedance equation, with q equal to 0.22 (Poisson's ratio 0.42), fits better over both high and low porosity. Regressing predicted on measured velocities gives slope 0.90 and R2 of 96 for the impedance equation, against slope 0.70 and R2 of 95 for the modified Wyllie-Wood.

## Sensitivity Analysis of Errors in Reserve Evaluations Due to Core and Log Measurement Inaccuracies

- ID: SCA1990-19EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: John Owens, Peter Cockcroft
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: sensitivity analysis, Archie equation, water saturation, volumetric reserves, cementation exponent, porosity uncertainty
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-19EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-19EURO)

A simple one-at-a-time ±10% sensitivity analysis of Archie parameters shows which core and log measurements most affect volumetric reserves, illustrated for Rotliegende gas and Brent oil examples.

Reserve estimates depend on water saturation, which is computed from Archie parameters (porosity, m, n, Rw, Rt). The authors propose a quick sensitivity analysis that perturbs each parameter individually by ±10% (with m=n=2) around representative values and recomputes reserves using a volumetric constant. Applied to a typical Southern North Sea Rotliegende gas field (1 Tcf assumed) and a Northern North Sea Brent oil field (100 MMbbl assumed), the results show reserves are most sensitive to porosity, then m and n, and least sensitive to Rw and Rt. In the Rotliegende case a 10% change in m changed GIIP by +14%/-17%, and n by about ±6-7%, while Rw gave about ±4%. Brent sensitivities were generally smaller, and responses to m and Rt are non-linear at larger errors. The method assumes independent parameters, clean formations or shale-corrected values, and consistent porosity definitions, and it helps prioritise coring, core analysis and logging effort.

## Core Analysis to Calibrate Well Log Interpretation

- ID: SCA1991-03EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Jan Rafdal
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: log calibration, Archie saturation exponent, shaly sand, North Sea chalk, J-function, core-log scale
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-03EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-03EURO)

Reviews how core data calibrate and verify log interpretation of lithology, porosity, saturation, permeability, net/gross and compaction, with North Sea examples and scale and uncertainty issues.

The paper reviews how core analysis is used to calibrate well-log interpretation for static and dynamic reservoir description. It covers lithology and clay mineralogy, porosity response equations, Archie saturation and shaly-sand corrections, J-function capillary pressure, net/gross, permeability, fluid contacts, recovery and rock mechanics. A North Sea chalk example shows density-log porosity and Archie Sw agreeing with core porosity (approaching 50%) and Dean-Stark saturations on average over the cored interval, despite thin laminations not resolved by the log. Brent 'mica' sands illustrate how radioactive minerals cause shaly log response and underestimated porosity unless calibrated with core. The saturation exponent can only be derived from core and depends on wettability. Scale differences between measurements and core and log representativeness are discussed, and improved standards are called for.

## Characterization of Devonian Shales with X-Ray Computed Tomography

- ID: SCA1991-22, year: 1991
- Authors: A. Ted Watson, Jim Mudra, Texas A
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: X-ray computed tomography, Devonian shale, xenon gas storage, adsorption, microfractures, dual-scan porosity
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-22.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-22)

Dual-scan xenon X-ray CT quantifies gas storage in a Cleveland Shale plug, detecting sub-resolution fractures and finding storage far above porosity.

Devonian shales are heterogeneous, with very low matrix permeability and fractures, so conventional core tests give averaged values of limited use. The authors applied a dual-scan X-ray CT method with xenon at 50 psia and room temperature to a 1.5 inch Cleveland Shale plug. Voxel-by-voxel differencing of evacuated and xenon-saturated scans gave a storage ratio (xenon attenuation in rock over bulk gas attenuation) that equals porosity unless adsorption, solution or densification occurs. Measured porosity of an adjoining sample was 6.3%, while the average storage of slice 3 was 0.15 and about a quarter of the slice reached 25%. Petrophysical models built from small, noisy core datasets are hard to make both accurate and physically interpretable. The authors present a symbolic regression workflow (correlation heatmap feature selection, genetic-programming SR, ensembling, conditional branching with max/min operators, and complexity-penalized model selection) implemented in a desktop GUI that integrates PySR and other SR packages. It was applied to 29 unconventional-reservoir core samples (28 after removing one outlier) to derive NMR-based permeability equations, which outperformed calibrated Coates and SDR models. Branching and threshold terms identified two data regimes, and PySR sometimes gave overly complex or physically inconsistent equations. For 30 carbonate samples, a PySR formation factor model gave m = φmacro + 1.9, comparable in performance to the Eureqa model, offering insight into cementation and tortuosity interdependence.

Repeated NMR and SIP measurements on Fontainebleau sandstone were non-reproducible due to amorphous silica films; weak alkaline leaching stabilized responses, revealing hidden bias in reference rocks.

Fontainebleau sandstone, a quartz reference rock, gave unstable NMR T2 distributions and SIP phase responses over repeated saturation-drying cycles, while porosity and DC resistivity stayed stable. Three strata (strongly cemented, intermediate, porous) were characterized with SEM, micro-CT, MICP, porosity-permeability, NMR and SIP before and after weak alkaline leaching (1 wt.% Na2CO3, 85 °C, 4 h). SEM showed intergranular wallpaper-like SiO2 films interpreted as amorphous silica. Leaching raised porosity from 8.4 to 9.7% and permeability from 0.1 to 21 mD in the tight strata, with little change in the porous strata. Short-T2 components (<30 ms) largely disappeared, and SIP phase scatter (e.g. 6.5–24 mrad at 1 mHz in tight strata) fell to about ±2–3 mrad. The authors recommend repeated measurements, controlled conditioning and combined multi-method interpretation, using leaching only as a diagnostic tool.

## Core-Based Geomechanical Profiling of a Continental Shale Oil Reservoir and Dynamic-to-Static Transformation Relationships of Mechanical Parameters

- ID: SCA2026-046, year: 2026

Well-site triaxial and Brazilian tests on 86 plugs from a 49 m Lucaogou shale oil interval yield geomechanical profiles and lithology-specific dynamic-to-static conversion equations for mudstone and sandstone.

Thin interbedding and heterogeneity in the lacustrine Lucaogou shale oil reservoir (Jimsar Sag) make static mechanical parameters hard to derive from logs. Eighty-six 10 mm plugs from a 49 m continuous core interval were tested in a containerized mobile rock mechanics laboratory: triaxial compression at 100 MPa confinement, Brazilian splitting, resistivity, chargeability and magnetic susceptibility, plus XRD. Triaxial strength ranged 190.76–672.26 MPa, elastic modulus 13.92–34.54 GPa, Poisson's ratio 0.12–0.36 and tensile strength 8.58–30.24 MPa, with sharp vertical fluctuations that carried into calculated in-situ stress and breakdown pressure profiles. Polynomial dynamic-to-static conversions were fitted separately for mudstone (R² 0.69–0.75) and sandstone (R² 0.74–0.82), with sandstone correlating better because of its more grain-supported, less heterogeneous structure. The small specimen size may bias absolute strengths upward, so the data are used mainly for relative variation and log calibration.
