## Developments in I-Sw Measurements

- ID: SCA1991-14EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Hans P.W. Zeelenberg, Bas A. Schlpper
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: resistivity index, continuous injection, saturation exponent, confining stress, temperature effect, RIMARC
- Cited by 8 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-14EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-14EURO)

Describes Shell's continuous injection resistivity index method and the RIMARC reservoir-condition apparatus, showing stress and temperature effects on saturation exponent n.

Resistivity index versus water saturation measurements are needed for log interpretation, and the capillary equilibrium method was too slow (about 6 weeks per sample). Shell developed an automated continuous injection (CI) technique using kerosene and brine, 70 bar confining stress, and a ~14 day drainage, with precision of about ±2% on n*. Tests at 200-304 bar stress on six sandstones showed n* changes that depended on porosity, with the largest decrease (1.58 to 1.41) in the 6.5% porosity sample. The RIMARC apparatus (up to 600 bar stress, 150°C) was checked on Bentheim sandstone and agreed with CI at room temperature. At 92°C, n was 1.70 for drainage and 1.72 for imbibition versus 1.85 in standard CI at the same net stress, attributed to a temperature effect.

## Laboratory Measurements of Capillary and Electrical Properties of Rock Samples at Reservoir Conditions : Effect of Some Parameters

- ID: SCA1990-23, year: 1990
- Authors: Daniel G. LONGERON
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: saturation exponent, cementation factor, wettability index Amott-IFP, porous-plate technique, effective overburden stress, resistivity index hysteresis
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-23)

Porous-plate resistivity and capillary pressure tests at reservoir conditions on sandstones and carbonates show n rises with stress history and oil-wetness, reaching 2.9 with crude oil.

Archie parameters m and n are usually measured at ambient conditions with simulated fluids, which can bias saturation estimates. The author adapted a porous-plate cell with four-electrode resistivity measurement to reservoir temperature, effective stress, crude oil and formation brine, and tested reproducibility (n = 2.45 in two runs). Raising effective stress from 3 to 20 MPa increased formation factor by about 15% in sandstone versus 12% in carbonate, with little effect on n (sandstone 2.08 to 2.11, carbonate 1.65 to 1.63). Drainage and imbibition resistivity indices differed by up to about 5% PV in saturation for strongly water-wet samples. Comparing refined oil with restored-state crude oil on nine samples, n increased in 7 of 9 as wettability shifted toward oil-wet, with a maximum of 2.05 to 2.90 in one sandstone, and a vuggy carbonate showed two-slope IR/Sw behaviour. The author recommends using crude oil and representative restored samples for determining n.

## A Saturation Interpretation Model for the Dielectric Constant of Shaly Sands

- ID: SCA1991-18, year: 1991
- Authors: M. T. Myers
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: dielectric dispersion, shaly sands, cation exchange capacity, Qbv, Maxwell-Wagner polarization, Hanai-Bruggeman
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-18)

Develops an empirical dielectric model (1 MHz–1 GHz) for shaly sands with clay and Maxwell-Wagner terms, showing clay dispersion scales with Qbv and is saturation independent.

The paper addresses estimating oil saturation and shaliness in fresh water shaly sands from dielectric measurements. The dielectric constant is modeled as a high-frequency limit (Hanai-Bruggeman, matrix 4.4, depolarization factor L=1/3) plus a clay double-layer polarization term and a Maxwell-Wagner geometric term. Fits on 27 clastic samples at 0.01 molal show clay dispersion scales with CEC per bulk volume (Qbv) independent of porosity, whereas Qv gives porosity-dependent slopes. The geometric term is linear in porosity and scales with frequency/brine conductivity. A 49-sample set at 0.01–0.60 molal shows the clay term decreasing with salinity and varying roughly as 1/√ω. Saturation tests with fluorinert show the clay term is saturation independent, while the geometric term decreases linearly with Sw, equivalent to a porosity or salinity change.

- ID: SCA1991-15EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Lucienne Bouvier, Sylvie M. Maquignon
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: saturation exponent, resistivity index, irreducible water saturation, bimodal pore size distribution, mercury injection, oolitic limestone
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-15EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-15EURO)

In a slightly oil-wet oolitic limestone, resistivity index shows three slopes tied to pore classes; a permeability-based law for n raises log-derived Swi to match laboratory values.

Log-derived irreducible water saturations in an oolitic limestone reservoir were far below those from laboratory capillary pressure curves. Fifteen samples were tested with porous-plate water-oil drainage with resistivity index, followed by mercury injection, and Amott/IFP wettability (index about -0.2, slightly oil-wet). Mercury curves scaled by a factor of 12 matched water-oil curves. Correlations of Swi and n with water permeability gave a new law; for example, Archie Swi of 30% becomes 46% at 100 mD, and laboratory values (69.1, 76.9%) are matched by 68.8 and 73.6% instead of 26.1 and 37.6%.

## Comparison of Saturation Exponent Data by the 'Porous Plate' and by the 'Continuous Injection' Technique with in-Situ Saturation Monitoring

- ID: SCA1992-15EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Russell Gray, Ben Trewin
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: saturation exponent, resistivity index, porous plate, continuous oil injection, X-ray saturation monitoring, end effect
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-15EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-15EURO)

Continuous oil injection with X-ray saturation monitoring is compared with porous plate for saturation exponent; they agree fairly for water-wet sandstones but injection fails for oil-wet tendency.

Saturation exponent measurements from the traditional gas/brine porous plate method were compared with a continuous oil injection technique using X-ray in-situ saturation monitoring. Four water-wet sandstones (7410 to 1.6 mD, porosity about 0.24-0.29) were tested, plus one aged sample in a secondary drainage cycle. Injection at about 0.02 cm3/hour desaturated samples over at least 21 days while resistance and X-ray saturation profiles were logged. For water-wet samples uniform saturation developed quickly and the resistivity index curves agreed fairly well, though continuous injection gave slightly higher n at low Sw, possibly due to a small end effect, as shown by a 10-element model. The aged sample showed uneven saturation from oil imbibition and an unrealistically high n until the profile became uniform. Continuous injection gives a continuous RI-Sw curve in much less time but no equilibrated capillary pressure curve.

## Resistivity Index Measurements Under Weak Capillary Forces

- ID: SCA2000-18, year: 2000
- Authors: Jos G. Maas, Niko van der Post, Krijn H. van der Gyp, John G.C. Coenen, Wim J. Looyestijn
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: resistivity index, continuous injection, Buckley-Leverett shock front, saturation exponent, steady-state X-ray saturation, capillary pressure gradient
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-18)

Shows that continuous-injection resistivity index tests can be distorted by Buckley-Leverett shock fronts under weak capillary gradients, and proposes design criteria and an X-ray steady-state alternative.

Continuous injection (CI) resistivity index measurements assume a flat saturation profile, which requires a capillary gradient strong enough to suppress a Buckley-Leverett shock front. Using an approximate analytical model and simulations, the authors show that weak capillary gradients give curved log-log I-Sw plots and saturation errors of 20 or more saturation units. They derive approximate criteria for a maximum oil injection rate (viscous vs capillary) and for gravity stability, and verify the rate criterion by simulation. They recommend checking designs against Hg-air capillary pressure and relative permeability estimates, and varying rate on the fly if curvature appears. As an alternative, an X-ray steady-state apparatus with multiple electrodes measures resistivity against local saturation, typically giving about 10 points in under two weeks and agreeing with CI where CI is valid.

## Techniques for the Measurement of Electrical Properties of Cores in the Frequency Range 10 Hz to 10 MHz

- ID: SCA1992-11, year: 1992
- Authors: Ali A. Garrouch, Mukul M. Sharma
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: complex impedance, dielectric constant, four-electrode measurement, contact impedance, tight gas sand, interfacial polarization
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-11)

Presents a corrected two-electrode and a new four-electrode technique for complex impedance and dielectric measurements of cores from 10 Hz to 10 MHz, relating critical frequency to characteristic length.

Dielectric measurements on partially saturated rocks are hampered by contact impedances and fringing in two-electrode setups. The authors measured 14 tight gas sand discs (two-electrode, HP fixture with guard electrode, contact impedance corrected using two disc thicknesses) from 10 Hz to 10 MHz. They also built a four-electrode circuit with high-impedance op-amps (200 megohm) in a Hassler-type holder, allowing 10 Hz to 1 MHz measurements at stresses up to 5000 psi with fluid flow; it matched independent 60 Hz resistivity within 5%. For tight sands, the lower critical frequency (reactance minimum) correlated with the characteristic length sqrt(K/phi), and relaxation time scaled linearly with length squared up to a limit; dielectric constant fell with increasing characteristic length, consistent with a Lima-Sharma sphere model. In Berea and sand-bentonite packs, dielectric constant decreased with increasing oil saturation, lower salinity, lower clay content, higher stress and greater oil-wetness, and data were reproducible. Four-electrode data at 0.1 MHz agreed within a factor of 2-3 with the Scott et al. empirical fit.

## Resistivity in Carbonates: New Insights

- ID: SCA2002-28, year: 2002
- Authors: M. Fleury
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: resistivity index, non-Archie behavior, carbonates, FRIM, double porosity model, NMR and mercury injection
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-28)

Double and triple porosity conductivity models, using NMR and mercury injection inputs, explain non-Archie resistivity index curves in carbonates measured continuously with FRIM, and support log calibration.

Resistivity index curves in carbonates often deviate from Archie's law, with local saturation exponents ranging from 1 to 3. Continuous RI curves down to about 4-5% water saturation were measured with the FRIM method on a Middle East packstone (ambient and reservoir conditions) and on Estaillade and Brauvillier limestones. A double porosity model (parallel networks) reproduces the flattening at low saturation; for the packstone n1=1.71, n2=0.25 and a=0.054, with f1=0.88 and Sc=0.4 from NMR and mercury injection. A triple porosity model (two networks in series plus microporosity in parallel) reproduces curves bending up at high saturation, e.g. n1=n2=1.5 for Estaillade. Microporosity, even below 10% of pore volume, provides a continuous water path with weak saturation sensitivity, and a fitted RI equation and 4th-order polynomial Sw(RI) are proposed for log calibration.

## Pore Scale Analysis of Electrical Resistivity in Complex Core Material

- Authors: M. A. Knackstedt, C. H. Arns, A. P. Sheppard, T. J. Senden, R. M. Sok, Y. Cinar, A. O. Olafuyi, W. V. Pinczewski, G. Padhy, M. Ioannidis
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: Archie cementation exponent, resistivity index, micro-CT image-based simulation, saturation exponent, carbonate microporosity, wettability
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 5
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_33.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_33)

Finite-element resistivity simulations on micro-CT images of twelve samples predict Archie m and n, matching experiment where available and revealing anisotropy, wettability effects and saturation-dependent n in carbonates.

The study addresses scatter in Archie's m and n in complex lithologies by simulating electrical resistivity directly on 3D micro-CT images (down to 2.85 µm/voxel) of twelve samples: sintered bead pack, Fontainebleau sandstones, laminated Aeolian sand, sucrosic dolomites, a model dual-porosity carbonate and two bioclastic carbonates. Bioclastic carbonates gave m≈2.3–2.35, consistent with Lucia's relation to separate macroporosity, and mixed-wet n varied strongly with saturation.

## Combined Complex Conductivity and Dielec-Tric Measurements on Core Samples

- ID: SCA1997-36, year: 1997
- Authors: Frank D. Börner
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: complex conductivity, dielectric permittivity, constant phase angle, broad band frequency dispersion, water saturation, surface area-to-porosity ratio
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-36)

Proposes a Combined Conductivity and Permittivity Model joining low- and high-frequency constant-phase-angle elements to describe rock electrical response from 1e-3 to 1e9 Hz, fitted to literature and sandstone data.

Water saturation determination from electrical or dielectric properties alone needs rock-specific calibration. The paper presents the Combined Conductivity and Permittivity Model (CCPM), a transfer function formed by a low-frequency and a high-frequency constant phase angle element in parallel, with four parameters (two amplitudes, two frequency exponents). The model was fitted to broad-band spectra of sandstones and other water-bearing rocks from the literature, and it described the data well within a considerable error range. Low-frequency parameters relate to water content, water conductivity and surface-to-pore-volume ratio. High-frequency parameters relate to water content and internal surface area and are nearly independent of water conductivity. Low-frequency exponents (1-p) were 0.007–0.03 for most silicate rocks, and high-frequency exponents n were about 0.9. A combined impedance-analyzer and network-analyzer setup for the same sample is proposed, but more experimental data are needed to validate the model.

## Saturation Exponent in Low Permeability Sandstone by Porous Plate and Centrifuge Desaturation Methods

- ID: SCA1993-07, year: 1993
- Authors: Joel D. Walls, Rock Physics, Marilyn Black, Carol Admire
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: saturation exponent, resistivity index, centrifuge desaturation, porous plate, x-ray saturation scanning, tight gas sand
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-07)

X-ray saturation scanning of tight sandstones shows centrifuge and porous plate desaturation give similar saturation exponents (average 1.38 vs 1.40), despite differing saturation gradients at high Sw.

Saturation exponent N from tight gas sands is critical for log water saturation, and centrifuge desaturation is much faster than porous plate in low-permeability rock but is often distrusted. Four Travis Peak and two Frontier samples were desaturated by both methods at 3700 psi net stress (2400 psi for Frontier) with NaI brine, while x-ray scanning tracked saturation versus position and two- and four-electrode resistivity was measured after 1 and 24 hours. Saturation profiles and N changed little between 1 and 24 hours. Centrifuge desaturation left higher Sw near the outflow end above about 50% Sw, but profiles were flat at lower Sw and controlled more by heterogeneity. Four-electrode N averaged 1.38 for centrifuge and 1.40 for porous plate, with an uncertainty of ±0.05, and N tended to rise as Sw decreased.

## Advances in Resistivity Measurements Using the Frim Method at Reservoir Conditions. Applications to Carbonates

- ID: SCA2003-31, year: 2003
- Authors: M. FLEURY
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: FRIM resistivity index, saturation exponent, non-Archie behavior, carbonate wettability, drainage-imbibition hysteresis, reservoir conditions
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-31.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-31)

Extends the FRIM continuous resistivity index method to reservoir conditions for carbonate drainage and imbibition, and proposes two semi-empirical RI-Sw models for non-Archie curves.

Resistivity index curves in carbonates are often non-Archie and hard to measure by standard capillary-equilibrium methods. The FRIM method, which uses radial electrodes that make the measurement quasi-insensitive to saturation profiles, was implemented in new apparatus at reservoir conditions (e.g. 115°C, dead oil, 30 bar net overburden, confining pressure up to 450 bar). Drainage experiments take about 10 days. Forced imbibition is done by waterflooding at stepped low-to-medium flow rates in 2-3 days, and simulations show the radial design only slightly underestimates RI, unlike ring electrodes. Carbonate samples showed saturation exponents from 1.4 to 2.75, curves bending up or down with wettability change, and strong drainage-imbibition hysteresis. Two three-parameter empirical equations were proposed to fit bending-down and bending-up RI curves.

## Effect of the Pore Structure on Resistivity Index Curves

- ID: SCA2007_34, year: 2007
- Authors: M. Han, M. Fleury, P. Levitz
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: resistivity index, saturation exponent, water film conduction, NMR T2 distribution, random walk simulation, carbonate micro-porosity
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_34)

A centrifuge-NMR-electrical method measures air-brine resistivity index curves on sandstones and carbonates; pore structure controls the saturation exponent, and water film conduction lowers n at low saturation.

The study examines how pore structure affects resistivity index (RI) curves in 3 sandstones and 10 outcrop carbonates, all water-wet. A new method combines centrifuge drainage, NMR T2 and saturation profiles, and radial-electrode resistance measurements, allowing six samples to be measured in about a week, with imbibition done spontaneously. Fontainebleau sandstone shows Archie behavior with n≈2 above 20% Sw, and n falls to about 1.5 below that. Carbonates fall into three further textures: micritic and macro-pores isolated by micro-pores have n≈1.6 with slight hysteresis; bimodal Estaillade shows a positive deviation below 40% Sw when brine is trapped in isolated micro-pores, explained by NMR T2 evolution.

## Frim: A Fast Resistivity Index Measurement Method

- ID: SCA1998-29, year: 1998
- Authors: M. FLEURY
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: resistivity index, saturation exponent, porous plate drainage, radial electrodes, semi-permeable membrane, non-uniform saturation profile
- Cited by 10 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-29)

A fast drainage resistivity index method using radial electrodes and a semi-permeable filter gives accurate Ir curves in hours to days without capillary equilibrium.

Resistivity index measurements are usually tied to slow porous-plate capillary equilibrium, while centrifuge or flooding methods suffer from saturation-profile effects. The FRIM method imposes one to three oil pressure steps on a small plug (40 mm diameter, 25 mm long) with a water-wet membrane at the outlet, measuring average saturation and radial resistance simultaneously. Experiments on an 80 mD Vosges sandstone (about 42 hours) and a 2400 mD synthetic core (about 2 hours) gave continuous Ir curves well fitted by Archie exponents of 2.05 and 2.08. 1D displacement and 3D electrical simulations show the radial (parallel-mode) measurement is insensitive to non-uniform profiles for Sw below about 0.7-0.8, but not at high Sw where percolation dominates. Two pressure steps appear optimal, and thick ceramics with higher pressure steps give similar duration and accuracy.

## Systematic Parameter Study for Formation Factor Modeling at the Pore Scale

- ID: SCA2015-050, year: 2015
- Authors: Matthias Halisch, Sven Linden, Jens-Oliver Schwarz, Sarah Hupfer, Andreas Wiegmann
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: formation factor, digital rock physics, micro-CT segmentation, voxel resolution, electrical conductivity simulation, cementation exponent
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-050.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-050)

Digital rock formation factor simulations on three rocks are shown to be highly sensitive to micro-CT voxel resolution and segmentation threshold, which strongly alter porosity and modeled F.

Numerical formation factor modeling on micro-CT images depends on scan resolution, segmentation and domain choices. Three rocks were studied: a porous carbonate (up to 48% porosity), a young tertiary sandstone and a cretaceous reservoir sandstone. The same 2 mm region was imaged at 2, 5 and 10 µm voxel size, segmented with Otsu and watershed methods, and F was computed with the GeoDict explicit-jump finite-difference solver under periodic boundary conditions. A ±2% threshold error changed porosity by ±2.5 to 6% in the carbonate, which loses intragranular voids at coarser resolution, but by less than ±2.5% in the micro-pore-free IG sandstone. Coarser resolution cut run time by a factor of about 50 for the carbonate and 130 for the sandstones, but can lead to different reservoir interpretations. Complementary SEM and mineralogical information plus high image resolution are recommended.

## Electrical Resistivity Imaging of Core Samples

- ID: SCA1990-18EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: P.D. Jackson, M.A. Lovell, C.A. Green, C.J. Evans, R. Flint, A. Forster
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: resistivity imaging, Formation MicroScanner, four-electrode array, formation factor, resistivity anisotropy, core calibration
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-18EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-18EURO)

Describes a four-electrode, multi-current-source imaging system (CORE-RES) producing high-resolution quantitative resistivity images of core, tested on schist and sandstone to calibrate FMS borehole images.

FMS borehole resistance images are high resolution but only relative, so they cannot be converted to quantitative resistivity or porosity. The authors built a computer-controlled core imaging system using a pad of 64 gold-plated point potential electrodes (4 by 16 grid, 0.2 inch spacing) and multiple feedback-controlled point current sources that generate a uniform current field. On a low-porosity metamorphosed Precambrian core compared with FMS data, resistivity ranged from 20 to 120 ohm-m (12 ohm-m saturant), showing more local variability than expected, though saturation was uncertain. In a 25% porosity Sherwood Sandstone, a carbonate-cemented altered band showed higher resistivity, and repeat scans reproduced major features with small differences attributed to pad positioning. Converting the images to formation factor showed large property variation within one core, and the system can also probe anisotropy by directing current axially or perpendicular to the core.

## A Novel Study of the Dielectric Dispersion in Porous Media in the Frequency Range 10 Hz to 10 MHz

- ID: SCA1997-25, year: 1997
- Authors: Ali A. Garrouch
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: complex impedance, dielectric dispersion, reactivity index exponent, Archie saturation exponent, double layer polarization, shaly sand
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-25)

Two- and four-electrode impedance measurements from 10 Hz to 10 MHz introduce a reactivity index exponent for saturation and show double-layer polarization controls clay-dependent dielectric dispersion.

The paper measures complex impedance of partially saturated Berea sandstone, tight-gas sands and Ottawa sand-bentonite packs (n-decane/NaCl brine) from 10 Hz to 10 MHz using two- and four-electrode setups. The Archie saturation exponent n is about 2.1 below 100 kHz but decreases at higher frequencies. A reactivity index exponent n* (from reactance ratios) is defined, with values of 3.5 at 10 kHz and 3.1 at 0.5 MHz for Berea, and for frequencies below 0.1 MHz it follows n* = 2n − p. n* can estimate saturation above 0.1 MHz where n fails. A generalized Maxwell-Wagner double-layer model shows dielectric constant scales roughly linearly with clay fraction, confirmed by tight-gas-sand data, implying double-layer polarization dominates dispersion.

## Resistivity Index Measurement Without the Porous Plate: A Desaturation Technique Based on Evaporation Produces Uniform Water Saturation Profiles and More Reliable Results for Tight North Sea Chalk

- ID: SCA2003-38, year: 2003
- Authors: N. Springer, U. Korsbech, H. K. Aage
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: resistivity index, saturation exponent, evaporation desaturation, tight chalk, 22Na tracer scanning, Archie
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-38.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-38)

An evaporation-based desaturation method using diluted brine replaces porous plates for resistivity index measurement in tight chalk, giving uniform saturation and a lower saturation exponent n of 1.73.

Porous plate drainage is very slow for tight Lower Cretaceous North Sea chalk and yields non-uniform saturation, biasing RI and n high. The authors saturate plugs with diluted brine and evaporate water until the original brine concentration is restored, which fixes Sw at 1/f, reaching Sw down to about 0.1 within a week. 22Na-spiked brine and gamma scanning of plugs showed that salt and water redistribute to a uniform profile within 5-6 days after evaporation to Sw = 17%. Salt remained mobile at Sw 13-14% but not at about 9%. Resistivity tests on 11 short 38 mm plugs with three saturation steps each gave n = 1.73 ± 0.09, well below the 2.1-2.2 used in log interpretation. Duplicate plugs showed a CV of about 10% per saturation step, and the method needs extensive sample handling.

## Non-Archie Resistivity Behavior in Model and

- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: non-Archie resistivity, vuggy carbonates, resistivity index, DDIF-NMR, mercury injection capillary pressure, dual porosity model
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 5

Drainage resistivity index measurements on real vuggy dolomites and synthetic vuggy media show non-Archie behavior; a dual-porosity model explains it qualitatively but misses low-saturation flattening.

Vuggy carbonates with matrix and vug pore populations deviate from Archie's law, and existing mixing-law and effective-medium models lack experimental validation. Drainage resistivity index was measured on three Silurian dolomites and three synthetic glass-bead media (two vuggy, one non-vuggy) using an in-house four-electrode cell with continuous humidified-air injection at 0.033 cc/min, after checking the setup on Berea sandstone. ## Resistivity Index and Capillary Pressure Characteristics of Reservoir Sandstones in Different Wettability Conditions

- ID: SCA1999-45, year: 1999

Multi-salinity clay correction raised a from 5.4 to 7.9 and m from 1.31 to 1.37, and a local NMR T2 correlation with resistivity index was built but not transferable to other samples.
