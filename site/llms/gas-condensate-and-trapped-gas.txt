## Capillary End Effects and Gas Production from Low Permeability Formations

- ID: SCA2005-26, year: 2005
- Authors: Richard Christiansen, Carolina Mayoral, Carlos Pereira
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: water block, capillary end effect, low permeability gas, hydraulic fracturing leakoff, Brooks-Corey relative permeability, liquid loading
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-26)

Simulations with 1D in-house and commercial reservoir models show water blocks from fracturing leakoff and imbibition cut gas production in low permeability formations by up to about 30%.

Water that leaks off during hydraulic fracturing or imbibes from liquid-loaded wells can form water blocks that impair gas productivity in low permeability formations. The authors used an in-house 1D incompressible simulator with capillary end effects (Bentsen-Anli capillary pressure, Brooks-Corey relative permeability) and the Eclipse 100 commercial simulator with radial and Cartesian fractured models, including a water vaporization workaround. In the 1D base case the flow-rate ratio peaks near 0.7 after five days and then declines slowly. It falls with lower drawdown, higher gas exponent (ng=4 gives a maximum ratio of 0.5), higher Pcs and deeper invasion. In radial-model results, 15-year cumulative gas loss was 32%, 20% and 5% for 0.1, 1 and 10 md. Loss rose with Brooks-Corey exponents (5–27%), and vaporization reduced loss from 24% to 20%. Capillary-driven flow is estimated to equal or exceed the mobility term in low permeability rock.

## Laboratory Measurement of the Residual Gas Saturation

- ID: SCA1991-23EURO, year: 1991
- Authors: Jean DELCLAUD
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual gas saturation, gas trapping, trapped gas diffusion, spontaneous imbibition, waterflooding, Land correlation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1991/1-SCA1991-23EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1991-23EURO)

Compares waterflood and capillary-rise methods for residual gas saturation in poorly consolidated sandstones, showing trapped-gas diffusion matters at ambient but not reservoir conditions, and proposes simplified procedures.

Residual gas saturation (Sgr) behind a water front was measured for a North Sea gas field using unconsolidated to poorly consolidated sandstones and Berea samples. Waterflood experiments at ambient and reservoir conditions (156 bar, 60°C) and spontaneous capillary-rise imbibition tests were cross-checked. Sgr was independent of core length (27-56 cm), injection rate and surface tension, but strongly dependent on connate water saturation, consistent with Land's relation. Waterflood and imbibition Sgr agreed (27.0% vs 26.8% PV), and laboratory values matched openhole resistivity log data. Trapped gas diffusion during imbibition at ambient conditions reduced gas saturation from 33% to 2.6% in 90 days, scaled as 1/sqrt(P), was disturbed by liquid flow, and was negligible at reservoir conditions. The author recommends waterflooding after 1-2 pore volumes or imbibition before the diffusion period.

## Distribution of Trapped Gas Saturation in Heterogeneous Sandstone Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2001-14, year: 2001
- Authors: SUZANNE Karine, HAMON Gérald, BILLIOTTE Joël, TROCME Vincent
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: maximum residual gas saturation, spontaneous imbibition, Fontainebleau sandstone, clay content, gas trapping, porosity trends
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-14)

About 360 spontaneous imbibition measurements on reservoir and Fontainebleau sandstones show maximum trapped gas saturation is controlled by porosity and clay content, with clay lowering Sgrm.

Maximum residual gas saturation (Sgrm) is key for estimating gas recovery under aquifer invasion, but it varies strongly in heterogeneous reservoirs. About 360 plugs from three gas reservoirs (two Far East, one West Africa) and Fontainebleau outcrops were measured by spontaneous imbibition of Isopar L into dry samples, with porosity, permeability, formation factor, XRD and thin sections. Sgrm ranged from 5 to 85% over porosities of 6 to 24%. Below about 14% porosity, Sgrm increases with decreasing porosity for clay-free Fontainebleau but decreases for shaly reservoir sandstones, and the trends merge near 25% at higher porosity. Regressions showed that porosity and clay content control Sgrm (R² about 0.70), with more clay giving lower Sgrm. No relation was found with clay type, grain density, formation factor or cementation factor.

## Depressurization Under Tertiary Conditions in the Near-Wellbore Region: Experiments, Visualization and Radial Flow Simulations

- ID: SCA2003-15, year: 2003
- Authors: P. Egermann, S. Banini, O. Vizika
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: depressurization, critical gas saturation, gas relative permeability, tertiary conditions, micromodel visualization, radial flow simulation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-15.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-15)

Core, micromodel and radial simulations show tertiary depressurization gives lower gas relative permeability than secondary, and Sgc varies with distance from the wellbore.

The paper studies solution-gas depressurization of waterflood residual oil in the near-wellbore region. Two coreflood experiments on Vosges sandstone (58 mD, C1C7 oil, brine injected at 30 cc/h while outlet pressure dropped below the 21 bar bubble point) were monitored by CT saturation profiles and history-matched with a depletion code to obtain Sgc and krg. Incremental oil of 35-42% OOIP was produced, and krg under tertiary conditions was around 1e-4, lower than under secondary conditions and consistent with literature. Micromodel visualization showed a double connection process (oil ganglia reconnection and gas connection) explaining the low krg. A radial numerical experiment gave Sgc of 26% near the outlet falling to 20% at the gas front, versus a uniform 24% in linear flow.

## Gas Phase Relative Permeability Characterization on Tight Gas Samples

- ID: SCA2011-13, year: 2011
- Authors: Y. Wang, Z. Chen, V. Morah, R. J. Knabe, M. Appel
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas relative permeability, pressure falloff, tight gas, Corey exponent, evaporation method, Klinkenberg slip factor
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-13)

A toluene evaporation plus nitrogen pressure-falloff method measures gas relative permeability on tight cores quickly; about 30 cores fit Corey exponents of ~2 (drainage) and ~3 (imbibition).

Gas relative permeability in tight gas cores is hard to measure with steady-state methods because they are slow and uncertain. The authors vary toluene saturation (mimicking water) by evaporation, determine it by mass balance, and measure nitrogen effective permeability with an unsteady-state pressure falloff technique. About 30 plugs from Wyoming and the North Sea (kabs 0.0006–0.3 mD, porosity 3–12%) were tested for drainage and imbibition, and the curves follow Corey models with exponents of ~2 for drainage and ~3 for imbibition. Critical gas saturation is uncertain (±0.05 at ~0.01 mD, ±0.10 at ~0.001 mD). Numerical history matching shows single-phase interpretation holds for Sw ≤ 0.5, while at Sw ≥ 0.6 mobile liquid and capillary end effects raise uncertainty. The slip factor b was lower at Sw = 0.2 (102 psi) than for the dry core (141 psi), which affects the fitted Sgc.

## Residual Gas Saturation of Sample Originally at Residual Water Saturation in Heterogeneous Sandstone Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2003-14, year: 2003
- Authors: SUZANNE Karine, HAMON Gérald, BILLIOTTE Joël, TROCME Vincent
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual gas saturation, Land's law, spontaneous imbibition, controlled evaporation, irreducible water saturation, NMR clay bound water
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-14)

Sixty sandstone samples show residual gas saturation at irreducible water equals maximum trapped gas saturation, controlled by rock quality rather than Swir, so Land's law extrapolation is invalid.

Residual gas saturation at irreducible water saturation (Sgr@Swir) was studied on sixty sandstone samples from two Far East gas reservoirs and Fontainebleau outcrops (porosity 0.06-0.25, permeability 0.1-3000 mD). Initial gas saturation was set by controlled evaporation of Isopar L, followed by spontaneous imbibition. NMR and CT scans showed homogeneous fluid distribution after evaporation, and Sgr-Sgi curves matched capillary desorption results on eight samples. Sgr@Swir ranged 0.05-0.75, was close to SgrM regardless of Swir, and showed the same opposite porosity trends, linked to clay-bound water, microporosity and pore size. Land's law applied to estimate Sgr@Swir gave a mean error near 0.04 and maximum near 0.08, so taking SgrM as Sgr@Swir is the better estimate.

## Mobilisation of Trapped Gas from Below the Gas-Water Contact

- ID: SCA2004-29, year: 2004
- Authors: A. Cable, D. Mogford, M. Wannell
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: trapped gas saturation, depressurisation, critical gas saturation, in-situ saturation monitoring, gas-brine relative permeability, filamentous illite
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-29)

Depressurisation of a 3 mD reservoir core composite with trapped gas at 117 barg showed gas mobilised after only 6-7% expansion, with extremely low relative permeabilities.

Gas fields in the East Irish Sea have a residual gas leg below the contact, and no gas flowed in field tests. A 32 cm four-plug composite (about 3.2 mD, 20.4% porosity) was taken to Swi of 0.29 PV and waterflooded at about 0.5 ft/day to trapped gas saturations of 0.47 and 0.41 PV at 117 barg. Two depressurisation experiments were monitored with in-situ saturation monitoring (ISSM). Critical gas saturation was only 0.03 PV above Sgt (6-7% expansion), and the first experiment recovered 70% of trapped gas when depressurised from 117 to 34 barg. Steady-state tests gave gas and brine relative permeabilities of about 0.01 where krg/krw=1, and the curves were history-matched by core flood simulation. Clay sensitivity (filamentous illite) and an effective gas permeability at Swi (6.64 mD) about twice the brine permeability were noted.

## Measurement of Relevant Gas Condensate Relative Permeability Data for Well Deliverability Predictions for a Deep Marine Sandstone Reservoir

- ID: SCA2003-33, year: 2003
- Authors: P. Ayyalasomayajula, N. Silpngarmlers, J. Berroteran, J. Sheffield, J. Kamath
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas condensate relative permeability, capillary number, krg/kro, well deliverability, steady-state coreflood, condensate blockage
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-33.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-33)

Steady-state gas condensate corefloods on four deep marine sandstones give krg as a function of krg/kro and capillary number, and the data significantly reduce simulated well productivity.

Well deliverability in gas condensate reservoirs depends on krg = f(krg/kro, Nc), for which little published data exist. Steady-state corefloods were run at 120°F on four Miocene sandstone samples (3 to 51 mD) using a three-component synthetic gas condensate designed to match the reservoir fluid's relative oil volume and viscosity ratio, covering krg/kro from 1 to 50 at several capillary numbers. Krg was found to be independent of absolute permeability at a given krg/kro, attributed to similar pore throat size distributions, and was lower than published North Sea data for a 20 mD rock. Coreflood simulations with mercury-injection capillary pressure showed negligible capillary end effects. Single-well compositional simulations with the measured data showed a significant productivity index decline from condensate blockage, with a sharp initial drop followed by gradual decline.

## Effect of Capillary, Viscous and Gravity Forces on Gas Condensate Mobility

- ID: SCA2002-24, year: 2002
- Authors: F. Kalaydjian
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: gas condensate relative permeability, capillary number, Bond number, fractal pore structure, critical condensate saturation, spreading coefficient
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-24)

A fractal capillary-bundle model predicts gas-condensate relative permeability and critical condensate saturation from capillary and Bond numbers, matching literature data without adjustable parameters.

Gas-condensate relative permeability and critical condensate saturation depend on interfacial tension, flow rate, gravity, wetting and pore structure, which simulators capture poorly. The authors model the pore space as a bundle of capillary tubes with fractal cross-section (fractal dimension DL) and use a Dullien-type capillary number CA that includes pore size. A threshold condensate saturation Stc, below which flow is capillary dominated, is derived as a function of CA and DL. Kr curves straighten as CA increases, and the model reproduces published near-critical data (Schechter and Haynes, Clashach sandstone, CA 12-400, DL=1.65; Bardon and Longeron, Fontainebleau sandstone, CA 2.5-168, DL=1.3). A Bond number-based expression gives the critical condensate saturation for spreading and non-spreading condensate. Trapping increases with fractal dimension and with decreasing spreading coefficient.

## A New Method to Determine Critical Gas Saturation and Relative Permeability During Depressurization in the Near-Wellbore Region

- ID: SCA2000-36, year: 2000
- Authors: P. Egermann, O. Vizika
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: critical gas saturation, solution gas drive, depressurization, CT scanner saturation, gas relative permeability, history matching
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-36)

Dynamic drawdown corefloods with CT gas saturation monitoring and history matching give near-wellbore Sgc and Kr; solution-gas Krg is orders of magnitude below gas-injection values.

Near-wellbore depressurization is studied by injecting undersaturated C1/C7 oil at 70 cc/h through a 38 cm Palatinat sandstone core (3.2 mD) while lowering outlet pressure below the bubble point, with CT-scanner gas saturation profiles and pressure records. Four experiments were run, two fully oil saturated and two at irreducible water (Swi 48-50%), with different drawdowns. History matching with a standard simulator (Exp 1) and an in-house nucleation-diffusion code with bubble mobilization criteria gave Kr and Sgc. Exp 1 showed bubble connection with a saturation jump of 24% equal to Sgc. Lower drawdown or higher gradient (Exps 2-4) showed dispersed bubble mobilization with a threshold pressure of 0.17 bar. Solution-gas Kro is higher and Krg several orders of magnitude lower than gas-injection curves. Sgc from an equivalent depletion rate of 6.6 bar/h extrapolates to 22%, close to the measured 24%.

## Experimental Techniques for the Measurement of Relative Permeability and in-Situ Saturation in Gas Condensate Near Well Bore and Drainage Studies

- ID: SCA1999-28, year: 1999
- Authors: Andrew Cable, Robert Mott, Mike Spearing
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: gas condensate relative permeability, capillary number, pseudo steady-state method, x-ray saturation monitoring, non-Darcy flow, condensate drainage
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-28)

Describes a pseudo steady-state high-rate gas condensate relative permeability method and x-ray in-situ saturation technique, showing mobility rises with capillary number and condensate drainage occurs in a long core.

Condensate banking reduces gas well deliverability, but high capillary number near the well may improve mobility. The authors applied a pseudo steady-state method (Fevang and Whitson) to a North Sea sandstone core (102 md brine permeability) with a 5-component synthetic fluid at reservoir pressure and temperature, measuring krg versus krg/kro at rates above 10 L/h. Single-phase tests showed non-Darcy flow (b = 4.8e8 1/m, 69 md), while two-phase tests at 170-224 bara showed differential pressure falling disproportionately with rate, i.e. mobility increasing with capillary number. Steady state was reached in under 0.5 hour, though large fluid volumes (5-10 L) are needed. An x-ray scanner was commissioned in a long vertical Clashach core condensate drainage test with iodo-decane dopant, revealing drainage and an outlet condensate bank over 16 days that PVT-cell volumes missed. Predicted undoped 2-phase saturation accuracy is about 2% PV.

## Relative Permeabilities for the Production of Solution Gas from Waterflood Residual Oil

- ID: SCA1998-17, year: 1998
- Authors: C.A. Grattoni, R.I. Hawes, R.A. Dawe
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: solution gas drive, gas relative permeability, critical gas saturation, waterflood residual oil, glass micromodel, depressurisation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-17.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-17)

Heating-driven solution gas release in a beadpack gave gas and water relative permeabilities far below Corey predictions; micromodels show unstable, intermittent gas filaments as the cause.

Gas relative permeability after critical gas saturation is poorly known for depressurisation of waterflooded reservoirs. Gas was released by heating pentane dissolved in heavy paraffin in a water-wet glass beadpack (6 D, 38.4 cm3 pore volume) at residual oil, with a 2 °C/hr heating rate. Gas became mobile at about 10% saturation and rose to 23%. Water relative permeabilities were of order 10^-2 and gas values 10^-5 to 10^-3, up to an order of magnitude below a renormalised Corey curve, consistent with Fishlock et al.'s core depressurisation data. Micromodel videos showed gas filaments repeatedly rupturing, being invaded by water and reforming, reducing effective flow area; network models would need algorithms for this cycle.

## Water-Gas Imbibition Relative Permeability: Literature Review, Direct versus Indirect Methods and Experimental Recommendations

- ID: SCA2022-24, year: 2022
- Authors: Fabrice Pairoys, Cyril Caubit
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual gas saturation, Brooks-Corey, Land hysteresis model, water-gas imbibition relative permeability, steady-state simulation, porous plate capillary pressure
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 6
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2022/SCA2022-24.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2022-24)

Reviews residual gas saturation and shows a Brooks-Corey plus Land approach matches measured water-gas imbibition endpoints in sandstone, while simulations show steady-state imbibition Kr is impractical in water-wet rock.

Gas displacement by water in aquifer-driven gas reservoirs is controlled mainly by residual gas saturation Sgr and the water endpoint Krw at Sgr rather than curve shape. The paper reviews Sgr measurement methods and the effects of Sgi, porosity, clay, rate, and wettability on Sgr. Seven clean sandstone RT1 plugs (porosity ~23%, 143-602 mD) underwent N2/brine porous-plate drainage and low-rate waterfloods at 3145 psi confining pressure. Brooks-Corey (λ=0.476) with Land's C=3.2 gave Krw Max of 0.142 versus 0.145 measured on average, and Sgr by material balance agreed with Dean-Stark within ±1 s.u. CYDAR 1D simulations of steady-state tests showed more than 50% of the gas is produced at the first fractional flow, dP is very low, and Kr points are only definable for 0.585<Sw<0.798, so full steady-state curves are impractical in strongly water-wet rock.

## A joint methodical case study for understanding severe formation clogginging processes within a highly utilized geothermal reservoir in the Pannonian Basin of Hungary

- ID: SCA2024_1014, year: 2024
- Authors: Rita Mwendia Njeru, Matthias Halisch, Arne Jacob, Andreas Weber, Péter Koroncz, Ferenc Fedor, János Szanyi
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: formation clogging, geothermal reinjection, micro-CT, digital rock clogging simulation, core flooding, pore throat size
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1014.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1014)

Core-flooding clogging tests, micro-CT and GeoDict pore-scale simulations on natural and artificial Dunántúl sandstone show natural samples clog irreversibly and shallowly, while artificial ones recover.

Reinjection wells in the Dunántúl Group geothermal sandstone of the Pannonian Basin clog quickly, and little is known of the pore-scale causes. Natural cores (27-31% porosity, 60-400 mD) and artificial cemented-sand proxies (30% porosity, ~320-370 mD) were flooded with a 0.2% calcium metasilicate suspension (10-17 µm) in 2% KCl at 4 ml/min. Pressure-drop impedance J(tD) showed stepwise, unrecovered permeability loss in natural samples, whereas artificial samples clogged initially and then regained permeability, suggesting grain rearrangement or cement dissolution. Post-test micro-CT porosity profiles showed deep infiltration (about two-thirds of length) in artificial samples but shallow infiltration (about 4-10 mm) in natural ones. GeoDict simulations reproduced this: artificial samples clogged a few dominant flow paths 1-1.5 mm deep with a flow velocity drop of a factor of 7, while natural samples clogged homogeneously within about 0.5 mm of the inlet.

## Development of in-Situ Measurements to Determine Reservoir Condition Critical Gas Saturations During Depressurisation

- ID: SCA2000-37, year: 2000
- Authors: Peter Naylor, David Mogford, Robert Smith
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: critical gas saturation, depressurisation, gamma-ray tracer saturation, volatile oil, reservoir condition coreflood, tracer adsorption
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-37)

Describes reservoir-condition depressurisation coreflood methods using gamma tracers, a PTFE/aluminium sleeve and liquid-piston cell to measure in-situ three-phase saturations and critical gas saturations (0.03–0.21).

Depressurisation of volatile, CO2-rich oils (up to 22 mole%) was studied in four reservoir-condition coreflood experiments (Miller sandstone, South Brae conglomerate) at up to 415 bara and 123°C, with depressurisation durations from 5 to 141 days. Three-phase in-situ saturations were measured using gamma-emitting tracers: Fe-59 ferrocene in the oil, and Cs-137 CsCl in the brine after Co-58 cobalticyanide failed through corrosion and precipitation. Critical gas saturations were 0.03 to 0.21 (about 0.025 in Experiment 4), and distributed saturation data reduced uncertainty in fitted relative permeabilities.

In situ x-ray saturation profiles in a single chalk core test give trapped versus initial gas saturation trends matching the Land Correlation.

Defining trapped versus initial gas saturation normally needs many tests with different initial gas saturations. Five tests on water-wet chalk cores (34-38% porosity, 1-7 mD) were run: four with live-fluid depressurization at 121-130 °C to evolve gas, and a fifth gas-then-brine flood at 79 °C. ## Comparison of Gas-Condensate Relative Permeability Using Live Fluid vs. Model Fluids

- ID: SCA2004-09, year: 2004
- Authors: N. R. Nagarajan, M. M. Honarpour, K. Sampath
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas-condensate relative permeability, live reservoir fluid, steady-state method, critical condensate saturation, model fluids, initial water saturation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-09)

Steady-state gas-condensate relative permeability tests in sandstone and carbonate composites show live reservoir fluids give lower gas and condensate krel than model fluids; initial water raises condensate krel.

Well deliverability predictions for gas-condensate reservoirs depend on relative permeability, usually measured with model fluids at low pressure and temperature. The authors built an apparatus for steady-state co-injection tests with live fluids at up to 10,000 psi pore pressure and 250 °F, with saturation from chromatography and meniscus imaging. ## Determination of Critical Gas Saturation and Relative Permeabilities Relevant to the Depressurisation of the Statfjord Field

- ID: SCA2004-33, year: 2004
- Authors: E.B. Petersen Jr, G.S. Agaev, B. Palatnik, J.K. Ringen, P.E. Øren, K.O. Vatne
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: critical gas saturation, depressurisation, gas relative permeability, history matching, mixed wettability, Statfjord
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-33.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-33)

Eighteen reservoir-condition depletion experiments on Statfjord Brent cores give critical gas saturation and gas relative permeability, history matched and tested in full-field simulation of depressurisation.

Depressurisation of the Statfjord Field requires critical gas saturation and gas relative permeability under virgin and waterflooded conditions. Eighteen depletion experiments were run on two composite Brent Group cores with recombined reservoir fluid at 91.1 °C and 271.5 bar bubble point. Depletion rates were 0.33, 0.86 and 2.1 bar/h, at Swi, after limited waterflood and at Sor. One-dimensional black-oil history matching gave gas Corey exponents of 3.3-3.5 for virgin and 3.5 for waterflooded conditions. Full-field simulations showed gas production depends strongly on critical gas saturation and krg, and that rock curves require upscaling.

## Measurements of Residual Gas Saturation Under Ambient Conditions

- ID: SCA2001-33, year: 2001
- Authors: Minghua Ding, Apostolos Kantzas
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual gas saturation, spontaneous imbibition, Land model, Amott index, forced imbibition, gas-water wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-33.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-33)

Automated spontaneous and forced imbibition tests on 27 plugs from four reservoirs show high residual gas saturation, dependent on initial water saturation and wettability, and only partly following Land's model.

Residual gas saturation to water influx is poorly documented yet critical for gas reserves above aquifers and for storage reservoirs. Twenty-seven plugs from four reservoirs (two North Africa sandstones, a West Canada sandstone and a carbonate) underwent primary and secondary spontaneous imbibition, followed by forced brine flooding at 2 cm3/hr, using an automated weight-recording imbibition apparatus.
