## Computer simulation of Special Core Analysis (SCAL) flow experiments shared on the Internet

- ID: SCA1997-19, year: 1997
- Authors: J.G. Maas, A.M. Schulte
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: SCAL simulation, capillary end-effect, history matching, relative permeability, centrifuge, unsteady-state
- Cited by 18 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-19)

Describes SCORES, a web-accessible Shell simulator for unsteady-state, steady-state and centrifuge SCAL experiments, and how history matching resolves relative permeability and capillary pressure interference.

Capillary end-effects and the interference between relative permeability and capillary pressure can bias SCAL results, with residual oil saturation overestimated by up to about 10 saturation units. Shell developed SCORES, a 1-D simplified version of its MoReS reservoir simulator, offered free through a WWW interface that returns results by e-mail as spreadsheets. The paper details the gridding, entrance/exit blocks, fluid inputs and outputs for USS, steady-state and centrifuge (including footbath, RPM table and gravity handling) experiments. It explains that raw JBN or Hassler-Brunner relative permeabilities reproduce production with zero capillary pressure (a 'trivial match') but not saturation profiles. The authors recommend history matching a (U)SS run, a single-speed centrifuge run and a multi-speed centrifuge run with one consistent relative permeability and capillary pressure data set.

## A New Versatile Relative Permeability Correlation

- ID: SCA2005-32, year: 2005
- Authors: Frode Lomeland, Einar Ebeltoft, Wibeke Hammervold Thomas
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: LET correlation, relative permeability, steady-state experiment, history matching, Corey correlation, s-shaped oil relative permeability
- Cited by 12 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-32)

Proposes the 3-parameter LET relative permeability correlation, showing it history-matches steady-state coreflood data where Corey and Chierici fail, and changes full-field water production forecasts.

Standard correlations such as Corey and Chierici lack the flexibility to represent relative permeability over the full saturation range, including s-shaped oil curves. The authors propose the LET correlation, a rational function with parameters L, E and T that control the lower part, the slope position and the upper part of the curve. It was implemented in the Sendra core flow simulator and used to history match differential pressure and production for two steady-state reservoir-condition experiments on North Sea composite cores. Corey and Chierici could not match either data set, while LET matched both and reproduced the s-behaviour of oil relative permeability in case 1 while remaining smooth. In a field model with a thin oil zone, replacing Corey with LET for the oil phase cut simulated well water production to about half over the first 4 years and removed the mismatch with well test data.

## Open Source Simulator Dumux Available for

- ID: SCA2011-08, year: 2011
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: DuMux, SCAL simulation, porous plate, continuous injection, resistivity index, capillary end effect
- Cited by 12 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-08)

The open-source DuMux simulator is adapted to simulate SCAL experiments (USS, SS, centrifuge, porous plate, continuous injection), validated against analytical solutions and MoReS, and used to analyze porous plate and CI measurements.

Relative permeability and capillary pressure measurements interfere through capillary end effects, so residual oil from lab data can be 10-15 saturation units too high; forward simulation is needed to separate them. DuMux, an open-source DUNE-based simulator, was adapted to simulate USS (imbibition and drainage), steady-state, single- and multi-speed centrifuge, porous plate (PP) and continuous injection (CI) experiments, including resistivity index, with a free web interface called SCORES. Validation against Darcy and Buckley-Leverett solutions was excellent, and centrifuge results matched MoReS within 5%. Differences were traced mainly to table interpolation, which shifted production curves by about 3%. Simulation of a PP experiment showed that non-equilibrium gives too high Scw and Pc at low Sw, and that saturation profiles can become heterogeneous with oil building up at the plate. Synthetic CI cases showed strong injection-rate dependence of differential pressure and resistivity: at high rate a constant saturation exponent of 2 appears strongly curved.

## Simultaneous Determination of Relative Permeability and Capillary Pressure Using Data from Several Experiments

- ID: SCA2004-17, year: 2004
- Authors: A. Sylte, E. Ebeltoft, E.B. Petersen
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: history matching, relative permeability, capillary pressure, multi-speed centrifuge, steady state, covariance analysis
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 5
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-17.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-17)

A multi-scenario history-matching feature in the Sendra simulator jointly fits steady state, unsteady state and centrifuge data to one set of relative permeability and capillary pressure curves, improving accuracy.

Single-experiment history matching gives accurate relative permeability and capillary pressure only in limited saturation ranges. The authors extend the Sendra core flow simulator with a multi-scenario feature that fits several experiments (steady state, unsteady state, single- and multi-speed centrifuge) simultaneously to one set of flow functions. A synthetic study with linearized covariance analysis and B-spline curves shows that the combined interpretation narrows confidence intervals for relative permeability across the saturation range, while capillary pressure is mainly controlled by the multi-speed centrifuge data. When two cores truly have different relative permeabilities, the result is an intermediate curve that is pulled toward the experiment with more data or lower error. Two North Sea water-displacing-oil cases, using Chierici or Corey relative permeability and extended Brooks-Corey capillary pressure, reconciled all experimental data with a single set of curves.

## New Coreflood Simulator Based on Independent Treatment of in-Situ Saturation and Pressure Data

- ID: SCA2002-07, year: 2002
- Authors: David J Element, Stephen G Goodyear
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: coreflood simulation, genetic algorithm, fractional flow, capillary end effect, in-situ saturation profiles, JBN method
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-07)

A coreflood simulator treats saturation and pressure data separately, using observed outlet saturation and a genetic algorithm to derive relative permeability; multi-rate floods with rate steps under 5x work best.

Capillary end effects make JBN interpretation of waterfloods underestimate recovery and suppress relative permeability. The authors recast Darcy's equations into fractional flow, end-effect and water mobility functions, so regression splits into three independent stages and uses the observed outlet saturation history as boundary condition. A genetic algorithm (population 50-100, several hundred generations) was validated on a noise-free synthetic multi-rate flood. It was then applied to a four-rate waterflood on a 6 cm carbonate plug (29% porosity, 14.8 md) with 2 mm resolution saturation profiles, excluding the non-uniform inlet 2 cm; the result gave higher recovery than JBN and a capillary pressure shape whose zero crossing is not uniquely defined. Synthetic design tests showed two rates (4 and 400 ml/h) gave a poorer fractional flow curve than four rates (4, 20, 100, 400 ml/h), and rate steps should not exceed a factor of five to ensure saturation overlap.

## Accuracy of Gas - Oil Relative Permeability from Two-Phase Flow Experiments

- ID: SCA1997-07, year: 1997
- Authors: M. Aarra
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas-oil relative permeability, centrifuge drainage, Bond number, capillary number, constant pressure gradient, parameter estimation
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-07)

Gas-oil relative permeability from long-core gas injection, constant pressure gradient and centrifuge drainage varies with Bond and capillary number, a dependence not explained by capillary end-effects alone.

Gas-oil drainage relative permeability is often calculated neglecting capillary forces, and the resulting errors are rarely discussed. Berea and Bentheimer sandstone cores (about 100 to over 2,000 mD) were tested with three methods. These were long-core gravity-oriented gas injection at 0.85, 1.43 and 8 times the critical rate with X-ray saturation scanning, constant pressure gradient gas injection, and centrifuge drainage at 500-2500 rpm. Higher gas rate gave earlier breakthrough, a more dispersed front and higher gas relative permeability, while oil relative permeability changed less. In centrifuge tests oil kr rose with rpm (Bond number 1e-7 to 1e-3), with a critical Bond number of about 6e-5 to 1e-4 depending on permeability. Simulations including capillary pressure still gave different kro curves at 500 and 2500 rpm, so the variation is not only an end-effect. Covariance analysis showed kr is well defined except near the endpoint saturations.

## Rate Selection for Waterflooding of Intermediate Wet Cores

- ID: SCA2001-20, year: 2001
- Authors: Arne Skauge, Trond Thorsen, Andre Sylte
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: waterflood rate effect, intermediate wettability, inverse relative permeability estimation, capillary pressure, USBM wettability index, covariance confidence intervals
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-20)

Unsteady-state waterfloods at several rates on intermediate-wet sandstone cores show relative permeability and estimated Pc vary with rate; low-rate floods history-matched with Pc are recommended.

The paper examines how flow rate affects water-oil relative permeability in intermediate-wet sandstone cores (20-600 mD). Each core underwent a USBM/Amott wettability test, then several waterfloods at different rates with cleaning and resaturation between floods, and a repeat spontaneous imbibition test confirmed wettability was preserved. Relative permeability was derived by analytical (JBN) and simulation methods, with the best matches from inverse estimation of Pc and kr simultaneously using splines, with confidence intervals from covariance analysis. Water relative permeability increased systematically with rate, outside confidence intervals, while oil relative permeability varied more complexly. Estimated Pc differed from measured static imbibition Pc even at high rate, and analytical kro was underestimated, so low-rate floods analysed by simulation including Pc are recommended.

## The Significance of Violated Assumptions on Core Analysis Results

- ID: SCA1999-31, year: 1999
- Authors: J.E. Nordtvedt, E. Ebeltoft, K. Kolltveit, E.B. Petersen, A. Sylte, R. Valestrand
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: JBN method, Hagoort centrifuge method, capillary effects, relative permeability, composite core, residual oil saturation
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-31.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-31)

Simulated unsteady-state, centrifuge and steady-state data show that neglecting capillary pressure or permeability heterogeneity biases JBN, Hagoort and composite-core relative permeability estimates, creating apparent rate dependency.

Traditional relative permeability interpretation assumes negligible capillary pressure and homogeneous cores, which are often violated. The authors generated synthetic unsteady-state, gas/oil centrifuge and steady-state composite-core data with a fully implicit simulator using known relative permeability and capillary pressure functions. JBN interpretation of unsteady-state data at 0.5–20 ml/min and Hagoort interpretation of centrifuge data at several RPM gave curves that absorb capillary effects, appearing rate and RPM dependent and overestimating residual oil saturation, frequently by about 10 saturation units. The Rapoport-Leas criterion was not sufficient to avoid the effect. For a three-sample composite core (200, 100, 50 mD) with 10% local permeability variation, using one harmonic-mean permeability gave relative permeabilities deviating over the whole saturation range, whereas using the harmonic mean of each sample gave nearly true curves.

## The Uniqueness and Accuracy of Porous Media Multiphase Properties Estimated from Displacement Experiments

- ID: SCA1997-09, year: 1997
- Authors: A.A. Grimstad, K. Kolltveit, J.E. Nordtvedt, A.T. Watson, T. Mannseth, A. Sylte
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: relative permeability estimation, inverse problem, B-spline regression, chi-square criterion, runs test, confidence intervals
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-09)

Proposes chi-square and runs-test criteria to validate relative permeability estimates from displacement data; shows Corey and Chierici fits fail while adaptive B-splines pass.

The paper addresses the ill-conditioned inverse problem of estimating relative permeability and capillary pressure from displacement experiments. It proposes two statistical criteria for accepting an estimate: the weighted least-squares objective J must be consistent with a chi-square distribution, and the number of runs in the residuals must lie within a confidence interval of m/2. In a simulated multirate unsteady-state drainage example (800 data points), Corey and Chierici fits gave J of about 20,000 and 1,500 against an expected value of about 800, and run counts of 132 and 316 against an expected 400, so both fail. A regression-based quadratic B-spline method, adding parameters stepwise, met the criteria at step 8, and linearized covariance analysis gave confidence bands containing the true curves. A second example shows that constant-rate production-only data with negligible capillary pressure determine only the ratio kro/krw, so the individual curves cannot be found.

## Capillary End Effects in Coreflood Calculations

- ID: SCA1996-34, year: 1996
- Authors: David D. Huang, Matt M. Honarpour
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: capillary end effect, Corey-Burdine equations, terminal oil relative permeability, microwave saturation monitoring, displacement pressure, drainage capillary pressure
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-34)

Analytical Corey-Burdine-based corrections for capillary end effects on terminal oil relative permeability and interstitial water saturation, plus simultaneous kr and Pc estimation, validated on a carbonate oilflood.

Capillary end effects distort end-point relative permeability and saturation computed from corefloods. The authors integrate Darcy's law with Brooks-Corey/Corey-Burdine relations to derive the steady-state saturation profile for oil displacing water in a water-wet core, and produce charts correcting average terminal kro and interstitial water saturation as functions of Pd/|ΔPL| and λ. For λ=2.1 and Pd/|ΔPL|=0.4, the averaged kro is 81% lower than the actual value and the average Siw is 0.164 PV too high. In a reservoir-condition oilflood on a 5 md carbonate core with microwave saturation monitoring, measured kro of 0.65 versus 0.51 averaged and Siw of 0.292 versus 0.324 gave λ=2.3 and Pd=3.28 psi. Predicted Pc and kr agreed with centrifuge, porous-plate, mercury injection and steady-state data, and the predicted saturation profile matched the measured one.

## The Importance of Special Core Analysis in Modelling Remaining Oil Saturation in Carbonate Fields

- ID: SCA2008-03, year: 2008
- Authors: S.K. Masalmeh, X.D. Jing
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: special core analysis, imbibition capillary pressure, steady state relative permeability, centrifuge history matching, oil-wet carbonate waterflood, cross-flow between layers
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-03.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-03)

SCAL capillary pressure and relative permeability data from an oil-wet Cretaceous carbonate are measured, history matched, and used in sector simulations showing their combined control on waterflood cross-flow and remaining oil.

The paper examines how special core analysis data affect waterflood performance and remaining oil saturation in heterogeneous oil-wet carbonate reservoirs. Drainage Pc from mercury injection and centrifuge with four fluid pairs matched well after IFT scaling using the same effective contact angle, provided samples were properly cleaned. Relative permeability was measured on eight samples by steady state with high-rate bump floods plus multi-speed and single-speed centrifuge, history matched numerically. In a 48-layer inverted five-spot sector model, relative permeability had a strong effect (~10 vs ~20 saturation units difference in lower-zone recovery) when imbibition Pc contrast between layers was low, but little effect when Pc contrast acted as a barrier to cross-flow.

## Impact of SCAL on Carbonate Reservoirs: How Capillary Forces Can Affect Field Performance Predictions

- ID: SCA2003-36, year: 2003
- Authors: S. K. Masalmeh, X. D. Jing, W. van Vark, S. Christiansen, H. van der Weerd, J. van Dorp
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: imbibition capillary pressure, waterflood sweep efficiency, carbonate wettability, layered reservoir cross-flow, reservoir simulation, SCAL
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-36)

Simulations and SCAL Pc measurements show negative imbibition capillary pressure in a layered Middle East carbonate holds water in the upper zone, explaining waterflood behavior without permeability barriers.

Water injected into a layered Middle East carbonate reservoir stays in the high-permeability Upper zone and bypasses the Lower zone, although core kv/kh is near unity and pressure communication exists. Seven simulation scenarios (3D 27-layer and 2D 160-layer models) varied viscous, gravity and capillary cross-flow under water-wet, zero and oil-wet imbibition Pc. Positive Pc gave good sweep of both zones, whereas negative Pc held water in the Upper zone; total recovery to breakthrough was the same, so history matching of total production alone can mislead. SCAL on 20 plugs (0.01–1000 mD) showed hardly any spontaneous imbibition, negative forced-imbibition entry pressures of 3–5 psi for 2–5 mD plugs, and Swc and Sor of 5–10%. A detailed radial model using measured Pc matched observation-well saturation logs, showing capillary forces alone explain performance; recovery was sensitive to Pc curve shape and to preserving low-permeability layers in the Upper zone.

## Carbonate SCAL: Characterisation of Carbonate Rock Types for Determination of Saturation Functions and Residual Oil Saturations

- ID: SCA2004-08, year: 2004
- Authors: S.K. Masalmeh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonate rock typing, imbibition capillary pressure, residual oil saturation, Leverett J-function, mercury injection capillary pressure, dual porosity
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-08)

SCAL on 20 heterogeneous carbonate plugs shows imbibition Pc and residual oil saturation do not follow static rock typing, so dynamic data must define rock types.

Carbonate reservoirs are heterogeneous and mixed-wet, yet saturation functions are usually assigned from static rock typing. Twenty plugs of 27-30% porosity (permeability 2-1000 mD) were characterised with thin section, SEM, NMR and Hg-air Pc. Water-oil Pc was measured by centrifuge and CAPRICI over primary drainage, imbibition after aging, and secondary drainage, interpreted by numerical simulation. Primary drainage entry pressure follows permeability, with dual porosity in the high-permeability group, and centrifuge curves match Hg-air for this set. The Leverett J-function split the high-permeability samples into three groups. Sor (5-18%) and imbibition Pc showed no correlation with permeability, drainage Pc or facies; samples s17 and s18 had Sor of 18% and 5%, explained by SEM pore structure. The authors conclude that dynamic rock typing using imbibition Pc, relative permeability and Sor is needed.

## Pseudo Steady State Technique for Relative Permeability Measurement by

- ID: SCA1994-20, year: 1994
- Authors: G. A. Virnovsky, Y. Guo, K.O. Vatne
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: pseudo-steady-state, relative permeability, ramping schedule, fractional flow, capillary pressure effect, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-20)

A pseudo-steady-state relative permeability method with continuously ramped flow rates is tested by simulation and Berea experiments, giving valid results faster than steady-state, best when combined with one steady-state point.

Conventional steady-state relative permeability tests need long times to reach equilibrium at each fractional flow. The authors ramp the oil and water injection rates slowly and continuously, and derive a non-linear optimal ramping path from an accuracy parameter γ, the difference between inlet and outlet fractional flow. Simulations with CENDRA, using Corey curves and a 497.7 mD core, show that with zero capillary pressure the ramping error in total mobility is below 1% at γ=0.01. A ramp with γ=0.02 was too fast, and one starting from low water saturation was dominated by errors at low Sw.

## Rate Effects on Water-Oil Relative Permeability

- ID: SCA2001-19, year: 2001
- Authors: A. L. Chen, A. C. Wood
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, flow rate effects, capillary end effects, mixed-wet sandstone, x-ray saturation scanning, imbibition
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-19)

Steady-state water-oil imbibition relative permeabilities on mixed-wet and water-wet sandstone composites were independent of interstitial velocity between 2 and 39 ft/day, within about ±3 saturation units.

Laboratory relative permeability is often measured at high rates to suppress capillary end effects, raising the question of whether results represent low reservoir rates. The authors ran steady-state imbibition tests on a mixed-wet Berea composite (30 cm, USBM -0.10) and a water-wet field-plug composite (25.4 cm) at 2, 6, 16 and 39 ft/day, with a recirculating system at 73°F and x-ray in situ saturation scanning. Capillary end effects were excluded by using only the unaffected central section, identified by saturation profiles and internal pressure taps, with Darcy's law applied. Water curves deviated from the average by about ±1 to ±2.5 saturation units, and oil curves by ±2.5 to ±3 units, comparable to measurement accuracy. In the mixed-wet core, the small oil curve shifts followed test order, from slow wettability change, rather than rate. The authors conclude high rates are preferred for laboratory measurements.

## Estimation of Relative Permeability and Capillary Pressure Functions Using Transient and Equilibrium Data from Steady-State Experiments

- ID: SCA1994-18, year: 1994
- Authors: J.E. Nordtvedt, I.E.I. Oxnevada
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, capillary pressure estimation, B-spline parameter estimation, inverse problem, covariance analysis, chalk drainage
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-18)

Relative permeability and capillary pressure are estimated simultaneously by inverse regression with B-splines on transient and equilibrium steady-state data, validated on simulated and chalk core data.

Conventional steady-state relative permeability tests assume uniform saturation and discard transient data, while capillary pressure usually needs a separate experiment. The authors fit B-spline representations of kr and Pc to production and pressure-drop data recorded throughout each rate fraction, using a regression approach with the CENDRA core flood simulator and Levenberg-Marquardt optimization. Spline knots are added guided by residuals across the sequence of rate fractions, and linearized covariance analysis gives 95% confidence intervals. A simulated water-wet drainage case recovered the true curves within the confidence intervals over most of the saturation range. In a primary drainage test on Beer Stone outcrop chalk with a rate step-up scheme, the data were matched well and the estimated Pc agreed with micro-membrane and mercury injection curves scaled by the Leverett J-function, except at high saturations. Confidence intervals were large below Sw of about 0.15, where saturations were not reached.

## Using Explicit and Implicit Methods to Calculate Relative Permeabilities from in Situ Measurements

- ID: SCA1990-25EURO, year: 1990
- Authors: Kristofer Kolltveit, Jan-Erik Nordtvedt, Frank Hovland, Bjørn A. Kvanvik, Bente Lie
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: relative permeability, drainage, radioactive tracer saturation, pressure gradient, history matching, Darcy's law
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-25EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-25EURO)

Oil-water relative permeabilities are derived from a long-core drainage test with tracer saturation and pressure data, using explicit Darcy analysis and implicit simulator history matching.

Relative permeability determination is limited by poor pressure gradient estimates. A drainage experiment on a 77.8 cm Clashack sandstone core (permeability 0.952 D, porosity 0.183) used a 22Na tracer in the water phase and a germanium detector to measure water saturation at two points 10.1 cm apart, along with differential pressures at those points and the inlet. In the explicit method, the pressure gradient is obtained from the pressures at equal saturation at the two points and a constant saturation front velocity (0.0067 cm/s), and Darcy's law then gives kr. In the implicit method, a simulator with spline-interpolated kr curves is fitted to the data by chi-square minimization. Saturation profiles are sensitive mainly to endpoint kr, whereas pressure gradients are sensitive to the intermediate low-total-mobility region. Delayed pressure transducer response was a problem and required a time-scale correction.

## Improvements of Coreflood Design and Interpretation Using a New Software

- ID: SCA2000-29, year: 2000
- Authors: A. FINCHAM, F. GOUTH
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: parameter estimation, relative permeability, steady-state imbibition design, capillary pressure heterogeneity, saturation profiles, unconsolidated sand
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-29)

CAROPT history-matching software is used to design an optimal steady-state imbibition experiment and to interpret an unconsolidated-sand waterflood with heterogeneous capillary pressure, showing heterogeneity strongly biases relative permeability.

CAROPT couples a core-flood simulator with an optimisation package to estimate relative permeability and capillary pressure from steady-state, unsteady-state and centrifuge experiments, with linear error analysis. For steady-state oil/water imbibition, three rate-schedule designs were compared on a synthetic case; the design adding total-rate variations at low water fractional flow gave the best accuracy over the full saturation range. It was applied to a carbonate sample, and the zero-Pc point (Sw 0.53 vs 0.56) agreed with a restored-state measurement. For unconsolidated 1-10 Darcy sands, a viscous-oil drainage pressure-drop method maps 1D permeability along the core. A waterflood with gamma-ray saturation profiles was matched with a single relative permeability and two capillary pressure curves (low-permeability zones <5 D, high >5 D). The homogeneous model underestimated oil relative permeability and sharpened the water curve, giving pessimistic recovery but optimistic breakthrough, and including saturation profiles reduced parameter uncertainty.

## Comparison of Four Numerical Simulators for SCAL Experiments

- ID: SCA2016-006, year: 2016
- Authors: Roland Lenormand, Kjetil Lorentzen, Jos G. Maas, Douglas Ruth
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: SCAL simulation, boundary conditions, end-piece pressure, unsteady-state relative permeability, steady-state relative permeability, centrifuge drainage
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-006.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-006)

Four SCAL core-flood simulators (CYDAR, PORLAB, SCORES, Sendra) are benchmarked on five SS, USS and centrifuge cases; they agree once inlet pressure boundary conditions are harmonized.

There has been no benchmark for numerical simulators used to design and interpret SCAL experiments, unlike reservoir simulators. Four simulators (CYDAR, PORLAB, SCORES, Sendra) were compared in direct simulation on five cases: steady-state imbibition with smooth and sharp mixed-wet Pc, unsteady-state imbibition with Pc, Buckley-Leverett without Pc, and primary drainage centrifuge. Average saturation and differential pressure curves essentially superimpose across the simulators, showing that extra grid blocks with Pc=0, direct boundary conditions and zero-width blocks are equivalent. Initial discrepancies in USS case 3 came from which phase pressure is measured in the inlet end-piece (water versus the highest, oil, pressure). The authors agreed that the highest pressure applies when the experiment starts at capillary equilibrium with oil in the inlet end-piece, and recommend verifying this experimentally. Input and result tables are provided as reference cases.

## Steady-State Relative Permeability Measurements and Interpretation with Account for Capillary Effects

- ID: SCA1995-02, year: 1995
- Authors: G.A. Vimovsky
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, capillary end effect, inlet end piece, semi-permeable membrane, phase pressure measurement, 2D simulation
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-02)

A multirate steady-state method corrects capillary effects in relative permeability, and 2D simulations show a membrane inlet end piece makes individual phase pressures measurable.

Conventional steady-state relative permeability interpretation neglects capillary pressure, causing errors especially near residual saturations. The paper presents a multirate method in which total rate is varied at fixed inlet fractional flow, giving inlet saturation, relative permeabilities and capillary pressure from average saturation and phase pressure drops. Tests on synthetic 1D data recover the input curves, while Darcy-based interpretation remains in error even at 2 cc/min. Individual phase pressures require a modified inlet end piece with semi-permeable membranes, which was studied in 2D simulations (60x4 grids, 18.8 cm core, rates 2.0 and 0.2 cc/min). Without a membrane, oil counterflow into the water tubing creates a water-pressure discontinuity (Type 1 end effect). The membrane removes it, but separate fluid injection leaves non-uniform inlet saturation (Type 2), reduced by low rates, longer cores and sandwiched injection geometry.

The study examines how oil viscosity (1 to 5290 cp, viscosity ratio 1 to 6780) affects residual oil saturation and the water relative permeability endpoint. ## Applications of the Intercept Method to Correct Steady-State Relative Permeability for Capillary End-Effects

- ID: SCA2015-001, year: 2015
- Authors: Robin Gupta, Daniel Maloney
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary end-effect, steady-state relative permeability, Intercept Method, gas-condensate, multi-rate corefloods, composite core
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2015/SCA2015-001.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2015-001)

The Intercept Method uses multi-rate pressure drop and saturation data at each fractional flow to correct steady-state relative permeability for capillary end-effects, collapsing rate-dependent curves into unique ones.

Capillary end-effects cause rate-dependent artifacts in steady-state relative permeability, and raising rate or core length is often impractical, for example in gas-condensate or clay-rich tests. The Intercept Method measures pressure drop at 2-4 total rates for each fractional flow; the pressure-drop intercept gives the end-effect pressure drop, and an intercept of a saturation plot gives the corrected saturation. It was applied to published gas-condensate data (Henderson et al., Ayyalasomayajula et al.), oil-water data (Virnovsky et al.) and an in-house gas-brine drainage test on an 8.7 mD composite. In the gas-brine test the corrections reached up to 51% in pressure drop and 0.06 in brine saturation, with R² above 0.99 for nearly all correction plots. In every case the rate-dependent curve families collapsed to unique rate-independent curves, and the apparent gas-condensate velocity enhancement is attributed to end-effect artifacts. The method needs no simulation, pressure taps or in-situ saturation monitoring, and also corrects plug-junction discontinuities and transducer zero errors.

The authors ran high-pressure, high-temperature N2/brine drainage and waterflood experiments on Estaillades outcrop and two reservoir carbonates, using micro-CT on 8 mm miniature plugs and robotic X-ray CT on a 38 mm reservoir plug. Differential imaging quantified sub-resolution porosity, and in-situ contact angles confirmed water-wet conditions. Trapped gas saturation decreased continuously with increasing brine flow rate even at N_ca below 10^-6, with no distinct capillary- or viscous-dominated regime. Pore-scale images and cluster analysis showed trapped globule fragmentation, mobilization (including in brine-filled pores), and coalescence into larger clusters, and macro-scale saturation profiles agreed.

SS and USS gas-oil relative permeability tests with 4D CT on one sandstone plug show opposite outlet end-effects, attributed to re-imbibition, capillary backpressure and dynamic Pc.

Gas-oil relative permeability was measured by steady-state and unsteady-state methods on the same sandstone plug (413 mD Klinkenberg, 19.5% porosity, Swir 15.5%) with N2 and doped Soltrol under 4D CT saturation monitoring. Relative permeability curves from the two methods were broadly consistent after history matching with SENDRA; SS krg reached only 0.09 and kro was 0.59 at Swir. The saturation profiles differed strongly at the outlet: USS showed the expected oil saturation rise (about 0.6), whereas SS showed an unexpected sharp oil saturation drop at the outlet in all co-injection steps. The history-matched Pc curves differed greatly (Skjeveland Co of 25 kPa for SS versus 1.6 kPa for USS). The authors attribute the discrepancy to local re-imbibition hysteresis, capillary backpressure (~10 kPa for 10 µm pores) and dynamic capillary pressure (5-20 kPa), and argue that spatially resolved saturation data are needed for interpretation.

An automated YOLOv8, OCR and optical-correction system tracks brine-oil menisci in visual separators, matching pump volumes within about 1-6% AARD and generally beating idealized human readings.

Manual reading of menisci in visual separators used in relative permeability tests is subjective and limited to about 1 mm resolution. The authors built a system with a custom ruler (squares, triangles, numbers), OCR, YOLOv8 meniscus detection, edge detection, PID camera tracking and Snell's-law correction for the borosilicate window. Twelve brine/light mineral oil tests at 22 °C and atmospheric pressure were run at pump rates from 0.005 to 5 mL/min, using a 744×480 monochrome camera (about 0.083 mm/pixel). The system beat an idealized ±1 mm human reading in 10 of 12 experiments, but errors grew at the lowest flow rates because of camera resolution and changes in the chosen meniscus edge. Tests at high pressure and temperature and with a higher-resolution camera remain to be done.

- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2026/SCA2026-076.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2026-076)

A physics-informed DeepONet with a finite-volume graph network trunk and B-spline relative permeabilities acts as a differentiable surrogate for interpreting unsteady-state coreflooding, matching DuMuX shock fronts.

Interpreting unsteady-state core floods for relative permeability is limited by analytical assumptions and by costly history matching. The authors build a PI-DeepONet whose branch takes B-spline control polygons plus rock-fluid parameters, and whose trunk is a finite-volume graph network trained with a weak-entropy and TVD loss to capture Buckley-Leverett shocks. The dimensionless formulation allows application across core sizes, and inversion uses gradient optimization initialized from JBN with Corey curves. The model was trained on an 80-cell mesh for 4000 epochs against DuMuX simulations (8 cm, 100 mD core, 1 cP water, 5 cP oil), with data loss converging to about 5e-5 and conservation residual at machine precision. Validation shows saturation shocks and Corey curves matching the DuMuX reference closely. The full inversion on measured data is described but not demonstrated with results.
