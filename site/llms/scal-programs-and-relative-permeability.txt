## Improved Special Core Analysis: Scope for a reduced residual oil saturation

- ID: SCA1996-01, year: 1996
- Authors: J.A. Kokkedee, W. Boom, A.M. Frens, J.G. Maas
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: residual oil saturation, wettability restoration, capillary end-effect, multi-speed centrifuge, oil relative permeability tail, history matching
- Cited by 12 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-01)

Shell shows that SCAL at representative wettability, with numerical history-matching of artefacts, gives residual oil saturations 10-15 saturation percent lower than old data, boosting tail-end production.

Historic SCAL data from mature fields, measured on cleaned water-wet samples and interpreted analytically, overestimated residual oil saturation. The paper describes restoring representative wettability by cleaning and ageing with crude, and handling artefacts such as capillary end-effects and insufficient experiment time to reach low oil saturations. Steady-state, unsteady-state and multi-speed centrifuge data are combined and history-matched with numerical simulation to obtain a single consistent set of relative permeability and capillary pressure curves. A centrifuge example shows ultimate recovery at least 25% above an early estimate. Table data for fields in Europe, Africa, the Middle East and North America show current residual oil saturations of about 10-20% versus old values of 25-40%. A dipping-reservoir simulation shows that the properly interpreted oil relative permeability tail gives post-breakthrough production through gravity drainage, potentially raising ultimate recovery by 20% or more.

## A Focused Relative Permeability SCAL Study Driven by Reservoir Management Decisions

- ID: SCA2002-02, year: 2002
- Authors: M C Spearing, A S Davies, D J Element, S G Goodyear, E J Law
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: in-situ saturation monitoring, core flood simulation, JBN analysis, capillary end effect, trapped gas saturation, unconsolidated heavy oil
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-02)

A focused SCAL program on unconsolidated heavy-oil sand used in-situ saturation monitoring and simulation to correct end effects, showing JBN and earlier 1992 relative permeabilities were suppressed.

The study designed a focused relative permeability program for an unconsolidated, ~8 D, 80 cp heavy oil reservoir by analysing the imbibition and drainage processes that matter most for recovery. Restored-state composite cores from the oil leg and gas cap were flooded at reservoir conditions (1356 psig, 30.5°C) with live oil, with gamma-ray in-situ saturation monitoring (ISSM). Mass-balance residual oil was higher than ISSM (e.g. Sor 56.1% vs 45.9% at low rate), attributed to oil holdup in rig pipework, and trapped gas was 34.3% by PVT versus 26.2% by ISSM. Capillary end effects and inlet viscous fingering were identified, and simulation corrected curves that JBN had suppressed. The 1992 data proved similarly end-affected; gas-cap Sorw was 18% PV, below the 25% predicted by a rule of thumb, and Kro at Sgt was 0.34.

## A Summary of Experimentally Derived Relative Permeability and Residual Saturation on North Sea Reservoir Cores

- ID: SCA2002-12, year: 2002
- Authors: Arne Skauge, Bård Ottesen
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: waterflood residual oil saturation, trapped gas Land constant, Amott-Harvey and USBM wettability, intermediate wettability, three-phase gas trapping, North Sea sandstone
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-12)

Summarizes more than 500 SCAL experiments from 30 North Sea fields, finding lower three-phase gas trapping and lowest waterflood Sor at intermediate wettability, with three intermediate wetting types.

Early field evaluation lacks SCAL data, so the authors compiled over 500 gas injection, trapped gas and waterflood experiments from 30 North Sea fields. Average remaining oil saturation was 0.16 for gas injection (Sorg) and 0.20 for waterflooding (range 0.04–0.45). Waterflood remaining oil is lowest for intermediate-wet cores, and wettability becomes more oil-wet at low Swi. ## Relative permeability, hysteresis and I-Sw measurements on a carbonate prospect

- ID: SCA2000-07, year: 2000
- Authors: N. van der Post, S.K. Masalmeh, J.G.C. Coenen, K.H. van der Gijp, J.G. Maas
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: X-ray steady state, relative permeability hysteresis, resistivity index, saturation exponent, mixed wettability, centrifuge
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-07)

X-ray steady-state, γ-ray and centrifuge measurements on aged high-porosity carbonate show relative permeability hysteresis and a saturation exponent of 3 (versus 2.2 unaged), reconciling log and core water saturation.

Logs in a high-porosity Middle East carbonate showed resistivities of 2000 Ωm, implying an unrealistic 1.5% connate water when using n=2.2 from water-wet continuous-injection tests. The authors used a modified X-ray steady-state apparatus with pressure and voltage probes to measure saturation and resistivity at several positions along aged cores (about 5 mD, 40% porosity), with decane replacing crude after washout. Oil relative permeability was higher and water lower in secondary drainage than in first imbibition, consistent with mixed wettability. Aged plugs gave n≈3 in three samples, giving connate water of about 5% consistent with SCAL, with little I-Sw hysteresis.

## Parameter Based SCAL - Analysing Relative Permeability for Full Field Application

- ID: SCA2014-080, year: 2014
- Authors: Einar Ebeltoft, Frode Lomeland, Amund Brautaset, Åsmund Haugen
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: LET correlation, relative permeability parameterization, trend models, initial water saturation, wettability, residual oil saturation
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2014/SCA2014-080.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2014-080)

Parameterizes SCAL relative permeability with LET correlation and wettability-consistent trend models versus Swi to build base, optimistic and pessimistic saturation functions for full-field simulation, shown on an NCS field.

Relative permeability from individual SCAL experiments is difficult to combine into saturation functions for full-field simulation. The authors verify each experiment by core-flood simulation, parameterize the curves with the LET correlation, and store end points and L, E, T parameters in a SCAL database with plug data. Trend models for Sorw, krw(Sorw) and the LET parameters as functions of initial water saturation are built from wettability physics and NCS data, then calibrated visually to field data with base, high and low bounds. For a field case, four Swi bins (0–0.15, 0.15–0.25, 0.25–0.35, 0.35–1.0) represent rock types, each with its own curves, avoiding the wettability-inconsistent curve shapes caused by end-point scaling. The result is smooth curves with uncertainty bounds, with more scatter noted in Lw and To because of low experimental sensitivity near Swi.

## SCAL for Gas Reservoirs: A Contribution for Better Experiments

- ID: SCA2016-023, year: 2016
- Authors: Arjen Cense, Jules Reed, Patrick Egermann, LR Senergy
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: residual gas saturation, gas-water relative permeability, Land equation, differential pressure limit, in-situ saturation monitoring, paleo contact
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-023.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-023)

Reviews pitfalls in gas-water SCAL (Pc, residual gas, unsteady and steady state floods) and recommends low differential pressure, limited injection volume, and QC of Sgr and endpoints.

Gas-field SCAL appears simple because gas systems are water wet, but gas compressibility and solubility make experiments difficult. The paper discusses capillary pressure choices (drainage vs imbibition, paleo contacts), and QC of residual gas saturation using compressibility tests, ambient SgrM measurements (73 samples) with the Land equation, and Brooks-Corey endpoint checks. Reviewed unsteady-state floods at 200 psi pore pressure showed Sgr declining once differential pressure exceeded about 10 psi (5% of pore pressure). Gas produced after breakthrough (about 2.9 ml after ~30 PVI) was attributed to incomplete brine-nitrogen equilibration, not dissolution at the inlet, so Sgr at breakthrough is representative. Steady-state imbibition tests suffered large saturation jumps at low fractional flow and end effects; simulations showed stabilization would take 10-30+ days, so they are not recommended for typical commercial labs.

A review of relative permeability: controlling factors, formation damage, gas-condensate, three-phase and EOR processes, reservoir-condition testing, saturation measurement technology and use of models in simulation.

This review covers the factors controlling relative permeability, including saturation, rock properties, wettability, hysteresis, initial saturation and flow rate. Empirical and statistical models, agreement with well tests, and pseudo relative permeabilities for simulation are addressed; no new experimental data are presented.

## SCA-9909 Relative Permeability Measurements on Heterogeneous Samples A pragmatic approach

- ID: SCA1999-09, year: 1999
- Authors: Aart Zweers, Wim Scherpenisse, Krijn Wit, Jos Maas
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: steady-state relative permeability, heterogeneous core plugs, capillary end-effects, phase-selective pressure measurement, reservoir simulation upscaling
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 6
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-09)

Simulations show steady-state floods on heterogeneous plugs with separate phase pressure drops and low rates reproduce field-scale relative permeabilities, enabling a statistical distribution of curves for reservoir simulation.

Standard SCAL flow experiments require homogeneous plugs, and CT screening can reject up to 90% of core, raising doubts about representativeness for heterogeneous reservoirs. The authors propose a modified steady-state method applied to a statistically representative plug set, measuring pressure drop separately in water and oil using water-wet and oil-wet filters. Using the MoReS simulator in 2-D, they tested layered plugs and isolated low- or high-permeability spots (contrasts of 2 to 100) with Corey curves (n=4), comparing plug-scale steady-state runs with plug-in-reservoir field-scale runs at 1 ft/d. Layered cases agreed within about 15% up to 30 times field rate, and spot cases agreed up to 30 ft/d, though effective field curves deviated from input curves by 30% (layers) and over 50% (spots). Limitations include restricted saturation range, poor access to low oil saturation due to end-effects, and few isolated-heterogeneity cases tested.

## Method to Recover Connate Water From Reservoir Cores Using Centrifuge and Surfactant-Enhanced oil for Formation Evaluation

- ID: SCA2026-020, year: 2026
- Authors: Hussain Al Qatari, Shouxiang Ma, Taha Okashah, Najebah M. Alsaleh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: connate water recovery, surfactant-enhanced oil, interfacial tension, centrifuge drainage, ion chromatography, formation water salinity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2026/SCA2026-020.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2026-020)

Centrifuging aged cores with surfactant-enhanced oil, which strongly lowers IFT, recovers part of irreducible water whose salinity can be measured by titration and ion chromatography.

Formation water resistivity for Archie-based Swi is hard to obtain because irreducible water is capillary-bound and cannot be sampled directly. The authors drained brine-saturated Indiana Limestone and Berea Sandstone plugs with crude oil by centrifuge, aged them for one month at 70°C and 1500 psi, then centrifuged them at higher speed with oil containing 2% oil-soluble surfactant. After calibration and dilution, the small brine volumes gave NaCl concentrations matching the prepared brines by both ion chromatography and titration.

## Defining a sample heterogeneity cut-off value to obtain representative Special Core Analysis (SCAL) measurements

- ID: SCA2019-024, year: 2019
- Authors: Niels Springer, Albert Hebing
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: heterogeneity number V, SCAL interpretation by simulation, AutoSCORES history matching, CT Hounsfield heterogeneity, unsteady-state relative permeability, Student t-test
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 11
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2019/SCA2019-024.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2019-024)

Simulating 70+ synthetic heterogeneous plugs with automated history matching and t-tests, the study derives scenario-dependent cut-off values of heterogeneity number V for reliable SCAL, validated on three sandstone plugs.

SCAL relative permeability data can be distorted by heterogeneous plugs, and no quantitative cut-off existed for the CT-based heterogeneity number V. The authors built more than 70 synthetic 3-D plugs (random porosity, speckled, layered, impermeable layers, fractures) in SCORES3D, simulated unsteady-state and multispeed centrifuge imbibition experiments, and history matched them with the new AutoSCORES tool. Student t-tests of extracted Corey parameters against homogeneous equivalents gave V cut-offs per scenario for N=3 samples: about 0.9 for random heterogeneity, 0.2 speckled, 0.1 for visible layering, and 0 for open fractures. Increasing the sample count to 10 changes the critical t value and lowers the cut-off for random heterogeneity to about 0.5. Gas-brine drainage USS tests on three Oberkirchener sandstone plugs with V≈0.26, one with stylolites, gave similar Corey parameters except krgwc, which was likely affected by salt precipitation.

- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: initial water saturation, micro-CT pore occupancy, porous plate, capillary end-effect, Bentheimer sandstone, core cleaning
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 7
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2022/SCA2022-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2022-16)

Micro-CT comparison of porous-plate, oil-flooding and centrifuge initial water saturation on Bentheimer shows porous plate is most robust; reversals trap oil and non-water-wet rock traps brine.

The paper compares porous-plate, viscous oil flooding and centrifuge primary drainage for establishing initial water saturation, using micro-CT (4-5 µm voxels) on Bentheimer mini-plugs, both water-wet and Surfasil-altered. ## Hybrid Drainage Technique application on bimodal limestone

- ID: SCA2023-006, year: 2023
- Authors: Victor Fernandes, Franck Nono, Benjamin N Nicot, Fabrice Pairoys, Henri Bertin, Jean Lachaud, Cyril Caubit
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Hybrid Drainage Technique, Viscous Oil Flood, Estaillades limestone, bimodal pore network, NMR T2 saturation profiles, imbibition relative permeability
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 5
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2023/SCA2023-006.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2023-006)

Compares Hybrid Drainage Technique with Viscous Oil Flood on bimodal Estaillades limestone; HDT reaches low Swi, invades small pores, and changes imbibition Kr and Sor.

Setting initial water saturation on bimodal limestone is difficult with viscous-controlled drainage, which can bypass the small pores. Estaillades limestone samples (38 mm x 50 mm, 103-260 mD) were drained by Viscous Oil Flood (VOF) or Hybrid Drainage Technique (HDT, viscous flood followed by a porous-plate capillary step), aged in crude oil and waterflooded, with NMR and micro-CT monitoring. VOF could not get Swi below about 35% even with flow reversal, whereas HDT reached Swi of about 0.20 (Pc 5 bar) with a homogeneous profile and no capillary end effect. At matched Swi of 0.38, the VOF waterflood history match fit Corey curves (Nw 1.8, No 3.3), but the HDT sample needed LET curves and showed higher Sor (0.74 vs 0.87 Sw at Sor) with a different Krw trend. Micro-CT showed oil trapped in the small pores after HDT waterflooding, indicating that pore occupancy after primary drainage governs imbibition results.

## Overview of the Let Family of Versatile Correlations for Flow Functions

- ID: SCA2018-056, year: 2018
- Authors: Frode Lomeland
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: LET correlation, relative permeability, capillary pressure, LETh, upscaling, parameter trend functions
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2018/SCA2018-056.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2018-056)

Overview of the 2018 LET correlation family for relative permeability and capillary pressure, introducing invertible LETh drainage Pc and LETs imbibition Pc with separate spontaneous and forced branches.

The paper reviews the LET family of empirical correlations for relative permeability and capillary pressure, used for interpreting flow experiments, upscaling and history matching. It presents LET and LETx Kr formulas, generic parameter trend functions linked to initial water saturation, and a conceptual model in which Swi controls wettability variation. The paper also notes that LET can be fitted analytically by linearizing in L, log(E) and T, and that it suits upscaling and history matching.

Steady-state kr with 2D X-ray saturation maps on small Bentheimer cores shows dynamic crude-oil aging alters wettability more strongly and uniformly than static aging, which gives patchy mixed-wettability.

## Effects of Using Live Versus Dead Crude Oils on Unsteady-State Water/Oil Relative Permeabilities

- ID: SCA1993-26, year: 1993
- Authors: G. M. Narahara, T. H. Blackshear Jr
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: live versus dead oil, unsteady-state waterflood, JBN relative permeability, brine elution, preserved cores, wettability
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-26)

Unsteady-state waterfloods with live and dead crude oils on preserved cores and Berea showed no consistent relative permeability difference where saturation ranges overlapped; Berea results were irreproducible.

The study asks whether relative permeabilities measured with dead (de-gassed) crude oil differ from those with live oil, since live-oil testing is costly. Unsteady-state waterfloods were run on composite cores for three crude oil systems: North Sea A (preserved core, 34° API), a French reservoir (preserved core, 37° API), and North Sea B (aged Berea, 23° API). Each composite was flooded with live oil and then dead oil, or in reverse order, at reservoir temperature and pressure. Saturations were checked by brine elution and vacuum distillation, and relative permeabilities were computed with the JBN method. Dead oil gave earlier breakthrough and a broader saturation range because of its higher viscosity. Where the curves overlapped, no consistent live-versus-dead difference appeared in the preserved cores; differences tracked initial water saturation (3-4% HCV spread after normalization). The Berea/North Sea B tests were not reproducible, probably because of unstable wettability and viscous fingering, so no conclusion was possible for that case.

Saturation fluctuations from heterogeneity were larger at low Sw and in USS tests than in SS tests, so end points were more robust than mid-range relative permeabilities.

Catalogs uncertainty sources in routine and special core analysis and proposes analytical and numerical (MCMC) propagation frameworks, with sensitivity examples for permeability and saturation uncertainty.

The paper addresses the many sources of uncertainty in routine and special core analysis, including transducers, sample geometry, dead volumes, Klinkenberg correction, gas-expansion porosity, centrifuge capillary pressure reading and parallax, and relative permeability references. It proposes a Type B analytical approach based on first-order propagation (with covariance for fitted parameters) for absolute permeability, porosity, relative permeability and centrifuge Pc. For transient relative permeability from history matching, it recommends numerical inverse modeling with Levenberg-Marquardt covariance and MCMC credibility intervals. Sensitivity examples show sample geometry (caliper, 3 readings) dominates apparent permeability uncertainty, followed by viscosity. For an unsteady-state test, transducer uncertainty (0.1 psi) becomes important above about 100 mD, and dead volumes increase saturation uncertainty at test start and after each change in saturation direction.
