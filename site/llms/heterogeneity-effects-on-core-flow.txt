## Relative Permeability and Capillary Pressure: Effects of Rock Heterogeneity

- ID: SCA1998-08, year: 1998
- Authors: A. Sylte, T. Mannseth, J. Mykkeltveit, J.E. Nordtvedt
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: core heterogeneity, relative permeability, capillary pressure, Leverett j-function, inverse modeling, composite core
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1998/SCA1998-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1998-08)

Synthetic and experimental 1D/2D cases show that treating heterogeneous cores as homogeneous or 1D causes large errors in estimated relative permeability and capillary pressure, even at 1:2 contrast.

Relative permeability and capillary pressure are normally inferred from flooding data assuming a homogeneous core, which can bias the results. The authors extended an implicit history-matching method (B-spline functions, CENDRA simulator, Leverett j-function option) to 1D and 2D permeability distributions. Synthetic oil floods (drainage with a rate bump) were generated for two-piece cores with contrasts of 2:1, 1:2 and 1:10 and different dividing angles, then inverted with simplified homogeneous or 1D permeability models. A permeability difference of only 3-4 mD (about 7%) between two 50 mD halves was enough for the initial SSR to exceed the expected variance-error level. A four-plug composite core waterflood (permeabilities 401-819 mD) gave a spurious plateau in oil relative permeability under a homogeneous assumption, which disappeared with a 1D heterogeneous model. Strong 1:10 heterogeneity gave useless results if not modeled, and a homogeneous model compensated more than a 1D model.

## Influence of Heterogeneity, Wettability and Coreflood Design on Relative Permeability Curves

- ID: SCA2000-23, year: 2000
- Authors: G. HAMON, C. ROY
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: relative permeability, small-scale heterogeneity, coreflood inversion, unsteady-state vs steady-state, saturation profiles, capillary pressure
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-23)

Numerical waterflood experiments show that along-bedding heterogeneity biases relative permeability from coreflood inversion, while across-bedding flow gives reliable curves if local permeability and saturation profiles are included.

Most coreflood interpretations assume homogeneous samples, yet small-scale heterogeneity can bias relative permeability. The authors review experimental methods for detecting heterogeneity, then simulate unsteady- and steady-state waterfloods on finely gridded 2D models, along and across bedding, and invert the synthetic production and pressure data for Kr. Along-bedding flow is the riskiest: unsteady-state inversion is badly biased, and steady-state is more robust but still pessimistic. Across-bedding flow gives near-true Kr curves when capillary forces are negligible. With significant capillary forces, multi-rate unsteady-state floods in weakly water-wet or oil-wet samples recover true Kr if local permeability and in-situ saturation profiles are used, as in the oil-wet case where Sorw was 0.31 versus a true 0.25 unless local Sorw was used.

## Identification of Parallel Heterogeneities with Miscible Displacement

- ID: SCA1999-33, year: 1999
- Authors: C. Dauba, G. Hamon, M. Quintard, F. Cherblanc
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: miscible displacement, longitudinal heterogeneity, tracer effluent curve, Peclet number, 2D simulation type curves, unsteady-state relative permeability
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-33.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-33)

Miscible tracer displacements, interpreted with 2D two-region simulations, identify lengthwise heterogeneity in cores and screen out samples that would give anomalous relative permeability curves.

Lengthwise heterogeneity (preferential paths, vugs, layers) distorts unsteady-state water/oil relative permeability, so the authors use brine miscible displacement (30 to 120 g/l) as a cheap screening test before waterflooding. Finely gridded 2D simulations (Transp/Modflow) of two-region cores produced type curves: early breakthrough with a long tail marks high permeability contrast, two steps mark moderate contrast, and high velocity accentuates differences, while cross-section heterogeneity is barely detectable. Experiments on unconsolidated sandstones, a shaly sandstone and vuggy dolomites were history-matched to give permeability contrast and high-permeability volume fraction, e.g. contrast 10 and 45% for D2, 10 and 60% for D3, 10 and 22% for N1, 1000 and 3% for vuggy A2, and 100 and 16% for A6. Matches agreed with CT and probe permeameter data, and samples with heterogeneous tracer curves gave anomalous relative permeability curves. CT failed to distinguish vuggy samples A2 and A6, whereas miscible tests did.

## Two Dimensional Imaging of Viscous and Capillary Effects in a Dipping Stratified Heterogeneous Media

- ID: SCA1994-13, year: 1994
- Authors: Henri BERTIN, Arne GRAUE, Terje EILERTSEN
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gamma-ray attenuation, 2D saturation imaging, capillary equilibrium, heterogeneous layered media, waterflood, capillary end effect
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-13)

Gamma-ray 2D saturation imaging of drainage and waterflood in a 45-degree dipping three-block Aerolith/sandstone medium reveals capillary redistribution and viscous-capillary competition controlling recovery.

The study addresses two-phase flow in layered heterogeneous media where average flow direction differs from the layering. A composite 5 cm block medium was built from two Vosges sandstone blocks (about 550 mD, 26% porosity) and a central high-permeability Aerolith-10 block, joined at 45 degrees with filter paper for capillary contact. Primary drainage at 5 ml/min, a 65-hour no-flow period, a 6 ml/h waterflood and a 2 ml/h second drainage were imaged with gamma-ray attenuation using NaI brine. During the no-flow period water saturation fell in the Aerolith block and rose slightly in the sandstone through capillary equilibrium. In the waterflood, water broke through at 0.38 PV and recovery was better in the low-capillary block, but the upper part of the Aerolith block was poorly swept, attributed to gravity versus capillary competition, possible loss of contact, and short injection time. Capillary end effects and dip angle strongly influenced recovery, and 2D saturation maps improved interpretation.

## The effect of heterogeneity on unsteady-state displacements

- ID: SCA2000-30, year: 2000
- Authors: Darryl Fenwick, Nicole Doerler, Roland Lenormand
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: JBN relative permeability, core-scale heterogeneity, unsteady-state displacement, pseudo capillary pressure, tracer test, streamline simulation
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-30)

Streamline simulations on heterogeneous permeability fields show JBN relative permeability is biased and saturation profiles need a pseudo-Pc; tracer tests can help separate heterogeneity from local Kr.

Unsteady-state relative permeability interpretation by JBN assumes a homogeneous core, but cores are often heterogeneous. The authors ran streamline (3DSL) simulations on 250x100 2D Gaussian permeability fields with correlation lengths of 0.01 and 1.0 and variances of ln K of 0.1 and 1, using Corey Kr (exponent 1 for tracer, 2), Pc=0 and viscosity ratios of 1 and 5. JBN effective Kr differed from local Kr even for small correlation length and variance, and a 1D Buckley-Leverett model with the effective Kr reproduced outlet flux but not the more spread saturation profiles at early times. Matching profiles required a pseudo-Pc, whose shape resembles a real capillary pressure curve. Effective Kr moved closer to local Kr at M=5, and an empirical model combining tracer and local Kr is proposed to back-calculate local Kr.

## The Impact of Using Composite Cores on Core Analysis Results

- ID: SCA1996-02, year: 1996
- Authors: K. Langaas, S. Ekrann, E. Ebeltoft
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: composite core, Leverett J-function, capillary end effect, core ordering, steady-state relative permeability, JBN method
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-02)

Theoretical study showing composite cores should be ordered by decreasing permeability along flow, lowest-permeability core at the outlet, to give the best residual oil saturation estimate under capillary end effects.

The paper examines how the ordering of individual cores in a composite core affects relative permeabilities from waterfloods when capillary end effects are present. Individual cores are assumed to share porosity and relative permeability, with capillary pressures scaled by a Leverett-J relation, using North Sea sandstone data (400, 600, 800, 1000 mD). Steady-state saturation profiles with capillary continuity are computed, and unsteady-state floods are simulated and interpreted with JBN. Ordering by decreasing permeability (lowest at the outlet) gives the best residual oil saturation estimate: 11.9% error versus 15.3% for increasing order, with Huppler's ordering in between. This is proved mathematically, independent of core lengths and mobility ratio. Huppler's criterion holds only for USS with zero capillarity, and steady state gives better relative permeabilities than USS.

## Two-Phase Flow in Heterogeneous Nodular Porous Media

- ID: SCA1992-23EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Henri Bertin, Waddah Alhanai, Azita Ahmadi, Michel Quintard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: nodular heterogeneity, gamma-ray attenuation, drainage and imbibition, quasi-static capillary equilibrium, oil-wet nodules, large-scale averaging
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-23EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-23EURO)

Drainage and waterflood experiments with gamma-ray saturation mapping in three nodular sand/sandstone media show quasi-static behaviour only when low-permeability nodules sit in sand; sand nodules recover slowly, oil-wet nodules not at all.

Two-phase flow in heterogeneous nodular porous media matters for reservoir prediction and upscaling. Three 2D-reduced models were built from sandstone (15 mD) and fine sand (7.4 D): sandstone nodules in sand (Type I), sand nodules in sandstone (Type II), and silanized oil-wet sand nodules in water-wet sandstone (Type III). Local properties were measured on isolated samples, and 2D saturation fields during oil drainage and waterflood were recorded by gamma-ray attenuation. Type I drainage saturations matched quasi-static capillary-equilibrium predictions, whereas Type II nodule saturations fell below isolated-sample irreducible values, requiring extrapolation of the capillary pressure curves. In Type II waterflood, the sandstone was fully swept after about 0.3 PV while sand nodules recovered oil slowly for over 30 PV, a non-quasi-static response linked to capillary pressure gradients at the interfaces and qualitatively reproduced by simulation. In Type III, water did not enter the oil-wet nodules and their oil could not be displaced.

## Upscaling Method of Relative Permeability from Plug Core to Whole Core

- ID: SCA2004-16, year: 2004
- Authors: S. Takahashi, N. Tokuda, T. Nakashima
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: relative permeability upscaling, genetic algorithm history matching, unsteady-state coreflood, X-ray CT saturation, heterogeneous carbonate, stochastic modeling
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-16)

Water displacement tests on a heterogeneous carbonate whole core and its plugs, matched with a genetic-algorithm simulator, show reservoir-style upscaling can predict whole-core kro/krw from plug data.

The paper addresses how to estimate whole-core relative permeability from smaller plug data. A water displacement test under X-ray CT was run on a heterogeneous Cretaceous carbonate whole core (3.5 in diameter, 32.2% porosity, 30.7 mD), followed by tests on six 1-inch plugs. Three plug tests were history-matched with 3D coreflood models using the genetic-algorithm program GEMAP to derive kro/krw and Pc curves for three pore-types. Plug kro/krw were upscaled with CAVLUP, and capillary-limit upscaling reproduced steady-state simulation at the experimental rate (0.05 cc/min), while viscous-limit matched a hypothetical 5 cc/min case. In 100 stochastic models the upscaled curves nearly converged, depending on pore-type fractions rather than permeability distribution, and the average matched the deterministic optimized model. A six-step workflow is proposed.

## Simultaneous Determination of the Two-Phase Flow and Hydrodynamic Dispersion Coefficients of Soils from Transient Immiscible and Miscible Displacement Experiments

- ID: SCA2005-58, year: 2005
- Authors: Christos Aggelopoulos, Christos D. Tsakiroglou
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: transient displacement inverse modeling, electrical resistivity index, capillary pressure, relative permeability, hydrodynamic dispersion, sand column
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-58.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-58)

Multi-point electrical resistance during transient oil-water and salt-tracer displacements in sand columns is inverted to estimate Pc, relative permeabilities and longitudinal dispersion; dispersion came out overestimated.

The paper presents a method to obtain capillary pressure, relative permeability and longitudinal dispersion of disturbed soils from transient displacement experiments with multi-point electrical resistance monitoring. Sieved sand (125-250 µm, porosity 0.4, permeability 35.8 D) was packed in PTFE columns with ring and rod electrodes. In n-dodecane drainage at Ca of 6.2e-8 and 3.1e-7, resistivity index fitted with a two-exponent relation gave segment-wise water saturation, and pressure drop plus saturation histories were history-matched by inverse modeling. ## Combining core floods and numerical simulation to measure flow properties in heterogeneous rocks

- ID: SCA2017-022, year: 2017
- Authors: Samuel Krevor, Simeon Agada, Sam Jackson, Catriona Reynolds, Ben Niu, United Kingdom
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: equivalent relative permeability, capillary pressure heterogeneity, X-ray CT saturation maps, steady-state coreflood, digital core model, CO2 storage
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2017/SCA2017-022.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2017-022)

Steady-state corefloods with 3D X-ray saturation maps parameterise capillary-heterogeneity models, whose simulations show equivalent relative permeability at low flow rates is about 5 times below the viscous-limit values.

Conventional relative permeability measurements require homogeneous cores and high flow rates, which are not representative of reservoir flow in heterogeneous rock. The authors propose a workflow in which steady-state corefloods with 3D X-ray CT saturation imaging are used to build a digital core model with spatially varying capillary pressure scaling, based on mercury porosimetry curves. Cores were Bentheimer (N2-water drainage), Bunter sandstone (CO2-brine drainage) and Berea (N2-water imbibition), with layering from about 5 mm to multi-cm. ECLIPSE 100 simulations on a 16×16×40 grid, with the variance of the scaling factors increased by 1.2 for Bentheimer, matched observed saturation maps better than the initial guess, though inlet gravity effects and end effects were not captured. Simulated equivalent relative permeability at low rates (7 ml/min for Bentheimer, 0.2 ml/min for Bunter) was about a factor of 5 lower than the viscous-limit curves. Berea simulations were not completed.

## Determination of Relative Permeabilities on Heterogeneous Samples

- ID: SCA2024_1077, year: 2024
- Authors: Fabrice Pairoys, Guillaume Lenormand, Roland Lenormand
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: relative permeability, Egermann method, Leverett J-function, saturation profiles, history matching, steady state
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 8
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2024/SCA2024_1077.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2024_1077)

Heterogeneous simulation methods (Leverett J-function, Egermann local Pc) are tested on carbonate waterfloods; only Egermann reproduces saturation fluctuations, while Kr stays close to the homogeneous result.

Saturation profiles in relative permeability coreflood experiments show fluctuations even on carefully selected samples, which homogeneous simulations cannot reproduce. Two datasets on tight carbonate composite stacks (semi-dynamic and steady-state imbibition at 100°C, 3000 psi pore pressure) with 1D X-ray saturation and porosity profiles were re-interpreted by history matching with CYDAR. Permeability profiles from Kozeny-Carman and Timur, with local Pc from the Leverett J-function, failed to reproduce the amplitude of the saturation fluctuations. The Egermann method, deriving local Pc curves from the saturation profiles, with an extra iteration, gave a good saturation profile match for the steady-state test but not for the semi-dynamic one, which lacks information on the positive Pc branch. The resulting steady-state Kr curves were nearly identical to the homogeneous ones (Sor 0.30 vs 0.35), but the heterogeneous interpretation improves quality control and gives a Pc envelope for uncertainty.

## Improvement of Fluid Distribution Description During Floods by Combined Use of X-Ray CT Scan and Continuous Local Resistivity Measurements

- ID: SCA2003-29, year: 2003
- Authors: Claudine Durand
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: X-ray CT scan, local resistivity, resistivity index, Archie saturation exponent, steady-state drainage-imbibition, Fontainebleau sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-29)

Combining CT scanning with continuous local resistivity along Fontainebleau sandstone plugs during steady-state floods identifies end-affected zones; discarding them gives saturation exponents n close to 2.

Resistivity measurements on cores are often corrupted by non-uniform saturation, so the actual fluid distribution needs to be checked. Steady-state oil-brine drainage and imbibition were run on three Fontainebleau sandstones (porosity 12.8-24.8%, permeability 280-2200 mD) in a cell inside a medical CT scanner. Six potential electrodes gave local resistances on 1.25 cm slices, and CT scans at plateaux gave local saturation. Local formation factors gave Archie m of 1.5-1.8, and end slices showed higher values. Oil accumulation at the inlet and capillary end effects at the outlet distorted RI-Sw data. Discarding the end slices lowered n by up to 0.4 in permeable samples, giving values close to 2. A bump to higher flow rate produced non-homogeneous fluid distributions unsuitable for RI calculation.

## A Nondestructive Method for Characterization of One Dimensional Permeability Distribution at the Core Scale

- ID: SCA2007_06, year: 2007
- Authors: A. Soltani, M. Le Ravalec-Dupin, M. Fourar, P. Egermann
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: permeability profile, miscible displacement, pressure drop, core heterogeneity, CT scanning, viscous displacement
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_06)

A 1D core-scale permeability profile is derived from pressure drop during piston-like viscous miscible displacement, validated by simulations and CT-monitored core experiments.

Core analysis usually assumes homogeneous samples, so the authors propose estimating the permeability profile along the flow axis from the pressure drop history while a high-viscosity miscible fluid (viscosity ratio about 60) displaces a low-viscosity fluid in a piston-like way. The core is treated as a stack of thin cross-sections, and the local permeability follows from the time derivative of the pressure drop. Streamline simulations of four-plug composite models reproduced the plug permeabilities almost perfectly for immiscible displacement, and with smoothing for miscible displacement. Laboratory tests used two composites of butted plugs (60 cp glycerin-brine at 15 cc/hr) and three heterogeneous cores, with CT imaging of the front. Agreement with plug and minipermeameter measurements was good, though noisier for high permeability samples and poor at the core ends. Bidirectional injection improved results, and the method is limited to one-dimensional heterogeneity.

## Pore-Scale Modeling of Three-Phase Flow: Comparative Study with Experimental Reservoir Data

- ID: SCA2009-30, year: 2009
- Authors: C. Nardi, O. Lopez, P.E. Øren, R. Held, E. B. Petersen
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, pore network model, spreading coefficient, wettability contact angle, Statfjord, steady-state measurements
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-30)

A three-phase pore network model on a reconstructed Statfjord rock, anchored to two-phase data, predicts CDI and CID relative permeabilities fairly well but fails on DDI saturation paths.

The paper presents a three-phase capillary-dominated network model on pore networks from digitized rock images. It includes wettability alteration, hysteresis, spreading layers and true three-phase entry pressures. The model was verified against Oak's Berea data, then anchored to two-phase waterflood data for a reconstructed non-water-wet Statfjord sample (Amott index 0.2). Predicted CDI relative permeabilities agree fairly well with steady-state measurements (Sorg 0.187 vs 0.186), though gas kr is underestimated at intermediate saturations. For CID, the computed Sgro of 0.27 greatly exceeds the measured 0.115, likely due to viscous remobilization of trapped gas at a capillary number near 1e-4 and buoyancy effects. DDI saturation paths were not reproduced, since experiments injected multiple fluids simultaneously, and a path-tracking algorithm is needed.

## Effects of Core Sample Dimensions and Orientation on Two-Phase Flow Properties Determination

- ID: SCA1994-12, year: 1994
- Authors: Renaud Gauchet
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: relative permeability, unsteady-state waterflood, core size effect, end effects, laminated reservoir, history matching
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-12.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-12)

Waterfloods on a full-size North Sea core and vertical and horizontal plugs from it show that plugs, especially horizontal ones in laminated rock, give unrepresentative relative permeabilities.

The study asks how sample size and orientation affect relative permeability from unsteady-state waterfloods in a laminated North Sea reservoir. A full-size core (10 cm diameter, 20.4 cm long) was flooded, then one horizontal and one vertical big plug (5 cm x 8.5 cm) were drilled from it and flooded under the same frontal velocity with gamma-ray saturation monitoring. Each experiment was history matched independently with a 1D simulator. Remaining oil saturation varied from 36.6% to 20.8% in the full size versus 23.3% to 21.4% in the vertical plug, and end effects covered about 30% of plug length. The horizontal plug showed early breakthrough (0.02 PV) from layers at about 30° to the axis not detected by CT, so 1D matching was invalid. Simulating the full-size flood with horizontal-plug curves gave 46.5% recovery versus 51.9% with the correct curves. Full-size samples with heterogeneities perpendicular to flow are recommended.

## Numerical simulations and 2D-imaging experiments of flow in tilted crossbeds

- ID: SCA1996-29, year: 1996
- Authors: Knut Arne Bsrresen, Arne Graue
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: crossbedding, 2D saturation imaging, spontaneous imbibition, numerical diffusion, waterflood, oil trapping
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-29)

Nuclear tracer imaging of waterfloods in a sandpack with a tilted low-permeability crossbed shows capillary-dominated imbibition and only temporary oil trapping; ECLIPSE simulations matched only qualitatively.

The study examines how a tilted, low-permeability crossbed affects multiphase flow in a 30 x 10 x 3 cm sandpack, with coarse sand (125-250 µm) at the ends and fine sand (60-125 µm) in the center at 30° dip. Waterfloods and oilfloods at low rate (3 ml/h) were imaged in 2D using a 22Na-labeled brine and a germanium detector, and flow was reversed to test 30° versus 150° dip. Oilfloods left higher residual brine in the crossbed, even at high capillary number. ECLIPSE simulations reproduced general trends and breakthrough but underestimated heterogeneity effects owing to numerical diffusion, while grid and relative permeability sensitivity were minor.

## The Effects of Wettability on Three-Phase Flow in Porous Media: Basic Theory and Network Modelling Results

- ID: SCA2001-36, year: 2001
- Authors: M.I.J. van Dijke, K.S. Sorbie
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: three-phase flow, pore network modelling, WAG injection, mixed wettability, multiple displacements, wetting films
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-36)

A 3D three-phase pore network model with mixed wettability, multiple displacements and variable outlet pressures simulates WAG; wetting films and multiple displacements strongly affect residual oil.

Existing three-phase network models mostly treat strongly water-wet or oil-wet media, so the authors develop a capillary-dominated 3D network simulator with pore-to-pore wettability variation. ## THE POTENTIAL EFFECT OF SOLUTION SEAMS ON RESERVOIR PERFORMANCE IN A SANDSTONE RESERVOIR A case study from an Ashgillan (Late Ordovician) reservoir in North Africa

- ID: SCA2001-56, year: 2001
- Authors: Khalifa Mohammed, Patrick Corbett, Dave Bowen, Andy Gardiner, Jim Buckman
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: solution seams, stylolites, probe permeametry, hydraulic flow units, vertical transmissibility, waterflood simulation
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-56.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-56)

Low-amplitude solution seams in a North African Ordovician sandstone have permeability of 1-6 mD and cut vertical permeability tenfold; simulations show seams parallel to bedding delay water breakthrough, crossing seams hurt recovery.

Low-amplitude stylolitic solution seams (1-2 per foot) occur in an Ashgillan sandstone reservoir in North Africa and may act as vertical flow barriers. Seams were characterised by ESEM, thin section, probe permeametry and core plug data (harmonic and arithmetic averaging methods), plus hydraulic unit classification, where rare HU7 plugs represent the seams. Seam permeability is 1-6 mD compared with 100-1000 mD in the matrix, with probe and plug estimates agreeing (e.g. 5 vs 5.2 mD, 2.17 vs 1.2 mD). Upscaling showed effective kh was little affected while kv dropped by an order of magnitude. In 2D simulations, seams parallel to bedding reduced gravity slumping, gave more piston-like sweep and later water breakthrough, whereas seams crossing the bedding gave earlier breakthrough, trapped oil and lower recovery.

## Visualization of Three Phases in Porous Media Using Micro Computed Tomography

- ID: SCA2003-21, year: 2003
- Authors: Freddy E. Alvarado, Abraham S. Grader, Ozgen Karacan, Phillip M. Halleck.
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: micro computed tomography, three-phase flow, intermediate phase, spreading coefficient, pore-scale saturation, image partitioning
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-21.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-21)

Micro-CT at 9 micron resolution maps water, benzyl alcohol and decane in sand, confirming benzyl alcohol as the intermediate phase predicted by the spreading coefficient.

The paper addresses the lack of three-dimensional pore-scale data on three-phase fluid distribution. A glass tube (7 mm ID) of loose quartz sand (about 1 mm grains) was scanned with a micro-focus x-ray CT at 145 kV, with 9x9x21 micron voxels.
