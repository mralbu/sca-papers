## Mechanisms of Oil Recovery by Carbonated Water Injection

- ID: SCA2009-26, year: 2009
- Authors: Mehran Sohrabi, Masoud Riazi, Mahmoud Jamiolahmady, Shaun Ireland, Chris Brown
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water injection, micromodel, oil swelling, CO2 dissolution, tertiary recovery, pore-scale visualisation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-26)

High-pressure micromodel tests show tertiary carbonated water injection recovers extra oil from n-decane (32.7%) and viscous oil (11.8%) mainly through oil swelling and coalescence.

Direct CO2 flooding suffers from poor sweep and water shielding; carbonated water injection (CWI) is proposed as an alternative that also stores CO2. Two water-wet glass micromodel experiments at 2000 psia and 38 °C compared n-decane (light oil) and a 16.5 cP mineral oil, with waterflood, CWI, then a second waterflood to strip CO2 and obtain dead-oil saturation. For n-decane, waterflood reduced oil saturation from 67% to 49%, and CWI gave a further 16% PV, i.e. 32.7% of the waterflood residual oil. For viscous oil, saturation went from 76.4% to 56.7% after waterflood and a further 6.7% PV (11.8%) was recovered by CWI. Swelling factors were about 94-105% for n-decane and 23% for viscous oil, so swelling and ganglion coalescence dominate in light oil, while viscosity reduction matters more in viscous oil.

## Mechanisms of Extra-Heavy Oil Recovery by Gravity-Stable CO2 Injection

- ID: SCA2008-20, year: 2008
- Authors: Mehran Sohrabi, Alireza Emadi, Mahmoud Jamiolahmady, Shaun Ireland, Chris Brown
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: heavy oil, CO2 injection, micromodel visualisation, oil viscosity reduction, double displacement, asphaltene
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-20)

High-pressure micromodel experiments show gravity-stable CO2 injection dilutes extra-heavy oil, enabling large additional recovery in subsequent waterflooding, while water presence hinders CO2 performance.

Non-thermal recovery of extra-heavy oil by CO2 is poorly understood at the pore scale. A water-wet, rock-look-alike glass micromodel (4 x 0.7 cm, 50 µm deep) was run vertically at 600 psig and 44 °C with an 11.5 °API oil (5500 cp at 60 °C, 11.6 wt% asphaltenes). The sequence was oil flood, 3-day waterflood, 2-day CO2 injection from the top, water, a second 24 h CO2 period, and final water, all at 0.01 cm3/hr (Ca 2.5E-7). Waterflood recovered little oil, but CO2 diluted the oil, turning it from black to brown, and the following water injection mobilised much additional oil. Water layers separating oil from CO2 reduced dilution. The second CO2 period gave little recovery, with oil darkening and shrinking by vaporisation. No major asphaltene precipitation was seen, and CO2 advanced by double displacement.

## Pore Scale Investigation of Carbonated Water Injection with and without Gravity

- ID: SCA2016-037, year: 2016
- Authors: S. Mahdavi, L.A James, T.E Johansen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water injection, micromodel, residual oil saturation, gravity effect, capillary and Bond number, oil film flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-037.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-037)

Glass micromodel experiments compare water flooding and carbonated water injection in horizontal and vertical orientations and at two rates; CWI lowers residual oil to about 50% versus 64% for water flooding.

The study examines carbonated water injection (CWI) for oil recovery at pore scale using etched homogeneous glass micromodels (450 D, 30% porosity) at 21 °C and 2.1 MPa. Water flooding and secondary CWI were run in horizontal and vertical orientations at production rates of 0.0008 and 0.004 cc/min, with image analysis of residual oil saturation. Gravity caused earlier breakthrough in the vertical micromodel (CW moved downward), while the horizontal case distributed CW more evenly. After about 7 PV, residual oil reached about 50% for CWI regardless of orientation or rate, versus 64% for water flooding. Lower production rates gave better sweep and lower residual oil after breakthrough. Trapped oil in throats was lower after CWI, attributed to oil film flow and reconnection from CO2 transfer to the oil.

## Carbonated Water Flood: What We Observed in Sand Pack Experiments

- ID: SCA2011-34, year: 2011
- Authors: Yannong Dong, Claudia Ishizawa, Ed Lewis, Birol Dindoruk
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water flooding, sand pack, CO2 solubility, oil viscosity reduction, injection rate, enhanced oil recovery
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-34)

Eight sand pack floods with viscous oil compare carbonated water and water flooding at four injection rates; carbonated water improves recovery and 2 PV/day is judged optimal.

Literature data on carbonated water flooding (CWF) are inconsistent, so a multi-year project began with Phase I sand pack floods testing injection speed. Eight packs (porosity about 0.30, permeability 1.7-3.7 D) were flooded with 70.7 cp Gulf of Mexico dead oil at 600 psig and 104°F using DI water carbonated to an average 17.6 cc/cc (99 SCF/STB). In four groups, one pack received water then carbonated water (tertiary) and one received carbonated water only (secondary), at 15, 1, 4 and 2 PV/day. Recovery improvement over waterflood ranged from 0.03 PV at 1 PV/day to 0.35 PV at 15 PV/day. Water broke through early (≤0.2 PV) at 15 and 4 PV/day, indicating unstable displacement. 2 PV/day was chosen as the representative optimal rate, since high rates give artificially high recovery that is not field-scalable. Pressure drop fell until breakthrough, which is explained by total mobility behavior.

## Improved Oil Recovery and Injectivity by Carbonated Water Injection

- ID: SCA2012-05, year: 2012
- Authors: M. Sohrabi, M. Tavakolian, A. Emadi, M. Jami, S. Ireland
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: carbonated water injection, coreflood, tertiary recovery, water breakthrough, injectivity, Clashach sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-05)

Coreflood tests on Clashach sandstone show carbonated water injection, secondary or tertiary, increases oil recovery, delays water breakthrough and lowers differential pressure versus plain waterflood.

Carbonated water injection (CWI) is proposed as a way to raise recovery using little CO2 where gas flooding is impractical. Three corefloods were run on companion Clashach sandstone cores (about 1100-1400 md, 32 cm long) with a South American crude (28.55 API, 8.54 cp) and synthetic seawater at 2500 psia and 100 F, injecting at 5 cm3/hr. Waterflood recovered about 41-43% OIIP, and subsequent tertiary CWI added 24% OIIP, with a repeat test at lower Swi (21%) giving similar recovery. Secondary CWI recovered over 55% OIIP, about 12.5% more than waterflood after 2.5 PV, with later water breakthrough and higher recovery at breakthrough. Differential pressure was lower for CWI despite higher viscosity (0.88 vs 0.80 cp), indicating better injectivity, though tertiary CWI needed much larger injected volumes.

## Using MRI to Study in Situ Oil Recovery During CO2 Injection in Carbonates

- ID: SCA2008-41, year: 2008
- Authors: A. Brautaset, G. Ersland, A. Graue, J. Stevens, J. Howard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2 injection, MRI saturation imaging, chalk, supercritical CO2, oil swelling, wettability
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-41.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-41)

MRI monitored waterflood and miscible liquid or supercritical CO2 injection in four Portland chalk cores at different wettabilities; CO2 after waterflood gave the highest recovery, up to 93.6% OOIP.

The study examines in situ displacement during waterflooding and subsequent miscible CO2 injection in low-permeability Portland chalk (about 4 mD, 48% porosity) with n-decane and D2O brine. Four cores with Amott indices from 0.15 to 1.00 were flooded at 2 ml/h with liquid CO2 (23 °C) or supercritical CO2 (40 °C) at 8274 kPa, imaged with high-resolution MRI. Images showed oil swelling, oil bank formation and piston-like CO2 displacement. CO2 injected at Swi gave the lowest total recovery (67.1% OOIP), while waterflood followed by supercritical CO2 in the less water-wet core P2 gave 93.6% OOIP with Sor of 4.8% PV. Liquid CO2 gave 81.0% (water-wet) and 74.6% (near neutral-wet). MRI signal vanished behind the CO2 front, so saturations during CO2 flooding relied on material balance; oil-CO2 MRI properties need further characterization.

## Pore-scale experimental investigation of in-situ wettability and displacement mechanisms governing WAG in oil-wet carbonates

- ID: SCA2019-038, year: 2019
- Authors: Ziqiang Qin, Maziar Arshadi, Mohammad Piri
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: WAG injection, micro-CT imaging, oil-wet carbonate, pore-fluid occupancy, in-situ contact angle, residual oil saturation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2019/SCA2019-038.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2019-038)

Micro-CT imaging of a miniature Fond du Lac carbonate core during aging and three WAG cycles shows how gas shielding improves sweep and recovery, diminishing after two cycles.

Pore-scale mechanisms of WAG injection in oil-wet carbonates are poorly understood. A 4.9 mm diameter Fond du Lac carbonate miniature core was flooded at 90°C and 600 psi inside a micro-CT scanner. After primary drainage (Swi 0.241) and about 15 days of dynamic aging with crude oil, three waterflood/nitrogen-injection cycles were imaged in 3D. Waterflooding left 0.445 residual oil saturation (41.4% recovery), and the first gas injection lowered it to 0.284. The second cycle reduced it to 0.188 and 0.122, and the third cycle to 0.121 and 0.105, so incremental recovery faded quickly. Trapped gas partially blocked earlier channels, opening new brine and gas flow paths and raising sweep efficiency, while remaining oil sat in small, poorly connected pores.

- Authors: A. Cable, M. Spearing, J. Bahamaish, Y. Dabbour, Z. Kalam
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: miscible gas injection, WAG, water blocking, tertiary gas flood, minimum miscibility pressure, carbonate composite core
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-05)

Reservoir-condition miscible gas, water, tertiary gas and WAG floods on a 52 cm carbonate composite show recovery rising linearly with initial gas slug size, with water blocking.

Miscible gas displacement data were needed for a low-permeability carbonate field (about 4 mD, 16% porosity). Floods were run at reservoir conditions (129°C, 28 bar above MMP) on a 52 cm composite of 10 plugs (1.66 mD) aged with live oil, at 3 mL/h. ## Can Field Wide Variations in Water Injectivity During Wag Be Explained by Differences in Rock Type?

- ID: SCA2002-20, year: 2002
- Authors: Jairam Kamath, Frank Nakagawa
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: WAG injectivity, CO2 flooding, asphaltene precipitation, chase brine, trapped CO2, San Andres dolomite
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-20)

Corefloods on McElroy field rock types showed no rock-type explanation for WAG water injectivity loss; a crude/CO2 soaked core showed injectivity loss from asphaltene plugging.

Some McElroy field (West Texas) injectors lost chase brine injectivity severely during WAG, while others did not. Conventional corefloods were run on composite cores of four rock types (good and medium quality moldic, fine intercrystalline, poor quality) from a good and a poor injector, with CO2-brine, toluene and crude systems at ambient and reservoir conditions (88 °F, 2000 psi). Chase live brine injectivity was comparable to the secondary waterflood (about 0.15 end point) and rose toward unity as trapped CO2 dissolved. CO2 injectivity exceeded waterflood injectivity, and no systematic rock-type or cleaned versus restored difference explained the field variation. A core soaked for 3 weeks after alternating 0.03 PV slugs of CO2 and crude (0.7 PV each) gave end point injectivities of 0.04 (live) and 0.3 (dead brine), versus 0.1 and 0.7 for the non-soaked rock. This suggests poor sweep plus heavy-phase (asphaltene) precipitation are necessary for injectivity loss.

## Effects of Paraffin Wax Precipitation During Cold Water Injection in a Fractured Carbonate Reservoir

- ID: SCA2004-28, year: 2004
- Authors: D. Maloney, A. Oesthus
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: paraffin wax precipitation, cold seawater injection, cloud point, fractured chalk, ESEM, water relative permeability
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-28)

Coreflods, ESEM imaging and thermal simulation show wax precipitation from cold seawater injection in chalk barely harms injectivity or recovery, so heating injection water is not justified.

Cold seawater injection into a 130 °C fractured chalk reservoir may precipitate paraffin wax below the ~25 °C cloud point, raising concerns about injectivity and recovery. Waterfloods were run on an analog outcrop chalk plug (1.5 mD, 35% porosity) cooled stepwise from 66 to 10 °C at Sor, and on two reservoir plugs (2.8 and 1.6 mD) cooled to 10 °C at high initial oil saturation. Cooling at Sor raised brine saturation (62 to 64%) through oil shrinkage and increased brine relative permeability from 0.05 to about 0.10. Statically cooled, oil-rich plugs needed threshold pressures of 0.6-0.8 bar to start brine flow but then recovered oil efficiently. ESEM/EDS showed wax as uneven masses adhering to mineral surfaces, more abundant at higher oil saturation. Thermal simulations showed the cold front lags the waterflood front, with under 0.3% of remaining moveable oil in wax-prone cold zones, though cold regions matter for future EOR.

Describes PVT and composite-core flood experiments on CO2 injection into depleted gas condensate, showing condensate vaporization and CO2 breakthrough after about 0.9 PV, supporting enhanced gas recovery and storage.

The paper outlines laboratory work preceding compositional simulation of CO2 sequestration with enhanced gas recovery in depleted gas condensate reservoirs. ## Measurement and Modeling of Gas-Oil Miscibility for Improved Oil Recovery

- ID: SCA2006-54, year: 2006
- Authors: Subhash C. Ayirala, Dandina N. Rao
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: vanishing interfacial tension, minimum miscibility pressure, Parachor model, pendent drop, slim-tube, CO2 injection
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-54.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-54)

Vanishing interfacial tension measurements on live decane-CO2 and Rainbow Keg River crude systems determine gas-oil miscibility quickly, agree with slim-tube data, and are matched by a mechanistic Parachor model.

Slim-tube miscibility tests take 4-5 weeks, so the paper evaluates the faster vanishing interfacial tension (VIT) technique, which extrapolates gas-oil IFT to zero. IFT was measured by pendent drop (RKR live crude with C2+-enriched gas at 2150 psi, 190°F) and capillary rise (live decane-CO2 at 160°F). For RKR oil, VIT gave a minimum miscible composition of 51.2 mole% C2+, versus 54.4 mole% predicted by the mechanistic Parachor model (about 6.2% deviation). For live decane-CO2, VIT MMP was 1764 psi against a literature slim-tube value of 1685 psi, and the model predicted 1758 psi (0.3% deviation). The authors conclude that VIT is validated and that the modified Parachor model predicts miscibility well despite small IFT deviations.

## Oil Recovery During CO2-Foam Injection in Fractured Chalk Rock at Reservoir Conditions

- ID: SCA2009-27, year: 2009
- Authors: John Zuta, Ingebret Fjelde, Roman Berenblyum
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2 foam, fractured chalk, foaming agent retention, STARS simulation, oil recovery, supercritical CO2
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-27)

CO2-foam coinjection in artificially fractured Liege chalk at 340 bar and 55°C recovers far more oil than pure CO2, matched by STARS simulation.

CO2 flooding in fractured carbonates suffers early breakthrough, and little is known about CO2-foam in fractured chalk. Fractured models were made by drilling a 0.3 cm hole in Liege chalk plugs and packing it with glass beads (effective permeability about 20 mD), with non-fractured plugs as references. Pre-formed foam (1 wt% ethoxylated sulphonate, 90% gas fraction) or pure CO2 was injected at 340 bar and 55°C after waterflooding. Oil recovery as % of Sorw was 83.9% for foam in the fractured model versus 31.2% for pure CO2, and 90.1% (foam) versus 81.8% (CO2) in non-fractured plugs. Foaming agent retention was 1.05-2.32 mg/g in non-fractured plugs (higher with oil) and 0.73-0.85 mg/g in fractured models, indicating slower transport from fracture to matrix. STARS history matching gave 80.4% and 84.9% recovery, and simulations showed that raising effective permeability from 20 to 50 mD lowered recovery rate and ultimate recovery.

## Permian Basin: Historical Review of CO2 EOR Processes, Current Challenges and Improved Optimization Possibilities

- ID: SCA2009-38, year: 2009
- Authors: Abiodun Matthew Amao, Shameem Siddiqui
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: CO2 EOR, minimum miscibility pressure, Permian Basin, miscible displacement, WAG injection, process optimization
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2009/SCA2009-38.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2009-38)

Reviews CO2 miscible EOR history in the Permian Basin, identifies operational and MMP-measurement gaps, and proposes a systems approach to optimizing recovery.

The paper reviews the history of CO2 miscible EOR in the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico, where low-cost natural CO2, favorable oil and high residual oil in place drove adoption. It tabulates operational challenges (sweep efficiency, early breakthrough, corrosion, asphaltenes, scale, injectivity) against industry solutions such as WAG, foams, 4-D seismic and horizontal wells. Literature and field reports indicate many projects perform below coreflood and slim-tube expectations, leaving billions of barrels unrecovered. They also note that there is no analytical basis for the injected CO2 volume or injection scheme. They call for a systems approach covering reservoir characterization, management, and flood pattern evaluation, and for standardized, field-representative MMP measurement.

Tertiary liquid and supercritical CO2 floods in Rørdal chalk and Edwards limestone show heterogeneity dominates recovery; wettability effects are unclear, with slightly oil-wet limestone somewhat favorable.

## Performance of Swag Injection Versus Alternating and Continuous Injection of Water and Gas in Low Gas-Oil Ift and Mixed-Wet Systems

- ID: SCA2012-17, year: 2012
- Authors: M. Sohrabi, S.M. Fatemi, S.A. Farzaneh, M. Jami, S. Ireland, K. Ahmed
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: SWAG injection, WAG injection, mixed-wet sandstone, near-miscible gas injection, coreflood, injectivity
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-17.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-17)

Near-miscible coreflood tests on mixed-wet sandstones show WAG starting with water recovers most oil, while SWAG underperforms waterflooding and causes high pressure drop.

The study compares continuous gas, waterflood, WAG and SWAG injection in mixed-wet 65 mD and 1000 mD sandstone cores. Tests used a methane/n-butane oil and gas system near its critical point (12.69 MPa, 38°C, IFT about 0.04 mN/m) with x-ray saturation monitoring. SWAG raised pressure drop from about 20 psi (waterflood) to 70-80 psi in the 65 mD core, indicating injectivity problems, and performed somewhat better in the 1000 mD core.

## Spontaneous CO2 Emulsion Generation; a New Approach for Mobility Control

- ID: SCA2013-026, year: 2013
- Authors: Alireza Emadi, Mehran Sohrabi
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2 emulsion, spontaneous nucleation, mobility control, micromodel, liquid CO2 surfactant coinjection, apparent viscosity
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-026.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-026)

Micromodel and coreflood tests show liquid CO2 with an anionic surfactant spontaneously forms emulsion droplets, giving very high apparent viscosity and improved oil recovery even at high oil saturation.

CO2 foam mobility control normally fails deep in reservoirs because low velocities and high oil saturation hinder bubble generation. The authors combined high-pressure micromodel visualisation with corefloods in a 2.5 D Fife sandstone core, using an anionic surfactant (HW-A) and a 617 cp heavy crude. Co-injection with surfactant gave apparent viscosities of about 4 cp (vapour CO2), 40 cp (SC CO2) and nearly 3000 cp (liquid CO2). In tertiary corefloods, CO2/surfactant co-injection raised recovery from 28% to 56% OOIP after 1 PV and recovered about 24% of Sorw after 0.4 PV versus about 12% for CO2 alone, with pressure drop one to two orders of magnitude higher.

## Carbonated Water Injection for EOR in One Dimensional Flow with Constant Pressure

- ID: SCA2013-090, year: 2013
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: carbonated water injection, compositional model, CO2 partitioning, oil viscosity reduction, interfacial tension, IMPES
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-090.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-090)

A 1D three-component compositional model of carbonated water injection at constant pressure shows extra oil recovery over water flooding, driven mainly by CO2-induced oil viscosity reduction.

The paper addresses carbonated water injection (CWI) as an alternative to CO2 flooding, which suffers from poor sweep. A one-dimensional, three-component (water, oil, CO2), two-phase compositional model with no free gas was built and solved with block-centred IMPES. Diatomite is a rare, high-porosity, low-permeability reservoir rock, and the study compares Lark reservoir diatomite from the Norwegian shelf with Fur outcrop diatomite from Denmark. The reservoir rock contains illite, kaolinite, muscovite and pyrite, with 48.6% porosity and 0.25 mD. ESH pyrolysis on 50 mg ground rock is converted to oil saturation and used to correct helium porosity for residual hydrocarbons, validated on Nini West plugs.

Oil saturation is normally obtained from core plugs by extensive solvent extraction, while ESH pyrolysis gives only oil fractions in vol.%. The authors derive equations converting ESH vol.% to saturation using porosity, and a porosity correction for hydrocarbons left in cleaned plugs after helium porosimetry. They apply this to thirteen glauconitic sandstone plugs from the Nini-4 well (Nini West, Danish North Sea): preserved, brine-stored, cleaned by three methods, restored, and scCO2-flooded. ## Evolution Mechanism and Application of Miscible Zone in CO₂ Flooding

- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2026/SCA2026-003.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2026-003)

Compares four MMP methods, defines CO₂ miscible zone edges, phase intervals and a miscibility degree, and validates a coupled flow-phase model against BLX field GOR data.

The paper addresses the lack of unified criteria for the miscible zone and degree of miscibility in CO₂ flooding. MMP of XH crude oil was measured by slim-tube (13.01 MPa), bubble rising (13.21), vanishing IFT (13.42) and capillary rising (13.84), with the faster static methods proposed to bracket MMP before slim-tube testing. Long 20 m slim-tube floods at 15 and 20 MPa (50 °C) defined the leading edge by CO₂ breakthrough (0.8 PV vs 0.5 PV) and the trailing edge by a GOR slope rise and increased C₂–C₁₅ in produced oil; recovery rose from 91.56% to 92.33%. CT online scanning showed the miscible zone grows with pressure, GOR-based intervals were classified from black oil to CO₂ main flow, and a multi-parameter miscibility degree was defined. A 1D model coupling two-phase flow, dispersion, adsorption and PR-EOS reproduced field GOR in the BLX fractured buried-hill reservoir with over 87% agreement.
