## An Integrated Approach to Assess the Petrophysical Properties of Rocks Altered by Rock/Fluid Interactions (CO2 Injection)

- ID: SCA2005-03, year: 2005
- Authors: S. Bekri, O. Vizika
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2 storage, rock/fluid interaction, carbonate dissolution, pore network model, mercury porosimetry, permeability-porosity evolution
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-03.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-03)

Homogeneous acid alteration of two carbonates shows permeability gains of 30-70% for ~2 porosity units; pore network modeling identifies the dissolution scenario matching the data.

Far-field CO2-induced dissolution is mimicked by placing static acid (pH 1) quasi-instantaneously in the pore space of thin carbonate slices at Swi, repeating treatments and measuring permeability between each. Two carbonates were tested: a homogeneous low-permeability limestone (1.76 to 2.35 md, porosity 22.9 to 24.5%) and a bimodal carbonate (468 to 762 md, 27.7 to 29.5%), giving 30% and 70% permeability gains. Mercury porosimetry before and after showed changes mainly in the larger pore radii, and the small pores were unaffected, probably because of irreducible water. A 3D pore network model calibrated on Rock 1 (mercury Pc and gas/water relative permeability) was used to test five dissolution scenarios. The scenario with reaction-limited dissolution in which pore bodies grow twice as fast as throats (S4) best reproduced the permeability-porosity trend and the pore size distribution change.

## Displacement and Mass Transfer of CO2/Brine in Sandstone

- ID: SCA2011-05, year: 2011
- Authors: H. Ott, S. Berg, S. Oedai
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2/brine drainage, CT saturation monitoring, history matching relative permeability, viscous fingering stability, CO2 dissolution mass transfer, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2011/SCA2011-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2011-05)

Unsteady-state CT-monitored CO2/brine floods in Berea sandstone give drainage kr and Pc by history matching, show stable displacement with channeling, and quantify CO2 dissolution effects.

The study addresses CO2/brine displacement and mass transfer relevant to geological storage. Unsteady-state core floods on a 380 mD Berea core at 45°C and 100 bar were monitored by medical CT, and kr and Pc were obtained by history matching, benchmarked against a decane/brine experiment on the same core. CO2/brine gave Corey exponents of about 2.5 (brine) and 3 (CO2), differing from decane/brine (4.5 and 2.8), suggesting more intermediate-wet behavior, and brine kr was higher than published steady-state data. Buckley-Leverett stability analysis (shock-front mobility ratio about 1.06) and simulations showed the displacement is not viscous fingering; the observed channeling stems from minor heterogeneity, best matched with a high-K, low-Pc layer. Unsaturated-fluid floods showed small water evaporation near the inlet and CO2 dissolution of about 0.09 PV in brine, and brine imbibition at residual CO2 showed dissolution-dominated CO2 loss (0.19 PV vs 0.31 PV expected).

## Experimental Investigation of Cooling Effects Resulting from Injecting High Pressure Liquid or Supercritical CO2 into a Low Pressure Gas Reservoir

- ID: SCA2008-40, year: 2008
- Authors: Daniel R. Maloney, Marcos Briceno
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Joule-Thomson cooling, CO2 injection, depleted gas reservoir, hydrate formation, CO2 phase change, slim tube
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-40.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-40)

Flooding a 50 ft sand-packed tube with 3,500 psig CO2 showed Joule-Thomson cooling is masked by heat transfer at 198 °F, but liquid-to-vapor transition at room temperature caused ice/hydrate blockage.

Injecting high-pressure CO2 into depleted gas reservoirs may cause Joule-Thomson cooling and hydrate or ice formation that impairs injectivity. The authors flooded an insulated 50 ft slim tube packed with 156 mD sand (80% methane, 20% brine, 200-500 psig) with 3,500 psig room-temperature CO2, measuring pressure and temperature at nine stations. In the 198 °F oven tests, heat transfer masked Joule-Thomson cooling and temperatures dropped by less than 10 °F; methane compression at the front even raised temperature. In cool-down tests, temperatures down to 30 °F occurred where CO2 changed from liquid to vapor, without flow impairment. In room-temperature tests, blockage formed within minutes near the liquid-to-vapor transition (about 122 cm from the outlet), cutting mass flow by 98%, and persisted until heated; later problems may arise as the near-well region cools.

## SCA2010-20

- ID: SCA2010-20, year: 2010
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: salt precipitation, CO2 sequestration, formation dry-out, micro-CT, supercritical H2S, capillary backflow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-20.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-20)

Core floods show dry CO2 injection into brine-saturated sandstone causes local salt precipitation that cuts absolute permeability but not CO2 effective permeability, while SC H2S in dry limestone produces water.

The paper examines two consequences of formation dry-out from injecting dry or under-saturated acid-forming fluids such as CO2 and H2S. Dry supercritical CO2 was injected into brine-saturated Berea sandstone (500 mD, 22% porosity) at 45°C and 100 bar, with micro-CT monitoring of saturation and precipitated salt. Mean solid-salt saturation was 4.6% and locally up to 20%, indicating capillary-driven brine backflow. Absolute permeability fell by a factor of 4, yet effective CO2 permeability rose by a factor of 5, and salt formed near CO2 flow paths, leaving them open. Tough2 simulations reproduced local precipitation through backflow but cannot capture the experimental saturation gradient because evaporation kinetics are not included. In the second experiment, SC H2S injected into dry Euville limestone (110°C, 110 bar) produced CO2 and about 1 ml of liquid water through a dissolution reaction, with dark sulfide-like alteration of clays. This shows that a dry zone is not necessarily chemically inert.

## Co-Injected CO2-Brine Interactions with Indiana Limestone

- ID: SCA2003-19, year: 2003
- Authors: Reid B. Grigg, Robert K. Svec
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2-brine coinjection, carbonate dissolution, carbonate precipitation, Indiana limestone, back-scattered electron imaging, tracer metals
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-19.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-19)

Co-injecting CO2 and tracer-doped brine through Indiana limestone at reservoir conditions caused dissolution and a solution channel, while Mn- and Sr-rich carbonate deposits formed near the channel tip.

Injectivity changes in CO2 WAG and sequestration projects may reflect carbonate dissolution and precipitation. A two-segment composite Indiana limestone core (56.5 cm, 17.35% porosity) was flooded at 100°F and 2000 psig outlet pressure with 1:1 CO2 and brine for about 155 PV. The brine carried Mn, Mg and Sr chlorides as tracers to give new deposits a compositional contrast with the rock. Porosity and permeability first decreased, then rose; a solution channel formed in segment A and permeability reached 1978 md. Chemical analysis and BSEI showed Mn up to 500 times baseline and Sr about twice baseline, peaking at 15 cm near the channel tip. Deposits were Mn-rich (about 66% Mn-carbonate) and showed zoned euhedral growth, indicating that dissolution and deposition occur in close proximity.

## A Special Core Analysis Approach to Create Gas Hydrates in Sediment Samples

- ID: SCA2005-29, year: 2005
- Authors: Sverre Hetland
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: gas hydrate formation, porous plate method, Bentheim sandstone, resistivity monitoring, gas consumption, hydrate saturation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-29.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-29)

Develops a porous-plate, semi-dynamic recirculation method to form methane hydrates in Bentheim sandstone, reaching up to 45% pore volume, quantified from pressure and gas consumption.

Realistic hydrate-bearing sediment samples are scarce, so the authors adapted special core analysis equipment to form methane hydrate in water-saturated Bentheim sandstone plugs. Static tests and dynamic gas injection gave only minor hydrate because a hydrate film at the gas-water interface halts further formation. A semi-dynamic method with a water-wet semipermeable porous plate at the outlet, with continuous gas injection at gradually rising differential pressure, caused repeated hydrate film formation and collapse and produced significant hydrate. Tests ran at 60-65 bar and 1-2°C in a closed recirculation rig, with system pressure, differential pressure and resistivity logged. Hydrate amount was calculated from gas consumption via the ideal gas law and sI hydrate stoichiometry (7.68 CH4 per 46 H2O, density 0.914 g/ml). Hydrate saturations up to 45% of pore volume were achieved, and hydrate appears to be homogeneously distributed, located at the gas-water interface.

- ID: SCA2008-53, year: 2008
- Authors: Hiroshi Okabe, Yoshihiro Tsuchiya
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: residual CO2 saturation, capillary trapping, X-ray CT saturation monitoring, carbonate heterogeneity, CO2-saturated brine, CO2 storage
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-53.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-53)

X-ray CT-monitored CO2-brine coreflood on a heterogeneous Middle Eastern carbonate shows CO2 flows through high-porosity zones and is trapped in lower-porosity zones, with 0.23 average residual saturation.

The study examines capillary trapping of CO2 in carbonate rock for saline aquifer storage. A 38 mm by 75 mm Middle Eastern carbonate plug (14.0% porosity, 6.7 md) was flooded with CO2 and brine at 40°C, 2,000 psig overburden and 1,420 psig pore pressure, with in-situ saturation monitored by X-ray CT using 15 wt% NaI-doped brine. The average trapped CO2 saturation was 0.23, excluding dissolved CO2, and average water saturation after CO2 flooding was about 0.6.

- ID: SCA2013-029, year: 2013
- Authors: H. Ott, C. H. Pentland, K. Eide-Engdahl, A. J. van der Linden, O. Gharbi, A. Makurat
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: CO2 sequestration, wormholing, calcite dissolution, reactive transport, CT imaging, Young's modulus
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-029.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-029)

CT-monitored core floods in Estaillades and Euville limestone show CO2-brine wormholing alters single- and two-phase flow, while only homogeneous dissolution markedly weakens elastic stiffness and strength.

Calcite dissolution during CO2 storage in carbonates may change flow fields and rock integrity. The authors ran CT-monitored single-phase tracer and CO2-brine drainage floods at 100 bar and 50 °C on Estaillades and Euville limestone, before and after reactive wormhole formation. ## Permeability Measurements on Hydrate-Bearing Sandstone Cores with Excess Water

- ID: SCA2016-082, year: 2016
- Authors: Stian Almenningen, Håkon Juliussen, Geir Ersland
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: methane hydrate, effective gas permeability, Bentheim sandstone, hydrate saturation, pore-filling hydrate growth, excess water
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-082.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-082)

Effective methane gas permeability was measured in hydrate-bearing Bentheim sandstone with excess water; permeability dropped from mD to μD below gas saturation 0.34-0.38, indicating pore-filling hydrate growth.

Permeability of hydrate-filled sediments controls gas production and CO2 injection, but data at intermediate-to-high hydrate saturations are scarce. Bentheim sandstone cores (porosity 0.22, 1.3-1.9 D) were brine-saturated to different levels, pressurized with methane at 8.3 MPa, and cooled to 4°C to form hydrate. Effective gas permeability was measured by Darcy's law at several low flow rates, with immobile water, giving hydrate saturations of 0.37-0.61. Gas relative permeability showed a transition at final gas saturation 0.34-0.38, from mD above to μD below, with reductions up to five orders of magnitude relative to the water-gas state. Cores with similar hydrate saturations differed widely in permeability, so permeability cannot be correlated to hydrate saturation alone when excess water and gas coexist. Comparison at equal gas saturation before and after hydrate formation points to pore-filling hydrate growth.

- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: fines migration, kaolinite, micro-PET imaging, DLVO theory, colloid filtration theory, ionic strength
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2022/SCA2022-39.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2022-39)

Micro-PET imaging of 64Cu-labeled kaolinite in Ottawa sand packs shows fines immobilized within ~2 cm and not remobilized by low salinity, unlike UV-Vis column results and models.

The study examines kaolinite fines migration in Ottawa sand packs under changing ionic strength, using UV-Vis effluent analysis, a 1D advection-dispersion-reaction model with colloid filtration theory and DLVO calculations, and micro-PET imaging of 64Cu-radiolabeled kaolinite. ## Diffusion measurements with hydrogen and methane through reservoir rock samples

- ID: SCA2023-032, year: 2023
- Authors: Julia Michelsen, Nils Langanke, Birger Hagemann, Sebastian Hogeweg, Leonhard Ganzer
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: hydrogen diffusion, underground hydrogen storage, effective diffusion coefficient, methane, water saturation, gas chromatography
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2023/SCA2023-032.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2023-032)

A novel pseudo-stationary one-chamber method measures hydrogen-methane effective diffusion in reservoir sandstones at storage conditions, finding trends with pressure and temperature that deviate from conventional correlations.

Underground hydrogen storage in depleted gas fields requires knowledge of H2-CH4 mixing, including molecular diffusion at reservoir conditions. A pseudo-stationary one-chamber diffusion cell was built: the sample and a large chamber are filled with hydrogen, methane is injected on the opposite side, and outflow composition is analysed by gas chromatograph. Effective diffusion coefficients were obtained by manually matching a 1D COMSOL model; 36 measurements on mostly sandstone samples covered 10–287.5 bar and 25–107 °C. Coefficients ranged from 5.0·10^-9 to 3.71·10^-7 m2/s and increased with porosity. For Bentheimer sandstone, D decreased with pressure up to about 75 bar and then increased, and decreased with temperature, both contrary to Fuller/Millington-Quirk correlations. Water saturation of 40% and 60% reduced D from 1.1·10^-7 to 4.0·10^-8 and 5.0·10^-9 m2/s. Three repeats gave a standard deviation of 5.6·10^-9 m2/s, and a Berea measurement was within the same order of magnitude as a literature value.

## Defining absolute permeability for CO2 injection projects: scope for increased economic storage capacity

- ID: SCA_2025_1037, year: 2025
- Authors: Jos G. Maas, Dario Santonico, Albert Hebing, Niels Springer
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: absolute permeability, Klinkenberg gas permeability, brine permeability, CO2 drainage relative permeability, Obernkirchener sandstone, CCS injectivity
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 14
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2025/SCA_2025_1037.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA_2025_1037)

Brine permeability in clean OBKN sandstone is statistically ~30% below gas permeability; the authors recommend normalising CO2-brine relative permeabilities to Klinkenberg gas permeability, which simulations show gives identical results.

Brine permeability is usually used to normalise CO2 drainage relative permeabilities, but gas permeability is often higher, giving kr above unity that commercial simulators reject. Four ceramic (corundum) plugs and four OBKN sandstone plugs were tested with Klinkenberg gas, light and viscous oil, and high- and low-salinity brine, with XCT homogeneity checks, QC re-measurements and t-test statistics. OBKN brine permeability (~7.7 mD) was 27–35% below gas permeability (~11.3 mD) regardless of salinity or aging, while oil and ceramic plug permeabilities matched gas permeability. Brine-oil differences were significant even at the 0.1% level, which the authors attribute to ionic/electrical double-layer effects. SCORES simulations of drainage with kro normalised to 100 mD (endpoint 1.63) or 166.7 mD gas permeability gave identical production, pressure and saturation profiles, so renormalising to gas permeability is recommended; Leverett-J capillary scaling needs an IFT adjustment. This could raise projected injectivity and improve CCS screening economics.

- Authors: Ali Al-Menhali, Samuel Krevor

- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-003.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-003)

Semi-dynamic CO2-brine capillary pressure measurements with X-ray CT on Berea sandstone, compared with MICP, give an effective core-scale contact angle of about 50° at 60 °C and 14 MPa.

Wettability of CO2-brine-rock systems affects CO2 storage and EOR, yet reported contact angles vary widely. ## Gas Relative Permeability Reduction Due to Waterblocking – a Laboratory Based Analysis

- ID: SCA2000-38, year: 2000
- Authors: Catherine Laroche, Jairam Kamath, Frank Nakagawa, Yanis Yortsos
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: waterblocking, gas relative permeability, evaporation regime, humid methane injection, gas condensate, methanol
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-38.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-38)

Humid methane floods of a liquid-saturated sandstone show displacement then long evaporation regimes; a model yields gas relative permeability, with evaporation driven by pressure gradient.

Waterblocking from aqueous completion fluids was suspected of reducing gas deliverability in a North Sea gas condensate field. Constant pressure-drop (10 psi) humid methane floods were run on a preserved 3-plug sandstone composite (16% porosity, 14 md, 16 cm) containing brine, methanol, isopropanol, toluene or brine-methanol mixtures, fully saturated or with trapped gas, for over 10,000 PV. NMR profiles and scaling analysis ruled out capillary end effects and viscous fingering, suggesting pore-level bypassing, and with trapped gas, evaporation of liquid rings reconnects trapped gas and raises Krg.

## Pore-scale Analysis of CO2-brine Displacement in Berea Sandstone and Its Implications to CO2 Injectivity

- ID: SCA2022-37, year: 2022
- Authors: Guangyuan Sun, Zhuang Sun, Andrew Fager, Bernd Crouse, Dassault Systèmes, San Francisco
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: lattice Boltzmann, CO2-brine relative permeability, wettability, viscosity ratio, CO2 injectivity, micro-CT Berea sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2022/SCA2022-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2022-37)

Lattice Boltzmann simulations on Berea micro-CT show viscosity ratio and wettability strongly affect CO2-brine drainage relative permeability, interfacial tension little; water-wet favors injectivity without fracturing.

The study examines how interfacial tension (30-45 mN/m), viscosity ratio (0.05-0.17) and wettability affect CO2-brine drainage relative permeability for CO2 storage. ## A Fast Method for Homogeneous Dissolution of Chalk Specimens for Laboratory Experiments – Documentation by X-Ray CT-Scanning and Scanning Electron Microscopy

- Authors: Claus Kjøller, Lykourgos Sigalas, Helle F. Christensen, Mona Minde
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: chalk dissolution, retarded acid, AcidgenTM FG, X-ray CT histogram, FEG-SEM, Stevns chalk
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-072.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-072)

Heat-activated AcidgenTM FG dissolves Stevns outcrop and reservoir chalk plugs homogeneously, verified by CT and SEM, whereas acetic acid dissolves them inhomogeneously from the outside.

Laboratory studies of chalk dissolution effects on rock mechanics are hampered by inhomogeneous dissolution. The authors treated Stevns outcrop chalk plugs (30 most homogeneous of 98) and 28 reservoir chalk plugs with retarded acid AcidgenTM FG (2, 5, 15% v/v) or acetic acid, using vacuum saturation, 48 h heating at 50°C, flushing, drying and repeated cycles. A laboratory workflow combining triaxial, poroelastic and supercritical CO2 flood tests with resistivity and acoustics on Buff Berea; Biot-Gassmann-Brie fits velocities, and a modified Reuss model is proposed.

The study develops an integrated lab workflow for CO2 sequestration characterization at deep aquifer conditions. - ID: SCA2000-01, year: 2000

- Method: laboratory experiment

Relative permeability tests on Southwest Andrews carbonate cores showed core damage in unflushed samples; mild miscible flushing and non-scaling brine avoided it in unsteady and steady state tests.

Relative permeability testing of Wolfcamp and Canyon carbonate plugs from three Southwest Andrews Field wells was hampered by low conductivity and heterogeneity; only about 10% of 184 plugs were successfully tested.
