## Trapped Gas, Relative Permeability and Residual Oil Saturation of an Oil-Wet Sandstone1

- ID: SCA2000-11, year: 2000
- Authors: J.G. Kralik, L.J. Manak, A.P. Spence, G.R. Jerauld
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, trapped gas saturation, oil-wet sandstone, water-alternating-gas, residual oil saturation, CT scanning
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-11)

Corefloods on preserved oil-wet sandstone show three-phase trapped gas is about half the two-phase value, trapped gas does not lower residual oil, and it suppresses water relative permeability.

Three-phase flow in native-state oil-wet sandstones is poorly covered by existing relative permeability models. The authors ran waterfloods, gasfloods and waterfloods to trapped gas on preserved composite cores from two sands (A-sand and C-sand) at reservoir conditions, with JBN relative permeabilities, tracer tests and CT scanning. Three-phase trapped gas was about 11-13%, roughly half the two-phase values (about 20-22% at similar maximum gas saturation). A lower-rate flood and CT scans showed this was not capillary desaturation or gas redistribution. Residual oil to waterflood was unchanged by trapped gas, while water relative permeability fell markedly and showed hysteresis and water trapping. Compositional WAG simulations with the generalized Jerauld model showed that lower trapped gas reduces retained gas by about 9-35% depending on the process and reservoir description, with little change in oil recovery.

## Three-Phase Relative Permeabilities and Trapped Gas Measurements Related to Wag Processes

- ID: SCA1994-21, year: 1994
- Authors: Arne Skauge, Johne A. Larsen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, WAG, trapped gas saturation, relative permeability hysteresis, Amott wettability index, unsteady-state displacement
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-21.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-21)

Unsteady-state gas and water injections on Berea and North Sea sandstones give three-phase relative permeabilities, trapped gas relations and a new gas hysteresis model for WAG.

The paper addresses microscopic displacement efficiency and three-phase relative permeability in WAG processes. Unsteady-state gas and water injection sequences were run on 40-100 cm composite cores of Berea, silanized Berea and four North Sea reservoir sandstones (30-2000 mD) at ambient or reservoir conditions (about 300 bar), with wettability characterized by Amott index. Residual oil saturation in three-phase flow was significantly lower than in two-phase waterflood or gas injection. Wetting-phase kr depended mainly on its own saturation, whereas gas kr showed strong hysteresis, lower for decreasing gas saturation, regardless of wettability. Trapped gas depended strongly on maximum gas saturation (0.17-0.21 in WAG tests) and fitted a Land-type equation; a new hysteresis model implemented in ECLIPSE reasonably predicted WAG effluent and pressure data.

## Residual Oil Saturation and Multiphase Relative Permeability Measurements on a Water-Wet Sandstone Reservoir and a Limestone Reservoir of Mixed Wettability

- ID: SCA1995-03, year: 1995
- Authors: P. Grivot, L. Bouvier, J. Fournier, A. Silverii
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, residual oil saturation, gamma-ray saturation monitoring, centrifuge, tertiary gas injection, oil isoperms
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-03.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-03)

Combining gamma-ray steady-state and automated centrifuge tests, the paper measures three-phase relative permeability and residual oil in a water-wet sandstone and a mixed-wet limestone, finding gas recovery lower in tertiary mode for the limestone.

Tertiary gas injection design requires reliable residual oil saturation and three-phase relative permeability data. The authors combined steady-state tests with dual-energy gamma-ray in situ saturation monitoring (high oil saturations) and automated video-monitored centrifuge tests (high gas saturations) at ambient conditions on plugs from a water-wet sandstone and a mixed-wet limestone. In the sandstone, water relative permeability was the same function of Sw in two-phase and three-phase drainage, so both datasets could be merged to build oil isoperms, and a Fayers or modified Stone 1 model fitted them. Residual oil was very low in both secondary and tertiary gas floods. In the limestone, Sorw depended mainly on permeability rather than wettability, and gas beat water in secondary recovery except at very low column heights and low permeability. Tertiary Sorwg values lay between Sorg and Sorw, so gravity drainage was less efficient in tertiary than in secondary conditions.

## Dual Energy Gamma-Ray Absorption Technique Applied to Oil Relative Permeability Determination During a Tertiary Gas Gravity Drainage Experiment

- ID: SCA1995-10, year: 1995
- Authors: S. Sakthikumar, V. Giry, Ph. Maquignon
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gamma-ray absorption, gas gravity drainage, three-phase relative permeability, in situ saturation monitoring, Stone model, capillary end effects
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-10)

Dual-energy gamma-ray absorption monitored three-phase saturations in a tertiary gas gravity drainage coreflood; history matching with a compositional simulator validated the Stone 1 oil relative permeability model.

Predicting tertiary oil recovery from waterflooded reservoirs depends strongly on the three-phase relative permeability model, which is rarely validated experimentally. A 58 cm composite water-wet sandstone core was waterflooded and then subjected to tertiary gas gravity drainage at 230 bar and 71°C with low-IFT equilibrium fluids, while a dual-source (Am-241/Cs-137) gamma-ray rig monitored oil, gas and water saturations in situ. Waterflood recovered 70% IOIP, but residual oil profiles were non-uniform (35% at top, 10% at bottom) owing to capillary end effects. Gas drainage produced tertiary oil, reaching 57% TOIP and 8.5% average oil saturation before core plugging at 1.3 PV gas, with near-zero oil saturation in the upper core. The Foulser method gave Kro falling from 1e-3 to 1e-5 as So dropped from 25% to 10%, and compositional simulation matched production and saturation profiles using the Stone 1 model.

## Recent Application of Special Core Analysis to Fault Rocks

- ID: SCA2006-15, year: 2006
- Authors: Suleiman Al-Hinai, Quentin Fisher, Bader Al-Busafi, Phil Guise, Carlos A. Grattoni
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: fault rock, gas relative permeability, vapour chamber, pulse decay permeametry, ultracentrifuge capillary pressure, transmissibility multiplier
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-15.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-15)

Gas relative permeability and capillary pressure of cataclastic fault rock were measured; including them in a Rotliegend simulation reproduces the 250 bar cross-fault pressure difference that transmissibility multipliers cannot.

Fault rocks affect reservoir compartmentalisation, but transmissibility multipliers based on single-phase permeability often make faults too leaky. Seven cores of cataclastic fault rock from the Lossiemouth fault (Hopeman sandstone, Scotland) were characterised by CT, SEM, porosity, pulse-decay, Klinkenberg and brine permeability (0.001-0.006 mD). Air-water drainage capillary pressure was measured by ultracentrifuge up to 15,000 rpm, with irreducible water saturation not reached and minimum Sw mostly above 40%. Gas relative permeability was measured by pulse decay on samples equilibrated in vapour chambers, showing a sharp fall with increasing water saturation and krg below about 0.01. In an Eclipse model, the transmissibility multiplier approach gave 96 bar in the second compartment after 20 years, whereas a discrete fault with measured multiphase properties gave 343 bar, matching the reported 250 bar differential.

## A New Method to Measure Water, Oil and Gas Saturation Profiles in Three-Phase Gas Injection

- ID: SCA1995-05, year: 1995
- Authors: O. VIZIKA, F. KALAYDJIAN
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: three-phase saturation profiles, gamma-ray attenuation, electrical resistivity, spreading coefficient, gravity-assisted gas injection, three-phase relative permeability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-05.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-05)

Combining electrical resistivity and gamma-ray attenuation gives three-phase saturation profiles in gravity-assisted gas injection; positive spreading coefficient gives far higher oil recovery (54% vs 13%).

The paper presents a method to measure water, oil and gas saturation profiles simultaneously during tertiary gas injection, using resistivity from eight electrodes for water saturation and Americium gamma-ray attenuation for oil and gas. It was applied to gravity-assisted gas injection in water-wet sandpacks (about 120 µm grains, porosity 0.35-0.36) with spreading (S=+13 mN/m) and non-spreading (S=-2 mN/m) fluid systems. Profiles agreed with double-energy CT-scanner profiles and with bulk saturations from weighing recovered fluids, with errors under 3% for water and 4.5% for gas saturation. Spreading conditions recovered 54% OOIP with local residual oil down to 2%, versus about 13% recovery and Sorg near 11% for non-spreading. Oil relative permeabilities derived from profile evolution were very low and, used in a simulator, gave a good history match of recovery curves.

## Effect of Saturation History on Three-Phase Relative Permeability: An Experimental Study

- ID: SCA2013-023, year: 2013
- Authors: A.H. Alizadeh, M. Piri
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, saturation history, spreading oil layers, gas injection, Bentheimer sandstone, CT saturation measurement
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-023.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-023)

Steady-state three-phase gas injection experiments in Bentheimer sandstone with CT saturation show water and gas kr depend on own saturation, and oil kr scales as So^4 at high and So^2 at low saturation.

Three-phase relative permeability is poorly characterized experimentally, and the effect of saturation history is uncertain. The authors built a closed-loop, fully recirculating coreflooding apparatus with dual-energy medical CT for in-situ saturations, and ran steady-state tests on vertical water-wet Bentheimer cores (about 2.4 D, 24% porosity) with brine/Soltrol 170/nitrogen at 800 psig and 20°C. Two secondary gas injections (initial oil saturation 0.90 and 0.65) and one tertiary gas injection (Soi 0.70) gave residual oil saturations of 0.108, 0.145 and 0.173, attributed to differing stability of spreading oil layers and oil/water contact angle hysteresis. Water and gas relative permeabilities depended only on their own saturations, while oil relative permeability was only weakly sensitive to initial oil saturation. Oil kro scaled as So^4 at high oil saturation and So^2 at low saturation, consistent with layer drainage theory.

## A Novel Experimental Apparatus for Determination of Three-Phase Relative Permeabilities at Reservoir Conditions

- ID: SCA1996-36, year: 1996
- Authors: E. Ebeltoft, J.E. Iversen, K.O. Vatne, M.A. Andersen, J.E. Nordtvedt
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, acoustic separator, X-ray saturation measurement, reservoir condition flooding, history matching, chalk
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-36.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-36)

Describes a closed-loop three-phase flooding apparatus with acoustic separator and X-ray saturation scanning for reservoir-condition relative permeability, with calibration tests and a chalk experiment history-matched.

Three-phase relative permeability data at reservoir conditions are scarce, so a new apparatus was built for simultaneous injection and recycling of water, oil and gas. It uses eight computer-controlled pump cylinders, a three-phase acoustic separator, an X-ray transparent carbon-fiber core holder, and a Cs-filtered X-ray scanner for saturation profiles. Calibration showed pump volume accuracy of ±0.01 ml, pressure within 0.1% of 70,000 kPa range, leakage below 0.1 ml/day, and separator error of ±0.3 cc (about 0.5% of a 55 ml pore volume). In a Berea steady-state test, average saturations from volume balance and X-ray correlated well, though X-ray underestimated water and overestimated gas saturation. Three steady-state experiments on a 2.27 md chalk at 20,700 kPa were history-matched with a B-spline parameterized simulator to estimate relative permeability and capillary pressure surfaces.

## Comparing Hysteresis Models for Relative Permeability in Wag Studies

- ID: SCA1995-06, year: 1995
- Authors: Johne Alex Larsen, Arne Skauge
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: WAG, relative permeability hysteresis, trapped gas, Land relation, three-phase flow, intermediate wettability
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-06)

Simulations of immiscible WAG compare Carlson, Killough and a modified SL hysteresis model, showing standard two-phase models cannot capture reduced gas and water mobility seen experimentally.

Standard two-phase relative permeability hysteresis models cannot reproduce the drop in gas and water mobility between primary and tertiary processes observed in intermediate-wet systems during WAG. The authors simulated 1D core-floods and a 2D reservoir cross-section (Eclipse 94) using the Carlson, Killough and a modified SL model with input taken from measured curves and a modified Stone I oil model. A tolerance limit added to the SL model's jump between high- and low-mobility envelopes stabilized the runs and halved CPU time for the 3000-day cross-section. In 1D core-floods, Carlson and Killough gave about 13.2% final oil saturation and SL 11.6%, versus 5% experimentally, from 66% initially. In 2D, SL gave delayed gas breakthrough (900 vs 400 days) and larger gas-swept area. Water mobility representation (primary, secondary or pseudo-hysteresis) strongly changed recovery and water segregation, and the Killough wetting-phase scheme was judged of little use for WAG.

## Three-Phase Saturation Measurement During Gravity Drainage and Tertiary Waterflood: Improvement of Dual Energy Gamma-Ray Attenuation Technique

- ID: SCA2004-06, year: 2004
- Authors: C. Caubit, H. Bertin, G. Hamon
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: dual energy gamma-ray attenuation, three-phase saturation, gravity drainage, tertiary waterflood, error propagation, Am241 Cs137
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-06.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-06)

A dual-source Am241/Cs137 gamma-ray device with separate detectors measures three-phase saturations in a 1 m core; error analysis shows oil saturation least accurate and guides optimized procedures.

Three-phase saturation measurement is needed for tertiary flood and WAG studies. The authors built a dual-energy gamma-ray rig with Am241 and Cs137 sources stacked vertically, each with its own NaI detector, which avoids Compton correction. They ran gas-oil gravity drainage followed by tertiary waterflood on a 1 m Aerolith 10 core (porosity about 0.40, aged to weakly oil-wet) using NaI-doped brine and Marcol 52 oil. Saturation-derived recoveries agreed with mass balance. Error propagation by differential and Taylor-expansion/covariance methods showed Am porosity is more accurate than Cs, oil saturation is the least accurate, and accuracy improves with gas saturation. A front-tracking procedure, using the dual-energy system only in the three-phase zone and Am alone elsewhere, strongly reduced scatter.

## Experimental and Theoretical Three-Phase Relative Permeability for Wag Injection in Mixed Wet and Low Ift Systems

- ID: SCA2010-09, year: 2010
- Authors: M. Sohrabi, H. Shahverdi, M. Jamiolahmady, M. Fatemi, S. Ireland, G. Robertson
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, WAG injection, near-miscible gas, mixed-wet core, relative permeability hysteresis, history matching
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2010/SCA2010-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2010-09)

Near-miscible WAG coreflood experiments in mixed-wet and water-wet cores yield two- and three-phase kr by history matching; existing three-phase kr models fail to match the data.

Three-phase relative permeability for WAG is usually derived from two-phase data with empirical models, which is uncertain for mixed-wet rock and low-IFT gas. Many two- and three-phase corefloods (water, gas, oil, WAG, SWAG) were run on a 1000 mD mixed-wet and a 65 mD water-wet Clashach core using a C1/nC4 fluid system near its critical point (IFT 0.04 mN/m, 1840 psia, 100°F). An in-house genetic-algorithm coreflood simulator history-matched production and pressure data to give 2D three-phase kr tables for all phases. In the mixed-wet core, all three phases' kr depended on two saturations; three-phase kro was about 30% and krg about 10% of two-phase values, and gas hysteresis exceeded oil hysteresis while krw showed little hysteresis. In the 65 mD core, hysteresis was still considerable, and Stone I/II, Stone Exponent and saturation-weighted interpolation in Eclipse gave widely varying predictions, none matching all data nor the continued oil recovery after gas breakthrough.

## Calculation of Three-Phase Relative Permeabilities from Displacement Experiments with Measurements of in-Situ Saturation

- ID: SCA1995-09, year: 1995
- Authors: O.O. Eleri, A. Graue, A. Skauge, J. A. Larsen
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, in-situ saturation, nuclear tracer imaging, trapped gas, hysteresis, Clashach sandstone
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-09)

Three-phase relative permeabilities in water-wet Clashach sandstone were derived from unsteady-state floods with tracer-imaged in-situ saturations; water kr depends on Sw alone, oil kr shows scatter and hysteresis.

The paper examines how saturation history affects two- and three-phase relative permeability in a water-wet Clashach sandstone core (234 md). Sequences of water, oil and gas injections were run with nuclear tracer imaging to measure in-situ saturations, and three-phase kr was calculated analytically (Virnovsky method). Two-phase displacements were also history-matched with a Levenberg-Marquardt optimization of kr and capillary pressure, which showed limits when only production and pressure data were used and when viscous fingering was not modeled. Trapped gas increased with initial gas saturation and did not fit a Land equation with C=1.9 (C≈3.0 fit better), and trapped gas lowered residual oil. Water kr depended on water saturation alone, gas kr appeared to depend on gas saturation at high saturations, and oil kr showed considerable scatter and hysteresis, suggesting dependence on other phases' saturations.

## Relative Permeabilities for Two- and Three Phase Flow Processes Relevant to the Depressurization of the Statfjord Field

- ID: SCA2008-23, year: 2008
- Authors: Egil Boye Petersen Jr, Arild Lohne, Kåre O. Vatne, Johan Olav Helland, George Virnovsky, Pål-Eric Øren, Numerical Rocks
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, steady-state history matching, Stone model, capillary end effects, Statfjord depressurisation, gas-oil capillary pressure
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-23)

Steady-state two- and three-phase relative permeabilities measured on Statfjord reservoir core at reservoir conditions; Stone and Baker models fail to predict three-phase data, and two-phase capillary pressure is inadequate.

Depressurisation of the Statfjord Field requires three-phase relative permeabilities, which are usually estimated from two-phase data using empirical models. Steady-state two- and three-phase measurements were made on a 27.3 cm composite reservoir core (873 mD, porosity 0.318) at 200 bar and 91°C with live-type fluids, covering increasing and decreasing gas saturation paths (DDI and IID series). Data were history matched with the Coreflow simulator using independently measured capillary pressure curves to correct for end effects. Eclipse simulations showed Baker's saturation-weighted interpolation and Stone 1/2 poorly reproduced saturations and underestimated pressure drop by 30-40% or more, while tabulated measured three-phase kr gave an excellent match. Matching required artificially high residual oil (0.10-0.25), and inlet capillary pressure estimates indicated three-phase Pcgo and Pcow far larger than two-phase values.

## Measurement of Three-Phase Relative Permeabilities of Various Saturating Histories and Wettability Conditions

- Authors: Lu Weifeng, Zhang Zubo, Liu Qingjie, Ma Desheng, Wu Kangyun
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: three-phase relative permeability, isoperms, CT dual energy scanning, saturation history, wettability, steady-state method
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2012/SCA2012-43.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2012-43)

Steady-state, CT-monitored three-phase relative permeability tests on water-wet and oil-wet sandstone show wettability controls isoperm shapes, while saturation history mainly affects non-wetting phase isoperms.

Three-phase relative permeability data are scarce for oil-wet rock and uncertain in isoperm shape. The authors ran steady-state water-oil-gas flow experiments on a water-wet (SX1) and a weakly oil-wet (SX2) Shanxi sandstone outcrop, using dual-energy CT (100 and 140 kV) for in-situ saturations and a PEEK coreholder to avoid end effects. Wetting-phase isoperms were similar for both histories, whereas non-wetting phase isoperms and isoperm positions differed, which is explained by trapping of gas and oil.

Three-phase relative permeabilities are derived by history-matching gas-injection production curves with tabulated Krw, Kro, Krg versus Sw and Sg, starting from a pseudo steady-state estimate.

## Factors Influencing Three-Phase Flow Parameters in Wag Experiments

- ID: SCA1999-32, year: 1999
- Authors: Arne Skauge, Ida Veland, Johne Alex Larsen
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: WAG, three-phase relative permeability, hysteresis, Land trapping constant, principal component analysis, capillary end effect
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-32)

IWAG core floods on two sandstones are history matched with a three-phase hysteresis model, and PCA of many simulations reveals which parameters control production and pressure.

The paper examines which three-phase flow parameters control oil recovery in immiscible WAG core floods (about 0.1 PV slugs, 1:1 water-gas ratio) on two North Sea sandstone composites, one water-wet (Amott index 0.45) and one intermediate-wet (+0.29). Experiments were history matched with the ECLIPSE three-phase hysteresis option (WAGHYSTR). For core A the match used a Land constant of 2.25, a gas reduction factor of 0.5, a water endpoint reduction of 2, and no coupling of residual oil to trapped gas. Core B needed full coupling of trapped gas to residual oil, Land constant of about 3-3.4, a gas reduction of 4 and a 10-fold water reduction, with WAG recovery reaching 92% of oil in place. Principal component analysis of a lattice-designed set of simulations showed maximum differential pressure correlates with water production and the three-phase water mobility reduction, and gas breakthrough with the gas reduction factor. The Land constant behaved as noise in the PCA. Capillary end effects strongly affected continuous G1W2 and W1G2 floods but little affected WAG slug injection.

## Gas/Oil Relative Permeability Curves for Deep Water Reservoirs

- ID: SCA1999-34, year: 1999
- Authors: G. Hamon, F. Gouth, P. Bergey, D. Gaujard
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas/oil relative permeability, unconsolidated sands, gravity drainage, multi-rate gas flood, grain sorting, Corey exponent
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-34.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-34)

Multi-rate gas floods on unconsolidated deep-water sands show oil Kr depends on grain sorting; assigning Kr by sorting in reservoir models changes reserves by about 14%.

Gas reinjection into unconsolidated, highly permeable deep-water turbidite sands depends on gas/oil gravity drainage, which is controlled by oil relative permeability. The authors describe quality control for unconsolidated plugs: an aluminium/rubber sleeve for coring and zero-strain loading, plus brine tracer floods with CT and X-ray profiles to reject heterogeneous samples. Multi-rate unsteady-state gas floods were history-matched with spline-represented Kr and Pc curves. Krog showed a log/log break, with exponent 3.7–6.5 at medium-high So and 1.5–2.5 at low So, attributed to film flow in a spreading system, so single-parameter Corey forms fail. Corey exponent and residual oil correlated with grain sorting (well-sorted: Sorg below 0.05, no 3.7–4.7; poorly sorted: Sorg ~0.15, no 6–6.5), not with porosity or permeability. Using the two extreme Kr sets across a reservoir model gave about 14% difference in reserves.

## Sensitivity Studies of Fluid Flow Across Water-Saturated Faults with High Entry Pressure

- ID: SCA2003-49, year: 2003
- Authors: M.T. Tweheyo, E. Knudsen, O. Torsæter, J.K. Ringen
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: fault seal, capillary entry pressure, compartmentalised reservoir, Eclipse simulation, mercury porosimetry, oil breakthrough
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-49.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-49)

Eclipse 1D simulations of oil flow across a water-saturated ceramic fault next to Berea show breakthrough and recovery depend on fault permeability, entry pressure and thickness.

Water-saturated, low-permeability faults with high entry pressure can compartmentalise reservoirs, as seen in Brent Province fields. The authors simulated two-phase oil/water flow with Eclipse in 1D composite models of Berea sandstone (reservoir) and a ceramic fault (k 0.00295 md, Pe 8 bar, 0.99 cm thick). Corey correlations and mercury porosimetry supplied relative permeability and capillary pressure. Sensitivities varied fault permeability (0.000295-0.0295 md), entry pressure (4-14 bar), thickness and initial reservoir water saturation. Breakthrough was delayed by over ten times for a tenfold thicker fault (232 h vs 14 h) and was earlier with higher permeability; performance was controlled more by permeability than entry pressure. Simulated breakthrough times (14, 8, 10 h) matched experiments (13, 6, 9 h) reasonably.
