## Capillary Pressure Probe for SCAL Applications

- ID: SCA1999-08, year: 1999
- Authors: J.N.M. van Wunnik, S. Oedai, S. Masalmeh
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary pressure probe, capillary end effect, relative permeability, oil-wet and water-wet filters, drainage and imbibition, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-08)

A small oil- and water-wet filter pressure probe is designed and tested on Berea, Bentheim and carbonate plugs to directly measure capillary pressure and reveal end effects.

Capillary end effects superpose capillary and viscous pressure gradients in relative permeability plug experiments. The authors present a detailed design of a capillary pressure probe using a ceramic water-wet filter and a Teflon oil-wet filter (1 mm2 active area, rated to 300 bar and 120 °C, filter failure near 10 bar). Probes mounted on the plug side were tested in a Caprici-type drainage/imbibition set-up on Berea (620 mD), Bentheim (1560 mD) and carbonate (1.6 mD) plugs. Probes responded to the capillary pressure and were insensitive to the viscous pressure build-up from oil or water accumulating at the end filters, which dominated the total plug pressure drop. Three-probe runs resolved the oil mound moving along the plug, and in the carbonate the entry pressure was 60-70 kPa.

## Improvement of Diaphragm Method for Drainage Capillary Pressure Measurement with Micro Pore Membrane

- ID: SCA1992-05EURO, year: 1992
- Authors: Wibeke L. Hammervold, Svein M. Skjæveland
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: micropore membrane, drainage capillary pressure, porous plate method, equilibration time, Berea sandstone, Nuclepore
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1992/1-SCA1992-05EURO.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1992-05EURO)

A thin micropore membrane replaces the ceramic plate in oil-brine drainage capillary pressure measurements on Berea sandstone, giving comparable curves with roughly ten times shorter equilibration time.

Ceramic porous plate capillary pressure measurements take weeks because the thick disk controls equilibration. The authors replaced the plate with thin water-wet membranes, testing Nuclepore polycarbonate and Millipore cellulose types, and selected a 0.08 µm Nuclepore membrane with 4 bar entry pressure. They built a coreholder and an endpiece with a support screen, glue and protective Millipore membrane to prevent twisting, rupture and leakage. Oil-brine drainage curves were measured on Berea cores (about 100-500 md) at ambient conditions, and two cores were compared against commercial porous plate data with fairly good agreement. Fitting 32 recovery curves with a Langmuir-type function gave an average characteristic time of 25.8 h for the plate versus 3.1 h for the membrane, about a factor 8-10 faster. Premature oil breakthrough still occurred occasionally, and the authors propose uncertainty-based rules for ending each pressure step.

## Advances in APEX Technology

- ID: SCA1990-04, year: 1990
- Authors: H.H. Yuan
- Method: theory or analytical model
- Keywords: constant-rate mercury porosimetry, APEX, pore throat distribution, Archie's lithologic exponent, permeability model, residual non-wetting saturation
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-04.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-04)

Bivariate pore-body and pore-throat distributions from constant-rate mercury porosimetry (APEX) feed parameter-free models of Archie's m and permeability, matching limited measurements on sandstones and carbonates.

Slow constant-rate mercury injection (APEX) resolves pore space into pore bodies (subisons) and throats (risons), each with entry pressure and volume. From these, subison and rison bivariate distribution functions are built, shown for Berea sandstone and San Andres dolomite. The subison distribution gives residual non-wetting saturation and the residual-initial relationship. The rison distribution, used in a parallel capillary tube formalism, gives Archie's lithologic exponent (m = 1 + log(S/2)/log φ) and permeability (k = 477φ/S Σ A_r/Pe²), with upper and lower bounds depending on how unfilled pore space is treated. Comparison with measurements on 1/2 inch and 1 inch samples (Berea, Bentheim, San Andres, Red River, chert) shows good agreement: sandstone m values lie near the lower bounds, carbonates near the upper bounds, and air permeabilities generally fall between bounds. The author notes the comparison set is limited and that more data and tests with one-ended mercury entry are needed.

## Improvements of the Semidynamic Method for Capillary Pressure Measurements

- ID: SCA1995-31, year: 1995
- Authors: R. Lenormand, A. Eisenzimmer, Ph. Delaplace
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: semidynamic method, capillary pressure, steady-state injection, CAT scanning saturation, relative permeability inversion, entrance saturation
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-31.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-31)

The semidynamic capillary pressure method is simplified by computing local saturation from average saturation, checked against CAT scanning, with numerical history matching used to fix inaccuracy near Swi.

The semidynamic method measures water-oil capillary pressure cycles by steady-state injection of one fluid, previously requiring local saturation measurements. Here local entrance saturation is calculated from average saturation and entrance pressure at several flow rates using the Ramakrishnan and Cappiello analytical derivative approach. It was tested on a simulated primary drainage and on a Vosges sandstone drainage experiment under CAT scanning, with eight oil rates from 5 to 500 cm3/h, saturations at 7 locations and pressures at 6. Calculated saturations match measurements at intermediate values but are inaccurate near Swi (0.20 calculated vs 0.19 measured) because differentiation amplifies noise in average saturation. Using the analytical results as a first guess for numerical simulation and fitting average saturation and local pressures gave Pc and Kr over the whole saturation range within ±2 saturation units.

## Importance of Porous Plate Measurements on Carbonates at Pseudo Reservoir Conditions

- ID: SCA2006-28, year: 2006
- Authors: M Z Kalam, K Al Hammadi, O B Wilson, M Dernaika, H Samosir
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: porous plate capillary pressure, saturation exponent, resistivity index, continuous injection, FRIM, principal component analysis
- Cited by 7 papers in the corpus, cites 6
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-28.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-28)

Porous plate capillary pressure and resistivity index data on 76 carbonate plugs serve as the reference, showing continuous injection and FRIM give uncertain saturation exponents; PCA flags outliers.

The paper compares porous plate (PP), continuous injection (CI), centrifuge and FRIM techniques for water-oil capillary pressure and Sw-RI measurements on carbonate cores at reservoir stress and temperature with dead crude. Over 70 plugs from four Middle East carbonate fields were tested, with PP as the reference method on all fields, CI on Field B and FRIM on Field D. PP gave consistent saturation exponents (e.g. 2.14 for Field A, 2.47 for Field B, 1.99 and 1.61 for Field D rock types). CI (n = 2.16, std 0.910) and FRIM (n = 1.80, std 0.252) showed large scatter and non-linear Sw-RI trends not seen with PP, with significant STOOIP impact. Principal component analysis identified outlier plugs (e.g. P19, P26, P40) and showed that irreducible water saturation and n-exponent drive unexplained variance.

## Micritic Limestones of the Middle East: Influence of Wettability, Pore Network and Experimental Technique on Drainage Capillary Pressure Curve

- ID: SCA2005-08, year: 2005
- Authors: Benjamin SALLIER, Gérald HAMON
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: mercury injection capillary pressure, centrifuge drainage Pc, mercury contact angle, oil-base mud invasion, Amott-Harvey wettability, microporous carbonates
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 8
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-08)

Centrifuge oil/water and mercury drainage Pc curves on micritic outcrop limestones differ greatly, and oil-base mud invasion biases Swi even after cleaning; wettability depends on pore structure.

The study compares first drainage capillary pressure from oil/water centrifuge and mercury injection on outcrop Urgonian limestone facies (R4, R3, R5, Orgon) analogous to Middle East Shuaiba/Kharaib. Companion plugs were screened by porosity, permeability, NMR, tomography and probe permeametry, then subjected to refined oil, crude oils, ageing, cleaning and oil-base mud filtrate, with Amott-Harvey, USBM and imbibition wettability tests. Water/oil and mercury Pc curves differ over the whole saturation range, with scaled ratios of 1 to 2, largest in microporous facies; an apparent mercury contact angle of 40–60° rising as throat size decreases would reconcile them. Ageing with crude oil and cleaning changed Swi by less than 5 saturation units, but oil-base mud invasion followed by cleaning raised Swi to 20% in R4 and 29% in Orgon, against 7.5–7.7% initially. Wettability alteration was larger in bimodal R4 than in purely microporous Orgon, and pore-network modelling with distributed contact angles reproduced the main drainage and imbibition trends qualitatively.

## Mercury Porosimetry Protocol for Rapid Determination of Petrophysical and Reservoir Quality Properties

- ID: SCA2000-21, year: 2000
- Authors: John Shafer, John Neasham
- Method: review or workflow
- Keywords: mercury injection capillary pressure, blank correction, closure pressure, sample dilation, unconsolidated sands, 1-D drainage
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-21.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-21)

Presents practical protocols for high-pressure mercury injection capillary pressure testing, covering jacketing, blank and closure corrections, sample dilation in compressible rocks, and unconsolidated sands.

High-pressure MICP (to 60,000 psia) can rapidly screen drainage capillary pressure and reservoir quality, but data validity depends on sample preparation and corrections. The paper discusses sample size, 1-D versus 3-D drainage (radial jacketing), mercury contact angle (140° vs 180°, a factor of 1.30), blank cell corrections, and closure pressure selection. Using operator-generated quartz blank correction instead of formula lowered MICP porosity by about 5% for sandstone (31.2 to 29.8%) and 26% for shale (3.66 to 2.71%). For compressible high-entry-pressure samples (0.03 md example, bulk compressibility ~20 microsips), unaccounted bulk dilation after mercury entry makes closure selection subjective and can produce pessimistic capillary pressure curves and low porosities. For unconsolidated sands, frozen plugs are jacketed with Teflon and nickel-alloy foil with end screens, and screen mesh must be matched to filling pressure and sample entry pressure to avoid erroneous porosity. MICP can be compared with grain-size-derived pore aperture distributions, but clay-bearing samples give optimistic curves relative to porous plate or centrifuge data.

## Capillary Pressure Characteristics of Carbonate Reservoirs: Relationship Between Drainage and Imbibition Curves

- ID: SCA2006-16, year: 2006
- Authors: S.K. Masalmeh
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary pressure hysteresis, core cleaning, mercury injection, centrifuge capillary pressure, contact angle distribution, carbonate wettability
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 6
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-16)

Compares centrifuge oil-brine and Hg-air capillary pressure on Middle East carbonates, shows poor cleaning distorts drainage but not imbibition, and proposes drainage-imbibition conversion and a Pc model.

Carbonate reservoir Pc data are needed for static and dynamic modelling, but oil-brine and Hg-air primary drainage curves often disagree. Centrifuge Pc (interpreted numerically) was compared with Hg-air on about 50 samples from a Cretaceous Middle East field. Mismatches were attributed to ineffective cleaning, which changes curve shape and traps water; one sample needed 10% trapped water and a 55° contact angle. Adding flow-through chloroform cleaning after Soxhlet gave an excellent match to Hg-air, while imbibition Pc on 32 samples was unaffected by cleaning method. Imbibition and drainage curves were converted with contact angles of about 108-111° for uni-modal samples, with larger pores more oil-wet in dual-porosity samples, and a three-term Pc model was proposed to fit both cycles.

A semi-dynamic apparatus with X-ray saturation monitoring measures a full capillary pressure cycle and relative permeability on a carbonate at reservoir conditions; duration scales with L² and K^-1/2.

## Porous Plates Influence on Effective Drainage Rates in Capillary Pressure Experiments

- ID: SCA2001-30, year: 2001
- Authors: Ove Bjørn Wilson, Bjørn Gunnar Tjetland, Arne Skauge
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: porous plate technique, drainage capillary pressure, layered ceramic plate, plate flux restriction, Bentheimer sandstone, stabilisation time
- Cited by 6 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2001/SCA2001-30.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2001-30)

Layered high-flux ceramic porous plates cut drainage capillary pressure experiment time by 3-10 times versus homogeneous plates, without changing saturation or electrical behaviour, supported by experiments and simulation.

Standard porous plate capillary pressure measurements are very slow, and the plate's flow restriction limits drainage rates. The authors tested layered KeraFlux ceramic plates (water flux 2-10 ml/h at 1 bar) against homogeneous Soil Moisture plates (0.13 ml/h) on Bentheimer sandstone and three North Sea reservoir sandstones (about 0.4-4000 mD). A simple capillary-tube network model based on the Washburn equation shows the plate matters most for high-permeability rock. Bentheimer drainage with layered plates reached final equilibrium in 41 days, versus roughly 99 days for the first step alone with a homogeneous plate. In a laminated reservoir, equilibrium took 71 days with layered plates and 207 days with homogeneous ones. 1-D simulation gave 3 versus 40 days for the 0.1 bar step. Drainage was 3-10 times faster across 2-4000 mD, with saturation distribution and electrical properties unaffected.

## A Fast and Accurate Method to Measure Threshold Capillary Pressure of Caprocks Under Representative Conditions

- ID: SCA2006-07, year: 2006
- Authors: P. Egermann, J.-M. Lombard, P. Bretonnier
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: threshold capillary pressure, caprock, CO2 storage, dynamic displacement, residual capillary pressure, brine production rate
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-07.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-07)

A constant-pressure-drop injection method derives caprock threshold capillary pressure from the break in brine production rate; it matches the step-by-step method and is about five times faster.

Threshold capillary pressure of caprocks is needed for basin modeling and gas or CO2 storage, but existing methods are slow (step-by-step), biased by viscous effects (continuous injection), or underestimate it (residual Pc). The authors propose injecting the non-wetting fluid at a constant pressure drop above threshold, starting with brine in the inlet dead volume. The slope break in brine production gives the virgin-region pressure drop through Darcy's law, and subtracting it from the total drop gives threshold Pc. On five samples (chalk to caprock, 0.0006-1.7 md) with an air/brine system, dynamic values (e.g. 6.2 vs 6 bar, 14.6 vs 17 bar) agreed well with the standard method, while residual Pc was systematically lower (e.g. 2.9 vs 6 bar). The test was about five times faster than the standard approach. A synthetic simulation showed local pressure taps could resolve threshold Pc heterogeneity (10, 20, 40 bar) along a composite core; gas solubility in brine is flagged as a possible artifact for CO2.

## Relative Permeability Effects Overlooked in MICP Measurements Transition Zones Likely to Be Smaller

- ID: SCA2016-013, year: 2016
- Authors: Jos G. Maas, Niels Springer, Albert Hebing
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: mercury injection capillary pressure, equilibration time, relative permeability interference, SCORES simulator, transition zone, saturation-height function
- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 3
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-013.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-013)

Simulations and about 40 MICP experiments show the default 10 s equilibration is too short; longer equilibration shifts plateau saturations by roughly 20 units, shrinking transition zones.

Standard MICP uses a default 10 s equilibration per pressure step, ignoring interference between capillary pressure and mercury relative permeability. An extended SCORES 1-D simulator showed apparent Pc curves deviate from true curves at low mercury saturation, more so for low permeability and longer samples. The Autopore electronics were extended to record intrusion and pressure time series, confirming intrusion had not stabilised at 10 s. Four-versus-four sample comparisons on Oberkirchener sandstone and Micromeritics reference material, using a suspend mode with readings at 10, 100 and 1000 s, showed statistically significant shifts (t-test, 5% and 1% levels) of up to about 20 saturation units. Chalk showed differential shifts of 5-12 units, while high-permeability Euville limestone and low-permeability Rotliegendes samples showed little measurable effect. An improved protocol using suspend mode between 0.1 and 0.8 PV is recommended, and the transition zone is likely smaller than currently estimated.

## Capillary Flow Porometry – Assessment of an Alternative Method for the Determination of Flow Relevant Parameters of Porous Rocks

- ID: SCA2013-007, year: 2013
- Authors: Matthias Halisch, Esther Vogt, Cornelia Müller, Angels Cano-Odena, Danny Pattyn, Patrice Hellebaut, Kees van der Kamp
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: capillary flow porometry, pore throat size distribution, mercury injection, Bentheimer sandstone, first bubble point, wetting liquid displacement
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-007.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-007)

Capillary flow porometry, using nitrogen to displace a nontoxic wetting liquid, is tested on Bentheimer sandstone and gives pore throat distributions matching mercury injection in about an hour per cycle.

Mercury injection porosimetry is slow, applies high pressure and contaminates samples with toxic mercury. The authors adapt capillary flow porometry (CFP), in which nitrogen displaces a perfluoroether wetting liquid (γ about 18 dynes/cm) with pressure steps, using a POROLUX 1000 instrument. A 4-5 mm slice from Bentheimer sandstone plugs (porosity 21.5%, permeability 593 mD, dominant throat size about 15 µm) was measured in six wetting-drying cycles of about one hour each. Results showed good reproducibility with about 200 points per curve, after discarding cycle 1 (sample holder leakage) and values above 50 µm. After polynomial fitting to the mercury bin sizes, the distributions agreed well with Hg injection, with somewhat more scatter on the flanks. The method preserves the sample, and the authors plan a larger cell, leakage improvements and tests on other sandstones and carbonates.

## Forty Comparisons of Mercury Injection Data with Oil/Water Capillary Pressure Measurements by the Porous Plate Technique

- ID: SCA1997-10, year: 1997
- Authors: H. N. Greder, V. Gallato, Ph. Cordelier, D. Laran, V. Munoz
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: mercury injection capillary pressure, porous plate, oil/brine drainage, irreducible water saturation, shaly sandstone heterogeneity, carbonate wettability
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-10)

Forty paired tests on adjacent samples show scaled mercury injection systematically underestimates irreducible water saturation versus reservoir-condition porous plate oil/brine drainage, with differences tied to heterogeneity, clay structure and carbonate mineralogy.

The paper asks whether mercury injection can substitute for reservoir-condition oil/brine porous plate drainage capillary pressure. Forty tests on adjacent samples (25 sandstones, 15 carbonates; Gulf of Guinea and North Sea) compared water saturation at 4 bar oil/brine Pc (DS) and displacement pressure. Mercury injection always gave lower Swi, with DS from 0 to 30 %PV in sandstones and up to 0.28 in limestones. Equilibrium time, sample dispersion and compaction explained little of the difference. In sandstones DS rose with clay content, shale lamination/bioturbation, pore-access heterogeneity (SI) and cementation exponent, probably through brine trapping in 1D oil placement. Clean well-sorted sandstones matched, while in carbonates DS was larger for limestones and calcite-rich samples, suggesting a wettability effect.

## A New Methodology to Evaluate the Impact of the Local Heterogeneity on Petrophysical Parameters (KR, PC): Application on Carbonate Rocks

- ID: SCA2004-18, year: 2004
- Authors: P. Egermann, R. Lenormand
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: local heterogeneity, relative permeability history matching, capillary pressure, saturation profiles, upscaling, carbonate coreflood
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 7
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-18)

A pressure-based upscaling method uses local saturation profiles to derive local Pc and kr, reproducing saturation fluctuations in carbonate coreflood experiments and giving confidence intervals for kr and Pc.

Local saturation fluctuations in carbonate coreflood experiments cannot be reproduced by conventional history matching using a single kr-Pc set or smoothed saturation inputs. The authors propose a method based on the fact that pressure profiles are smoother than saturation profiles, so simulated pressure from a homogeneous history match can be combined with measured local saturations to compute local Pc values at each measurement position. These local Pc curves are fed back into the simulator for one further kr optimization, and transient profiles give local fractional flow and displaced-phase kr. A synthetic multi-rate imbibition case (8 cm core, 11-47 md) shows saturation deviations up to 10% between homogeneous and heterogeneous cores while pressure profiles remain similar. Applied to a gas/oil composite carbonate core (~30% porosity, 10 md) with CT saturation monitoring and a single water/oil carbonate plug (29%, 14.8 md), the method matches production data and local saturation profiles after one iteration, with Pc and kro values scattering around the global curves to define an uncertainty interval.

## A New Technique for Measuring the Breakthrough Capillary Pressure

- ID: SCA2002-40, year: 2002
- Authors: Jonathan D. Smith, Ioannis Chatzis, Marios A. Ioannidis
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: breakthrough capillary pressure, constant rate injection, glass micromodel, mercury porosimetry, Berea sandstone, threshold pressure
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-40.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-40)

A constant-rate injection of air or mercury, with inlet pressure monitored, gives breakthrough capillary pressure accurately in glass micromodels and Berea sandstone, agreeing with drainage height and porosimetry.

Breakthrough capillary pressure is usually found from stepwise pressure drainage or the inflection of porosimetry curves. The authors inject a slug of air or mercury at constant rate (about 1.8e-2 cm3/min) with a syringe pump and take the peak inlet pressure as breakthrough pressure. In eight glass micromodels the values agreed with drainage capillary height tests within about 1-7%. ## Porous Plates Influence on Effective Imbibition Rates in Capillary Pressure Experiments

- ID: SCA2002-16, year: 2002
- Authors: Ove Bjørn Wilson
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: porous plate, layered porous plate, spontaneous imbibition, forced imbibition, characteristic time, capillary pressure
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 7
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-16)

Layered and homogeneous porous plates were compared in drainage and imbibition on North Sea cores; layered plates equilibrate faster, with a time saving factor of 2.86, same capillary curves.

Porous plate capillary pressure experiments are very slow, and layered high-flux plates have been shown to speed up drainage. This study extends the comparison to spontaneous and forced imbibition using four pairs of twin North Sea plugs (porosity 0.268-0.272, permeability 1717-2421 mD) at 157 bar net confining pressure and 85°C with stock tank oil. Plugs were tested with layered Keraflux plates (hydrophilic and hydrophobic) or homogeneous Soil Moisture plates, using strict equilibrium criteria of 48 hours without change in saturation or resistivity index. Layered plates reached final equilibrium in 76, 86 and 108 days versus 247, 238 and 279 days for homogeneous plates, a total time saving factor of 2.86. A characteristic time from first-order exponential fits of transient production gave a characteristic time ratio that falls as a power function of drainage pressure and rises with dSw/dPc, tends to unity at endpoints, and follows an exponential trend in forced imbibition. Near-zero-pressure spontaneous imbibition was not well described by a single exponential.

## Heterogeneity Study Through Representative Capillary Pressure Measurements - Impact on Reservoir Simulation and Field Predictions

- ID: SCA2004-32, year: 2004
- Authors: J-M. Lombard, P. Egermann, R. Lenormand, S. Bekri, M Hajizadeh, H Hafez, A Modavi, M Z Kalam, Abu Dhabi

- Cited by 4 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-32.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-32)

A semi-dynamic method interpretation gives sets of local Pc curves capturing heterogeneity in six carbonate rock types at reservoir conditions, improving simulation inputs versus single-curve methods.

Imbibition capillary pressure is needed to interpret waterfloods and calibrate reservoir simulation, but single-curve methods ignore sample heterogeneity. The authors extend the IFP semi-dynamic method (up to 130°C, 330 bar, X-ray in situ saturation monitoring) by solving the average pressure field with ATHOS using the analytical Pc and krw curves, then combining it with local saturation profiles to get Pc at each measured location. ## Can We Really Measure the Relative Permeabilities Using the Micropore Membrane Method?

- ID: SCA1996-37, year: 1996
- Authors: R. Lenormand, P. Delaplace, P. Schmitz
- Method: numerical simulation
- Keywords: micropore membrane, relative permeability, porous plate, capillary pressure, transient production, analytical model
- Cited by 5 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-37.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-37)

Experiments, numerical simulation and an analytical model show relative permeability from membrane transient production is poorly determined for liquid/liquid systems unless membrane resistance is low.

The paper examines whether relative permeability can be derived from transient effluent production during capillary pressure measurements with porous plate or micropore membrane methods. They then iterate the contact angle (0–89°) at each pressure step until converted saturations match PcRI saturations, with an independent wettability test (Amott-Harvey or a contact angle on a silica-coated QCM sensor) selecting between θ and 180°−θ. Default values (32 mN/m, 30°) deviated strongly from PcRI data, whereas matched angles gave R² of 0.9997 across four water-wet/neutral samples. In an oil-wet example (QCM angle 158°, 8 months), the match deviated by 13–31% (MSE 0.001875) at higher capillary pressures, which the authors attribute to IFT rising from 19.3 to 28.3 mN/m as wettability shifted.

## Hysteresis in Relative Permeability from Cyclic Water-Oil Corefloods

- ID: SCA_2025_1070, year: 2025
- Authors: Victor Fernandes, Halidi Abdoulghafour, Amin Rezaei, Fabrice Pairoys, Marcel Bourgeois, Franck NONO, Cyril Caubit
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: relative permeability hysteresis, WOWO cyclic coreflood, Amott* wettability index, Killough model, in-situ X-ray saturation monitoring, turbidite sandstones
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2025/SCA_2025_1070.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA_2025_1070)

Six cyclic water-oil-water-oil unsteady-state corefloods on turbidite sandstones show cycle-dependent mobility reduction in oil and water Kr that Killough's hysteresis model cannot reproduce.

Relative permeability hysteresis was studied with six WOWO unsteady-state corefloods on aged turbidite sandstone cores (80–3060 mD) at 90 °C and 10 bar, using formation brine and dead oil. In-situ 2D X-ray saturation monitoring (CXBOX) provided saturation profiles for history matching with CYDAR, giving Kr and Pc curves for primary drainage, two imbibitions and two drainages. Amott* indices computed from the interpreted Pc curves ranged from water-wet (about 0.43 for SLT1-10 and KLT2-8 first cycle) to neutral/slightly oil-wet (-0.28 to -0.35 for KLT1-8). Every core had at least one cycle crossing the primary-drainage bounding curves, and oil mobility fell systematically with cycle number. More water-wet cores showed little Krw hysteresis between I1 and D2, while less water-wet cores showed water mobility reduction. Higher trapped water after drainage did not necessarily give higher oil recovery in the next imbibition. The authors conclude Killough's model cannot predict hysteresis in these mixed-wet systems.
