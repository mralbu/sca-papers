## Comparing Core Analysis Data to Field Observations for Gravity Drainage Gas Injection

- ID: SCA1999-04, year: 1999
- Authors: Arne Skauge
- Method: field or reservoir case study
- Keywords: gravity drainage, gas injection, residual oil saturation, oil relative permeability, centrifuge, single-well tracer test
- Cited by 3 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-04.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-04)

Core gas-oil relative permeability and residual oil data for the Oseberg field are compared with field tests, showing Sorg below 0.1 and stable gravity drainage.

The paper summarizes SCAL data used to select updip gas injection with gravity drainage for the high-permeability Oseberg sandstone field in the North Sea. Long-core gravity-stable gas injection, centrifuge drainage and gas flooding were used to derive oil relative permeability and residual oil saturation. Long cores gave remaining oil of about 0.08-0.15 and Corey exponents of about 4-5. Centrifuge and gas-flood kro agreed below about 400 mD but diverged at higher permeability. Sorg showed no unique trend with permeability or initial water saturation. Field data, including a gas single-well tracer test (Sorg below 0.1), sponge coring (0.15-0.2) and TDT-P gas front monitoring (5 cm/day), broadly supported the lab results. TDT-derived saturations (about 0.2-0.35) were inconsistent with the other methods. Gas injection was more efficient than waterflooding (0.25 vs 0.4 oil saturation at breakthrough).

## Analysis of Viscous Fingering Reproducibllity in Consolidated Natural Porous Media

- ID: SCA1995-04, year: 1995
- Authors: Arlindo da Costa e Silva
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: viscous fingering, X-ray CT saturation imaging, unstable immiscible displacement, self-similar response function, beam hardening correction, fractional flow
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-04.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-04)

CT-imaged unstable waterfloods in two Vosges sandstone cores show viscous fingering is reproducible, gravity- and heterogeneity-controlled, self-similar, with rate-dependent oil relative permeability and fractional flow.

The study uses X-ray CT to visualize viscous fingering during waterfloods (0.823 cP KI brine displacing 244 cP mineral oil) in two homogeneous, water-wet Vosges sandstone cores (417 and 497 mD, porosity about 0.23). Porosity and saturation were computed voxel by voxel from CT scans, with beam hardening correction bringing CT porosity (0.227, 0.232) in line with conventional values. Eight unstable floods at 0.3 or 0.5 cm3/min, including runs with the core inverted, showed repeated finger locations that changed when the core was turned, indicating deterministic fingering controlled by gravity and minor heterogeneity. Saturation data collapsed onto single response curves versus xD/tD. Oil relative permeability and fractional flow depended on injection rate, while water relative permeability did not, and a slope break indicated fingering and stable zones. Solvent-cleaned sample #1 showed more scatter than sample #2, suggesting solvent alteration of the rock.

## Gas and Water Flood Gravity Drainage in Viscous Oil Field Developments

- ID: SCA1999-35, year: 1999
- Authors: Nicola Sargent, David Element, Stephen Goodyear, Jane Johnston
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas gravity drainage, viscous oil, sandpack, gamma attenuation saturation monitoring, oil relative permeability, waterflood fractional flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-35.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-35)

Gas and water gravity drainage experiments in sandpacks with oils of 2-210 cp show viscosity-independent relative permeability and fractional flow, with gas injection often preferable for viscous oils.

Viscous oil fields in the UKCS are high-permeability unconsolidated sands where gas injection into a gas cap or water flooding are alternative development options. Gas/oil and water/oil gravity drainage experiments were run in three dry-packed sandpacks (2.5 cm diameter, about 70 cm long, about 5 Darcy, 47% porosity) using Magna lubricating oils from 2 to 210 cp. Saturations were monitored in situ by single-energy gamma attenuation to avoid capillary end effects, and fractional flow and relative permeability were derived. For gas drainage, oil relative permeabilities were independent of viscosity and significantly higher than in consolidated sandstone, allowing very low effective residual oil saturations under suitable conditions. Water flood fractional flow curves also showed no significant viscosity dependence, and comparison of recoveries indicates gas injection can be preferable for high-viscosity oils, especially in more permeable reservoirs.

- ID: SCA2007_48, year: 2007
- Authors: Wagirin R. Paidin, Dandina N. Rao
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas assisted gravity drainage, wettability alteration, silanization, Hele-Shaw model, fractured porous media, tertiary gas injection
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_48.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_48)

Hele-Shaw model GAGD experiments show oil-wet packs and a simulated vertical fracture improve nitrogen gravity drainage oil recovery over water-wet, unfractured cases.

The study extends earlier GAGD work, which was limited to water-wet media, by testing wettability, a vertical fracture, and injection mode (secondary or tertiary). A Hele-Shaw model (1445 cc) was packed with glass beads or silica sand (about 5 Darcy), with n-decane, water and nitrogen. Oil-wet packs recovered more oil, averaging about 12 %IOIP more than water-wet ones; secondary-mode increments ranged from 8.4 to 14.6 %IOIP. A vertical fracture increased recovery by about 6-7.8 %IOIP on average, and fractured oil-wet 0.13 mm runs gave 9.6 %IOIP incremental recovery.

## Use of Dimensional Analysis for Scaling Immiscible Gas Assisted Gravity Drainage (Gagd) Experiments

- ID: SCA2005-50, year: 2005
- Authors: Madhav M. Kulkarni, Amit P. Sharma, Dandina N. Rao
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas assisted gravity drainage, Bond number, capillary number, gravity number, dimensional analysis, Hele-Shaw physical model
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-50.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-50)

Dimensionally scaled GAGD corefloods and Hele-Shaw bead-pack experiments match field Bond and capillary numbers; oil recovery correlates logarithmically with gravity number across lab and field scales.

Gas Assisted Gravity Drainage (GAGD) experiments were designed using dimensional analysis so that Bond and capillary numbers matched ranges from nine gravity-stable field projects. Eight immiscible secondary-mode tests were run in 1-ft and 6-ft Berea corefloods (500 psi) and a 2-D Hele-Shaw glass-bead physical model, varying grain size, fluid pair (decane or paraffin with N2 or CO2) and injection rate. Oil recovery rose logarithmically with both Bond and capillary number, and a miscible coreflood point fell on the same trend. Multiple regression showed Bond number has a stronger effect than capillary number (significance 0.0084 vs 0.0234). Plotted against gravity number, lab and field recoveries collapsed onto a single logarithmic correlation, and dimensionless time scaling mapped 10 minutes of model time to roughly 69-127 days in the Dexter Hawkins field.

## Pore Scale Phenomena of Heavy Oil Recovery Using Vapor Extraction

- ID: SCA2002-26, year: 2002
- Authors: Ioannis Chatzis, Porous Media
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: VAPEX, heavy oil, glass micromodel, snap-off, gravity drainage, butane vapour absorption
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-26)

Glass micromodel and sintered glass-bead visualizations of butane VAPEX show a thin draining interface, snap-off vapour trapping enhancing mass transfer, and constant interface advance and oil production rate.

VAPEX pore-scale physics are poorly characterized, so butane extraction of ~80,000 cP heavy oil was visualized in etched glass network micromodels (two square networks, 1.36 and 3 mm spacing) and in thin slabs of sintered 600 µm glass beads. Drainage occurs in a monolayer of pores at the interface, with diluted oil flowing as films along the first row of pores and the interface forming dendritic peaks and valleys. Capillary snap-off traps vapour pockets that dissolve and dilute oil below terminal menisci, increasing mass transfer area. The interface advanced at constant velocity, giving constant live-oil production, and slab results matched the 2-D models; no asphaltene precipitation was seen. Using scaling (Q ∝ L^0.55, Q ∝ K^0.47), an example field case (20 m pay, 10 D, 1000 m well) gave a predicted 71 m3/day of live oil containing about 40 wt% solvent.

## Mechanism of Oil Recovery by Gagd in Water-Wet and Oil-Wet Conditions

- Authors: Weifeng LV, Zhenpeng LENG, Qingjie LIU, Zubo ZHANG, Desheng MA, Shiguan DENG, Danyong LI, Tong LI
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas assisted gravity drainage, CT scanning, three-phase saturation, wettability, oil bank, nitrogen injection
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2013/SCA2013-086.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2013-086)

CT-scanned nitrogen GAGD after high water cut in water-wet and oil-wet sandstone cores shows an oil bank forms and recovery gains are larger in the water-wet core.

The study examines how gas assisted gravity drainage (GAGD) recovers oil at high water cut and how wettability affects it. Two similar sandstone outcrop cores (about 23% porosity, ~850 mD), one water-wet and one weakly oil-wet, were waterflooded to 99.5% water cut and then flooded with nitrogen from the top at 0.01 ml/min. Tests were at ambient temperature and 5 MPa back pressure, so reservoir conditions were not reproduced.

## Gas Injection and Heterogeneous Wettability: What Is the Relevant Information That Petrophysics Can Provide

- ID: SCA1997-08, year: 1997
- Authors: Olga VIZIKA, Jean-Patrick DUQUERROIX
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas injection, heterogeneous wettability, three-phase relative permeability, dual energy CT scanner, history matching, sandpack
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1997/SCA1997-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1997-08)

Gravity-assisted gas injection in water-wet, oil-wet and layered-wettability sandpacks shows an oil-wet stratum cuts recovery; simulations using homogeneous-core Kr and Pc reproduce the heterogeneous case.

The paper examines how a decimetric wettability heterogeneity affects gas injection. Gravity-assisted secondary gas injection was run in ~50 cm long unconsolidated sandpacks (porosity ~0.35, permeability ~10 D): uniformly water-wet, uniformly oil-wet (silylated), and two water-wet sections separated by a 2 cm oil-wet layer, with 3-phase saturation profiles from dual-energy CT. Oil recovery was 86.8% OOIP (Sorg 11%) in water-wet, 74.8% (21%) in oil-wet and only 67.6% (24%) in the heterogeneous pack, because the oil-wet layer behaves like a fracture that isolates and traps oil in the upper water-wet part. History matching of homogeneous cases gave three-phase relative permeabilities, which together with centrifuge capillary pressures reproduced the heterogeneous recovery and gas profiles well. Omitting capillary pressure in the heterogeneous simulation gave a flat gas profile and poor recovery prediction.

## Wettability Effects in Three-Phase Flow Due to Gravity Drainage

- ID: SCA1999-46, year: 1999
- Authors: C.A. Grattoni, M.B. Pingo Almada, X. D. Jing, R.A. Dawe
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gravity drainage, three-phase flow, Bond number, Capillary number, wettability, bead pack
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-46.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-46)

Gravity drainage experiments in water-wet and oil-wet bead packs show Capillary number alone fails to correlate oil recovery; a new combined Bond-Capillary number correlates it well.

The study examines how wettability affects three-phase gravity drainage by gas, relevant to gas-cap expansion and crestal gas injection. Experiments used transparent glass bead packs (20x10x0.6 cm, porosity 42%) with water, paraffin and air, in water-wet and oil-wet states, at connate water and after waterflooding. Capillary and Bond numbers were redefined for three-phase flow using fluid morphology and saturation-weighted density differences. In water-wet primary drainage the Bond number rose linearly to 2.24, while in the oil-wet case it was nonlinear and stayed below 1, with a more fingered front. After waterflooding, Bond numbers reached 1.61 (water-wet) and 1.1 (oil-wet). A combined number N = a NB - b(μd/μg)NCa correlated oil recovery well, whereas the Capillary number alone did not.

## Vertical Water Injection in a Hetereogeneous Sand Column; Experiment and Analysis

- ID: SCA2000-45, year: 2000
- Authors: Svein H Midtlyng, Jostein Alvestad, George Virnovsky
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gravity stable waterflood, gamma ray saturation monitoring, capillary continuity, mixed wettability, history matching, vertical flow rig
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-45.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-45)

A vertical sand column with embedded cores was waterflooded and gamma-ray monitored; simulation showed capillary continuity between sand and cores was lost, so the long-rig concept failed.

Gravity-dominated waterfloods in mixed-wet North Sea reservoirs show falling oil saturation behind the front, and properly scaled SCAL tests would need a long vertical rig. A small-scale test column was built from packed sand with one reservoir core and two Bentheimer cores cast in with epoxy, aged in crude, and flooded with water at 1 ml/h from the bottom for 47 days, then shut in for 13 days and flooded for 8 more. Gamma-ray attenuation gave saturation profiles. Water breakthrough came at 15.4 days with 369 ml of oil produced, and total production was 398 ml, or 80% of STOOIP. History matching with a 1D two-phase model indicated that capillary continuity between sand and cores was broken, and that the sand stayed water-wet (Amott index 0.9) because aging failed. Post-breakthrough oil came mainly from the sand. A steady-state analytical model that assumes capillary continuity reproduced the profiles only qualitatively and mismatched the cores.

## Experimental Investigation of the Gas-Oil Drainage Performance of Water Flooded Rock

- ID: SCA2002-04, year: 2002
- Authors: Sjaam Oedai, John van Wunnik
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas-oil gravity drainage, centrifuge relative permeability, residual oil saturation, saturation history, three-phase relative permeability, oil spreading
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-04.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-04)

Centrifuge gas-oil gravity drainage experiments on water-flooded sandstone plugs show lower remaining oil than waterflooding, slightly worsened by waterflooding and largely restored by oil re-saturation.

The study assesses gas-oil gravity drainage (GOGD) with immiscible gas in a water-flooded sandstone reservoir. Plugs from channel, shoreface and mouthbar facies were aged and spun in air in an automated centrifuge with camera detection of produced oil and water, following three histories: drainage at connate water (OG), after waterflood (OWG), and after waterflood plus oil re-saturation (OWOG). ## Estimation of Oil Relative Permeability from Gravity Drainage Experiments: Models and Accuracy

- ID: SCA2002-13, year: 2002
- Authors: B. PEDRERA, H. BERTIN, G. HAMON, L. DALLET
- Method: data analysis or correlation
- Keywords: gravity drainage, oil relative permeability, gamma-ray attenuation, inversion method, saturation profile noise, capillary pressure
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2002/SCA2002-13.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2002-13)

Oil relative permeability from gravity drainage is derived by analytical and inversion methods; both agree, but saturation noise makes low-saturation values unreliable, and the analytical result is a good inversion starting point.

Gas/oil gravity drainage experiments were run on 1 m long, 5 cm diameter Aerolith-10 sintered cores (porosity about 38%, permeability 5-7 D) at irreducible water saturation, with saturation profiles measured by gamma-ray attenuation. Oil relative permeability was computed with an analytical method that uses the final capillary-equilibrium profile for capillary pressure, and with the CAROPT inversion code. Tests on simulated profiles recovered the input Corey exponent well (3.1 vs 3). Adding noise of ±2 saturation points, or the observed ±10 point noise, gave good averaged curves at high oil saturation but a spurious slope change toward optimistic values at low saturation. Inversion matched the analytical results when initialized with the analytical kro, the measured Pc, and fitted to recovery plus the last profile, but its error bars were large near residual oil saturation. Flow regime at very low oil saturation therefore cannot be concluded from these data.

## Details of Gravity Drainage of Heavy Oil During Vapour Extraction

- ID: SCA2004-23, year: 2004
- Authors: L. James, I. Chatzis
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: VAPEX, heavy oil, micromodel, gravity drainage, butane vapour, pore-scale film flow
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2004/SCA2004-23.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2004-23)

Glass micromodel VAPEX experiments with butane show live oil drains in only one to two pores at the interface, and interface velocity predicts live oil rates.

VAPEX heavy oil recovery was studied in etched glass micromodels with different pore structures, using n-butane at 23°C against oil-saturated models at 25°C, while tracking interface position and butane uptake. The interface advanced linearly with time except near the top, at about 0.69 pores/h (0.12 cm/h) in model OC-1 and 1.33 pores/h in DC-1. Diffusion times exceeded drainage times, implying convective mixing and an effective diffusivity near 2.5E-05 cm2/s.

## High-Resolution Imaging of High Mobility Ratio, Immiscible Displacements

- ID: SCA2005-16, year: 2005
- Authors: Guo-Qing Tang, Anthony R. Kovscek
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: viscous fingering, mobility ratio, X-ray CT saturation imaging, waterflood, shock mobility ratio, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2005/SCA2005-16.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2005-16)

CT imaging of waterfloods in a homogeneous Berea core with oils of 0.97-155 cp shows front instability grows with mobility ratio and rate; shock mobility ratio predicts stability.

Waterflood of viscous oil at high mobility ratio lacks fine-scale in-situ saturation data. A 52 cm, 377 md strongly water-wet Berea core, characterized by CT for porosity and permeability and found homogeneous, was waterflooded with n-decane, blandol and drakeol (endpoint M of 0.07, 2 and 12) at 0.35, 1.05 and 2.11 m/d. Reviews lab-scale VAPEX heavy oil experiments and scale-up equations, and reports interface advancement measurements showing production rate scales with model height and interface velocity is length-independent.

VAPEX solvent-based heavy oil recovery lacks standardized core analysis and scale-up methods. High-pressure coreflood tests in a mixed-wet Clashach sandstone show near-miscible gas, WAG and SWAG injection give high oil recovery, with SWAG highest at breakthrough.

## Impact of Displacement Rate on Relative Permeabiilty and Residual Saturation in Gas-Assisted Gravity Drainage Process

- ID: SCA2008-11, year: 2008
- Authors: S. Ghotbi
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gravity drainage, gas injection, Hagoort method, Corey relative permeability, critical rate, capillary end effect
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2008/SCA2008-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2008-11)

Gas-injection gravity drainage experiments in sand packs, analyzed with an extended Hagoort method, show higher injection rates raise residual oil and lower oil relative permeability.

Increasing gas injection rate to speed gravity drainage raises viscous forces relative to gravity, which may reduce displacement efficiency. The authors ran nitrogen/n-decane downward displacements in vertical sintered glass-bead packs (3-4 cm diameter, 60 and 120 cm long, about 3.5-16.5 Darcy) at rates from 0.5 to 5 times the critical rate. They extended Hagoort's backward method to give Corey-type oil relative permeability from production data without pressure data, and history-matched gas relative permeability at high rates. Higher injection rates gave higher gas and lower oil relative permeability and much higher residual oil saturation above the critical rate. In lower-permeability, capillary-dominated packs, capillary end effect correction was needed, and higher rates partly produced oil held by the capillary threshold height.

## Microscopic Displacement Efficiency of Alkane and CO2-Based Gaseous Solvents with Heavy Oil

- ID: SCA2010-19, year: 2010
- Authors: M. Darde, G. Darche

Three 1D corefloods of extra-heavy oil with C2-C3, CO2-C3 and pure CO2 solvents gave large microscopic recoveries (83%, 79%, 58%), but history matching awaits PVT data.
