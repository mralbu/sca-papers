## Visualization of Two- and Three-Phase Flow in Fractures Using X-Ray Computed Tomography

- ID: SCA2006-25, year: 2006
- Authors: Freddy E. Alvarado, Abraham S. Grader, Phillip M. Halleck
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: x-ray computed tomography, fracture aperture, closure stress, three-phase flow, asperity contact area, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 2 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2006/SCA2006-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2006-25)

High-resolution x-ray CT maps fracture aperture versus closure stress and the distribution of water, benzyl alcohol and decane in tensile fractures in Berea sandstone.

Multiphase fracture flow modeling needs fracture geometry under stress and phase occupancy, which earlier methods could not provide. Tensile fractures were created in 25 mm Berea sandstone cores and scanned dry at about 25 micron resolution under confining pressures of 3.5 to 17.2 MPa. Raising stress from 3.5 to 17.2 MPa cut fracture volume from 99.4 to 83.2 mm3 and mean aperture from 273 to 229 microns (15%). Asperity area rose from 1.58% to 5.69%, and the roughness factor from 1.03 to 1.12. Using tagged fluids, two-phase scans showed mobile oil at irreducible water saturation as disconnected globules while the wetting water stayed connected. Three-phase scans (water/benzyl alcohol/decane at 25.7/41.6/32.7%) showed decane globules in large-aperture regions, surrounded by benzyl alcohol.

## Property Measurement in Naturally Fractured Devonian Shale Cores Using a New Pressure Pulse Method

- ID: SCA1993-01, year: 1993
- Authors: Xiuxu Ning, Jin Fan, Stephen. A. Holditch, W. John Lee, Texas A
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: pressure pulse method, naturally fractured core, Devonian Shale, matrix permeability, fracture conductivity, leak compensation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-01.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-01)

Extends a pressure pulse method to fractured low-permeability cores with multiple or off-center fractures and gas leaks, and measures matrix and fracture properties in eleven Devonian Shale cores.

Matrix and fracture properties of naturally fractured, extremely low permeability cores are hard to measure separately. The authors extend their pressure pulse method with simplified parallelepiped models for off-center and multiple fractures. They derive new analytical solutions that include gas leaks and add a numerical leak compensation based on the late-time pressure decline. Eleven Devonian Shale cores from well FMC#78 were tested with history matching. Matrix porosity was 1.7–5.4%, matrix permeability 1.9e-9 to 5.5e-8 md, fracture conductivity 6.65e-6 to 1.03 md-in, and fracture width 0.13–3.15 microns. Two cores without continuous fractures were artificially fractured to make testing feasible.

## Prediction of Fracture Direction using Shear Acoustic Anisotropy

- ID: SCA1987-11, year: 1987
- Authors: David P. Yale, Eve S. Sprunt
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: shear wave birefringence, acoustic extinction, shear acoustic anisotropy, maximum horizontal stress, anelastic strain relaxation, stress-relief microcracks
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1987/SCA1987-11.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1987-11)

Shear-wave birefringence and acoustic extinction on oriented cores identify maximum horizontal stress and hence fracture direction, outperforming anelastic strain relaxation in naturally fractured formations.

Fracture direction, which controls drainage and waterflood patterns, follows the maximum horizontal in situ stress. Stress-relief microcracks formed in cores are aligned preferentially, producing shear-wave birefringence. The authors describe two techniques on oriented cores: vertical shear velocity anisotropy with parallel-polarized transducers, and acoustic extinction with cross-polarized transducers rotated through 360°. Signal at extinction is 5 to 50 times smaller than at 45°, allowing anisotropies as small as 0.1% to be resolved, whereas shear velocity anisotropy is typically only 1–5%. In cores from six naturally fractured wells, strain relaxation was 90° in error in 42% of cores versus 7% for acoustic anisotropy. Acoustic results were within 15° of strain relaxation, tiltmeter, core fracture and minifrac overcoring directions.

## High-Velocity Pressure Loss in Sandstone Fractures: Modeling and Experiments

- ID: SCA1999-27, year: 1999
- Authors: E. Skjetne, T. Kløv, J. S. Gudmundsson
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: Forchheimer equation, self-affine fracture roughness, inertial resistance, weak inertia regime, Berea sandstone, gas flow in fractures
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1999/SCA1999-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1999-27)

A centripetal-acceleration model predicts inertial resistance scales as a power law of fracture width set by roughness exponent; nitrogen flow in fractured Berea cores confirms it.

High-velocity pressure loss in rough fractures is modeled as pressure drag from centripetal acceleration as flow tubes impinge on self-affine wall roughness. The model gives an inertial resistance b proportional to w^-(3/z-2), with z the roughness exponent. Nitrogen flow experiments were run on longitudinally fractured Berea cores (7.45 cm long, 3 cm diameter) with 20 fracture widths set by steel shims from 50 µm to 1 mm. Data were fitted with the Forchheimer equation at Re of about 8 to 1861. The fitted power gave z = 0.77 (95% CI 0.72-0.83), close to profilometry values of 0.67-0.75, with a residual width of about 200 µm, roughly one grain size. The Forchheimer linear term was negative for some larger widths, and a weak inertia regime preceded the Forchheimer regime, with crossover near Re 400 to 800 for wider fractures.

## Effects of Shear-Fracture Displacement and Orientation on Fracture Topology and Absolute Permeability

- ID: SCA2007_52, year: 2007
- Authors: Sultan Al Enezi Abraham Grader, Phillips Halleck
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: shear fracture, Berea sandstone, X-ray micro-CT, Klinkenberg permeability, fracture aperture distribution, bedding orientation
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2007/SCA2007_52.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2007_52)

Shear-fractured Berea sandstone samples were CT-scanned and tested for permeability; fracturing raised permeability 19-110%, with displacement and bedding orientation controlling fracture connectivity and porosity.

Prior studies of shear fracture effects on permeability were inconsistent and did not relate flow to fracture morphology. Four dry Berea cores (25 mm by 70 mm), cut parallel or perpendicular to bedding, were fractured in triaxial compression at 10.3 MPa confining; two were displaced a further 0.2 mm. Nitrogen Klinkenberg permeability was measured before and after fracturing, and micro-CT (0.030 x 0.030 x 0.033 mm voxels) was used to map fracture porosity and aperture. Permeability increased in all samples (19% and 35% for parallel, 110% and 60% for perpendicular). Perpendicular fractures were larger and better connected; displacement reduced fracture porosity and enlarged asperity areas. Displacement increased permeability gain in the parallel case but reduced it in the perpendicular case.

## Stressed Permeability in Shales: Effects of Matrix Compressibility and Fractures - a Step Towards Measuring Matrix Permeability in Fractured Shale Samples

- ID: SCA2016-027, year: 2016
- Authors: Marisa B. Rydzy, Jorge Patino, Nours Elmetni, Matthias Appel
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: stress-dependent permeability, pulse-decay, micro X-ray CT, fractured shale, permeability hysteresis, Montney
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-027.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-027)

Pulse-decay permeability versus confining stress on ten Montney shale plugs classified by micro-CT fracturing shows fractures dominate stress dependence and hysteresis; matrix permeability can be estimated at high stress.

Fractures in shale plugs, mostly induced during coring and handling, can dominate gas flow and mask matrix permeability. Ten as-received Montney plugs were classified by micro-CT fracture density and type, then tested by pulse-decay at 1900 psi pore pressure through a confining-stress cycle from 2700 to 8300 psi. Permeability fell exponentially with stress and then stayed nearly constant on unloading. CT of a Vaca Muerta mini-plug showed hairline fractures closing at about 1000 psi and thicker ones at about 4000 psi. An equation that predicts permeability stabilization at high stress gave projected matrix permeabilities of about 1-298 nD for some samples, while others could not be fitted.

## Impacts of Wettability on Oil Recovery in Fractured Carbonate Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2000-25, year: 2000
- Authors: Arne Graue, Robert W. Moe, Thomas Bognø
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: wettability alteration, fractured chalk, nuclear tracer imaging, waterflooding, Amott-Harvey index, history matching
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 4
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2000/SCA2000-25.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2000-25)

Nuclear-tracer 2D imaging of waterfloods in whole and fractured chalk blocks at two wettabilities, history-matched with a simulator, shows similar recovery but different recovery mechanisms.

The study examines how wettability affects waterflood recovery in fractured carbonate using large Rørdal chalk blocks (about 20x10x5 cm), made strongly or moderately water-wet (Amott-Harvey Iw 0.4-0.8) by aging in crude oil at 90°C. Blocks were waterflooded whole and then with embedded or interconnected fracture networks, while 22Na and 60Co tracers imaged injected and in-situ water saturations in 2D. Recovery was similar for both wettabilities when the permeability increase after fracturing was low (about 42%PV oil in strongly water-wet CHP-12, versus 32%PV in fractured moderately water-wet CHP-14, with a permeability increase of about 11). At strongly water-wet conditions fractures held up the water front by capillary hold-up, whereas at moderately water-wet conditions water crossed the fractures early. SENSOR simulations matched best with measured Pc and optimized kr, and including wettability heterogeneity improved the match for CHP-14. Matching also required more capillary contact across fractures at less water-wet conditions, suggesting an additional mechanism not in the model.

## Complementary Imaging Techniques Applying Nti and MRI Determined Wettability Effects on Oil Recovery Mechanisms in Fractured Reservoirs

- ID: SCA2003-08, year: 2003
- Authors: A. Graue, B.A. Baldwin, E. Aspenes, J. Stevens, D.P. Tobola, Green Country Petrophysics
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: nuclear tracer imaging, magnetic resonance imaging, fractured chalk, wettability, water bridges across fractures, spontaneous imbibition
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-08.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-08)

NTI on fractured chalk blocks and MRI on stacked plugs show wettability, fracture width and rate control how water crosses open fractures via hydraulic filling or bridges.

Water movement across fractures in Rørdal outcrop chalk was studied at three wettabilities (Iw about 1.0, 0.6-0.7, 0.3) to understand oil recovery mechanisms in fractured reservoirs. Nuclear tracer imaging (about 1 cm resolution) monitored waterflooding of large fractured blocks, while MRI (about 300 µm) imaged decane in 1-3.5 mm fractures between stacked plugs, with D2O replacing brine. At strongly water-wet conditions, water did not enter the open fracture until the inlet plug reached its spontaneous imbibition endpoint, even at 5 times field rate, and then filled the fracture hydraulically. At moderately water-wet and nearly neutral-wet conditions, water drops grew into bridges across the fracture well before the imbibition endpoint, giving viscous communication. Bridge formation depended on fracture width (none at 3.5 mm for Iw=0.6), injection rate and wettability, with larger drops at lower water wetness.

## Mapping Fracture Apertures Using Micro Computed Tomography

- ID: SCA2003-50, year: 2003
- Authors: Z. Karpyn, A. Alajmi, C. Parada, A. S. Grader, P.M. Halleck, O. Karacan.
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: micro-CT, fracture aperture map, layered Berea sandstone, thresholding segmentation, fracture-matrix flow
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2003/SCA2003-50.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2003-50)

Micro-CT images of induced fractures in layered Berea sandstone yield 2D aperture maps and 3D fracture volumes, showing larger apertures where the fracture crosses high-permeability layers.

The paper addresses the difficulty of resolving fracture topology with medical CT scanners. Layered Berea sandstone cores (18-22% porosity, 25-50 mm diameter) were fractured with a Brazilian-type test and scanned with a medical HD250 (0.25x0.25x2 mm) and an industrial OMNI-X micro-CT (20-50 µm). Fractures were extracted by thresholding, erosion and dilation, and the distance between the surfaces was projected onto a common plane to build aperture maps. Medical scans overestimated fracture width and blurred detail, while micro-CT resolved apertures from about 50 µm up to about 1 mm and contact asperities. Aperture correlated with the porosity and permeability of adjacent layers, and the 3D fracture volume can be used for multiphase flow simulation.

## Matrix-Fracture Interactions During Gas Injection: A Pore-Scale Experimental Study

- ID: SCA2016-029, year: 2016
- Authors: M. Sabti, A.H. Alizadeh, M. Piri
- Method: digital rock or imaging
- Keywords: micro-CT imaging, three-phase flow, spreading oil layers, fractured Berea sandstone, layer drainage, gas injection
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 2
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/2016/SCA2016-029.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA2016-029)

Micro-CT imaging of gas injection into a partially fractured Berea sample shows spreading oil layers keep matrix-fracture oil connectivity at high gas rates, while threshold pressure and gas accessibility govern at low rates.

The study examines whether spreading oil layers help move oil from matrix to fracture during gas injection in fractured porous media. A miniature Berea sandstone plug (10 mm × 40 mm, fractured over half its length) was imaged with a Zeiss VERSA micro-CT at 2.5 µm resolution during oil drainage (Swi 31%) and secondary gas injection with a doped brine/Soltrol/nitrogen system at 3.45 MPa and 20 °C. Gas rates from 0.015 to 7 ml/min were used, and images were taken at the fracture, fracture end and matrix-only locations. At low rates, gas filled the fracture and drained large matrix pores adjacent to the fracture by piston-like displacement, controlled by threshold capillary pressure and gas accessibility. At high rates, layer drainage through stable spreading oil layers continued to reduce oil saturation slowly (over three days at 7 ml/min) and maintained matrix-to-fracture oil connectivity, and oil saturations in the fractured and matrix-only sections became nearly equal.

## Imaging Saturation During Flow in Fractured Chalk: Emphasizing Recovery Mechanisms, Capillary Contjnuity and Scaling

- ID: SCA1996-09, year: 1996
- Authors: Bjern Gerry Viksund', Sverre Hetland', Arne Graue', Bernard A. Baldwin
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: nuclear tracer imaging, spontaneous imbibition, fractured chalk, capillary continuity, Ma-Morrow scaling, stacked cores
- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1996/SCA1996-09.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1996-09)

Nuclear tracer imaging of spontaneous imbibition in Dania outcrop chalk, single and stacked cores, shows good capillary continuity across fractures and improved rate collapse with Ma-Morrow scaling.

The study quantifies the roles of viscous and capillary forces in fractured chalk, ahead of a planned 2D fractured chalk reservoir model. 22Na-labelled brine and a movable detector gave in-situ 1D brine saturation profiles during spontaneous imbibition and waterflood in Dania outcrop chalk plugs, including 1D, 2D and 3D exposure and horizontally and vertically stacked cores acting as fractured systems. Low-permeability core CHD-9 (5 mD) pulled brine from CHD-8, giving a saturation jump at the fracture. The Ma and Morrow scaling brought the imbibition curves into close agreement for different exposed areas and somewhat closer for different lengths and stacked systems.

- Cited by 1 papers in the corpus, cites 0

X-ray CT monitoring of water invasion and NaI tracer diffusion in a fractured chalk core shows the natural fracture, likely calcite-filled, blocks both convection and diffusion.

Fine natural fractures in contaminated fractured chalk from the Negev may be sealed, complicating remediation planning. ## The Determination of Fracture Toughness of Rocks by Chevron-Notched Brazilian Disk Specimens

- ID: SCA1990-14, year: 1990
- Authors: X. L. Zhao, J.-C. Roegiers, M. Guo
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: fracture toughness, chevron-notched Brazilian disk, Mode I fracturing, hydraulic fracturing, size effect, stress intensity coefficient
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1990/SCA1990-14.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1990-14)

Proposes the chevron-notched Brazilian disk for rock fracture toughness in lateral crack directions; tests on sandstone and limestone show higher toughness for the smallest disks.

Existing ISRM methods (Short Rod, CENRBB) give only Mode I toughness and restrict crack direction, so lateral propagation relevant to hydraulic fracturing cannot be tested. The authors adapt the chevron-notched Brazilian disk (CDISK), deriving a dimensionless stress intensity coefficient Y* from the Munz simplified method and straight-crack solutions, and tabulating minimum values for disk and saw sizes. Thirty-six disks (2.125, 3.125 and 4.125 in. diameter) of Vosges sandstone and Indiana limestone were dried and loaded in an MTS frame at 0.04 in/min with load cycling. Mean K_Ic was 0.76, 0.48 and 0.58 MPa√m for sandstone and 1.55, 1.36 and 1.25 MPa√m for limestone at 2.125, 3.125 and 4.125 in. respectively. The smallest disks gave the highest toughness (37% above 3.125 in. for sandstone), indicating a size effect. The compressive stress near the crack tip is argued to limit the process zone, so toughness can be taken directly from peak load.

## The Measurement of Compressibility of Fractured Rock

- ID: SCA1993-18, year: 1993
- Authors: X.L. Zhao, Q. Ma, J.-C. Roegiers
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: continuum damage mechanics, damage tensor, rock compressibility, fracture porosity, hydrostatic confining pressure, Berea sandstone
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1993/SCA1993-18.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1993-18)

Uses continuum damage mechanics and cyclic uniaxial loading with hydrostatic pressure to measure compressibility of damaged Berea sandstone and Cordoba Cream limestone, finding compressibility rises with damage.

Fracture-based models need crack dimensions and counts that are hard to estimate, so the authors use continuum damage mechanics with a measurable damage tensor D. Berea sandstone and Cordoba Cream limestone cores (2.125 in. by 4.25 in.), cut in three directions, were subjected to cyclic uniaxial loading to create damage, interspersed with hydrostatic confining pressure tests (up to 10,000 psi for Berea, 3,000 psi for limestone) to measure compressibility. P- and S-wave velocities gave dynamic moduli. Compressibility increased with damage, and the initial change with pressure related to damage level, while at high pressures compressibility was nearly independent of damage. Theory agreed with measurements at large damage but underpredicted compressibility at small damage, attributed to less crack sliding and pore-dominated closure. Higher Poisson's ratio gave a larger compressibility ratio.

## Characterization of Fractures and Multiphase Flow in Fractured Permeable Rocks Using NMR Imaging Techniques

- ID: SCA1994-02, year: 1994
- Authors: Songhua Chen, Xiaoli Yao, Jinli Qiao, A. Ted Watson
- Method: measurement technique or apparatus
- Keywords: NMR imaging, inversion recovery spin-echo, fracture characterization, T1 weighting, fractured sandstone, two-phase displacement
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-02.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-02)

Inversion-recovery spin-echo NMR imaging selectively suppresses fracture or matrix signal to identify fractures, image matrix faster, and monitor two-phase displacement in fractured cores.

Fractures in permeable rock are hard to detect with conventional spin-echo NMR images because of poor contrast and signal-to-noise between fracture and matrix. The authors use an inversion-recovery spin-echo sequence with the inversion time chosen to null either the fracture or the matrix signal, exploiting their different T1 relaxation. Tests used Berea, Arkansas Gold and Calico samples and Indiana limestone with natural or artificial fractures (beads up to 250 µm) in a 2-T imager. Fractures under about 100 µm and low-porosity fractures in laminated Berea were identified unambiguously, and suppressing fracture signal revealed matrix bedding in Calico limestone. Shorter TR/TI pairs cut imaging time to 40% with better S/N. Apparent fracture widths were larger than bead sizes because of linebroadening. A D2O-oil displacement in a fractured Arkansas Gold sandstone showed water bypassing through the fracture in the central region.

## Evaluation of Fracture Aperture and Wettability, Capillary Properties of Oil-Bearing Fractured Granite

- ID: SCA1994-10, year: 1994
- Authors: Pham Anh Tuan, Martyntsiv O.F, Tran Le Dong
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: fractured granite basement, fracture aperture, resistivity-permeability relationship, Amott wettability index, contact angle, spontaneous imbibition
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 1
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1994/SCA1994-10.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1994-10)

Lab study of fractured granite from offshore Vietnam: fracture aperture from simultaneous permeability and resistivity, plus wettability and imbibition showing water-wet matrix and intermediate-wet fractures.

Fractured granite basement reservoirs (Bach Ho field, offshore Vietnam) have macrofractures, microfractured matrix and tight matrix. Air-water capillary pressure curves on ten full-size cores (226-19250 mD, porosity 3-10%) gave a fracture/vug partition coefficient of 0.22-0.46, so microfractured matrix holds most pore volume; matrix cementation exponent m was 1.36. Amott-IFP (130-140°C, 50 bar) and contact-angle tests showed water-wet matrix (WI>0.8, 41-62°) and mostly intermediate-wet fracture surfaces (75-112°). Spontaneous imbibition gave high recoveries, higher than in carbonate matrix of similar porosity, and was lower in samples without residual water.

Fractured granite of the Bach Ho field is highly heterogeneous, which complicates core analysis. ## SCA1995-26

- ID: SCA1995-26, year: 1995
- Authors: David P. Tobola, Bernard A. Baldwin
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: capillary continuity, fractured chalk, magnetic resonance imaging, gas-oil displacement, water-oil displacement, fracture contact area
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-26.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-26)

Gas and water displacement of decane in fractured chalk cores with MRI shows contact area matters for gas drive but not water drive; natural fractures behaved as fully continuous.

The study examines capillary continuity across fractures in chalk using Niobrara outcrop cores with sawn fractures at 100, 10 and 3% contact area and two naturally fractured North Sea cores. Decane displacement at residual water by nitrogen or D2O injection was monitored by production profiles and MRI saturation images. ## Dry Gas Injection in Fractured Chalk Reservoirs - an Experimental Approach

- ID: SCA1995-27, year: 1995
- Authors: Lars Qlyno, Noway. Knut Uleberg, NTH. Curtis H. Whitson
- Method: laboratory experiment
- Keywords: gas injection, fractured chalk, molecular diffusion, gas-oil IFT, vaporization, gravity drainage
- Cited by 0 papers in the corpus, cites 0
- [PDF](https://www.jgmaas.com/SCA/1995/SCA1995-27.pdf) · [On the site](https://mralbu.github.io/sca-papers/#SCA1995-27)

High-pressure lean gas injection experiments on stacked chalk cores with a fracture annulus, plus compositional simulation, show vaporization and diffusion give significant oil recovery, with gravity drainage limited by IFT gradients.

Gas injection in fractured Ekofisk chalk was studied with composite cores (up to ~52 cm of stacked Liege outcrop chalk, plus one Ekofisk core) in a holder whose 1.5 mm annulus simulated a fracture. Recombined Ekofisk fluids were used at 6000 psia and 270°F, with lean separator gas circulated in the fracture at different initial water saturations, for secondary and tertiary cases. Little free oil was produced, and the gas became enriched in a few days. The recovery is consistent with the pre-test PVT estimate of 30% OOIP for a gas/oil volume ratio of 1.9. A 3D pore-scale model couples Navier-Stokes flow with reactive transport in rough fractures, showing permeability first rises by dissolution then falls by mineral precipitation.

The paper addresses how coupled flow and geochemical reactions change fracture permeability in geological formations. It develops a model coupling Navier-Stokes flow with a reactive transport model in 3D rough fractures, including mineral dissolution and precipitation. The model is solved with a mixed finite element method in the open-source FEniCS platform. Preliminary results show permeability rising rapidly early through self-enhanced dissolution, then decreasing significantly later due to mineralization. Fluid composition strongly affects the process dynamics, and interactions between fractures control the magnitude and distribution of permeability changes.

Triaxial flooding of North Sea sandstone with in situ micro-CT shows fracturing raises permeability, with the enhancement ratio inversely related to initial matrix permeability and flow concentrated in fractures.

The study examines how flow evolves as fractures form and interact with the matrix in North Sea sandstone. Single-matrix and composite (low/high permeability) 25.4 mm plugs were fractured in a custom triaxial flow cell under water injection (250 psi confining, 200 psi inlet) while pressure, flow rate and load were recorded. Dual-resolution micro-CT (12.7 and 25.4 µm) was combined with a U-Net-type CNN trained on synthetic fractured volumes to segment matrix, pores and fractures (Jaccard 0.86 in 2D). The fractured-to-unfractured permeability ratio k_D reached about 5.5 for the single-matrix sample and 3.5 for the composite, and was inversely related to initial matrix permeability. Fracture apertures ranged from 10 to 400 µm, and flow stayed concentrated along fractures with diminishing matrix contribution.

Validated multi-scale digital rock models of laminated Cangdong shale simulate depletion recovery, showing micro-fractures add about 7% recovery at plug scale and boost early well production.

The paper examines how primary, secondary and micro-fractures affect oil recovery in a laminated shale oil reservoir (Kong-2, Dagang oilfield). Sister plug A was characterized with HF NMR, pressure decay, GRI crushed analysis, pyrolysis and MICP (porosity 7.8%, Swanson permeability 2.3 µD, median throat radius 33 nm). Plug B was imaged at six resolution levels and upscaled into a digital rock model (porosity 6.5%, permeability 2.5 µD) that matched plug A. Density functional hydrodynamics simulations of depletion showed micro-fractures help most in the poorly connected, clay-rich upper layer, reducing its initial water saturation from 31.5% to 14.5%. At plug scale, primary and secondary fractures recovered 14.5% of the oil and micro-fractures added about 7% more. Well-scale models of an 1840 m, 80-stage horizontal well showed the upper layer contributes little, while micro-fractures raise early rates but speed up decline.
