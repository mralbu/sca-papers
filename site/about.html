About

The Society of Core Analysts (SCA) has held a symposium every year since 1987, and its papers are the working literature of core analysis: capillary pressure, relative permeability, wettability, NMR, resistivity, digital rock. The papers are mirrored at jgmaas.com as some two thousand PDFs sorted by year. This site puts all 2,064 of them on one searchable list and one map, so you can see what has been written on a subject, which papers the community keeps citing, and what sits next to a paper you already know.

A personal project, not affiliated with the SCA. The papers belong to the SCA and their authors.

How the papers were organized

The corpus is every PDF on jgmaas.com, 1987–2026. A few clean-ups decide what counts as a paper:

  • For 1991 and 1992 the server has a one-page preview ("click here to download the complete version") next to each full paper. The 97 previews are dropped.
  • 1990–1992 had a separate European symposium, whose papers reuse the same numbers. They are kept as their own series, with IDs ending in EURO.
  • One-page short papers and posters (2019, 2022, 2023) have their own title and authors, so they stay.
  • One PDF (SCA2014-051) is truncated on the server and cannot be read. The mirror has almost nothing for 2020.

Each paper keeps the ID from its file name (SCA2014-051, SCA_2025_1011), and that ID is its link on this site: #SCA2014-051.

Reading the papers

marker turns each PDF into Markdown, with its figures and captions. About 200 papers, all of 1987–1993 among them, are scans without a text layer; marker reads those with OCR. From the Markdown, hand-written rules take the title, the authors, the abstract and the reference list. Where the rules find nothing, nothing is guessed.

A large language model then read each paper once and wrote the TL;DR, a short summary, keywords, a method label from a fixed list of eight (laboratory experiment, numerical simulation, digital rock or imaging, …) and its pick of the paper's most telling figure. The model could also fill a title, author list or abstract the rules had missed, but only if a check confirmed it against the paper's own text: that added authors for 39 papers, abstracts for 4 and titles for 2, and rejected 6 titles it had composed. The abstract shown on the site is always the paper's own. Everything the model wrote is marked AI-generated on the site, and can be wrong: the PDF is the reference.

Embedding

To place papers by what they are about, each one becomes a vector: an embedding of its title, abstract and the first ~500 words of its introduction (math, tables and Markdown removed), made with bge-m3. Papers with similar vectors are about similar things. The Similar tab of each paper lists its 8 nearest neighbours by cosine similarity.

bge-m3 was chosen over SPECTER2, a model trained on scientific papers, with a test the embeddings never see: papers that end up close together should cite the same work. With bge-m3, a paper's nearest neighbours have more similar reference lists (0.158 TF-IDF cosine, against 0.147 for SPECTER2 and 0.055 for random pairs).

The map

The embeddings have 1,024 dimensions. UMAP squeezes them to two for the map, keeping each paper near its closest neighbours. Separately, it reduces them to 10 dimensions, where HDBSCAN finds dense groups: 30 topics of at least 20 papers each. Papers that fit no group well (352 of them) are put in the nearest topic and drawn fainter. The topics are named by the language model from each one's distinctive words (c-TF-IDF over titles and abstracts, as in BERTopic) and its most-cited titles.

Citations and importance

Each reference in a paper's list is matched to a paper in the corpus, by an explicit SCA number (SCA2003-12, SCA 9114) or, more often, by a fuzzy match of the title together with the year and the first author's surname. When the number and the title disagree, the title wins, because cited numbers are often mistyped. A hand check of samples found nearly all matches correct, and 92% of the references that mention the SCA are matched. The result is a graph of 1,663 citations between SCA papers.

Cited by counts the citations a paper receives from other SCA papers; PageRank also weighs who cites it (scaled so the average paper scores 1). Both count only citations inside this corpus: a paper cited mostly in journals can score low here.

What is published

Only derived data: titles, authors, years, abstracts, topics, map coordinates, citation links and scores, the AI summaries and one small thumbnail of a figure per paper. The PDFs, their full text and their reference lists are not published; every paper links to its PDF on jgmaas.com.

For language models

llms.txt lists the topics with links to one file per topic (papers in PageRank order, with their summaries), and llms-full.txt has every paper on one line each.
